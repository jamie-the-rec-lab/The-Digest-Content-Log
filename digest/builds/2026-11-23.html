Skill Based Hiring: Where Are We At in 2026?
  • 86% of employers in Greenhouse's own survey have changed their hiring process because of fake or AI-generated applications, says CEO Daniel Chait, and 48% of those added a live skills test or work sample.
  • Employers in the same Greenhouse survey rank job skills (60%) and ability to learn (51%) as the most important qualities, yet career history is still the top factor in who advances.
  • When assessments put candidates off, the cause is usually poor communication, argues the panel's job post specialist: tell candidates why it is used, that every candidate does it, what good looks like and how long it takes.
Recruiting Future20 Nov 2026
What Candidate Fraud Looks Like In Practice
Samantha Swift · Chief Marketing Officer @ Cyberr
  • About 20 minutes into the interview, Cyberr's interviewer messaged Swift that the candidate was not who he claimed to be. Asked to switch off his background and pass a hand in front of his face, the candidate refused.
  • Checks after the call traced the phone number the candidate gave to a site offering fake numbers, and the degree on his CV did not exist in that form at the Irish university named.
  • Cyberr's own job advert gave away its technology stack and migration plan, which Swift says made it easy to create a perfectly tailored CV and LinkedIn profile.
10x Recruiting by Metaview19 Nov 2026
AI in Recruiting: How Moniepoint Raises the Hiring Bar
Chloe Seeley · VP and Global Head of Talent @ Moniepoint
  • Within five minutes of finishing an interview, Moniepoint's interviewers get a private Slack message with feedback on four areas, including question quality and rubric coverage, and it always starts with the positives.
  • An executive recruiter on Moniepoint's talent team built the interviewer feedback agent in about two months, pilot included, and now maintains it as part of her role.
  • When an interviewer misses part of the rubric, a Slack automation tells the next interviewer to cover it and suggests a question to ask.
PromptMates Live19 Nov 2026
Inside the Hiring Stack: Live from San Francisco
  • Zapier's Dan McCarthy tests each recruiting task on Claude's Haiku, Sonnet and Opus models against a fixed answer key before choosing one, running it 10 to 100 times per model.
  • In McCarthy's live demo all three Claude models scored 30 out of 30 on a messy pipeline spreadsheet, and Haiku cost $4 per 1,000 runs against $41 for Sonnet.
  • Ashby CEO Benji Encz warns against sending every applicant to an AI interview: shortlist on the CV first, invite only people who could be a fit, then follow up with feedback.
Recruiting Future19 Nov 2026
Restoring Trust In Hiring
Bradley Clark · Head of Talent Acquisition @ Article
  • Article, a furniture brand handling about 20,000 to 25,000 applications a year, saw a growing number of candidate survey comments accusing it of AI rejection, even though a human makes every hiring decision.
  • Clark tells candidates how AI is used at multiple touch points, from the job posting and confirmation email to the rejection email, and each one links back to a single career site page.
  • Candidates at Article hear the interview rule in advance: one warning for suspected use of an AI chatbot mid-interview, and if it has to be said again the interview is over.

TA Unboxed20 Nov 2026
Is sourcing about to make a big comeback?
  • Set aside 20% of your sourcing technology budget or capacity to test alternatives to LinkedIn Recruiter, suggests Darren Bush, then compare shortlist quality, response rates and recruiter hours.
  • Source before the job is advertised: map the market, search your ATS and CRM for past candidates and ask your network, so what you learn can shape the brief at kickoff.
  • Glen Cathey's 'meta-sourcing' means asking people who are not interested or available who else they rate, and Bush suggests tracking those introductions as a team measure.
RecOps Roundup20 Nov 2026
Week of November 16th, 2026
  • Many compensation teams run their own internal study of how much a particular skill or qualification adds to an offer, and recruiters rarely get to see it.
  • Sharing that skills pricing with candidates would be more precise than telling them the company aims for the middle 50% of the salary range, the lead piece argues.
  • The lead piece floats a candidate-facing salary calculator, where the likely offer rises or stays flat as interviews uncover skills, and admits most companies are not built for it.

  • UK vacancies fell by 22,000 (3.0%) to 690,000 in August to October 2026, according to early estimates from the Office for National Statistics.

Labour Market Outlook: Autumn 2026
  • 62% of UK employers plan to recruit in the next three months, near historical lows, in the CIPD survey of 2,051 senior HR professionals and decision-makers.

AI For Humans20 Nov 2026
OpenAI's New Astra Is Messy But Gemini Neon Is... Good?
  • Early testers including Every's Dan Shipper said OpenAI's new GPT-6.2 Astra was not great, and the hosts relay OpenAI's response that the model has been changed since that early testing.

AI & I by Every18 Nov 2026
Notion's Founder on the One Thing Your AI Is Missing
Ivan Zhao · Cofounder and CEO @ Notion
  • Notion's Ivan Zhao sorts software into three buckets: tools worth being able to modify yourself, everyday tools someone else should craft for you, and enterprise software you rely on for security, which he personally would not recommend building.
  • A year ago people said software was dead and you could vibe code everything, Zhao says, and nine months ago they realised home-built tools need maintaining.
  • Zhao argues the durable thing a business owns is its data and context, because AI agents can now write both the workflow logic and the interface around it.
EDPB · news11 Nov 2026
EDPB selects automated decision-making in recruitment as the topic of its 2027 coordinated enforcement action
  • The EU's data protection regulators have picked automated decision-making in recruitment, including AI-driven decisions, as the topic of their sixth coordinated enforcement action.
  • The EU action will focus on candidates' right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing, and their right of access, where automated candidate screening tools are used.
  • National data protection authorities in the EU will join on a voluntary basis in the coming weeks, and the action itself will be launched in the course of 2027.

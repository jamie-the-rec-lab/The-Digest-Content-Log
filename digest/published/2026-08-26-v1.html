In this issue

One theme keeps surfacing: the durable human skills AI cannot copy (domain knowledge, character, communication and critical thinking) are what make a hire last. On the ops side the message is simple, every step of your process has to earn its place. There is also fresh jobs data, UK permanent hiring steadying after a 45-month decline, while US openings run well ahead of actual hires. Plus new releases from Greenhouse, Ashby and Juicebox with MCPs that plug your recruiting tools into ChatGPT and Claude, and finally the EU is delaying its new rules for AI in hiring.

Recruiting Future26 Aug 2026
What strategic HR really means
Jackson Lynch · Founder @ Talent Sherpa
  • CHRO turnover spikes in the first 18 months under a new CEO, because the incoming boss brings a fresh thesis and the sitting HR leader is still running the system built for the last one.
  • Agree what the new CEO actually wants from HR in your first 90 days. Lynch's point: they assume you will adapt, and by the time the gap shows, the trust has already gone.
  • Strategic HR means explaining your work in revenue, margin, speed and risk, not HR metrics. Lynch says most HR leaders cannot connect what they build to the numbers the business is judged on.
PromptMates Live26 Aug 2026
Case study: hiring with AI recruiting agents
Joe Leggat · Founding Talent Operator @ Jack & Jill
  • Candidates talk to Jack, the candidate-side AI agent, with no role in front of them, so you get an honest read on what they actually want instead of a pitch tailored to your job.
  • Intro response rates run around 40% because candidates are pre-engaged, far above standard cold outreach where signal-to-noise is the usual problem.
  • Agents handle coordination, custom intros and note-taking, but Leggat is blunt that scheduling still needs a human, because the AI has no view of a candidate's real life.
Offer Accepted by Ashby25 Aug 2026
Hiring for durable skills to build teams that last
Veronica Salcido · Head of Talent @ GrowthAssistant
  • When GrowthAssistant audited failed hires, over 80% traced back to durable skills like character, communication and growth mindset, not the hard skills the scorecard was built around.
  • Thread those durable skills through hiring, onboarding, coaching and reviews, so the signal you screen for is the signal you develop and measure later.
  • Get the CEO or founder to champion the framework first. Salcido found that is the fastest way to make a hiring model stick across the org.
Building the Talent Machine25 Aug 2026
The craft of great recruiting
Cameron Murray · Founder @ Venture Talent
  • The best talent is available for about three days after they hear about your company, so a slow early-stage process loses people before the first call.
  • Treat candidates like clients, not calendars. Murray flips the usual dynamic where the hiring manager's schedule wins, and books around the candidate instead.
  • Recruiters earn the right to push back on a bad brief by first delivering a couple of unconventional profiles that land, which builds the trust to say 'don't hire that person'.
Recruiting Roadshow by Greenhouse25 Aug 2026
Fast in the right direction
Andrew Delabar · Talent Operations @ Hudson River Trading
  • If eight in ten candidates clear your hiring-manager screen, that stage is just agreeing with the recruiter, and Delabar says you have added five days for a rubber stamp.
  • Track reschedule, decline and pass-through rates on every stage, then cut or redesign any step that does not actually change a decision.
  • Speed without direction is slow. Delabar's line: a fast, directionless recruiter is like a race driver flooring it without knowing the track.
Brainfood21 Aug 2026
From talent acquisition to organisational performance
  • Time-to-fill and cost-per-hire only tell half the story. The panel's push: measure whether a hire actually improved business performance, not just how fast the seat was filled.
  • Durable skills matter more as AI spreads, because character, communication and critical thinking are the parts a model cannot do for you.
  • Curiosity with data is the core skill for TA leaders: learn your managers' KPIs and bonus drivers so you become a resource-allocation adviser, not a headcount filler.
10x Recruiting by Metaview20 Aug 2026
Why honesty is a competitive advantage in executive recruiting
Ted Maguire · Founder @ Opal Partners
  • Maguire maps 1,000 to 4,000 people per exec search and ranks each one, spending six to eight weeks upfront so outreach starts from the right list, not a funnel of whoever replies.
  • Start outreach with your second-tier targets, not the stars, so the interview panel and materials are tested before you approach the people you most want.
  • Drop the sales pitch. Maguire argues senior candidates have finely tuned detectors, and only pure honesty, including telling a client their comp is too low, actually works.
The Debrief20 Aug 2026
The hardest pitch in TA: mission-driven talent and the long game
Darin Lim Yankowitz · SVP Recruitment @ Teach For America
  • Nearly 90% of Gen Z say purpose is essential to their career and almost half have walked away from a role over poor values alignment, so mission has to be sold, not assumed.
  • Teach For America builds a campus flywheel: each cohort that admires the year above makes the next intake easier to recruit, turning 20 hires into 25, then 35.
  • Gen Z prospects care about the next 24 months, not a 15-year arc, so a current member or recent alum showing 'who you could become' beats any title or status pitch.

TA Unboxed21 Aug 2026
Would this change how you view AI interviewing?
  • Instead of a rejection, TA Unboxed floats giving every AI-interviewed candidate a feedback report on a three-band scale, Developing, Meets, Strong, rather than a fake numeric score.
  • Build in a right of appeal: if the AI missed context, candidates need a route to a human, or the report is just a nicer-looking rejection.
  • The backdrop is a trust problem: 48% of applications get no response at all, so transparent feedback becomes both a compliance move and an edge.
PromptMates Newsletter24 Aug 2026
One recruiter, a team of bots, one daily briefing
  • One recruiter ran a full hire from inside Slack using five single-purpose bots for email, the ATS, offers and onboarding, wired through one coordinator bot.
  • Three slash-commands, post-role, log-call and flag-for-offer, cover about 80% of the Slack workflow, with the bots handling the rest in the background.
  • Measure sourcing agents by conversations started, not candidates surfaced. Their rule: ten real conversations in a week is a good week.
Full Stack Recruiter15 Aug 2026
Why the AI job numbers look fine, unless you're 23
  • Employment for 22 to 25-year-olds in AI-exposed roles sits about 19% below where it would be if it had kept pace with their less-exposed peers.
  • The hit runs through hiring, not firing: young workers in exposed roles are not being pushed out, there are simply far fewer openings for them.
  • Codified, teachable knowledge gets automated first, so point juniors at roles heavy on tacit skills like reading a room, handling escalations and supplier relationships.
RecOps Roundup21 Aug 2026
Week of August 17th, 2026
  • Spot bulk AI applications by the email pattern: auto-apply tools often generate addresses like 820jeremy@gmail to route replies, since most people do not start an email with numbers.
  • The advice is not to auto-flag those candidates, but to audit how many are in your system and how far they progress before deciding anything.
  • A shared read this week: Johnny Campbell's argument that a typical recruiting process has 80 steps but only 18 are non-negotiably human, so question the rest.

Remade.mdJul 2026
Stop tokenmaxxing, start ROI-maxxing
  • Gate every AI experiment at two weeks: it need not prove full ROI, but it must show it earns another sprint, which kills zombie pilots.
  • A worked example: Nobel Recruitment lifted sourcing reply rates from 19.7% to about 40% by having agents pull missing context from the ATS and transcripts, not the recruiter's memory.
  • Fund by evidence, not seniority. Everyone gets a baseline budget to experiment; the bigger scale budget is earned by results, so a junior with a winning idea can outspend a director.

iCIMS · reportAug 2026
Workforce Report, August 2026
  • US job openings ended July 17% above the 2025 baseline while hires stayed flat, the widest demand-to-hiring gap iCIMS has recorded this year.
  • Manufacturing openings jumped 29% above baseline but hires fell 6%, so employers have applicants in the funnel and are struggling to convert them.
  • The manufacturing applicant pool is getting younger: under-45s were 85% of applicants in July, up from 79% a year earlier.
REC / KPMG · reportAug 2026
UK Report on Jobs, August 2026
  • Permanent placements stabilised in July, ending a 45-month decline, with London and the Midlands posting renewed growth, in the capital at a near four-year high.
  • Temp billings rose again, among the strongest growth in three years, and demand for short-term workers increased for the first time in two years.
  • Pay pressure is building even in a soft market: starting-salary inflation hit a six-month high and temp wage growth a 26-month high.
Pin · reportJul 2026
Recruiting Outreach Benchmarks 2026
  • LinkedIn messages reply at 17% versus about 5% for automated recruiter email, a 3.4x gap, so lead outbound with LinkedIn where you can.
  • The first three touches earn 93% of all the replies a sequence will get, so long chase sequences add effort for almost nothing.
  • Pairing email with LinkedIn more than doubles response: two-step multi-channel sequences hit 46% reply versus 20% for email alone.
EU AI Act · newsAug 2026
High-risk AI hiring rules delayed to December 2027
  • The EU's Digital Omnibus, in force from 27 July 2026, pushes the AI Act's high-risk obligations for recruitment and employment AI from 2 August 2026 back to 2 December 2027.
  • Transparency duties still bite now: you must tell candidates when AI is used, and the ban on emotion recognition in hiring stays in force.
  • So the compliance clock eases, but do not down tools. Keep documenting how your AI screening and interviewing tools reach their decisions.

  • Your existing permissions and admin controls carry over, so the AI only sees what the user is already allowed to see.

How I AI by Lenny’s Podcast24 Aug 2026
How I manage 15 AI agents 24/7 as a solo founder
Ryan Carson · Solo Founder @ Untangle
  • Carson's 'Watchdog' habit: when he feels out of the loop, he runs one routine that checks each account for activity and errors, a repeatable status prompt you could copy for your live reqs.
  • He manages many agents with a dead-simple system: a top-priority 'P0' folder for what matters now and a paper list of weekly priorities, not a fancy dashboard.
  • More output is not more value. Carson deliberately ships less and spends the saved time talking to real customers, a useful check on 'automate everything' energy.
Lenny's Podcast9 Aug 2026
The playbook for building high-talent-density teams
Adam Ward · Head of Talent @ Cursor
  • The 'funnel of doom': reach out to 100, get 20 replies, and by definition those are not the top 20%, just the people you caught on a slow day.
  • Go after the 1% with three steps: scope exactly what great looks like, map the market to name the roughly 50 people who fit, then relentlessly pursue them.
  • Ask referrers a sharp question, not a vague one. 'Who's the most collaborative engineer you've worked with?' beats 'who's the best engineer you know?'

The recruiter's judgment call
Stefanie Fackrell · Founding Recruiting Lead @ Glimpse
  • Fackrell trains the AI first screen against the hiring manager's actual intake criteria, then passes only the top 10 to 15% through to protect senior leaders' time.
  • She spots an AI-written CV by its identical format and interchangeable summary paragraph, then ignores formatting entirely and reads the achievement bullets for real data.
  • In nine weeks at Glimpse she hired 18 people and grew the company by 65%, running 20 to 30 interviews a week solo across 15 open roles.
Brainfood15 Sep 2026
Future of hiring
Matt Wilson · Co-founder & CEO @ Jack & Jill
  • Jack & Jill runs paired agents: Jack represents the individual jobseeker, Jill represents the hiring company, and Jill connects employers to people already inside Jack's network.
  • Jack opens with a 15 to 20 minute call with a jobseeker, roughly a recruiter coffee chat, then scans every job listing posted online on their behalf.
  • Jack already works for close to 400,000 jobseekers, concentrated in London, San Francisco and New York.
PromptMates Live14 Sep 2026
Build a candidate landing page with Claude
Dan · Senior Talent Engineer @ Zapier
  • Give Claude a job description plus a screenshot of a company page and tell it to lift the colours, typography, spacing and layout, and it returns a branded candidate landing page.
  • Package the finished prompt into a reusable skill and the next role's landing page takes about 60 seconds rather than an hour.
  • Use Sonnet rather than Opus for this kind of build, and crop the design screenshot tightly instead of grabbing the whole page, to keep the cost down.
Recruiting Future11 Sep 2026
Is AI creating talent debt?
Matt Campbell · Managing Director @ Alvarez & Marsal
  • 80% of AI projects fail, roughly twice the rate of ordinary IT projects, because organisations run them as a technology rollout rather than an organisational change.
  • Campbell's five As sort every task: avoid, assist, augment, automate, autonomous. Map your team against them before anyone opens a headcount spreadsheet.
  • 80% of white collar employees use AI their IT team never signed off on, so the individual gets the benefit while the organisation carries the data risk.

Recruiting's Next AI Problem Isn't Speed
  • 67% of HR professionals would let AI interview candidates autonomously, against just 34% of employees, in a BambooHR study of 1,608 US workers.

TA Unboxed11 Sep 2026
The problem with TA reporting and what to do about it
  • An ageing req is a lagging signal rather than an early warning, because by the time it ages the problem has usually already happened.
  • Manager signals are the most underused of the three: time to review CVs, time from interview requested to first interview completed, and shortlist engagement.
  • TA reports time to fill and source of hire, while senior leaders want to know when the person starts and which roles are putting delivery at risk.

iCIMS · reportSep 2026
Workers Are Teaching Themselves AI Skills Faster Than Employers Train Them
  • US job openings finished August 13% above the August 2025 baseline while hires managed only 2%, and iCIMS reads the gap as a conversion problem rather than a sourcing one.
  • Self-teaching for AI skills rose from 22% to 30% in a single year among US job seekers, while employer-provided training moved from 19% to 17%.
  • 61% of the 1,000 US job seekers iCIMS surveyed report proficiency with a GenAI tool like ChatGPT or Copilot, against 18% for prompt engineering and 17% for AI ethics or governance.
ONS · report15 Sep 2026
Labour market overview, UK: September 2026
  • The Office for National Statistics estimates UK vacancies fell to 702,000 in June to August, the lowest outside the pandemic since 2014.
  • UK payrolled employees fell by 145,000 in the year to August 2026 to 30.2 million, on an early estimate the ONS calls provisional and likely to be revised.
  • UK pay excluding bonuses grew 3.5% in the year to May to July, but the private sector managed 2.9% against 6.3% in the public sector.
Bullhorn · report15 Sep 2026
Staffing Industry Indicator, week ending 5 September 2026
  • US staffing hours were up 7% on a year ago in the week ending 5 September, adjusted for holiday impact, across a sample of US firms using Bullhorn's software.
  • US Light Industrial staffing hours ran 12% up year over year, the highest level of 2026, while Office and Clerical was the only segment in decline at 7% down.
  • US temporary help employment has grown by roughly 8,500 jobs a month from January through August, a contrast with the decline pattern of 2023 to 2025.
Indeed Hiring Lab · report25 Aug 2026
UK Labour Market Snapshot, August 2026
  • UK job postings on Indeed grew 0.8% in the month to 14 August but are still down 9.4% year over year and 31% below early-2020 levels.
  • UK youth unemployment for 16 to 24 year olds now stands at 16.2%, close to a 12-year peak and up from 9.3% in mid-2022.
  • AI now appears in the description of 9.4% of UK job postings, and Indeed finds AI in job titles is more common outside tech than inside it.

GeMCP

How I AI by Lenny's Podcast16 Sep 2026
Muse gets AI agent UX right
  • Muse asks consent in stages, first "may I go find out some information about you?" and then whether it may use what it found, rather than one auto-approve toggle.
  • Its activity feed logs every tool call behind a finished task in a readable view, so you can see exactly how a result was produced.
  • Good permission design does not buy good execution: Muse's shopping run missed the right shoe colourway and pulled an apparently wrong cinema listing.
City & Guilds · news14 Sep 2026
Employability report reveals growing human skills shortage as employers turn to AI recruitment tools that prioritise technical skills
  • 64% of UK businesses say candidates with employability skills such as face-to-face communication and problem solving are getting harder to find, in a City & Guilds survey of 908 employers.
  • 38% of UK employers told City & Guilds that shortages in employability skills are restricting productivity and business growth.
  • 69% of UK employers surveyed believe AI screening tools prioritise technical skills over the human capabilities their business needs, and 43% already use AI to screen or filter candidates.

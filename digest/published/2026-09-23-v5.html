This week in the world of recruiting

Candidate fraud comes up twice this week, from opposite sides. Lauren Furey of Proof, an identity verification vendor, points to organised programmes run out of North Korea. Adrian Russo, a VP of Talent rather than a vendor, puts the motive on stealing IP and names China too. Both want it owned by IT, security and an executive sponsor, not by whoever buys the tool. Elsewhere, Plaid runs recruiting support like a helpdesk, and Jake Lamb has built 20 Claude skills across his hiring funnel. Bullhorn puts US staffing hours 11% above last year, and Pin and Workable shipped new features. Colorado and Connecticut now require employers to tell candidates when an automated tool is used in hiring, and to explain it.

Candidate fraud is a business problem
Adrian Russo · VP of Talent @ Swish Analytics
  • Watch your baseline applicant volume by role: Russo's full stack engineer openings normally draw about 500 applicants, so an unexplained jump to a thousand with no campaign change should raise alarms.
  • Russo puts the fraud motive on stealing IP rather than a paycheck, naming China and North Korea as the main source countries and Iran as a much smaller factor.

Recruiting Future18 Sep 2026
The Real Risks Of Candidate Fraud
  • Furey works at Proof, which sponsors this episode and sells identity verification into exactly the problem she is describing.
  • The proxy trick: a candidate attends one interview stage, then someone else takes the next, which works because there is "no through line for who are you expecting to meet with" once a new interviewer joins.
  • Furey's fix: verify identity before the hiring-manager call or phone screen rather than at application, saving the photo in the ATS as a reference point, or gating access behind a biometric selfie for tougher cases.

How to Build Claude Skills for Recruiting
  • Jake Lamb built a screen summary skill that combines a CV with the Ashby notetaker transcript, cutting his writeup time from four or five hours a week to about 30 minutes.

What Happens When AI Starts Doing the Work?
  • Recruiting work is often evaluation rather than generation, so build a "judgment model" that returns a decision plus a confidence score, useful for outreach QA and application triage.
  • In a Zapier survey of 518 executives at companies with an AI governance policy, just 15% give AI full unsupervised autonomy, while 38% say ungoverned AI has already cost them time, money or reputation.
  • Basis turned its onboarding knowledge into an AI-executable skill and compressed a two-hour first-day process into 30 minutes.

Quality, cost and capacity: the TA metrics that matter
  • Google's internal research found four interviews enough to predict whether someone should be hired with 86% confidence, a ceiling worth knowing before you add a fifth round out of habit.
  • TA Unboxed's worked example for a quality of hire composite scores structured interview hires at 4.3 out of 5 against 3.1 out of 5 for informal chat hires. It is an illustration of how to build the measure, not presented as a benchmark.

TA Unboxed18 Sep 2026
The 60 metrics of TA: market, funnel and speed
  • Gem's 2025 benchmark for a three-stage outreach sequence is a 26% reply rate and a 14% interested rate.
  • Their worked example has sourced candidates converting at 15 times the inbound rate, 6 hires from 80 sourced against 2 from 400 inbound.
  • Time to fill and time to start are different measures, and their example shows a 40-day time to fill against a 92-day actual time to start once notice periods and checks are counted.

Staffing Industry Indicator, week ending 12 September 2026
  • US staffing hours ran 11% above 2025 levels in the week ending 12 September, even with a short Labor Day week in the mix.

MultiState · news21 Sep 2026
State AI employment laws: what changed for hiring, monitoring and bias in the 2026 session
  • States considered 155 bills addressing AI in the workplace this legislative session, and only Colorado's SB 189 and Connecticut's SB 5 actually enacted notify-and-explain rules for automated hiring tools.

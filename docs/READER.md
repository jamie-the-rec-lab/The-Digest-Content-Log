# Reader profile: who the digest is for

Read this before selecting AND before writing. Every item is chosen and phrased
for THIS reader. If an item does not serve them, drop it even if it is in window.

## Who they are
Talent acquisition people across the UK, the US and English-speaking Europe.
Mostly in-house TA leaders, recruiters and RecOps, plus switched-on agency folk.
Commercially minded, sharp, and time-poor. They are recruiters, not engineers.

No single market is the default. An item does not have to be relevant everywhere,
but it does have to be clearly relevant somewhere, and the reader has to be able
to tell which.

## Where they are with AI (this is the important bit)
Early-intermediate, and honest about it. They have built some lightweight
automations, and maybe done a bit of "vibe-coding" that clearly looks
AI-generated rather than crafted. They can drive AI tools but have not gone deep,
and they never will have as much hands-on time as a specialist, because they
have a full recruiting job to hold down. So:
- Do not pitch to beginners (they are past "what is a prompt").
- Do not pitch to builders or engineers either (assume they cannot and will not
  write real code or stand up infrastructure).
- Meet them ONE realistic step ahead of where they are.
- When a source shows something advanced, for example someone building their own
  tool, translate it into what this reader could actually do with an
  off-the-shelf tool or a simple workflow, never "go and build the integration
  yourself".

## What they want from each item
Either a tactic they could try this week, or a number they could quote to a
stakeholder. Practical over theoretical, always.

They are also deciding what to spend their limited listening and reading time on
this week, and there is far more published than they can get through. So every
item does two jobs at once. It gives them something they can use without leaving
the page, and it tells them honestly what they would get by clicking through.

**The digest is a way into high quality content, not a substitute for it.** The
point is not to save them from ever opening the source. It is to make it obvious
which two or three of this week's items are worth their time, and to be right
about it. An item they click into and find thinner than the entry promised costs
more trust than a week with fewer items.

## They care about
Usable AI in hiring, real benchmarks, named tools, concrete tactics, and sharp or
contrarian takes that would change how they act.

## They do not care about
Vendor puff, generic "future of work" thought-leadership, AI hype with no real use
case, and anything that needs engineer-level skill or a big time investment to
action.

## Market context (matters now the audience is mixed)
- **Always name the market on a data point.** "UK permanent placements fell for a
  45th month" not "permanent placements fell". A reader must be able to tell at a
  glance whether a figure is about their market. Same for US, EU and global
  numbers.
- **Spell out a local body or law if the name alone would not land.** A few words
  is plenty. Do not over-explain.

## Level and tone
Plain English. Explain any jargon in one clause. Assume they can drive tools but
cannot read code. Respect their time. Sharp and useful, never basic, never
preachy.

**Language:** British English spelling throughout, which is the house voice.
But avoid UK-only shorthand that will not travel. Use the term the wider industry
uses, and where a word genuinely differs by market, pick the one the item is
about or make it plain. CV and resume are the obvious pair. Never an em dash or
an en dash.

## Access (hard rule for report items)
Only feature a report the reader can actually open for FREE. If the full report is
behind a paywall or needs a PAID subscription to read, do NOT include it in the
digest, even if it sits in the library. A free email signup is acceptable; paying
money is not. The digest link must never lead the reader to a paywall. When a
report's headline summary is free but its full data is paid, only include it if the
link goes to the free summary and the bullets rely only on the free figures.

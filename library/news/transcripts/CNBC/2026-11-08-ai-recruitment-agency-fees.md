# AI is speeding up recruitment, and putting agency fees under pressure

- **Source:** CNBC (Lindsay Dodgson, reporter)
- **Date:** 2026-11-08
- **URL:** https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/ai-recruitment-agency-fees.html
- **Fetched:** 2026-11-09
- **Extraction:** partial. The article was read through a page-summarising fetch, not as a raw copy, so the figures and quotes below are what that extraction returned and the wording is not guaranteed to be verbatim. Single-source market-news item (not regulatory or legal); the survey figures are the article's citations of Bullhorn, LinkedIn and Adecco, which were not checked at source.

## Key points
- UK-focused piece on whether AI efficiency will push recruitment agency fees down. Agencies typically charge a percentage of a placed candidate's first-year salary. Recruiters quoted are split.
- A 2026 Bullhorn survey of nearly 300 UK and Irish recruitment professionals found 37% said AI made candidate screening 26% to 50% faster.
- LinkedIn's 2025 UK Future of Recruiting report found talent acquisition professionals using generative AI saved an average of 18% of their working week.
- Adecco says its UK AI recruitment tools saved around 15% of the time spent on recruitment processes; across the Adecco Group, AI has supported more than 2.5 million candidate interactions, about half outside normal working hours. Adecco is running six AI recruitment agents in the UK and France.
- VIQU Group (Birmingham) reports turnover up 47% over 18 months.
- Matt Collingwood (VIQU) predicts only a third of today's agencies may survive the next five years; James Wright (Velocity Leadership) says 60% to 70% of staff at large executive search firms could lose their jobs.
- David Berwick (Adria Solutions): simply forwarding CVs is becoming hard to justify: "AI is making that service increasingly difficult to justify."
- Matt Collingwood: "I see every reason for our fees to hold firm, or even increase, if the value we deliver continues to grow."
- Paddy Lambros (Dex): "It's very expensive, it's very slow."
- Chris Stirling (Stirling Q&R): "AI is making the easy part of recruiting faster."
- Niki Turner-Harding (Adecco UK and Ireland): AI agents do not make hiring decisions; humans do.

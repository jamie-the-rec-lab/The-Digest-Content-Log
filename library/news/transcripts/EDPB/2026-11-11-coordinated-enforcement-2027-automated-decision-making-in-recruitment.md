## Full text of the EDPB news item

EDPB News, 11 November 2026

Brussels, 11 November – During its latest plenary, the EDPB has adopted a statement on the first review of the functioning of the adequacy decision of the Republic of Korea and the final version of the recommendations on the application for approval and on the elements and principles to be found in Processor Binding Corporate Rules (BCR-P). In addition, the Board selected the topic for its sixth coordinated enforcement action (CEF).

The EDPB statement on the first review of the Republic of Korea adequacy decision focuses mainly on the assessment of amendments to the Korean data protection legal framework, both on the commercial aspects and concerning access and use of personal data by Korean public authorities*.

"The mutual recognition of equivalent data protection standards between the EU and the Republic of Korea in 2025 represented a major step confirming the growing alignment between both regulatory frameworks. We welcome amendments in the Korean data protection legislation that have increased the level of protection of individuals and facilitated the processing of data by controllers while maintaining a level of protection essentially equivalent to that of the GDPR." EDPB Chair, Anu Talus

For example, the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) was amended to introduce a new general right to withdraw consent, a right to request the suspension of processing of personal data, as well as a right to refuse an automated decision (incl. by AI systems) or request an explanation if the decision significantly impacts the data subjects' rights or obligations.

Furthermore, regarding onward transfers, the EDPB welcomes the changes to the PIPA on the legal regime applicable to international transfers of personal data. The changes clarify the need for cross-border transfers of personal information to rely on specific transfer tools (Art. 28-8 PIPA), offering therefore alternative solutions to consent.

The EDPB also welcomes the strengthening of enforcement and sanctions powers of the Korean DPA (PIPC).

The EDPB agrees with the Commission's proposal to consult the Committee established under Art. 93(1) of the GDPR to conduct a review of the adequacy decision every four years.

### Final version recommendations Processor BCR adopted

Following public consultation, the EDPB has adopted the final version of the recommendations on the application for approval and on the elements and principles to be found in BCR-P. BCR-P are a transfer tool that can be used by a group of undertakings or enterprises to transfer personal data outside Europe to processors within the same group. BCRs create enforceable rights and set out commitments to establish a level of data protection essentially equivalent to the one provided by the GDPR.

The recommendations are now applicable to all BCR-P holders. All BCR-P holders, as well as all new and ongoing BCR-P applicants should bring their BCR-P in line with the recommendations, either during the application process or as part of their 2028 annual update, depending on their specific situation.

In line with the Helsinki statement's objective to strengthen the dialogue with stakeholders, the Board has also released a report on the outcome of the dedicated public consultation, as well as a track changes version of the recommendations.

### Coordinated Enforcement Framework: topic selected for 2027

The EDPB picked the topic for its sixth coordinated enforcement action, which will concern automated decision-making (ADM), including AI-driven ADM, in recruitment. More specifically, the coordinated action will focus on the enforcement of the right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing and the related right of access, in the context of the use of automated candidate screening tools, including AI-driven ones. DPAs will join this action on a voluntary basis in the coming weeks, and the action itself will be launched in the course of 2027.

You can find more information about the CEF, as well as the reports on the previous CEF actions, here.

### Note to editors

* On 17 December 2021, the European Commission adopted an adequacy decision recognising that the data protection framework of the Republic of Korea offers an adequate level of data protection. The adoption of an adequacy decision involves: a proposal from the European Commission; an opinion of the European Data Protection Board; an approval from representatives of EU countries; the adoption of the decision by the European Commission.

The Commission is required to periodically review the adequacy decisions, and to report its findings to the European Parliament and the Council. Regular reviews ensure that the essential equivalence standard is preserved.

The process for the first review of the functioning of the adequacy decision took place in 2025 and, in particular, consisted of an online review meeting on 17 October 2025. The Commission invited the EDPB to take part in the review meeting in line with Recital 221 of the Korean adequacy decision.

On 23 July 2026, the European Commission issued the Report to the European Parliament and the Council on the first review of the functioning of the adequacy decision for the Republic of Korea, along with a Commission Staff Working Document (SWD(2026) 231 final). The Commission concluded that the Republic of Korea continues to ensure an adequate level of protection for personal data from the European Union to entities in the Republic of Korea subject to its amended data protection framework.

In light of the outcome of the review, the EDPB agrees to the European Commission's proposal to move to a four-year review cycle of this adequacy decision.

## Second source: Handley Gill, "Use of AI in Recruitment Target of EU GDPR Data Protection Enforcement Drive" (Nicola Cain, 19 November 2026)

Extracts read, verbatim. This is a consultancy's commentary and it sells services in this area.

Confirmation of the announcement:

"On 11 November 2026, the EDPB announced that European Data Protection Authorities' Coordinated Enforcement Action for 2027 would target the use of automated decision-making (ADM), including AI-driven ADM, in recruitment, focusing on the enforcement of the Article 22(1) GDPR right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing and the related Article 15(1)(h) GDPR right of access, in the context of the use of automated candidate screening tools, including AI-driven ones."

Pull quote, Nicola Cain, CEO & Principal Consultant, Handley Gill Limited:

"While adoption of AI across the recruitment industry and hiring process has been widespread, with AI often embedded throughout the recruitment lifecycle, an understanding of the legal implications of such AI use and the implementation of appropriate safeguards has not. And now organisations risk being exposed. The imminent enforcement action announced by data protection authorities across Europe should provide the catalyst for data controllers to audit their use of AI in recruitment and urgently adopt any necessary remediation to ensure that if they are contacted by regulators, they have a positive story to tell."

On the EU AI Act: "Will the Coordinated Enforcement Action consider the application of the EU AI Act to the use of AI in recruitment? Not directly." It quotes the Coordinated Enforcement Framework: "GDPR is the basis and the authority under which the annual coordinated action within the Coordinated Enforcement Framework will be executed" and "other relevant EU legislation… will be left outside the scope".

On the UK: "While the UK ICO is no longer a member of the EDPB, it had already identified the use of automated decision-making in recruitment as one of its priority focus areas in its 2025 AI and Biometrics Strategy." It adds that the ICO's updated Recruitment and Selection guidance "remains outstanding and overdue", and that the ICO published in March 2026 a report titled "Recruitment Rewired: an update on the ICO's work on the fair and responsible use of automation in recruitment".

Areas it says organisations should focus on (its list):

- "establishing a lawful basis for processing"
- "recognising when they are making a decision, which has a legal or similarly significant effect, including when they are rejecting candidates on more than binary grounds (e.g. if they have the right to work)"
- "implementing meaningful human involvement effectively"
- "ensuring that special category personal data is not being inferred"
- "conducting a data protection impact assessment ('DPIA')"
- "publishing sufficiently granular transparency information, including meaningful information about the logic involved, and the significance and envisaged consequences of processing for the individual"
- "conducting, or assessing the results of, monitoring for fairness and bias and accuracy of inferred data"
- "granting candidates the ability to contest a decision in a timely manner"
- "ensuring that data processing agreements do not permit the use of candidate data for AI training purposes"
- "complying with data subject rights requests"

What it says to do now:

"Organisations shouldn't wait until they are contacted by their supervisory authority as part of the Coordinated Enforcement Action to investigate and, if necessary, remediate their use of AI in recruitment." Its three steps: "Implement an audit of their use of AI in recruitment to understand whether and how AI is being used, for what purposes, using what models, what information is provided to affected individuals and what safeguards are in place"; "If AI is in use in recruitment, analyse whether any of their use cases fall within the scope of the prohibition at Article 22 GDPR, i.e. involve a solely automated decision having a legal or similarly significant effect and, if so, whether they meet the relevant conditions for processing"; and "Be alert for communications from the supervisory authority and ensure that these are promptly directed to the DPO or your data protection lead."

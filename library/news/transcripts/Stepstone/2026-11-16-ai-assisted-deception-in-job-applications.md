- **Source:** The Stepstone Group, Düsseldorf (press release from its "Hiring Trends Update", distributed via EQS News; the issuer is solely responsible for the content)
- **Date:** 2026-11-16 (09:13 CET per the EQS line)

## Method

- 7,652 candidates and 1,227 recruiters in Germany.
- Fieldwork 24 September to 13 October 2026.
- Candidate data weighted to represent Germany's working population by age, gender and education. Recruiter data unweighted.
- The breakdowns by type of deception are based on the 505 recruiters who identified or suspected AI-assisted deception in the previous twelve months, not on all 1,227.

## Recruiter findings (Germany)

- "Four in ten recruiters (41%) in Germany have identified or suspected cases of AI-assisted deception" in the past twelve months.
- "13% report confirmed cases" and "a further 28% suspect such activity".
- "73% of recruiters say they have become more sceptical when assessing job applications".
- Confirmed cases: "55% have identified exaggerated claims on CVs", "43% fabricated references", "33% AI-generated answers during live job interviews".
- "26% have encountered hidden instructions embedded in application documents" (prompt injections aimed at automated screening).
- "over one in five (22%) report cases where someone other than the applicant attended the interview".
- "A further 8% have even encountered deepfakes in video interviews".
- Suspected but unconfirmed cases: "exaggerated claims on CVs (71%)", "AI-generated answers during job interviews (30%)", "fabricated references (23%)".

## Candidate findings (Germany)

- "Around half of candidates use AI when applying for jobs" (no exact percentage given).
- "One in six candidates have used AI to exaggerate their skills in job applications".
- "59% prefer a recruitment process without AI".
- "61% would not apply for a job if AI made the final hiring decision".

## Spokesperson

Dr Christina Langer, Senior Economist at The Stepstone Group, argues that AI will change how companies assess applications and verify candidates' suitability, and that an honest, imperfect answer in an interview often says more about a person than a polished AI-generated one (paraphrased by the extraction). Verbatim: "It's about getting to know the person behind the application."

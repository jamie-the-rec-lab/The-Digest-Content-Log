The Forward Deployed Engineer is the new unicorn, "the hottest job in tech". The author has seen this before: "When everyone moved to the cloud, every sysadmin job on earth got renamed 'DevOps Engineer' overnight."

(Sponsor section: a satirical advert for "Title Inflation Ltd", a joke sponsor. Flag as sponsor content; not a real product.)

### 1. So what actually is an FDE?
Palantir coined the term in the mid-2000s, embedding engineers directly with US government customers; internally "Deltas" paired with "Echos" (Deployment Strategists). "A normal engineer builds one capability for many customers. An FDE builds many capabilities for one customer." A real FDE writes production code, inside a paying customer's environment, sticks around after go-live, and feeds what they learn back into the core product. It is not new: SAP's engineers were forward deployed when it started out.

### 2. Is the demand real?
"Bloomberry analysed 1,000 FDE job ads and found postings up 1,165% year on year in 2025, with a median base salary of $173,816. Indeed data reported by Business Insider had them up 729% year on year by April 2026, and Indeed India saw a nearly 19x jump by June." "And the big players are putting serious money behind it: AWS has put $1bn into a new FDE organisation, and Google Cloud has committed $750m." Supply: "Gartner's Mukul Saha told Channel Dive there are roughly 2,000 active FDEs", against over 5,000 FDE listings on Indeed in April alone.

### 3. DevOps deja vu
DevOps was coined in 2009 as a set of practices, then turned into a job title. Bloomberry found the FDE title used for three different jobs:
- The Builder FDE, 60% of ads: the real thing, 70-90% coding, 30-50% travel, embedded with customers.
- The Sales Engineer+, 30%: a solutions or sales engineer role with a new hat; demos, POCs, hands off after the sale.
- The Internal Tools Builder, 10%: GTM engineering or RevOps; internal customers only.
"So 60% of the time, it's an FDE" (author's reading of the table above; the other 40% is businesses either inflating titles or not knowing what FDE is). Gartner said lots of providers use "forward deployed" as a label for implementation, pro services, solutions engineering or AI consulting.

### 4. Is your role really an FDE role?
Eight intake questions: who is the customer; when does the job start and finish; how much of the week is writing code and does it go into the customer's production environment; how many accounts at once; who do they report to; is there a quota, commission or OTE; what happens to what they build; how much travel. Mapping table: external customer, post-sale, mostly coding = FDE; pre-sale, demos, lots of accounts, reports into Sales, OTE = Solutions or Sales Engineer; post-sale, configures product, little custom code = Implementation Engineer; internal only = GTM Engineer or RevOps. Tip: tag every FDE candidate who withdraws and log the reason.

### 5. Is your candidate really an FDE? (numbered 6 in the email)
Six first-screen questions (e.g. "Tell me about something you built that's running in a customer's production environment right now. Who looks after it today?"; "Talk me through your worst day on site."). Real FDE: "I shipped", "I owned", measures adoption and uptime. Probably a Solutions Engineer: "I demoed", "technical win", measures deals closed. Caveat: "Looking at 100 FDE LinkedIn profiles, Bloomberry found 22% of current FDEs came from solutions engineering or architecture."

### TL;DR
"The role is real. It's old, it's well paid, and done properly it's genuinely hard." But about 40% of the job ads, per Bloomberry's data, are not really FDE roles.

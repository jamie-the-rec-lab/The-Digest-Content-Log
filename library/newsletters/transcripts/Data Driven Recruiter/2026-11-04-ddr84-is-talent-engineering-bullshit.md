"When recruiters aren't empowered, we fiddle with the stuff we can control. You can't fix the hiring manager who takes ten days to submit feedback, or the comp bands, or headcount planning. But you CAN update your LinkedIn headline in about fourteen seconds."

### 1. What is talent engineering?
Most roads lead to Viet Nguyen, Head of TA at AirOps (ex Netflix and Vercel), whose idea is that "LLMs, MCPs and open APIs finally let you rebuild recruiting like a product, replacing the duct-taped mess of ATS + CRM + Slack + Airtable + Zapier." His build list: sourcing bots that generate qualified pipeline; a market intelligence system tracking competitor moves; an internal referral and backchannel database; AI-powered candidate prep. "Metaview split TA into three seats: Talent Partners (relationships and closing), Talent Ops (process, data, compliance) and Talent Engineers (AI, automation, building). Gem described it as a product manager for TA processes." a16z launched a Talent Engineer Fellowship, an eight-week cohort, with Dan McCarthy (Senior Talent Engineer at Zapier) among the faces. Metaview says that without AI, a talent engineer is "just a fancy ATS admin."
Three camps: recruiter who builds (AirOps, Metaview, Gem); engineer who recruits (a16z fellowship, Rich Zou); ops, evolved (Gem, Headcount365).
"Pave's Hot Jobs Index sorted the explosion of 'X - Engineer' titles into three buckets. AI Engineer is established. GTM Engineer and FDE are genuinely new functions. Talent Engineer landed in the third bucket with Marketing Engineer and Legal Engineer, the “re-designed roles”. Pave’s test for a real emerging job is whether it spreads across more and more companies over time. Talent Engineer doesn't pass that test…yet." Pave also reckons pay inside these bolted-on engineer titles will spread wider. Full Stack Recruiter made the same point: teams rename everyone "partners" while the job underneath stays identical.

### 2. Where does it fit in a recruitment team?
The author's own classification (not gospel): recruiting plus building tools for yourself = AI-enabled recruiter (TA); recruiting plus building tools the team uses = senior recruiter with a systems remit; building and running talent tools, no reqs = technical talent ops (Recruiting Systems Lead, or Talent Engineer); writing production code for hiring products = software engineer. Test: "Will I own any reqs?" and "Will I get canned for not hiring those reqs, irrespective of my output in other areas like tooling?" If yes to both, you're a recruiter. Dan McCarthy argues the first talent hire should come with deep recruiting experience.

### TL;DR
"Its too early to tell. The term is up for grabs." Some people are building an exciting version of talent functions; others use Talent Engineer as a new LinkedIn title. Be clear on your role's actual responsibilities.

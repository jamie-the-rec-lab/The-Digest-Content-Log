# The Future of Recruiting: Talent Advisor vs. Talent Engineer

- **Source:** Full Stack Recruiter
- **Date:** 2026-08-27
- **Fetched:** 2026-09-03
- **Public archive:** https://newsletter.fullstackrecruiter.net/p/future-of-recruiting-talent-advisor-vs-talent-engineer

## Core argument

Recruiting is splitting into two directions as AI absorbs administrative work: the **Talent Advisor** ("the person who helps a hiring manager make better decisions about the role, the market, the interview process, and the tradeoffs behind a hire") and the **Talent Engineer** ("the person who builds the systems, data flows, AI workflows, rules, and tools that let a talent team produce better work with fewer manual steps"). A rare third group becomes a **Talent Architect**, combining both.

## Evidence cited

- Microsoft study of 6,000 knowledge workers (6-month randomized field experiment): workers given a generative AI tool "spent 3 fewer hours on email each week," while meeting time "did not change in a meaningful way" - individual work automates first, collaborative work stays stubborn.
- Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, Lindsey Raymond study of 5,179 customer support agents using generative AI: "Productivity rose 14% on average, while productivity among novice and lower-skilled workers improved by 34%."
- Field experiment with 758 Boston Consulting Group consultants: on 18 tasks that fit GPT's capabilities, AI users "completed 12.2% more tasks and worked 25.1% faster, with quality scores more than 40% higher"; on a task outside the tool's capability, "AI users were 19 percentage points less likely to reach the correct answer."
- Microsoft/Carnegie Mellon survey of 319 knowledge workers, 936 examples of GenAI use at work: "Higher confidence in AI was associated with less critical thinking. Higher confidence in one's own ability was associated with more critical thinking."

## Practical takeaway

For the Advisor path: "take one live requisition and go deeper before you source... Challenge one requirement." For the Engineer path: "pick one repeated task that annoys your team and follow it end to end... Automate one piece."

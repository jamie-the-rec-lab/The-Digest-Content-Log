### Build of the Week: How to Update Your Interview Templates for Pay Transparency Compliance

Alan Price, Head of TA at Deel, updated the company's interview process before the EU Pay Transparency Directive transposition deadline of June 7, 2026. The changes are operationally straightforward, but most recruiting teams have not made them yet.

**Step 1: Audit your current question bank**
Pull every question your team uses in screening calls. Flag anything that asks about current pay, past pay history, or minimum acceptable salary.

**Step 2: Remove the prohibited questions**
Stop asking about current salary, salary history, and minimum acceptable pay. Each anchors the conversation to a candidate's past, not the role's value.

**Step 3: Build the compliant replacements**
Replace with: what are your salary expectations for this role, what is your ideal compensation range, and does the posted salary range work for you? These anchor to the role's value, not the candidate's history.

**Step 4: Update your interviewer training and materials**
The changes only work if your interviewers know them. Update internal guides and add a brief allowed/not-allowed list to interview prep materials.

**Step 5: Set a recurring compliance review**
Pay transparency laws vary by jurisdiction and keep evolving. Schedule a quarterly review against current requirements. The EU Directive is one layer; Colorado's revised AI law adds more if you hire in the US.

The allowed questions do more than satisfy compliance. Asking what a candidate expects for this role focuses the conversation on current market value. It removes historical compensation gaps before they become a negotiation anchor.

### Framework of the Week: The AI Fluency Check

AI adoption is not the same as AI fluency. A team can have tools in place and still lack the habits, judgment, and workflow clarity needed to use them well.

Before adding another AI tool to your recruiting stack, check three levels:

- Individual fluency: Can recruiters explain where AI helps, where it fails, and when human judgment needs to stay in the loop?
- Workflow fluency: Are the steps, data, handoffs, and review points clear enough for AI to support the process without creating a new mess?
- Organizational fluency: Does leadership know which AI bets are strategic, who owns governance, and how success will be measured?

Most teams measure AI adoption by access: who has the tools, who logged in, who tried a prompt. That misses the real question.

The key insight: AI fluency is not whether your team can use the tool. It is whether your team knows how the work should change because the tool exists.

### Big Idea: AI Doubled Recruiter Call Time

A new report from the American Staffing Association and Prodoscore found recruiters spent 286 minutes per week talking to candidates and clients in Q1 2026, double the call time from Q1 2024.

In the same period, average AI tool adoption moved from one tool per recruiter to 1.36.

The key insight here is that the time AI saved did not disappear into productivity reports or headcount reductions. Recruiters used it to pick up the phone.

Stephen Dwyer, CEO of ASA, described the pattern: AI has given recruiters more time to focus on developing the connections that drive long-term growth.

Sam Naficy, CEO at Prodoscore, called doubling call time in two years a significant finding that tells the story of where AI adoption is actually paying off.

Context matters here: applications per hire have tripled since 2021 to more than 300 per open role. Volume exploded.

AI absorbed the administrative load that would have otherwise buried recruiters.

### How to Build an Audit-Ready AI Hiring Process

TA leaders from Anthropic, Metrea, and GoDaddy built defensible AI hiring systems before anyone came knocking. Their shared principle at the Greenhouse Open for Ops session: governance is a pre-launch decision, not a post-crisis binder.

**Step 1: Map every AI touchpoint in your funnel.** List every place AI and automation touch your process: sourcing, screening, scheduling, interviewing, communications, and analytics. Candidates already assume AI runs more of the funnel than it does. You cannot correct a perception you have not mapped.

**Step 2: Document what AI does and what it explicitly does not do.** Build one shared governance document with employment legal and infosec covering the use case, decision variables, training plan, and candidate-facing language. If those partners help write it, sign-off is embedded from the start and the audit record exists before anyone asks for it.

**Step 3: Define what meaningful human oversight actually means.** Apply the filter Elisha Law-Simmonds uses at Anthropic: ask whether the AI use is beneficial for both sides, not just the hiring team. Candidate selection, application review, and interviewing are areas where humans should remain deeply involved.

**Step 4: Govern before you scale.** The governance threshold is when a tool becomes your documented process, the thing you train every new recruiter on. Once it touches every candidate, legal and security should have reviewed it before it reaches that scale.

**Step 5: Build cross-functional relationships before you need them.** Stand up a shared channel with security, legal, and procurement on day one of your AI buildout. A standing cadence with employment legal, where questions move in both directions, is infrastructure, not a courtesy.

The first conversation with legal and security should not happen after you have built something. That conversation earns trust for gray-area asks later, and it catches problems before they reach candidates.

### Worth Seeing & Reading

**AI Hiring Adoption Outruns Results.** ManpowerGroup Talent Solutions and Everest Group surveyed 80 C-suite, CHRO, and senior TA leaders in the U.S. and U.K. More than 90% report actively deploying AI in talent acquisition, but fewer than 5% have achieved transformational results. AI is inside the hiring process. The operating model around it has not caught up.

**Anthropic Launches Claude Corps.** Anthropic launched Claude Corps, a national fellowship program that will train 1,000 early-career workers to use Claude and place them with nonprofits across the U.S. Fellows will receive a $85,000 salary and benefits for a 12-month, full-time placement. The program is backed by an initial $150 million commitment and frames AI workforce disruption as something builders must help absorb, not just explain.

**ICO Scrutinizes Automated Hiring.** The UK Information Commissioner's Office says automated decision-making in recruitment is now a key regulatory focus. After speaking with more than 30 employers, the ICO found many may be using solely automated decisions without the safeguards required under UK GDPR. The warning is direct: transparency, meaningful human involvement, and bias monitoring are no longer optional governance extras.

**AI Efficiency Gets Expensive.** Prof G Markets argues that the AI replacement thesis is running into a cost problem. Enterprise AI spend is rising fast, but many companies still cannot clearly measure the return. For TA and HR teams, the lesson is simple: AI adoption needs cost governance alongside legal and workflow governance, or "efficiency" becomes another untested assumption.

### Framework of the Week: The Collaborative Post Framework for LinkedIn Reach

LinkedIn's collaborative posts let you invite up to five people or Pages to co-author a public post. If they accept, the post appears on their profile too, expanding visibility beyond your own audience.

Before publishing, ask:

- Who has credibility with the audience this post needs to reach?
- Who has a real reason to stand behind this message?
- Whose audience overlaps without duplicating yours?
- Is the post useful to all collaborators, or only to you?
- Does the post make each collaborator look credible, not promotional?
- Is the ask clear enough that collaborators know why they were invited?

Most teams treat LinkedIn distribution as something that happens after the post is written. Collaborative posts flip the sequence. The distribution path needs to shape the post before it goes live. The key insight: collaboration is not a reach hack. It is a credibility decision. AI can help you write the post. Distribution still depends on who is willing to put their name on it.

### Your AI Is Hiring in Its Own Image

Recruiting AI does not only rank candidates. It teaches the hiring team which signals count.

At SHRM26, Jacqueline Grant, founder and CEO of The Management Academy, warned that AI hiring systems can overlook "hidden talent": career switchers, military veterans, workforce program graduates, adult learners, and candidates with nonlinear backgrounds. The problem is not lack of capability. It is recognition. These candidates often carry the right skills in forms the system was not built to see.

Grant names three failure points. Visibility fails when nontraditional credentials never surface. Interpretation fails when experience is not translated into business capability. Confidence fails when hiring managers distrust unfamiliar backgrounds and default to candidates who look like prior hires.

That is where AI risk compounds. If the system favors historical hiring patterns, and humans treat those recommendations as objective, exclusion becomes easier to justify.

Run a 30-day visibility review. Identify which career switchers, veterans, workforce program graduates, adult learners, and nonlinear candidates made it past your first screen, then audit the variables that filtered out the rest.

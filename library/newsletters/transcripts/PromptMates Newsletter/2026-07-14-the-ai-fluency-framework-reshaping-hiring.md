### Build of the Week: How to Wire a Lean TA Stack Around a Single AI Operating Layer

Michael Brown, one month at Counsel Health, assembled an AI-orchestrated recruiting stack without adding headcount, wiring sourcing, ATS, AI layer, and compliance chain around Claude as the connector.

**Step 1: Pick an ATS that handles more than candidate tracking.** Ashby runs AI notetaking, fraud detection, agent automation, scheduling, and dynamic offer letters from one platform. When your ATS covers six functions, your AI layer has one clean data source rather than piecing exports from multiple tools.

**Step 2: Automate sourcing before you hire a sourcer.** Noon AI integrates directly with LinkedIn and routes candidates into Ashby automatically. In month one at Counsel Health, the setup replaced a dedicated sourcer. The system handles outreach while the recruiter focuses on qualified conversations.

**Step 3: Build Claude as the operating layer with MCPs.** Custom MCPs connect Claude to Ashby, your knowledge base, Granola, Notion, and Google Workspace. Query your pipeline, pull meeting notes, or check offer status without switching tools. Weekly talent reports run automatically.

**Step 4: Automate the offer-to-close compliance chain.** Pave Visual Offer Letters triggers DocuSign on acceptance, closes the role in Ashby, and initiates a Checkr background check automatically. Design the sequence once, then step out of it.

**Step 5: Make admin tools queryable, not just organizational.** Google Workspace and Notion are the team's memory layer. Claude accesses them via MCP, making documentation and email history available in conversation instantly.

Pro Tip: Build the MCP layer before you need it at scale. Every system you connect becomes accessible in one conversation, and the time recovered from context-switching accumulates each workday.

### Worth Seeing & Reading

**Recruiting Is Now a Battle Between AI and AI.** Cadient analyzed 3,000 resumes and found 9 out of 10 salaried resumes had logical inconsistencies likely driven by AI, and 3 out of 4 resumes submitted to top employers contained AI-generated content. Katherine Loranger, CHRO at Scotts Miracle-Gro, describes the response: deploying AI to screen AI-inflated applications and weighting practical performance tasks over resume review.

**AI Screeners Aren't the Only Inconsistent Ones.** An engineer ran the same resume through HackerRank's AI screener 100 times with identical settings. Scores ranged from 66 to 99. Glen Cathey's reframe: in one study, two human recruiters scoring the same resume disagreed by 41 percentage points. The variance is not unique to AI. It has always been present in human review.

**Startups Are Relocating Employees, Not Households.** Lovable's head of people argues that relocation support is still too focused on the employee who signs the offer, while ignoring the partner, children, routines, and local community that determine whether the move actually sticks. For startups competing globally for AI talent, relocation is becoming less of an immigration checklist and more of a retention strategy.

**How to Evaluate AI Capability Without Asking About Tools.** The talent acquisition director at Fleek argues that screening by tool exposure misses the real signal: growth trajectory relative to starting point. The practical test (give candidates Claude Code and see what they build) reveals ceiling and curiosity far better than asking about prior experience. A candidate with no Claude history but explosive curiosity may outperform someone who name-drops tools without depth.

### Framework of the Week: The Protected AI Build Block

Tracy St.Dic's TA team sets aside one uninterrupted five-hour block every three weeks for recruiters, sourcers, and operations partners to step away from delivery work and build practical AI automations together.

Using Zapier MCP and SDK, the team turns recurring recruiting bottlenecks into working tools, including:

- A daily candidate digest from Ashby
- Automated interview debrief preparation
- A Chrome extension for LinkedIn Recruiter

To run your own build block:

- Reserve five uninterrupted hours on a recurring schedule
- Include recruiters, sourcers, and recruiting operations
- Choose one repetitive problem from the team's daily work
- Define the desired output before choosing the technology
- Build and test a simple first version during the session
- Put it into a real workflow and collect feedback
- Assign an owner to maintain and improve it

The key insight: teams build AI capability faster by solving their own operational problems than by completing passive training.

Bottom line: Protecting five hours every three weeks gives practitioners the space to turn recurring friction into useful automation without waiting for engineering resources.

### Big Idea: The AI Fluency Bar Just Moved

"Asking candidates whether they use AI tools measures the wrong variable. Zapier's updated framework, built from nine months of hiring data and performance analysis, measures growth trajectory and function-specific reasoning, not just current tool usage." — Tracy St. Dic, Global Head of Talent at Zapier.

Tracy St. Dic published version two of the company's AI fluency framework in April 2026, after iterating on the original since May 2025.

The update came from examining data on which candidates scored high in interviews and which actually drove impact once inside.

The framework has three levels. Mindset is the entry point: curiosity, willingness to experiment, understanding where AI adds value. Most assessments stop here, treating any AI usage as sufficient evidence.

Strategy goes further: does the candidate understand how their role is changing and do they have a vision for it?

Builder Skills is the technical layer. Can they use tools, iterate on outputs, and treat AI as a work partner rather than an autocomplete shortcut?

The version two change that matters most: Zapier shifted from assessing where a candidate currently stands to assessing the direction and speed of their growth. A candidate who started building with AI six months ago and is accelerating outscores someone who plateaued after two years of use. Current ability is a secondary signal. Trajectory is the primary one.

For your next open role, add one question that assesses trajectory rather than current usage: What did you build or automate with AI in the last 90 days that you could not have done 90 days before that?

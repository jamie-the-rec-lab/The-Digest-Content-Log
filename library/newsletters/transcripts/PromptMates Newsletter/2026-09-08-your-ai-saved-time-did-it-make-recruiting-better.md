## BUILD OF THE WEEK: Turn a New Job Approval Into a Recruiting Launch in Five Minutes

Casey Firey, Head of Global Talent Operations at Camunda, shared how her team automated the work that follows a new role approval, turning days of recruiting setup into a five-minute workflow while keeping hiring managers in control.

Step 1: Trigger the workflow. Once a role is approved, the workflow pulls existing competencies, frameworks, and pre-vetted interview questions from Camunda's internal systems.

Step 2: Build the hiring plan. It generates the job description and role-specific interview questions, then checks that the interview process covers the competencies the team needs to assess.

Step 3: Run talent market intelligence. The workflow analyzes talent pool size, geography, and competitive positioning, giving recruiters useful market context before the search begins.

Step 4: Source and prepare the shortlist. It finds candidate profiles and shows why each person could fit, where gaps exist, and what recruiters should probe. Candidates are then pushed into Findem for outreach.

Step 5: Put everything where the team works. The recruiting brief is shared in a new Slack channel and uploaded to Ashby. Hiring managers can still edit the output without recruiters chasing materials across multiple systems.

Bottom line: the value isn't generating one job description faster. It's connecting the entire recruiting kickoff into one workflow, turning days of setup into roughly five minutes while keeping human judgment in the process.

## WORTH SEEING & READING

**AI Interview Screeners Need Human Oversight.** Zapier is using AI interview screeners to help manage candidate volume, but the system is not replacing recruiter judgment. The team treats AI as an additional signal in the hiring process, with humans still responsible for reviewing candidates and making decisions.

**Why AI Agents Still Won't Replace Your HR Department.** HR teams could shrink 30 to 50% by 2030 while the work itself becomes more specialized, with hundreds of skills and new AI agents entering the operating model. The challenge isn't simply adding agents, it's deciding which work belongs to AI, which belongs to people, and how the two should work together. (Josh Bersin)

**What Happens When You Don't Automate the Outreach.** Thomas Forstner took a deliberately low-volume approach: 30 to 50 ideal candidates, hiring manager involvement, and roughly 15 minutes spent personalizing each message. The result was a 60%+ positive acceptance rate, a reminder that candidate outreach may be one place where efficiency isn't the goal.

## PLATFORM UPDATES

**OpenAI** takes on entire workflows: GPT-6 Astra is built for complex, multi-step work across research, browsing, documents, spreadsheets, and presentations. For talent teams, that points toward AI handling complete workflows rather than generating one isolated job description, email, or report.

**Claude** lets teams share Skills more selectively: Claude Team and Enterprise users can now share custom Skills with specific people or groups instead of giving the entire organization access, making it easier for recruiting and HR teams to standardize workflows while controlling who can use or edit them.

**Oracle** adds AI agents across HR: Oracle is adding specialized AI agents to its HCM platform that can keep job structures current, identify internal talent, guide employee development, and help workforce plans adapt as business needs change.

## BIG IDEA: AI Is Making Time-to-Fill the Wrong Recruiting Metric

"The goal isn't to have fewer people. It's to have the right people doing the right things, backed by tech that handles the rest."

Most recruiting teams are introducing AI one task at a time: writing a job description, scheduling an interview, or summarizing candidate notes. That may save time, but it does not necessarily improve recruiting. Adding AI to an inefficient process can simply make that process move faster.

The bigger opportunity is to redesign the work. Administrative tasks can move to AI while recruiters focus on advising hiring managers, understanding talent markets, building relationships, and making judgment calls. That also changes how success should be measured. Time-to-fill and cost-per-hire still matter, but they do not show whether the business gained the capability it needed.

SHRM proposes a broader measure: time to capability. Instead of asking only how quickly a role was filled, teams should ask how quickly the business obtained what it needed through hiring, internal mobility, reskilling, outsourcing, automation, or AI.

Are we automating the recruiting function we already have, or building the one we will need next?

Welcome back to PromptMates Weekly. This week: why better decisions may beat faster automation, how Lovable fixed broken debriefs, and why a slower-growing workforce changes recruiting.

## Build of the week: Build an AI Playbook Your Team Will Actually Use

Mind Robotics wants everyone using AI, but instead of starting with a giant rulebook, created a short set of principles for what good AI-assisted work looks like:

1. Make the human accountable: no labels or disclaimers required, but if your name is on the work you need to understand it, defend it, and be responsible for whether it is right.
2. Add the "could you explain it?" test: before sharing AI-assisted work, ask whether you could walk someone through it without looking at the document.
3. Put a cost on AI slop: producing something should take longer than consuming it. If a two-sentence prompt spits out ten pages nobody wants to read, the ten pages probably are not the deliverable.
4. Scale verification with the stakes: do not fact-check a brainstorm like a board memo.
5. Turn individual wins into team standards: a shared channel where employees post useful prompts and workflows, and when something consistently works it becomes a repeatable team process.

## Worth seeing and reading

- **The AI trust gap:** a BambooHR study of 1,608 US workers found workers spend 87 minutes a day using AI, more of it troubleshooting than advancing work. The bigger gap: "67% of HR professionals would let AI interview candidates autonomously, versus just 34% of employees."
- **How Lovable took the loudest voice out of hiring decisions:** Lovable's recruiting team built a no-names voting tool so interview debriefs are not swayed by whoever speaks first or loudest; interviewers share their reads before discussion and vote simultaneously.
- **The 30-minute call that lifts conversion up to 15%:** a 12-month data review found a 30-minute prep call two days before a late-stage interview "lifted conversion by 4 to 15%," largely by helping candidates show up more confidently.
- **80% of failed hires came down to durable skills, not hard skills:** GrowthAssistant traced most failed hires to durable skills like communication, critical thinking and character, and rebuilt hiring around them; half of each recruiter's KPI is now tied to 90-day quality of hire.
- **Warden Watch:** Warden AI launched a free public tracker for AI-in-hiring regulation, currently monitoring "29 regulations and six lawsuits across 25 jurisdictions."

## Platform updates

- **Anthropic** launched Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, cutting agentic workload costs by roughly 25%, with savings of up to 45% on complex tasks through cheaper cached-data pricing, plus a preview of Enterprise Frontier Safeguards letting companies keep sensitive data in their own cloud.
- **LinkedIn** is making its AI-powered job search available worldwide and beginning to retire classic job search in September, letting candidates describe the job they want conversationally.

## Big idea

"The US workforce will grow just 3.5% over the next decade. The bigger hiring problem may not be too much AI, but too few workers." Total US employment is projected to grow 3.5% through 2035, compared with 10.9% over the previous decade, per BLS projections, with healthcare driving much of the new demand as an aging population needs more care. The newsletter argues this shifts the strategic question from "how do we automate hiring?" to "how do we hire when there are fewer workers available?"

## **BUILD OF THE WEEK**

## 🔨 How to Build a Background-Check Triage System Your Team Can Trust

Ariel Chen runs People Ops for a two-person team at Figma tracking more than 100 background checks a month across 12 countries.

Every check showed the same generic pending status, so catching the one that actually mattered meant opening every report by hand. She had never built an automation before. Here is how she built one her team could trust.

### Step 1: Connect your vendor to Zapier

Connect your background-check vendor to Zapier through its API and webhooks. Chen started here with zero automation experience, learning what a webhook does by watching one fire.

### Step 2: Build a live status-sync workflow

Pull every vendor event into a shared Google Sheet, logging each one to an append-only record so any report can be reconstructed later.

### Step 3: Layer in daily AI triage

Add daily AI by Zapier triage that reads each pending screen and assigns real urgency, not just a flat pending flag, based on how close the candidate is to a start date.

### Step 4: Route escalations to a senior approver

Send anything that hits the danger zone to a senior approver for escalation. Automation flags urgency; a person still makes the call on every report.

### Step 5: Test for five clean days before trusting it

Run the automation against your source of truth for five consecutive clean days, screen by screen, before your team relies on it for a single decision.

## **BIG IDEA**

**Two recruiters used MCP to catch a comp-band gap their consultant missed.**

“The recruiters furthest ahead with AI right now are not writing better prompts. They are connecting data they already had to a tool that can finally reach it.“ —

Clara Vallejo at Remote and Nathalie Grandy at Linktree both used Model Context Protocol connections to pull hiring data directly into Claude and ChatGPT, instead of exporting spreadsheets and stitching them together by hand.

Grandy's team found 70% of product marketing candidates were negotiating 15% above the company's official comp bands, evidence her comp consultant's data had missed.

She took that finding to finance and got the bands corrected.

Vallejo's team had a healthy interview funnel, but interview-to-offer was stuck around 10%. Connecting Sigma and BrightHire data let her build an interviewer leaderboard, standardize assessment criteria across the team, and pull interview-to-offer up to roughly 18% within six weeks.

Neither leader called herself technical going in. Both started by asking the AI tool questions about their own hiring data and building from whatever came back. Neither treated the output as a final answer. Every artifact still went through a human who owned the decision.

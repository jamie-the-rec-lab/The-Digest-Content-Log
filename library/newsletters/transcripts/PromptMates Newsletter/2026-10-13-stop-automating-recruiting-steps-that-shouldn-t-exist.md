### Build of the week: turn five interviews into one candidate closing brief

Joshua Gill at Luma built an AI-powered closing brief that pulls scattered interview insights into one page before a recruiter makes the final pitch, combining Metaview transcripts and Ashby scorecards and delivering it through Slack.
- Step 1: Gather the interview context. Pull transcripts from Metaview and scorecards from Ashby, capturing what the candidate has said matters to them across interviews rather than relying on the closing recruiter's memory.
- Step 2: Generate a one-page closing brief: what to lead with, where the candidate's priorities match the offer and where they don't, likely objections and suggested responses.
- Step 3: Add the next conversation prompts: questions worth asking, details to confirm before an offer, and who the candidate should speak with next.
- Step 4: Deliver it inside the recruiter's workflow. When a candidate reaches the final stages in Ashby, send a Slack DM linking to the brief. Recruiters can also request one with a /closing command.
- Step 5: Keep the recruiter in control. Use the brief's call mode during the closing conversation. The AI prepares the context but never contacts candidates or makes hiring decisions.

### Big idea

AI is exposing how many recruiting steps exist because of history, not because they improve hiring. "The question is no longer just which recruiting tasks AI can automate. It is which tasks still deserve to exist." Recruiting processes accumulate steps over time: another approval after a bad hire, another screen after an overwhelmed hiring manager, another handoff because the ATS was configured that way years ago. Bonnie Dilber put it well: eventually the process becomes something teams inherit rather than design. Before deciding whether a step belongs with a recruiter, hiring manager or AI, ask what decision the step improves and what would happen if you removed it. If nobody can answer clearly, automation may be the wrong next move. Before automating a step, ask: if we were designing this process today, would we add this step at all?

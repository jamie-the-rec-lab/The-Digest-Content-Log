### Worth seeing and reading

**The $6.3bn hack that began with a job interview.** "The BBC puts October's theft from Binance, which the FBI attributes to North Korea, at a record $6.3bn. Binance's own report says it started with recruitment. An engineer was approached on a legitimate job platform, sat a video interview with a fake HR representative and a second round with a fake "CEO", then was told to download an "assignment" that installed malware on his work laptop." "fake candidates are already a known hiring risk, and here the fake was the employer, with a take-home assignment as the way in." Source: Chainalysis (https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/binance-hack-dprk-employment-fraud/).

**How one company got non-engineers building.** "OpenAI's case study on cult.fit says non-engineering teams built more than 20 internal apps with Codex in recent months, including an HR workflow for performance reviews, scheduling and feedback collection." Method: a large hackathon, a one-day session where each management team member built something, twice-weekly engineering office hours, engineers embedded in selected functions. Source: https://openai.com/index/cultfit-builds-with-codex

**Clay made the application a project.** Clip from PromptMates' October show: Jen Ayala explains how Clay replaced CV review for its product support role with a three-part job simulation. "Recruiters make every decision, and candidates can opt out of AI assessment." Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BxL-h9Q29YY

Fewer are getting time back.** "Employ's 2026 Recruiter Nation survey of more than 1,200 HR and recruiting professionals finds 68% of teams use AI, yet only 18% of recruiters using it have shifted more than half their time to higher-value work, down from 26% in 2025. ### Big idea: AI can do junior tasks. It can't do the junior job.

Jason Furman, former chair of the White House Council of Economic Advisers, "argued that AI could replace most of its roughly 20 economists, and his follow-up suggests a skeleton crew of about half that, with most of the juniors gone." Claudia Sahm, a senior economist there in 2015-16, pushes back on the task-lens method: "A task list tells you what a person does on a given day," Sahm writes. "It does not tell you why the organization hired them."

"AI models tend not to call out bad ideas; people do". She budgets $2,400 a year for AI and has no research assistant: "The AI did not replace a junior. It replaced nobody." Questions raised: what the role is for, what juniors would do instead, what counts as output ("not ten times the memos; it would be better memos"), where cuts fall (she "would strongly push back on cuts that fall disproportionately on juniors"). Asking what AI can replace is "an insufficient question". Source: https://stayathomemacro.substack.com/p/ode-to-the-juniors

### Build of the week: Agentic hiring's no-show problem: 31% didn't turn up

Evgenii Arsentev "handed the front of one US-facing telemedicine company's hiring funnel, for a phone-based role, to a Claude agent and published what happened. It is one company's data (36 days, small numbers, no control group)".

- "Over 36 days it read 1,973 applications in detail (65 to 100 hours of recruiter time, by Arsentev's estimate), added 142 candidates to the CRM and booked 138 human interviews."
- "37 booked interviews ended in a no-show, as many as failed the supervisor's interview (also 37). Only 68.6% of bookings were attended, so 31% didn't turn up."
- "On day 15, 37 candidates who had passed their interview were still waiting for a hiring decision, the oldest for 13 days."
- "Only 2 of the 7 candidates it rated HIRE were still working at day 36, against 6 of the 12 it rated MAYBE."
- "All 3 candidates rated NO were rejected with no record of a person reading the transcript, and 2 of them had already passed a human interview. Candidates were not told that the video interviewer was an AI avatar or that a model scored them, and were not offered a human route."

The rules: "in the UK, solely automated decisions with significant effects carry extra rights under UK GDPR, and the EU AI Act classes AI used to screen or rank candidates as high-risk." Source: https://arsentev.ai/press/ai-hiring

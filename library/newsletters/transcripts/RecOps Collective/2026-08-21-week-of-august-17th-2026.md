A Tell in the Automated Apply Game By Jeremy Lyons
To most recruiters who are staring at that thousands-of-resumes-per-req pile, the debate between fraud or candidates using AI resources to apply en masse is a moot point (it is very different just FYI). Anyone who looks at the numbers per req likely misses the point as well since they probably view it through a lens like Sales, where more is better, not more quality is better. But for folks in the TA/RecOps space, we’re always looking for the ripple or the tell to help our teams, and I’ve started to notice one.
No burying the lede, what is it?
Most AI-assisted tools need a way to track so they will generate email addresses to use and route back when there is a hit. The convention they have opted to use is ##firstnamelastname (e.g., 820jeremy@gmail or 20jlyons@).
Why can this be a tell?
Conventionally, most people don’t start their email address with numbers. A user name maybe, but not an email. If someone wants a professional email there is a high likelihood they would opt for something without numbers.
So what should we do?
I’m not going to tell you to create immediate flags or tags off that. That would be silly and possibly punish people who have been fed poor logic (e.g., “An ATS sorts based on email address so if you have a number at the beginning you’ll launch to the top”). I also leave room for being totally wrong here and don’t want to try and fearmonger anyone. My recommendation is to run an audit to see how many candidates with emails like that in your system there might be, see how far they are progressing, and if there is anything suspicious about their candidacy.

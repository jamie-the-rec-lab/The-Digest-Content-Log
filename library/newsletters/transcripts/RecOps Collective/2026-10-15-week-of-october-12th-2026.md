## Newsletter

Lead piece: Four Types of Problems.

The ability to define the problem is key to first principles thinking and the 5 Whys. The difficulty is that what looks like one problem can be masquerading as another, so the piece identifies the four most common types in TA and RecOps.

- Money: probably the most common. We want to do X but need Y, and it wasn't budgeted or was reallocated; or we must cut spend but keep output; or a blank check paralyses decision making. When money is the issue you get creative (lighter contracts, build what you need, less established brands) or comfortable (an always-buy mentality, less defined taste). A money problem can also look like a knowledge or people problem, because gaps in accomplishing a goal point there first and you often need to rationalise the spend.
- Time: unlike money, time spent is gone forever. When a TA team goes down a wrong path, there is no rewind. AI reduces time on tasks but the time investment must pay off. Time is also needed to uplevel skills and network. A time problem can look like a knowledge problem (it takes time to learn; AI can execute quicker but if it breaks and you don't understand the reasoning you can't fix it) or a money problem (resource-constrained teams feel deadlines hard).
- Knowledge: both learning and experience, largely internally driven. A gap is usually solved with people or a tool, but both require experience (you must learn a tool's intricacies to get the ROI). Gaining knowledge takes time, because you can learn the wrong thing or need to unlearn a bad habit. A knowledge problem can look like a people or time problem.
- People: people can solve problems or make them more combustible. They manifest through emotionality (teams not getting along, overwork) or lack of people. Because AI agents tend to acquire human names, the author groups them here too: too much dependency on one person or one agent to play hero ball. A people problem can look like a money or time problem.

### Compensation’s Little Black Skills Book and Dynamic Candidate Pricing

If we think about TA/RecOps as a skill tree with limited points to allocate towards mastery, many of us spend the bulk of those points in the middle of the game of TA on things like candidate experience, data reading to stop a leaking bucket, or programmatic elements. Maybe you allocate points towards the beginning so that you can stop legal, compliance, or data problems before they start. But for many of us, we stop investing points at the end game past the TA-People handoff and getting Offer Letters right, and that might actually be a problem because we don’t know how Compensation thinks about candidates.

**Why are you taking shots at Compensation?**

I’m definitely not. However, we should all care about how Compensation thinks about the hiring game. For many non-startups, FP&A approves the range that gets posted and HRBPs fight for internal equity, but Compensation ultimately gives the salary recommendation. And how do they make that recommendation? They either do a comp analysis or pay for one (e.g., Radford Job Codes) of like-sized companies in their space and industry. BUT they also do their own internal study to determine the value of a particular skill or qualification that adds more to the offer.

Do they hand over that list to TA? Nope. It’s confidential, and if they did, then they know that human behavior would rush to see if they over/under valued themselves

Could they? 100%, and it would actually provide more precise pricing than just telling a candidate that we aim for the middle 50% of the range.

**Dynamic Pricing**

But imagine, for a second, that Compensation handed over their little black pricing book.

Would that change the candidate experience? The answer is yes because you could build a candidate-facing calculator that demonstrated the likely offer a candidate could receive. As they interviewed, that salary number could go up or stay flat based on new information uncovered during the interview process (e.g., maybe they didn’t list a skill on a resume, but it comes up repeatedly in the interview process and is a needed target skill).

**Do we need this?**

Need is a strong word here. Most companies aren’t built for this and rightfully so. In a world where a skill can be commoditized by AI performing the task, the ability to spot what looks wrong and correct it at the point of origin is more highly valued than the ability to perform the task from scratch.

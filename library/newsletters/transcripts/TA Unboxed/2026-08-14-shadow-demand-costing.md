# What Shadow Demand is costing you
- **Source:** TA Unboxed
- **Date:** 2026-08-14
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-14
- **Public URL:** https://taunboxed.substack.com/p/what-shadow-demand-is-costing-you

## Newsletter

**Shadow Demand: What Cancelled Reqs Really Cost**

According to industry analysts, roughly a third of the jobs your company advertises will never meet a candidate. That's not my number; DataPeople analysed 150,000 active job postings and found a 35% failure-to-hire rate across open roles. That's excluding internal transfers and posts that never attracted a single applicant.

Look across companies, and the pattern is the same. Three out of four fail to hire for more than 20% of their open requisitions. Half are running a no-hire rate of 30% or higher.

So a significant share of the roles we open never end with a person in a seat.

Even if you think that's high (which I do as well), I'm willing to go out on a limb and say that it's probably (on average) 20% of all roles opened that get cancelled. That's still 1 in 5.

What we don't hear about, or what doesn't get talked about, is this: a role goes live, work happens against it, sourcing, screening, sometimes interviews, and then it just stops.

Budget freeze. Restructure. A manager who's had second thoughts. The business quietly decided it didn't need what it asked for, and you didn't receive an explanation.

I want to give that problem a name: shadow demand.

It's important to label it because when you're reporting, you can show the business what's real demand vs shadow demand.

**What shadow demand actually is**

Shadow demand is demand that is presented as real, formalised, approved, live and never materialised into a hire. It looked exactly like every other req in your system.

A shadow-demand req and a real req are indistinguishable on day one.

You only find out which one you had by how far it travelled before it stopped.

**Waste compounds the further it travels**

The cost of shadow demand isn't fixed. It scales with how deep into the funnel the role gets before it dies.

Cancel it in week one, before sourcing starts, and the cost is close to zero: a req that never should have opened, closed cleanly.

Cancel it after a recruiter has sourced against it, and you've burned recruiter hours that could have been spent on real demand.

Cancel it after candidates have been screened or interviewed, and now you've wasted their time too: people who took calls, prepped, and maybe turned down other conversations to stay in your process: for a role that was never going to exist.

Cancel it after a hiring panel has run interviews, and you've now burnt hiring manager and interviewer time on top of everything else, some of your most expensive, hardest-to-schedule hours spent on a role that was never going to materialise.

Even worse, if it dies at the offer stage after you've made a candidate believe they have the job, the cost isn't just internal time. That's employer brand damage, a candidate who may have walked away from another offer, and a story that you don't want circulating in the market.

The same cancelled req costs you almost nothing at the top of the funnel and real money and reputation at the bottom. Shadow demand doesn't announce which end it's going to land on until it's already there.

**Almost nobody actually monitors this**

Most ATS platforms will tell you a req was cancelled. What they won't tell you, in any structured, reportable way, is the thing that actually matters:

- Why it was cancelled — budget, restructure, scope change, manager changed their mind, need resolved internally, role never should have opened
- Who made the decision to cancel it — which manager, which business unit, which layer of the org made the call
- What it cost — not just that time was spent, but how much, in whose hours, at what stage
- What stage has it reached — sourcing, screening, first interviews, panel/final interviews, offer. Not when it was cancelled on the calendar, but how far it actually got

That third one is the gap worth closing first, because it's the one that turns a shrug into a business case.

**A rough formula for what it costs**

You don't need perfect data to make this real; you need something directionally honest that you can apply consistently. A simple version that you can iterate on:

Waste = (recruiter hours × loaded hourly rate) + (hiring manager & panel hours × their loaded rate) + a brand-cost flag if it died at offer stage

Worked example: a role sits open for 14 weeks and dies after panel interviews. Say that's 20 recruiter hours at a loaded rate of £35/hour, plus 12 hours of hiring manager and panel time at a blended £70/hour. That's £700 in recruiter time and £840 in HM/panel time — £1,540 wasted, before you've even accounted for candidate goodwill or the next role those panel members didn't have time to interview for instead.

If you don't have clean hourly data yet, a simpler relative version works for a first pass: score every cancelled req on weeks open × stage reached (sourcing = 1, screening = 3, panel = 6, offer = 12). You won't get a ££ or $$ figure, but you'll get a ranked list of which cancellations actually hurt, and that's usually enough to start the conversation.

**This shouldn't be TA's cost to quietly absorb**

I suppose this is the message I want to get across. It's not enough as a TA function just to report the number of cancellations back to the business. I suppose that's what the majority of you are doing.

Right now, when a req gets cancelled, TA has to suck up the cost and resources.

20% of the roles that our team worked on ended up being cancelled, so we lost 20% capacity. If you think about it, that number is alarming.

It just gets folded into "the cost of doing business," which means nobody outside TA has any reason to change their behaviour, because nobody outside TA has ever seen the number.

That needs to change.

Reporting should include a line the business has never had to look at before: here's what we opened and never delivered, here's what it cost in hours and reputation to get there, and here's which units and which managers are generating disproportionate amounts of it.

Make that visible and start bringing the data to the Leadership meetings.

Will a manager who knows their cancelled reqs get named in a report think twice before opening a role?

Maybe, maybe not. But a business unit shown to be responsible for a third of the org's wasted hiring spend has a much harder time treating it as TA's problem to solve quietly in the background.

Worth asking yourself: if you pulled every req cancelled in the last twelve months, scored the waste, and reported it back to the units that raised them, how differently would your business think about opening a role next time?

# The problem with TA reporting and what to do about it

- **Source:** TA Unboxed
- **Date:** 2026-09-11
- **Fetched:** 2026-09-16
- **Public archive:** https://taunboxed.substack.com/p/the-problem-with-ta-reporting-and

## Newsletter

By Darren Bush, Founder, TA Unboxed.

Ageing reqs are the industry's default early-warning signal, but Bush argues they are actually a lagging one: "By the time a req is ageing, the issue has usually already happened." He compares it to noticing a problem only once "the pan has boiled over and there's water everywhere."

## Why TA reports miss the point

- **Data diligence:** hiring teams do not always put the right information into the system, since feedback happens in calls, emails and side conversations and someone has to remember to log it in the ATS afterwards.
- **TA metrics look back, the business looks ahead:** TA reporting tracks time to fill, time to hire, conversion rates and source of hire, but senior leaders want to know "when will the person start" and "which roles are putting delivery at risk."
- **Too much reporting is retrospective:** it shows which SLAs were missed and which stages took too long, "reporting through the rear-view mirror."

## Three signal types to build reporting around

1. **System signals** from the ATS/CRM: dwell time, days in stage vs benchmark, hiring velocity, no activity on the req, approval delays, no qualified candidates after recruiter review.
2. **Recruiter signals**, kept simple so they do not become admin work: an alignment flag at kickoff, escalation flags, confidence on whether the role is workable, shortlist conversion, notes on blockers like salary, location or brief quality.
3. **Manager signals**, described as the most underused: time to review CVs, time from interview requested to first interview completed, interview conversion to next stage, shortlist engagement.

Bush is building a "control centre" over the next few months that combines system data, recruiter judgement, manager behaviour and business context to show which reqs are healthy, drifting, or need intervention, and plans to share progress "over the next 6 weeks."

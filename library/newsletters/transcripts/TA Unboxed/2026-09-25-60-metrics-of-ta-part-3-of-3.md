### The streetlight effect

There's an old joke about a man searching for his keys under a streetlight late at night. A stranger stops to help and asks, "Is this where you lost them?" "No," the man says, pointing into the darkness. "I lost them over there." "Then why are you looking here?" "Because this is where the light is."

For me, that joke epitomises how I describe TA metrics and dashboards. We gravitate towards metrics like time-to-fill and interview-to-offer ratios because the ATS makes them easy to track. The metrics don't explain whether we hired well, or what is likely to happen next. Like the man under the streetlight, we're looking where the data is visible rather than where the answer might be.

To be clear, I'm not saying that we should throw these away; these metrics aren't wrong. The problem is when they become our entire definition of what good TA looks like. Goodhart's Law states that when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. Push hard enough on time to fill, and the number may improve while the quality of the decision worsens.

I shared the 60 metrics not to show what you need to be doing, but to ask: are we looking at the right numbers?

### The maturity ladder

Gartner's analytics framework separates analytics into four types: descriptive, diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive. I've adapted that model here as a maturity ladder for TA. Not all 60 metrics are equal, and most teams never climb past the first two rungs.

- Descriptive. What happened? Time to hire. Source of hire. Roles closed rate. Necessary, but they only report the past.
- Diagnostic. Why did it happen? Pass-through rates. Trends for offer declines. Time in each stage. Now you're explaining, not just counting.
- Predictive. What's likely to happen? Total Obtainable Market. Hiring predictability. Can you forecast time to fill from pipeline health before the quarter starts?
- Prescriptive. What should we do next? Time to alignment. Submittal-to-HM accept ratio. Google's Rule of 4. Metrics that tell you to pause sourcing, realign the profile, or cut the fifth interview.

Look at your current dashboard and count the rungs. Where I'm going and what I'm building is: can we work in the predictive column? Can we get better at not just predicting, but also flagging when there is an issue with the hiring system?

### My reporting framework

The way I now think about TA reporting is that it has three useful layers. Most TA dashboards are built for performance reporting. If I look at our RPO SLA layer, it consists of performance reporting. They tell us what happened. The better ones add operational reporting. They show us what is happening right now: open roles, ageing requisitions, candidate movement, recruiter workload and hiring manager bottlenecks. But most TA functions miss the predictive layer: using the system's live state to forecast what is likely to happen next. This is the layer the business desperately needs.

### The rear-view mirror problem

I went back through the 60 core metrics from Parts 1 and 2 and classified each by its primary use. I excluded the two bonus metrics, Hiring Velocity and Hiring Plan Completion Rate, so the base stays at 60.

45 of 60, or 75%, are rear-view metrics. They report something after it happens: an application, stage move, offer, hire, start, cost, or employee outcome.

15 of 60, or 25%, can be used as forward indicators (or predictors). These include market size, obtainable market, pool burn, time in stage, candidate waiting time, pass-through rates, pipeline conversion, weekly submissions and recruiter capacity.

Most TA reporting tells the business what happened; however, the business is asking a different question: When will I see these hires in my company? Time to start only tells you how long completed hires took. Roles open beyond 60 days tell you that the miss has already happened. Even pass-through rates are only useful when applied to the live pipeline.

The business needs a forecasted start date for every requisition. Call it Hiring ETA. It should combine:

- the number of active candidates at each stage
- the probability of moving from that stage to hire
- the typical days remaining to offer
- offer acceptance and accepted-offer-to-start rates
- notice periods, checks and onboarding lead time
- recruiter capacity and role difficulty

The output should be simple: expected start date, confidence range and forecasted starts by month.

### The ones that didn't make the 60

- Trust Index. A composite of candidate trust, hiring manager trust in recruiters, and executive trust in TA. Low trust creates bottlenecks no process fix will solve.
- Hiring Predictability. Forecasted hires versus actual hires delivered. Target 85 to 95% accuracy. This is how TA earns credibility with finance.
- Interviewer Capacity. The leading indicator almost nobody puts in the dashboard is interviewer capacity, which is knowable before the req opens and explains a large share of the delay that shows up later as an ageing req. (Credit to Kat Stam.)
- Highest-Impact Talent Problem of the Quarter. A qualitative pick from executive priorities. It forces TA to align with what the business actually cares about right now.
- The HERO Index. A stakeholder-centric framework that unifies metrics across recruiters, hiring managers, candidates and the business. Early days, but the direction is right.

Expect at least two of these to be standard within three years.

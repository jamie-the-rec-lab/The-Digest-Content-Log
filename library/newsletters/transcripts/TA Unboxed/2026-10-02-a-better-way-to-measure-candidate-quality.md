# A better way to measure candidate quality

- **Source:** TA Unboxed
- **Date:** 2026-10-02
- **Fetched:** 2026-10-07
- **Public:** https://taunboxed.substack.com/p/a-better-way-to-measure-candidate

## Newsletter

The hiring funnel doesn’t tell you what you think it does.
When you’re looking at a role report at any given moment, who is the best candidate for the role?
Who do you believe, with confidence, you may end up hiring for that role?
There’s someone in your pipeline, maybe still at the screen stage, maybe not even shortlisted yet, who you’d bet on. The one where your gut says, "This is the person.” And somewhere else in the same pipeline there’s a candidate who’s been through three interview rounds, and you’re still not convinced.
The funnel doesn’t show any of that. It just shows the number at each stage. Volume vs Quality.
The further through the process, the better the candidate must be. But that’s not how hiring actually works.
What you actually have as a snapshot on a live role is something more like this:
28 people at application, scored nowhere
8 people who’ve been screened, some promising but only the recruiter knows this.
4 people shortlisted, mixed confidence (but you can’t see that)
2 people in the HM interview stage: one the HM is lukewarm on but wanted to see, and one you think is genuinely exceptional. As a bystander, though, reading the TA report, I can’t see this.
The only metric that truly reflects quality is whether the candidate is hired.

What if you scored every candidate at every stage?
I’ve seen this done in places. If you have interview scorecards and managers fill them out (if that’s a big if), then you can see this at the interview stage.
However, I’ve been working on scoring at every stage. That’s screening, HM shortlist, interview stage 1 to (X), and the debrief. This is what I’m going to show you now.
It’s simple in concept: every candidate gets a confidence score — 1 to 5 — at every stage gate they pass through.
From 1-5, how confident are you that they match the requirements to the role.
Scored independently by the recruiter and the hiring manager, with both involved.
One score. Locked at each stage; you can’t retroactively revise it once the next stage begins.
Now your funnel has a second dimension. Not just where candidates are, but how strongly you believe in them at that point.
What changes when you have scores
A 4-rated candidate at the screening stage is more valuable than a 2-rated candidate at the final interview. Scores let you see that. Without them, the funnel implies the opposite.
Trajectory becomes visible. A candidate who scores 4 at the screen, 4 at the shortlist, then 2 at the HM interview is telling you something. It might mean the brief wasn’t properly set. It might mean the HM’s expectations weren’t calibrated. Without the scores, you just see a rejection outcome. With them, you can diagnose why.
The alignment gap becomes data. Recruiter scores a candidate 4. The hiring manager scores them 2. That’s not just a difference of opinion; that’s evidence that there is something wrong with the quality of the brief/kick off call. That gap, surfaced early, saves you weeks.
The quiet frontrunner becomes visible. You might have someone at the screening stage who’s been given a 5, but because they’re not far through the process, they’re easy to overlook. Compare that score against the rest of the pipeline, and you'll know where to focus your energy and who to protect if you need to move fast.
Side note: You could build a report showing every 5-rated candidate who has reached the screening or interview stage, their current stage, how long they’ve been there and how their confidence score has changed over time. That gives you a live view of the strongest candidates in the pipeline, not just the ones closest to an offer.
Exit scores tell you things outcomes can’t. When a candidate withdraws or gets rejected, what was their last confidence score? A pipeline of 4s and 5s who exit early might tell you your process is too slow and you’re losing good people to faster-moving competitors.
The bigger prize
Everyone is talking about quality of hire, but no one is mentioning pipeline quality. Why not? Run this framework for six months and you start to accumulate something most TA functions don’t have: a hiring quality dataset.
You can correlate confidence scores at each stage with offer acceptance rates. With 90-day retention. With hiring manager satisfaction scores. When it comes to time-to-productivity, I’m sure the scenarios are endless.
Over time, you learn what a 5 at screen actually predicts.
You learn whether recruiter scores or HM scores are better leading indicators of a successful hire.
You learn which roles have a structural problem because no candidate ever scores above a 3 past shortlist.

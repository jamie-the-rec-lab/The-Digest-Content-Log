# How to spot top talent in your ATS

- **Source:** TA Unboxed
- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Fetched:** 2026-10-17
- **Public:** https://taunboxed.substack.com/p/how-to-spot-top-talent-in-your-ats

## Newsletter

Last week I wrote about giving candidates a confidence score from 1 to 5 at each stage of the hiring process. That adds a quality lens to the funnel. By having a match score, you can see how strongly the recruiter and hiring manager believe in each candidate, not just where they are in the process.

You can also start connecting those scores to outcomes: which sources produce the strongest candidates, which scores turn into hires, where confidence rises or falls, which parts of the process produce the best quality.

Today I want to introduce another layer, focusing on two things. One, "You see this candidate? They're great. We need to pay attention to them." Two, "How can we report on this?"

### The part of recruiting we may have lost

Before recruiting became surrounded by systems, controls, administration and red tape, the recruiter's role had one remit: find me two or three really good candidates and provide evidence on why I should hire them. Recruiters were expected to use their judgement to take the role, screen candidates and bring in candidates who can do the role. Somewhere along the way, that part of the job has become harder to see. But where do we record the recruiter saying: "I've found some really good talent here. Pay attention to this person."

### The one to watch

I am building something like this into an Avature workflow. (For information purposes, I'm rolling out Avature ATS and CRM at Kyndryl.) For now, I am calling it "one to watch". The recruiter screens candidates and has the option in the screening form to mark a candidate as "one to watch", someone they believe stands out. The purpose is for the recruiter to endorse a candidate and say: "This person could definitely do the role."

It captures a judgement at a point in time while the outcome is still unknown. As a TA Director, you can visibly see which roles have a strong pipeline with confidence. A busy pipeline tells me how many people are moving through it. A strong pipeline tells me there are people we believe could do the job.

### The manager gets a view too

The hiring manager should be able to do the same thing. The recruiter and the manager look at the process from different positions. The recruiter may spot someone early because they recognise the pattern. The hiring manager may see something in a candidate who has only just entered the process. They may endorse different people. Managers will sometimes have favourites, but a favourite at the beginning is not the same as a guaranteed hire at the end. People change their minds as they see more evidence.

So the marker is not saying "This is definitely the person we will hire." It is saying "This person is worth keeping warm. There is something here." That keeps the signal useful without pretending that hiring judgement is fixed.

### From a candidate marker to a quality signal

Once the recruiter and hiring manager can independently mark candidates, you can ask: How often does the recruiter spot the person who is eventually hired? How often does the hiring manager? How often do they both identify the same candidate? At what stage was the candidate first spotted? Were they kept engaged as the process moved on? How often does early confidence hold up? This lets you look at talent spotting as a capability.

The reporting becomes more useful across multiple roles. Imagine reviewing 20 or 50 live roles and asking: have we identified at least one candidate that someone genuinely believes could do the job? If the answer is no, you have an early warning, and you can see the problem before the role reaches the end of the process with no credible option left. Alongside stage counts, you can see how many candidates have been marked as one to watch.

### Keeping good candidates warm

If a recruiter marks someone as one to watch, you might want to ensure they receive faster feedback, more deliberate communication, a check-in if the process slows down, and clearer attention from the hiring manager. A candidate in the final interview is not automatically more valuable than someone at the screening stage; they have simply travelled further. The person at screening may be the strongest person you have seen so far. If nobody records that, the system cannot help you protect them.

### Timestamp it when you spot them

When you introduce this, make sure it is timestamped and logged. You don't want recruiters to move candidates to the offer stage and go back into the system and say, "I knew right from the beginning this was the person." If you knew at the beginning, record it then. Otherwise, we're measuring hindsight, not talent spotting, and recruiters will game the system.

If you want to implement this, start small:
- Add a "one to watch" field at CV review and recruiter screening.
- Record who marked the candidate.
- Automatically capture the timestamp and stage.
- Add a short note on what stood out relative to the role requirements.
- Preserve the original record. No backdating or overwriting later.

Then review what happened to those candidates. Don't turn it into another huge piece of administration. That would miss the point.

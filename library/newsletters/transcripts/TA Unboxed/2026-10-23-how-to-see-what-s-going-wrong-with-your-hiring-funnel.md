(Email headline: "How to put the Fluid Clock into practice. The Fluid Clock, part two: start small, agree on the rules and turn the picture into action.")

Part two of the author's Fluid Clock idea, a clock face on each job showing where hiring is moving within expectations and where time is expanding beyond them. The goal "is not only to look back but also to see in real time where you're getting stuck."

1. **Pick a small group of jobs.** A repeatable job family, or five to ten roles in one team; a skilled recruiter and a supportive hiring manager; include the owner of reporting or system setup.
2. **Define what starts and stops each clock.** For every stage agree the starting event, the ending event, working vs calendar days, and who owns the next action. Use working days for feedback and internal decisions but keep total calendar time visible at job level, "The candidate still experiences the whole wait."
   Three ways to represent a stage: the whole review window (first submission to last recorded decision, elapsed not summed); the oldest outstanding request (who has waited longest); the typical candidate wait (median submission-to-decision including rejections). Author would start with the whole review window. "A five-day group review window and a three-day commitment for each CV aren't interchangeable."
3. **Agree the pace at kickoff.** A joint recruiter and hiring manager agreement, starting from historical data for comparable roles. Example: "For similar roles, feedback has taken about three working days. Can we commit to that here? Who covers it if you're unavailable?" Keep the original target and record revisions with date, reason and who agreed. "My gut estimate, not a hard statistic, is that around 20% of roles see changes to requirements, skills, banding or even location."
4. **Capture only what the clock needs.** Use timestamps the system already captures; audit kickoff forms ("those four-page kickoff documents"). Paused roles: show total elapsed time and active time; record who authorised a pause, why, and when. "A manager's slow response isn't a pause."

Pilot review questions: did the records reflect what actually happened; did the timing measure answer the question; did the view reveal missed waits; did signals lead to action; was capture manageable. The Fluid Clock is still a model the author is developing.

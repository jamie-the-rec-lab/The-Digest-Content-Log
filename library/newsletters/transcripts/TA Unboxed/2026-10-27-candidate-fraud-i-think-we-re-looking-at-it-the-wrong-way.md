# Candidate fraud: I think we're looking at it the wrong way.

- **Source:** TA Unboxed
- **Date:** 2026-10-27
- **Fetched:** 2026-11-04
- **Public:** https://taunboxed.substack.com/p/candidate-fraud-i-think-were-looking

## Newsletter

Sponsor (Pinpoint, flag as sponsor content): Pinpoint is described as the ATS for multi-stream hiring; also promotes a Pinpoint "How to combat candidate fraud" webinar on Thursday 29 October.

"There's a lot of talk about candidate fraud right now. However, I think the term is too broad." The label covers someone polishing a CV with ChatGPT, inventing experience, using AI to answer interview questions, someone else taking the interview, and an organised criminal trying to gain access to company systems. The author spoke with TA professionals, corporate security and technology providers, and has been listening to Stacy Zapar on organised candidate fraud. He replaces his original five-level model with three: Presentation. Misrepresentation. Fraud & Exploitation. Each raises a different question:

1. **Presentation (Authenticity):** AI-improved CVs and tailored applications; automated applications are put here too ("Someone applying to 100 jobs using automation isn’t necessarily being dishonest."). Question: "Are we assessing the person, or how well they present themselves?"
2. **Misrepresentation (Capability):** exaggeration, fabrication, and assessment deception (prohibited AI help, hidden coaching, someone else's work). "This is definitely growing, as more and more candidates try to 'beat the ATS'."
3. **Fraud & Exploitation (Intent):** "Stolen identities. Proxy interviews. Synthetic candidates. Organised fraud networks." "Level 3 candidates can behave and imitate level 1 and 2 instances." Two candidates can both invent experience and pass the interview; "Detecting deception isn't the same as establishing malicious intent." Needs identity checks, evidence-based risk assessment and closer TA, HR and corporate security collaboration. The author cites "the OpenAI case (where a recording device was placed in the Exec boardroom)" (unsourced in the email).

Conclusion: "before you implement any new technology to detect candidate fraud, make sure you are clear about what you're actually trying to detect."

# How good are your recruiters at spotting talent?

## Newsletter

Sponsor (Metaview, flag as sponsor content): fillmore, "Metaview's AI coworker that finds candidates, runs personalised outreach, and autonomously books screening calls."

"One to Watch, Part 2: How a team vote led to Standout, and what a simple reporting field could help us learn about talent recognition." The author is exploring this in an ATS implementation (Avature). A recruiter flags a candidate who particularly stands out after CV review or screening; the field was originally "Endorsed", and after a team vote (against "Great Prospect") became "Standout" ("We landed on Standout."). It is a leading indicator: see which roles have standout candidates without opening every record.

**Talent Recognition Rate (illustrative figures, not real data):** a recruiter fills 40 roles in twelve months and flagged the eventual hire as Standout before the first interview 28 times: "Their Early Talent Recognition Rate would be 70%."
**The catch (hypothetical):** James flags two Standouts per role (80 total), Sarah one (40 total); both flag the eventual hire in 30 of 40 roles (75%). "Sarah reached that result with half as many Standout flags", so her flag is a stronger signal, though James may not be wrong. Look at both the hit rate and what happened to the other flagged candidates.
**Hiring managers too:** measure how often recruiters and managers recognise the same candidates, who identifies the eventual hire, and whether agreement increases after interviewing.
**When did you spot them?** Record when each person first made the judgement. Implementation: one field, Standout Yes/No, available at CV screening, screening interviews and later stages; date-stamp it and record the stage "Otherwise, someone could reach the offer stage and retrospectively claim they knew the candidate was brilliant from the beginning."
Concern raised by LinkedIn commenters: whether this introduces bias.

## Newsletter

Sponsor (Metaview, flag as sponsor content): fillmore, "Metaview's recruiting agent": "fillmore finds candidates, researches the context they’ve shared publicly, drafts personalised outreach and coordinates screening calls." The aim is to "take repetitive sourcing and scheduling work off the recruiter’s plate".

Standout, Part 3, the final part of the series by Darren Bush: what the unexpected hire can teach us about screening, interviews and the talent we overlook.

"What if the most useful candidate to study is the one nobody initially expected to hire?" Part 2 explored measuring how good recruiters and hiring managers are at recognising talent via a "Standout" flag. "But tracking who stood out, and at what stage, could also help us learn from candidates we didn’t initially flag."

1. Every hiring process has its frontrunners. A typical shortlist of, say, five candidates: the recruiter is particularly confident about two, the hiring manager has their own favourite, and "the candidate who eventually gets hired wasn’t one of those initially identified as particularly strong, let alone flagged as ‘standout’." Is that a problem with the evaluation process, the recruiter or the hiring team?

2. What changed our minds? For "Candidate D", perhaps previous job titles didn't match expectations or the relevant experience wasn't highlighted; then in interview something changed (problem-solving, demeanour, communication style). "Maybe our original screening criteria were too narrow. Maybe, as we all know, we’re placing too much weight on what’s on the CV/Resume." Also possible: the hiring manager's requirements changed, or not enough evidence at the earlier stage. "I think it’s within our remit to understand what changed."

3. Patterns in the candidates we underestimate. "What if you discover that a meaningful proportion of eventual hires weren’t initially flagged as Standout by either the recruiter or the hiring manager?" Questions to investigate: were they changing careers, non-traditional backgrounds, skills better demonstrated in interviews than CVs, more common in particular roles? "You could uncover problems in how roles are defined, candidates are screened, or assessment evidence is interpreted." No data is given; this is a hypothetical.

4. Improving screening. If some candidates consistently perform better in interviews than screening suggests, use that to improve the hiring brief, recruiter screening questions and assessment process. "As AI interviewer platforms are introduced, what data and evidence could they provide that would let us spot things different from a CV/Resume screen or a Recruiter screen?"

5. Standout candidates we didn't hire. Scenario: three candidates flagged Standout, all reach final interview, one is hired. "In most ATS reporting, they’re unsuccessful." The author argues Standout could become part of a talent rediscovery process: "For me, it’s even better than the current silver medalist model."

6. Conclusion. "I’ve always believed recruitment has two fundamental jobs. Find relevant talent. Get reliable evidence of whether they can do the job. Standout sits between those two things." "From the responses I’ve received, several of you are looking to implement this in your ATS." (No number given.)

# We’re so focused on fake candidates, we’re missing the fake recruiters

- **Source:** TA Unboxed
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Fetched:** 2026-11-16
- **Public:** https://taunboxed.substack.com/p/were-so-focused-on-fake-candidates

## Newsletter

Sponsor (Metaview, flag as sponsor content): Metaview Screening, "their voice-led AI screening agent. It sits between application review and the human interview". Vendor points listed: "Humans still make the final decision", "Screening does not auto-reject or auto-approve anyone", "It is voice-led, with no AI avatar".

By Darren Bush. Subtitle: "Recruitment Fraud: We're Only Looking at Half the Problem". Section heading: "Your employees’ secret job search could be a security risk".

Builds on his earlier piece on five levels of candidate fraud (2026-10-27): "We shouldn’t treat a candidate using AI to improve their CV the same way we treat someone using a stolen identity to infiltrate an organisation. But that discussion only looks in one direction. What happens when the recruiter is the fraudster?"

**Two types of fraud. Three groups at risk.** Fake candidates targeting employers; fake recruiters targeting genuine jobseekers ("impersonate employers, advertise non-existent jobs, collect identity information, demand money, or persuade people to download malicious software"); and the hidden third: "The job seeker may already be someone else’s employee. An attack on an individual can also become an attack route into their current employer." "That’s why I think we need to stop seeing recruitment fraud as only a TA problem."

**Binance: the interview that became an attack route.** Timeline as given in the newsletter, from Binance's own account:
- June: "An engineer downloaded tampered developer software. Binance detected the incident, replaced the device and rotated credentials."
- September: "Someone posing as a project director contacted another engineer on a legitimate job-hunting platform." A live first-round interview with a supposed HR representative who shared a social profile with personal photos and videos; a second interview with the fictitious company's supposed CEO via a chat app; then the technical assessment. "The engineer was asked to download a coding assignment onto his work computer. The repository contained malware that ran during setup, avoided detection and gave attackers a foothold."
- 20 October: "Attackers used that compromised device and an internal AI tool to reach two more engineers’ computers. They obtained a deployment permission token."
- 29 October: "They used stolen credentials and a malicious container to reach wallet-signing operations. Binance reported $6.34bn in affected digital assets, with approximately $2.85bn frozen or intercepted by 3 November."

"One crucial detail: the fake interview was part of a months-long, multi-stage attack, not the sole cause." "The deception works because it looks like a process we all recognise."

**The security blind spot.** Citing Matt Buckland: people often keep their job searches private. "Would they ask their company’s security team to investigate an unfamiliar link or file if doing so revealed they were looking for another job?" "We can’t build a reporting culture that requires people to disclose their career intentions." "Companies should explain how to report suspicious external recruitment approaches confidentially. They should warn employees about unfamiliar interview software, unexpected links, executable files and coding assignments, and make it clear that security teams want early reports — including when someone has already clicked."

**Recruitment is a shared trust problem.** TA, Cybersecurity, and HR and employee communications need to think together. "This isn’t an argument for adding checks to every interaction or treating all candidates as suspicious." "hiring now sits at the intersection of identity, trust, access and money. There will be more layers and more checks, so my advice: start preparing for this and be proactive about what you can do for your TA org and company."

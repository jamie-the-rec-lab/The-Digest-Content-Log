## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Cassie Leemans, VP of Talent at Craft Ventures, who calibrates early-stage founders on what top 1% talent actually looks like.  ·  **Show:** 10x Recruiting, 27 Mar 2025
**Tags:** candidate calibration, ai strategy, talent density, headcount planning, executive search, hiring manager alignment

**When to use this:** a founder is calibrating "top 1%" against pedigree instead of proven ability.

**Big idea:** You cannot judge the top 1% on paper in the AI era, so calibration means pushing founders off school and degree signals and forcing real conversations with candidates.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- When a founder starts listing school and degree as proxies, Leemans counters by asking what makes that person more qualified than someone who codes at home for fun on weekends and never went to college.
- Treat identifying someone on paper as only one part of the job. Because AI now muddies resumes, competencies such as communication in a finance role can only be verified by talking to the person, which takes a lot of reps and hours.
- Reframe headcount as a vanity metric. Founders now flex reaching X million ARR with 10 people, a shift Leemans credits to David Sacks and the burn-multiple conversation.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Growth at all costs has been gone for about two and a half years.
- Her husband, a recruiter who has never written a line of code, builds his own recruiting tools with Vercel, Cursor and Claude.

**Contrarian take:**
- Leemans argues that with the right tooling you no longer need as many people in any role, and headcount has always been a vanity metric.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Next time a brief leans on pedigree, ask the hiring manager to name the observable output that proves top 1%, then design a conversation to test for it.

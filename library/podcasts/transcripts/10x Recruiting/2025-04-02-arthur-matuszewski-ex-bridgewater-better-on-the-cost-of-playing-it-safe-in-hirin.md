## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Arthur Matuszewski, Managing Partner at Carrara, ex-Bridgewater and ex-Better.com, one of the sharpest talent thinkers on portfolio-style hiring.  ·  **Show:** 10x Recruiting, 02 Apr 2025
**Tags:** candidate calibration, structured interviewing, talent density, executive search, hiring manager alignment, recruiting metrics

**When to use this:** your process optimises to avoid bad hires and quietly filters out your most exceptional, unconventional candidates.

**Big idea:** Minimise false negatives rather than false positives, because you have the least information about a candidate at the moment you make the offer, and the biggest cost is the extraordinary person you rejected.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Keep a hiring anti-portfolio. At Bridgewater they studied candidates they rejected who went on to a Thiel fellowship or built a unique venture, then diagnosed what conventional indicators made them miss the lateral potential, such as the intern who turned in nothing but produced a binder of insights, or the student with a 2.0 who was running a trading book.
- Hire for the work the job actually requires, not for maximal pedigree. Too many Fulbrights as executive assistants means people debating the nature of time instead of scheduling the meeting.
- Derive culture from business strategy, not the reverse. Bridgewater's dot collector and radical measurement existed because its business was reducing human error in investing decade over decade.
- Accept that interviewing is imperfect and get ruthlessly good at fixing misses quickly rather than fetishising getting each hire right.
- Focus on the work and show it. Send weekly snippets of what you did, and be intellectually honest enough to kill sunk-cost ideas the moment they stop working.
- At Carrara, index on demand not supply. Hold a mirror to founders showing what the market is telling them, and frame a hire like a procurement decision of "this is the market, this is price to value, this is the trade".

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Church says he was wrong roughly 60% of the time versus his Carta CEO Henry, which reshaped how he weighs his own opinions.

**Contrarian take:**
- Matuszewski argues halo resumes and fancy titles no longer signal value, partly because those institutions faltered and productivity rose after big tech shed non-producers, and in a world where outperformers wield AI, only points on the board count.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Start a hiring anti-portfolio and track the strong candidates you passed on to review where your heuristics failed.
- Reframe your next exec hire as a market trade and present founders the evidence rather than selling your recommendation.

## Episode description

What if your biggest hiring mistakes aren’t who you hire — but who you don’t?

In this episode of 10x Recruiting, Arthur Matuszewski (Managing Partner at Carrara, ex-Bridgewater & Better) unpacks the high cost of false negatives in hiring — the exceptional people who slip through the cracks because they don’t fit the usual mold. He shares how Bridgewater tracked its “hiring anti-portfolio” to learn from the candidates they rejected who went on to do extraordinary things, and why your talent strategy should be built around maximizing upside, not minimizing risk.

Arthur and Nolan get into how to build more intellectually honest organizations, why pedigree is a poor proxy for performance, and what it means to truly focus on the work.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Jeff Moore, VP of Talent Operations and Workspaces at Toast, who spent 13 years leading elite recruiting teams at Google, worth hearing for a candid, experience-heavy view of exec recruiting, scaling teams and recruiter performance.  ·  **Show:** 10x Recruiting, 30 Jul 2025
**Tags:** executive search, ta leadership, talent density, sourcing, recruiter productivity, recruiting metrics, capacity planning

**When to use this:** You are walking into a leadership role with a big buildout to do, or you want to rethink whether you still need armies of sourcers.

**Big idea:** Now that finding people is a commodity, the alpha in recruiting has moved to relationships, judgement and treating a job change with the gravity it deserves.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Build long-horizon exec relationships: the recruiter who landed Jeff kept in touch for eight years, called on five or six roles, and knew his motivations, his daughter's age and his Boston timeline, so he only ever pinged relevant opportunities.
- Get exec buy-in for a big buildout by being explicit on cost, articulating this is what I am doing, why, what it will cost per hire and what you get, then just run the math.
- Assess recruiting hires with an informal case study, a GCA-style problem such as how would you hire a person on the moon, to see if they collaborate and pick up what you are putting down.
- Ask candidates and recruiters for the one hire they would tell their family about at Thanksgiving, which reveals both their passion and whether they understand how that hire impacted the business.
- Treat a job change as one of life's most stressful events alongside marriage, bereavement, having a kid and buying a house, and go only as fast as the candidate wants to go.
- Evaluate recruiter performance as a marriage of art and science, the what and the how, being willing to eat a miss of two hires against a goal of 20 if every VP says the recruiter is outstanding.

**Contrarian take:**
- Jeff argues talent density is a buzzword that should mean different things at different companies; a recruiter who hits their goals every quarter for years is an A player, and you do not need everyone to be an A-plus, since inevitable performance issues remain even then.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Jeff scaled the Toast recruiting team from 22 to roughly 80 in three to four months; his Google team had been around 70 people; his own passive process at Toast took about four months and seven or eight conversations, including five or six with his future boss.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before joining a leadership role, get crystal clear on your buildout plan and cost-per-hire narrative so you never feel reactive pressure later.
- Question whether sourcers add value on commodity roles and redeploy that headcount toward relationship-heavy searches like executive search where sourcing still matters.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Richard Yan, Head of People and Talent at Airwallex, a former private equity and M&A consultant who has also built people functions at Booking Holdings group companies, DoorDash and Carta  ·  **Show:** 10x Recruiting, 04 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, human-ai collaboration, building ai tools, reference checks, candidate calibration, executive search, ta leadership, interview design

**When to use this:** your leadership is citing "AI efficiency" to justify layoffs or freezes, or you need a sharper way to test judgment in an AI-saturated interview process.

**Big idea:** AI does not replace judgment, it raises the bar for it, and most "AI layoffs" are really overhiring dressed up as a strategy story for investors.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Treat any "AI made us leaner" headline with suspicion: Yan argues companies "overhired... and it's very convenient to say oh it's you know with AI we can do much more" when the real driver was hiring too fast during the low-rate era, then needing a face-saving story for a correction.
- Decentralise AI adoption rather than running it top down. Airwallex lets teams build their own tools, then has people "share their successes and their failures" internally, which "inspires a little bit of internal competition" and spreads adoption faster than a central AI taskforce dictating tools.
- To expose "AI slop" in written work, do not accept the first polished answer. Yan's rule: "any leader is going to double click and say hey, tell me more, like what do you mean by this" because AI is "very good at sounding confident" but does not contextualise the reality of the business it is writing about.
- For senior case-study interviews, borrow the technique Yan describes from a client: let the candidate use AI to prepare, then when they present, keep firing second and third order follow-up questions "to see how far, how deep the person can go", since reading out an AI-generated answer collapses under real probing.
- For senior and executive hires, judgment and taste are still assessed the old way: "the best way to assess for those things today is with back channel references... using that person's career history and their wins and losses from the people who have worked most intimately with them."

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Airwallex's people and talent team has grown from about 40 people (when the company was roughly 1,100 people) to about 150 people supporting a company of about 2,800 today, and the team is "hiring more than ever before."

**Tools & prompts:**
- Airwallex's internal AI stack for the people team is Claude, Gemini, plus an internally built vibe-coding platform that lets non-engineers on the team build their own tools.

**Contrarian take:**
- Yan, self-described former AI pessimist, now argues AI is a net job creator rather than a destroyer at Airwallex: "I think clearly AI is creating more jobs" and that layoffs blamed on AI are usually overhiring correcting itself, not genuine automation-driven headcount reduction.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- In your next senior-hire debrief, add one mandatory round of "tell me more" follow-up questions on any written case study answer before scoring it, specifically hunting for whether the candidate can go a level deeper than what is on the page.
- Run a back-channel reference conversation focused explicitly on judgment and taste, not just competence, for your next leadership hire.

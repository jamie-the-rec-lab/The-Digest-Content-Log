**When to use this:** you need to brief your TA leadership or a vendor conversation on what the EU AI Act actually requires right now, versus what can wait.

**Big idea:** the EU AI Act does not ban AI scoring, ranking or matching candidates, it makes providers and employers prove the system is trustworthy, and most of that obligation does not bite until 2 December 2027, so the real risk this year is being caught unprepared when the guidance lands.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Know the three live dates: 2 February 2025 brought in the ban on prohibited AI uses plus "AI literacy" duties (the people responsible for AI in your org "have to be literate in the context of the AI that they're using"), 2 August 2026 brought in transparency duties (telling candidates when they're interacting with AI), and the big one, the high-risk obligations for scoring, ranking and matching tools, was deferred to 2 December 2027.
- High-risk status is not just about a candidate-scoring tool. Redston: "it's any system and it's its intended use as well... it's about any system that has a material impact, a material influence on that decision," which he said could reach "all the way to programmatic advertising" if it shapes who even sees a job ad.
- For an AI interviewer or chatbot, a simple spoken disclosure is likely enough to satisfy transparency: Farmer agreed that a line like "Hi, I'm Hong, I'm your AI interviewer" "would be sufficient... to make it clear that you are interacting with AI," and the more human-like the system sounds, the more that disclaimer is needed, not less.
- Watch your own team's vibe-coded tools, not just vendor products. The panel's warning: someone inside your own TA team could take "a relatively administrative CV parsing tool that's looking for spelling errors" and tweak it so it "ranks the CVs or promotes certain keywords or influences decision-making," which can turn an internal script into a high-risk system without anyone deciding that on purpose.
- The Act is extraterritorial by design. Farmer: "if you are a UK business interviewing globally for a role, and you're dealing with an individual who's based in Spain, for example, you're pulled into the legislation that way as well," so being outside the EU does not exempt a UK employer hiring EU-based candidates.
- Do not wait for the 2027 deadline to get compliant. One panelist noted vendors are already losing deals over it: "if you haven't got your house in order when it comes to compliance under the EU AI Act, even though you've got time, you're going to start... coming a cropper when it comes to the procurement process because TA teams... are getting savvy and asking the right questions of vendors."

**When to use this:** you are deciding whether to replace a stack of disconnected point-solution AI tools with a single orchestrated multi-agent platform, or you are trying to get your team's AI experimentation under control.

**Big idea:** the shift from disconnected AI point tools to agentic AI is not about buying another tool, it is about whether agents can hand off to each other natively, and vendors pushing single-platform orchestration have an obvious commercial interest in that answer being "no, use us for everything."

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Jovio's pitch for a single orchestrating agent claims it can cut admin to "only 20% admin and then 80% done on high value impact work" by having one agent conduct sourcing, outreach, screening, AI interview and scheduling natively rather than stitching separate tools together.
- Morning Star built a dedicated in-house role on the people and culture team "entirely devoted to helping enable our entire organization" to adopt AI tools, so every build-versus-buy decision runs through one person with real expertise rather than ad hoc experimentation.
- Morning Star runs an internal "AI academy" that almost the entire global team has been through, moving the org from an early "vibe coding" phase where people jumped to an AI solution "before they'd even defined the problem" to a more disciplined evaluation process.
- On keeping sensitive data out of general-purpose AI: Mary Kay's team will not build an ATS or CRM inside a tool like Microsoft Copilot because of data security and privacy guarantees, and instead keeps candidate PII inside the ATS and uses embedded AI within that environment.

**Contrarian take:** Paul Bacon (Jovio) flags that recruiters building their own AI tools can become "a distraction" from their actual day-to-day work, comparing it to a colleague who "spent an entire evening building this AI animated video" purely because building AI things is "kind of intoxicating."

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before adopting any new agentic AI tool, ask the vendor specifically how it hands data and tasks off to your existing tools, not just what it can do standalone.
- If your team is in an unstructured AI-experimentation phase, name one person to own AI tool evaluation and adoption decisions rather than letting each recruiter pick their own.

## Episode description

Disconnected AI vs. Multi-Agent Ecosystems: Building a TA Tech Stack in the Agentic AI Era

For the past few years, talent acquisition teams have been bolting AI tools onto their existing tech stacks - an AI scheduler here, a chatbot there, a CV parser with some machine learning sprinkled on top. It's worked, sort of!

But we're now entering the agentic AI era, where autonomous AI agents don't just assist with tasks; they execute entire workflows, hand off work to other agents, and make decisions that ripple across your entire hiring process. 

The question is no longer "Which AI tool should we buy?" but "How do we architect a system where multiple AI agents actually talk to each other?" A disconnected stack of point solutions risks creating more friction than it solves, while a well-designed multi-agent ecosystem could fundamentally reshape how recruiting gets done. In this session, we'll explore what it takes to build a TA tech stack that isn't just AI-enabled - it's agentic by design.

The Point Solution Problem – Why your current stack of disconnected AI tools is creating data silos, workflow gaps, and a fragmented candidate experience
What "Agentic AI" Actually Means for TA – Moving beyond chatbots and auto-schedulers to autonomous agents that can source, screen, engage, and coordinate across the hiring funnel

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Matt Wilson, co-founder and CEO of Jack & Jill, a company under 18 months old that just closed a Series A of around $40 million on the day of this interview  ·  **Show:** Brainfood Live On Air, 15 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai agents, ai sourcing, candidate ai tools, recruitment marketing, human-ai collaboration, ai strategy

**When to use this:** you want to understand the "agent for every candidate" model, where an AI agent represents the individual jobseeker rather than the employer, and what that means for how vendors will source and match talent.

**Big idea:** Jack & Jill has built two paired AI agents, Jack for individual professionals and Jill for hiring companies, and pitches this as a fix for how inefficient job search already is: even a strong personal network only exposes someone to "maybe only getting introduced to like 1%" of the roles that exist for them.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Jack, the candidate-side agent, runs an onboarding call of "about 15 to 20 minutes" with a jobseeker, comparable to a coffee chat with a recruiter, to capture their experience, ambitions and salary expectations in detail, then uses that to scan "every single job listing that gets posted anywhere online" on their behalf.
- Jack's scope runs end to end: supporting applications, running mock interviews with feedback, benchmarking compensation, and even doing a dry run of negotiating an offer.
- Jill, the employer-side agent, is loaded with context on the role, required skills and company culture, then connects the hiring company to people in Jack's candidate network.
- Jack currently works for "close to 400,000 people today" globally, concentrated in London, San Francisco and New York, and Wilson states the stated goal is "to take this to a billion people over the next couple of years."

**Contrarian take:** Wilson argues that most AI vendors building for the existing hiring workflow are "supercharging the inefficiency rather than" rethinking how job search and hiring should actually work, since the current model (job boards, networks, agencies) exposes both candidates and employers to only a small fraction of relevant matches.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- If you are evaluating candidate-facing AI tools, ask what percentage of the market a candidate-side agent can realistically cover versus a traditional network or job board, since that coverage gap is the problem Jack & Jill is explicitly targeting.

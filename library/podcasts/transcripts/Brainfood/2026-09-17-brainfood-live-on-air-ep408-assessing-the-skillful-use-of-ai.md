## Actionable summary
**Guest:** David House, senior engineering lead at G2I (a staffing and recruiting business that now supplies engineers to AI frontier labs), who has hired dozens of engineers since agents became standard. Note that G2I is a vendor and he closes the episode by pitching it.  ·  **Show:** Brainfood Live on Air Ep408 (host Hung), 17 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai coding tools, interview design, take-home assessments, structured interviewing, skills-based hiring, ai upskilling, human-ai collaboration, candidate calibration, engineering hiring

**When to use this:** you are screening or briefing on software engineers and need to judge how well they use AI agents, now that clean code and polished GitHub projects are no longer a signal.

**Big idea:** The engineering skill that matters has moved from writing code to verifying it, so assessment should test how a candidate delegates, prompts and verifies, not what they can recall.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Replace recall questions such as "what is a list comprehension" with open, conversational questions on three themes: delegation (too little forfeits productivity, too much leaves work too big to review), prompting (is your judgement baked into the prompt) and verification, which House calls probably the most important.
- Tailor those questions to the role. House's example is that a healthcare role puts more weight on verification, while a scrappy startup puts more weight on delegation.
- Keep take-home tests, but change what you score. House's team had AI vibe code the same one-day take-home from a single prompt and built verifiers for it. The AI scored nearly perfectly, and three quarters of human candidates scored worse, which he treated as a negative signal about their involvement.
- Follow every take-home with an interview probing fine-grained detail of what they built. One candidate with a near perfect score was knocked out because he could not answer detail questions and had evidently vibe coded it.
- Use a live exercise to see how someone uses AI. House sets a deliberately easy task and tells the candidate the code is not being assessed. In one 25 minute session no code was written because the candidate spent it pitching their agent orchestration tooling.
- Keep the final yes or no with a human. House found the latest model was overly conservative and punished every mistake equally, so he used AI for high signal information and made the call himself.
- Pre screen CVs with AI by asking it to take a critical eye to every line, search the internet to verify the companies exist, judge whether the person could have done those things in that role, and surface contrarian evidence that claims are inflated.
- Ask candidates how their trust in AI has changed over time. House says everyone he would trust to teach him AI describes a pendulum and a measured view of where it is good and bad.

**Tools & prompts:**
- The CV check prompt is described in House's words as asking the AI to "take a critical eye to every single one of these lines" and to "surface any contrarian evidence that might suggest that these claims are inflated". No specific product was named for it.

**Contrarian take:**
- House disagrees that take-homes are dead. He also argues that junior engineers are undervalued, said he is "not at all concerned" about juniors lacking pre AI reps, and cited an intern who was building in three months what he took three years to build.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Ask each engineering hiring manager what skilful AI use looks like on their team and in their codebase, then write one delegation, one prompting and one verification question for that role.
- Add "how has your trust in AI changed over time" to your screening call.

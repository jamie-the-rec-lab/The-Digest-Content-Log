## Actionable summary
**Host:** Hung, hosting a live panel on Brainfood. Panellists: Alla Pavlova (hands on recruiter, Amsterdam), Glo (recruiter at Lovable), Andrew (senior staff engineer and director of engineering, Monzo), Pedro Olivier (HR tech founder, now building search and rescue robots), Sai (founder and CEO, Crowdcast), George Lorac (founder, Worktech) and Brandon (co-founder and CEO, Scalus).  ·  **Show:** Brainfood Live on Air Ep409, 18 Sep 2026
**Tags:** building ai tools, rec tech, ai governance, recruiter workflow, recruiter productivity, context engineering, ai agents, recruiting operations, talent intelligence, human-ai collaboration

**Vendor flags:** Brandon (Scalus) appears as a sponsor and sells an ATS, so his pricing offer and his claim that the winners are open, flexible platforms are self interested. Glo works for Lovable, the vibe coding product the panel keeps citing. Sai runs Crowdcast, the platform the show streams on, and says his product is moving toward being a general platform. Treat all three as vendor views. Brandon's agency owner anecdote is second hand and unnamed.

**When to use this:** you are deciding whether recruiters on your team should build their own tools with a vibe coding product, and what rules to put around it.

**Big idea:** Vibe coding is viable for small, single purpose tools, and the control that matters is the blast radius if something goes wrong, not the size of the company.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Build a throwaway micro app for one conversation. Alla used one prompt, spoken through a dictation app, in Claude Design to make a live board for a hiring manager with no interview time. Moving a slider showed the hire slipping from 4 December to January once an eight week notice period was added. It took about five minutes plus a few minutes of waiting.
- Decide what to build by asking who will use it, how often, and what data it holds. Glo says anything touching PII is probably not worth building, and that change management takes days at a startup, weeks at a scale up and months at a large company.
- Use the blast radius test that George Lorac names. Ask whether a failure affects only you, your team, or candidates. Sai runs three levels at Crowdcast: personal tools have no restrictions, internal admin tool pages are low bar, and anything on the customer facing app is engineers only, with a designer signing off.
- Build with the team rather than alone. Alla suggests a scheduled 30 minute Friday session where the team picks one problem and builds it together.
- Treat recruiter built tools as a demand signal. Andrew says Monzo looks at what hiring teams are vibe coding and asks why, then decides whether to harden it or buy it. Monzo sees this because staff only use approved AI providers that it can observe.
- Glo built an internal talent and competitive intelligence tool on public data only, for example what a target company is building over the next six months and which talking points to use with a candidate. It later became a Slack agent that queries data and does some sourcing.
- Expect a maintenance bill. Brandon's example is an agency owner running four recruiters who said 80% of his time was now spent maintaining the systems he built. Pedro and Glo both warn about tool sprawl and say the tool must have credibility with the team.
- Giving agents guard rails is now a management job. Sai describes a shared Slack agent with read only production access that support staff use to investigate sessions, and says his job is deciding who may ask it to do what.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Scalus pricing offer, per Brandon: send your existing contracts, they add them up and halve the total. This is a vendor offer and is unverified.
- George Lorac says he has evaluated about 100 startups for an HR tech pitchfest, and that some who did not make the cut had vibe coded products and could not answer what SOC 2 is.
- Alla says she gets "thousands" of CVs that match a job description perfectly, sometimes "a thousand" CVs for one job in one day in some regions. This is anecdotal.
- Hung says "some of the stats that some of the ATS's have come out with" show fewer recruiters than three years ago but a similar placement rate. No source or figure was given, so do not quote it.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Claude Design, Lovable and Cursor are named as vibe coding tools, and Whisper Flow for dictating prompts. Sai says Crowdcast's team builds on Notion, connected to email, for CRM style work.

**Contrarian take:**
- Andrew argues that AI will fill ever larger gaps between SaaS products, so the idea that only the small edges of SaaS disappear is "very optimistic". Sai predicts specialised SaaS will struggle and general building block platforms will thrive, which is also what his own company sells.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Write a one page rule for your team using three tiers (personal, team, candidate facing) and ban candidate or PII data from any recruiter built tool.
- Pick one recurring stakeholder argument, such as unavailable hiring managers, and build a five minute visual for it.

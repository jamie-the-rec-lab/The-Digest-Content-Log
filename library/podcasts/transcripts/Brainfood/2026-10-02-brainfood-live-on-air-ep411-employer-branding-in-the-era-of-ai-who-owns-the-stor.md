**When to use this:** hiring managers or leaders want to launch their own vibe coded career pages, or you are asked how to make your employer brand show up in ChatGPT and other LLM answers.

**Big idea:** When anyone can build a career site in minutes, the site stops being the advantage, and what matters is owning approved source content, guard rails, and proof of your brand claims that LLMs can find and verify.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Separate three assets before debating control. Brian Cheney distinguishes the career site (the whole experience and EVP content), the career page (where people search jobs), and landing or job family pages. His view is that a head of finance can reasonably build a job family page, but nine times out of ten lacks the information and guard rails to build a site.
- Allow a nested model. Hung and Mo describe a master career site owned centrally, with team pages built on top. Mo's idea for BCG is a compliant vibe coding sandbox using approved components and data points, with an automated compliance check, ATS connection and GDPR check before publishing.
- Make the career site a content engine, not just a destination. Mo is "doubting more and more" that the career page is the entry point, and suggests it becomes the content infrastructure that feeds LLM answers and local pages.
- Make LLM visibility the step above SEO. Brian says SEO stays the baseline, then add FAQs written so an LLM can lift an answer, frequency of mentions, and third party content such as Reddit, Glassdoor and employee posts on LinkedIn.
- Prove the specific brand value you claim. In Basad's Rotterdam hospital test, the LLM rewarded the hospital whose career site had consistent evidence of its stated focus on development, including a section listing available training. His forecast is that companies focused on specific brand values with supporting data show up more.
- Test with a vendor trial before committing. Ashley says Jovio has a builder programme with free credits, and that landing pages are a softer entry point than a full career site.
- Measure applicant quality, not volume. Hung asks how to measure fewer applications but better hires from a career site, and says nobody has answered it yet.

**Data & benchmarks:** (all self reported by panellists, none sourced to a published study)
- Basad: 11% of the 563 biggest employers in the Netherlands have a chatbot on their career site this year.
- Basad: across five sessions with TA and employer brand leaders at a recruitment firm, at most 20% of the room used any AI tool, and most who did said Copilot because they were forced to.
- Basad's test asking LLMs for top employers found about 50% of results began with the letters A to E, from research done by a master student. Brian added that LLMs favoured companies listed on more best employer lists over those ranked higher.
- Basad: for four Rotterdam hospitals, 95% of the LLM's comparison feedback came from the hospitals' career sites.
- Jason: at HCA Healthcare his team ran 4,000 brands, over a million visitors a month and 100,000 hires a year, and he says over 50% of hires come off the career site. That second figure is unsourced and specific to healthcare.
- Ashley: the first draft of a generated site takes two to three minutes, and the live poll showed zero attendees whose career site is built by natural language prompts.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Jovio career site builder (build from a prompt, from a URL that pulls branding, or from an HTML artifact made in Claude or OpenAI). Ashley says it edits page by page and will not do a single prompt full site overhaul. Basad says his own events site stays on WordPress because it is easiest to get right.

**Contrarian take:**
- Hung, by his own admission partly tongue in cheek, argues that quantity beats quality for LLM visibility because LLMs count mentions rather than ratios.

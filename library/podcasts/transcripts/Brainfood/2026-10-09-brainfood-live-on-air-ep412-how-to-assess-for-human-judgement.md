## Actionable summary
**Host:** Hung Lee, Brainfood Live, with Richard Clayton (leadership development), Jess von Bank (co-founder, Now to Next, an AI transformation firm) and Joseph Braithwaite (business consultant who built a job description to resume capability matching tool)  ·  **Show:** Brainfood Live On Air, Ep412, 9 Oct 2026 (sponsored by Greenhouse)
**Tags:** human-ai collaboration, skills-based hiring, work trials, interview design, ai screening, ai bias, early career talent, candidate calibration, recruiter career growth, ai strategy

**When to use this:** a leader says humans will supply judgement while AI does the processing and you are asked how to hire for that, or when AI-polished applications all look equally good.

**Big idea:** Judgement is what you apply when the rules run out, it is domain specific and built through experience, and because AI makes everyone look articulate it cannot be read from documents and has to be observed.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Use Jess von Bank's filter for what to hand to AI: if it is a no-brainer, give it to AI, and if it requires a brain, keep it with a person. Panelists agreed that where a rule can be applied, execute the rule.
- Define judgement narrowly. Richard Clayton said it means wrestling with contradictory sources inside a domain you know, so someone good at judging candidates may judge other things badly.
- Test live behaviour, not self-report. Jess described a Kroger floor assessment for "fast, fun and friendly" and said the real test is putting people in a complaint situation and watching the first five seconds. Joseph Braithwaite's version: show me you can do it rather than scoring tics and flags.
- Grade interviews for complexity of thinking with a rubric, as leadership development does. Clayton said you can set rules and a rubric in AI that scores the level of complexity in answers within a domain.
- Notice sense-making answers. Jess hired a student because, asked if he trusted AI, he answered that he trusted his own judgement of AI.
- Add a "twilight layer" of human visibility. Jess, borrowing Ethan Mollick's dark factory idea, said most teams should keep humans able to see, interrupt, inspect and explain the output.
- Treat psychometric tests as weak. Clayton dismissed them for self-reporting; the panel gave them no endorsement for judgement.
- Protect the apprenticeship pipeline. Joseph and Jess argued that cutting junior hiring and learning reps leaves no one able to challenge AI in five to ten years, while Hung Lee doubted organisations would trade short-term efficiency for training.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Clayton cited research (not named or sourced on air) that AI raises task productivity about 200% but raises rework and judgement checking about 860%.
- He also said three or four years ago only 11% of CHROs reported having a leadership bench.
- The live poll on how attendees assess judgement showed interview in the lead, with two for psychometric test, one for functional test and one for work trial.

**Contrarian take:**
- Joseph Braithwaite argued the ATS pre-filters candidates so the shortlist is already biased, and that it should go back to being a filing system with a person reviewing the rest. Hung Lee countered that the pool is dirty and recruiters should build a filter instead.
- Jess von Bank: AI does not make us smarter, it makes us look smarter.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Add one live work sample or simulation to your final stage for any role where you say judgement matters, and score it against a written rubric.
- Ask candidates how they use AI and how they checked its output, then listen for a view of their own.

## Episode description

HOW TO ASSESS FOR HUMAN JUDGEMENT

As AI takes over more of the heavy lifting in recruitment — screening CVs, drafting interview questions, even conducting first-round interviews — the skills we hire for are shifting. Technical proficiency can increasingly be automated, augmented or verified by machines. What's becoming harder to replace, and harder to find, is human judgement: the ability to weigh imperfect information, read the room, make a call under uncertainty, and be accountable for the outcome. But if judgement is the thing we need most, how on earth do we assess for it? In this episode of Brainfood Live on Air, Hung Lee and our panel of experts dig into the science (and the art) of evaluating one of the most elusive qualities in any candidate.

What Is Judgement, Anyway? – Defining the capability before we try to measure it, and why it matters more in an AI-augmented workplace
The Failure of the Hypothetical – Why "what would you do if..." questions reveal rehearsed answers, not real judgement

Judgement Under Pressure – Whether stress-testing candidates actually predicts on-the-job decision quality

## Actionable summary
**Host:** Hung Lee with co-host Alla Pavlova, and guests including Elise (leads training and education at Metaview, the episode sponsor), Guillaume (sourcing trainer and recops lead), Wim (sourcer), Chloe (recops and security), Marcel (AI recruitment engineer trainer) and Chad Horn (global head of talent sourcing, ServiceNow)  ·  **Show:** Brainfood Live On Air, Ep413, 16 Oct 2026
**Tags:** sourcing, ai sourcing, ai agents, talent intelligence, recruiting operations, candidate fraud, recruiter career growth, talent pools, outbound sourcing, ai upskilling

**When to use this:** you run or sit in a sourcing team and need a view on what to automate, what to keep and how to reposition the role.

**Big idea:** Sourcing defined as typing queries into a database and deciding whether to contact someone is being automated, so the surviving work is building, talent intelligence, relationship and engagement, and checking AI output.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Move sourcers upstream. Chad Horn said ServiceNow's sourcers now sit closer to the business, build talent pools and pipelines ahead of need, and use market data to influence location strategy, compensation, benefits and profile criteria, leaving requisition filling to recruiters and agents.
- Start in your own ATS before searching externally. Marcel said many companies jump straight to LinkedIn and forget past candidates with phone numbers already on file.
- Treat candidate fraud as new top-of-funnel work. Chloe described three cases (fake persona, identity theft, identity leasing), said the signals from tools still need checking, and recommended training recruiters, sourcers, hiring managers and interviewers so different people spot different red flags.
- Do not rely on filters. Guillaume argued that LinkedIn Recruiter shows only a small slice of a large result set (he said a 20K result search shows 5%) and that inactive profiles are treated as dead, so filter-based searches fail; he said he could prove it.
- Look where AI-optimised profiles are not. Wim noted unoptimised profiles are being pushed to the algorithm's dead zone, echoing Glenn Cathy's old tactic of searching incomplete profiles.
- Compete on outreach. Alla Pavlova said her reply rate is still close to 90% because the message includes something specific, and Hung Lee added that the relationship, not the copy, drives replies.
- Keep a learning habit. Alla books 30 minutes a week of self-learning and suggests one new tool per day, week or month.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Metaview claims (vendor figures from its own employee): Fillmore was generally available, made about a third of Metaview's sales hires last quarter (eight hires), did 86,000 hours of recruiting work in 30 days, and in a case study filled a software engineer role in 13 days from one email that was answered in 27 minutes. A senior marketing role took 30 days. Fillmore runs in Slack only, not Teams, with no timeline given.
- Guillaume's elasticity point: an IT team can absorb 600 projects a year instead of 10, but a company will not hire 270 people instead of 30 to 40, so fewer recruiters and sourcers will be needed.

**Contrarian take:**
- Guillaume (opening keynote at SOSU): if your job is identifying data in a database, saying yes or no and contacting with empathy, a machine will replace you; if you are only a user of tools you will be replaced, so build.
- Marcel predicted more sourcing demand because open roles now attract a tsunami of AI and fake applications, so firms should source quietly instead of posting.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Pick one task in your sourcing week that is pure retrieval and build or prompt an agent for it, then use the freed time on outreach and market insight for one hard-to-hire role.
- Write a one-page talent insight for a hiring manager on a hard role that includes a location or compensation recommendation.

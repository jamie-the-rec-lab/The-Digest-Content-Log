## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Heather Colvin, Senior Technical Recruiter at Smart Data (an IT staffing and software consulting firm that hires for clients across the US). She is an outbound sourcer, conference-circuit speaker, and heavy LinkedIn commenter worth listening to for a practitioner, individual-contributor view of recruiting rather than leadership theory.  ·  **Show:** Building the Talent Machine, 16 Dec 2025
**Tags:** recruitment marketing, recruiter career growth, employer storytelling, in-person sourcing, sourcing, ta leadership, candidate communication, staffing and agencies

**When to use this:** Reach for this when you are an agency or corporate IC recruiter who wants to build a personal brand, source people who have no online profile, and use AI to fix the resume and interview process rather than bolt it on.

**Big idea:** Recruiting is humble people work, so the durable advantage is showing up imperfectly and human (in comments, on stage, and on the phone) at a time when AI is pushing everything towards sameness.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Comment-led growth: Colvin often gets more impressions on her comments than her posts, and Adam Posner first knew her because she "leaves the best comments"; she treats commenting as a low-barrier way to be discovered before you can post confidently.
- Put impressions in human scale: Colvin and the host argue the emotional gap between 700 and 1,000 impressions is bigger than 1,000 to 10,000, and that 700 impressions is roughly 100 people who would overwhelm you if they turned up at your door, so vanity metrics should be re-read as a filled room.
- The 72-hour rule: Colvin's outbound agency playbook (branded "prompt to placement, an AI recruiting playbook" at Ben Mena's summit) that she is turning into an actual book to earn more speaking slots; she stresses re-framing it for corporate recruiters who work inbound rather than her reactive outbound world.
- Cold-call opener with personality: when someone answers an unknown number she says "go you for picking up a number you don't know, by the way this is Heather, I'm a senior technical recruiter," and on voicemails with no name she says "Brandon or not, hope you have a blessed day"; she deliberately fumbles because "AI is not going to fumble," using imperfection as proof of being human.
- Selfie strategy at events: at RecFest she chose to photograph people she had never met rather than existing friends, then posted the photos afterwards, because she remembered wanting someone to shout her out when she was unknown.
- Lead with grace on the ATS resume trick: rather than assume bad intent, ask the candidate what made them do it, since a resume coach may have inserted the trick without the candidate knowing.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Host cites roughly 35,000 followers as his audience, framed as "bigger than Madison Square Garden."
- Grayscale (sponsor) claims teams cut time to fill by 30% and cut ghosting in half.
- A Forbes article dated 2 July 2025 profiled someone who grew on LinkedIn through commenting without posting.
- Colvin was laid off in 2018 after 11 years at one job and joined LinkedIn at the end of 2018 with zero connections; she notes her small town of about 1,000 people probably has only around 20 LinkedIn profiles.

**Tools & prompts:**
- ChatGPT at 20 dollars a month, pitched as better than paying 400 dollars to a resume writer: speak aloud into voice mode answering the questions a recruiter would ask (or "what would you say to your friend about what you do professionally"), then tell it to write that in bullet-point format; to tailor, "paste a description in there and just say reorganise this based on the interest of this role" without changing the content.
- Practise interviews by pulling up interview questions in ChatGPT voice mode, answering them, then asking how close the answer is to STAR format.
- AI sourcing tools on month-to-month contracts for about 175 dollars, framed as less than a pair of Jordans, with a phone-number lookup to enable cold calls.
- Match2 (m-a-t-2) by Elaine Orler, a candidate-experience product that surfaces one application to a company's other matching requisitions so a candidate is considered for many roles from a single apply.

**Contrarian take:**
- Colvin says giving away your full playbook costs nothing because "even if you tell people how to win, maybe 5% will go do it."
- The host argues conferences are wrongly used as a reward for top performers, and that L&D should fund IC recruiters and coordinators to attend for skill development the way go-to-market teams are sent, even tying a scanned badge or qualified lead to revenue.
- Both argue AI interviews solve the employer's problem while ignoring candidate fatigue: one interview for the company can be number 80 for the individual (50 roles at 15 minutes each is about 12.5 hours), so the fix is HR-compliant feedback loops and surfacing candidates across multiple reqs.
- The host claims the current labour market is a recalibration back to pre-pandemic levels, and Colvin adds the downturn began before AI was public, driven by 2021 to 2022 over-hiring rather than AI.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Add ChatGPT ATS parameters or lobby your vendor to ignore any text below 10-point font that is white, to defeat hidden keyword-stuffing on inbound resumes.
- Rebuild your resume by talking into ChatGPT voice mode and converting it to bullets, then reorganise against a pasted job description.
- Start commenting with informed perspective plus personality on other people's posts before worrying about your own posting cadence.

## Episode description

Recruiting has always been a people business. Somewhere along the way, we let metrics, tools, and platforms convince us otherwise.

This conversation with Heather Colvin was a long-overdue reset.

Heather is a Senior Technical Recruiter at Smart Data, a familiar face on the conference circuit, and one of those rare people whose presence translates just as clearly online as it does in real life. What stood out in this episode wasn’t tactics or tools. It was Heather’s conviction that community, courage, and showing up imperfectly are still the real differentiators in recruiting.

We talk about what happens when online relationships move offline. How meeting at events like RecFest changes the texture of connection. Once you’ve shared a laugh, a conversation, or bad conference coffee, relationships stop being transactional. That shift matters in a profession built on trust.

Heather also breaks down the long, quiet seasons of growth most people never see. The early days of posting on LinkedIn. Learning to add value in comments before building a platform. Showing up consistently before anyone was paying attention. That path led to real relationships, real invitations, and real momentum.

A major theme in this episode is reframing influence. Numbers can distort reality. A thousand impressions may feel small on a dashboard, but enormous if those people were standing in your living room. Recruiting influence compounds through individual moments, not vanity metrics.

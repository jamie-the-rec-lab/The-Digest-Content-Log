**When to use this:** You're hiring technical talent and your sourcing feels like a guessing game; you want to understand what great technical sourcing looks like.

**Big idea:** Great technical sourcing isn't about keywords or database mining; it's about understanding how people with target skills congregate (GitHub repos, open-source communities, tech Twitter), what projects they work on, and what growth arcs they follow.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Character arc sourcing: Instead of searching for keywords in a resume database, think about candidate growth trajectories; someone who went from junior backend engineer to DevOps specialist to SRE shows the learning pattern you probably want.
- Community-based sourcing: Find where target talent congregates (GitHub, Stack Overflow, tech Twitter, specific Slack communities) and spend time there building relationships before you have a specific role.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- For your next technical hire, instead of posting a job and screening resumes, spend one hour on GitHub identifying people who have built projects relevant to your needs; reach out to 10 with personalised messages.

## Episode description

Martel Howard is building the talent machine over at Coalfire.
He’s one of those people on the internet who consistently puts out great content, cares about the community, and is passionate about the craft of sourcing.

I sat down with Martel Howard to talk about sourcing the way it actually shows up in the work and some of the latest tooling. Martel is a technical sourcing specialist who thinks about profiles in the sense of character arcs which is pretty cool interpretation of keyword search.

We got into:

• Why curiosity is still the most valuable skill in sourcing

• Why it is not enough to read text on a resume

• How some of the best hires come from reading the gray.

He shared how:

• Building candidate personas creates institutional memory so teams are not starting from zero on every req

• How sourcing done well can dramatically improve pass through rates at the top of funnel.

We also spent time on Boolean, AI tools, and what changes as sourcing becomes more about talent intelligence.

Martel and I agree that AI is helpful, but only if you understand the foundations first. We talked about A/B testing search strategies, pulling silver medalists from the ATS, writing outreach that actually gets replies, and why sourcing is shifting toward a more strategic advisory role. The through line was simple. Data is easy to share, insight is harder to apply, and the real value of a sourcer is knowing how to use that insight to evolve the search.

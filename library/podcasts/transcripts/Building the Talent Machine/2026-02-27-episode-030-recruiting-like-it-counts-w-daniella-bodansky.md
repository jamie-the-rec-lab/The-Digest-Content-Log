**Tags:** recruiting operations, quality of hire, candidate calibration, scorecards, work trials, recruiting metrics, hiring manager alignment

**When to use this:** You're building a recruiting process that needs to balance speed (we need people fast) with quality (we can't afford hiring mistakes) and you want to know what actually predicts hire success.

**Big idea:** Recruiting processes that balance speed and quality require ruthless prioritisation of which assessment methods actually predict on-the-job success (spoiler: unstructured interviews and gut feelings don't) and which signal-to-noise ratios are worth your time.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Signal-to-noise ratio assessment: For each step of your hiring process, ask: What fraction of candidates pass this stage, and of those who pass, what fraction succeed on the job? If the correlation is weak, that step is noise; cut it or redesign it.
- Strong yes versus soft yes: Use explicit calibration language ("strong yes," "soft yes," "no") instead of five-point scales; train your team to flag "soft yes" decisions for deeper review because those are your prediction-error risk zone.
- Work trials over case studies: When assessing competency, give candidates actual work to do (pairing session, take-home project, limited-scope assignment) instead of case interviews; work performance predicts job performance better than case performance.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Audit your hiring process: For each stage, calculate: (1) % of candidates who advance, (2) % of hired candidates who are still employed and performing well 12+ months later. If the advance rate is high (80%+), your stage has low signal; redesign or cut it.

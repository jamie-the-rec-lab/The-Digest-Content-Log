## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Cameron Murray, founder of Venture Talent, a solo agency doing go to market recruiting for seed to Series B AI startups, previously in house at Amazon.  ·  **Show:** Building the Talent Machine, 25 Aug 2026
**Tags:** gtm hiring, early-stage hiring, startup hiring, hiring manager alignment, recruiting operations, recruitment marketing, candidate experience, staffing and agencies

**When to use this:** Use this when a hiring manager or founder is not taking the process seriously, when RecOps is starting to matter more than the candidate, or when you are trying to build inbound business development as a solo or agency recruiter.

**Big idea:** Great recruiting is judgement and trust built with the hiring manager, not candidate retrieval, and for early stage companies the one RecOps investment that actually matters is fast, candidate first scheduling.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Track a submittal to fill ratio and work backwards from it. Cameron aims for seven candidates submitted per hire, so if he has not submitted seven, he does not expect a placement yet.
- Read hiring manager readiness through behaviour rather than words. He treats promptness to meetings, quick responses and genuine curiosity about feedback as the signal a founder is serious about hiring.
- Prioritise the candidate's calendar over the hiring manager's. He argues treating a candidate's time like a client's produces both a better experience and faster scheduling.
- Niche deliberately and say no to out of lane work. Cameron only takes seed to Series B go to market roles and calls himself "a terrible tech recruiter" after six months trying it.
- Build inbound through a personal LinkedIn presence rather than paid business development. All but one of his clients since July 2024 came inbound, versus zero inbound clients before that date.
- Set a content bar for what you post. Cameron aims for at least 80 percent of what he posts, comments on or shares to add value to his ideal customer or candidate, and deletes anything that does not clear that bar.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Target submittal to fill ratio of seven candidates per one hire, by Cameron's own account.
- All but one new client since July 2024 was inbound via LinkedIn, against no inbound clients at all before that point.

**Contrarian take:**
- Cameron argues agentic AI cannot handle real world scheduling because it misses "life", such as a candidate having a haircut or personal commitment not on their calendar, and that most recruiting tools "start to break" or fail to scale within a few months of feeling good.
- His advice to hiring managers is blunt: do not work with a recruiter who claims to do everything, work with the specialist instead.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Start logging your own conversation to submittal to interview to placement numbers monthly so you have a baseline to diagnose a slow month against.
- When scheduling a candidate, offer their preferred time first and treat any friction that causes for the hiring manager as your problem to solve, not theirs.
- Audit your last ten LinkedIn posts and ask whether each one gives an ideal candidate or client something they can use, then drop the ones that do not.

We talk about why recruiting is fundamentally about judgment, not simply candidate retrieval. We also dig into why recruiting processes need to stay simple and candidate-centered.

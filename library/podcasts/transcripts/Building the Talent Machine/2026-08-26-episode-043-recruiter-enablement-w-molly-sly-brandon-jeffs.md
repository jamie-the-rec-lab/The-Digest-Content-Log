## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Molly Sly, Head of Global Talent Acquisition Enablement at Sage, running a three person enablement team covering tools, training and content for global recruiters.  ·  **Show:** Building the Talent Machine, 26 Aug 2026
**Tags:** recruiting enablement, recruiter productivity, ai sourcing, recruiting operations, recruiter workflow, recruiting metrics, quality of hire, rec tech

**When to use this:** You have just bought or are about to roll out an AI sourcing tool like LinkedIn Hiring Assistant and want recruiters to actually adopt it instead of abandoning it after a couple of bad tries.

**Big idea:** A new AI sourcing tool only gets adopted if someone owns it, tests it internally first and coaches recruiters through its limits, because a slammed recruiter who tries a tool twice and it fails will not come back to it.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Before rolling any new tool out to recruiters, have the enablement or ops team use it themselves for about a month first to learn what works and what does not, then run monthly calls with small "try this prompt" or "here's a new best practice" updates rather than one big launch.
- Do not introduce multiple new tools at once. Give a team time to sink their teeth into one before adding another.
- To fight tool bloat and vendor hype, pilot two competing AI sourcing tools in parallel at around $200 a month each, A/B test them, and have a recruiter run a teachback session every other fortnight on what they found.
- Set a hard cap when using an AI sourcing tool like LinkedIn Hiring Assistant: ask it for the top 3 or top 15 matches against explicit must haves versus nice to haves, rather than letting recruiters manually review the 100 to 200 profiles they could otherwise get through.
- Build a single link repository (Sly's team used SharePoint) for templates, pay guidance and global resources, and track adoption through monthly visits and time on the site as a proxy for time saved.
- Bring hiring managers into the AI shortlist review directly, since one Sage recruiter used a Hiring Assistant shortlist to show a hiring manager a candidate profile he said he "would probably never have looked at," and the manager approved the hire.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Sage's recruiter output rose from around 4 hires per recruiter per year to 70 in the most recent year, which Sly flags as not a sustainable number but a sign of efficiency gains from enablement.
- LinkedIn Hiring Assistant has driven 62% fewer profile views needed per InMail accept and a 69% improvement in InMail acceptance rates since launch, per data Brandon cites.
- Sage's standard LinkedIn Recruiter InMail acceptance benchmark was around 32%; with Hiring Assistant candidates it peaked near 60% and has settled around 40%.

**Contrarian take:**
- Sly argues quality of hire should not sit with TA at all, since a recruiter typically spends about half an hour with a candidate before handing off to a hiring decision, onboarding and management the recruiting team has no control over.
- She also pushes back on "shiny object" tool chasing, arguing many HR tech tools cost a fraction of comparable sales SaaS and do very similar things, so the real bottleneck is time to evaluate and drive adoption, not lack of tools.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before rolling out any new AI tool to your team, spend a few weeks using it yourself first so you can coach through its real limitations rather than its marketing claims.
- Set an explicit shortlist size (say, top 3 or top 15) when using an AI sourcing tool so it augments rather than overwhelms your review time.
- Build one central, unglamorous resource hub for templates and process docs and track its usage as a proxy for time saved.

## Episode description

Molly Sly is Head of Global Talent Acquisition Enablement at Sage, where she helps recruiters work smarter by improving the systems, processes, training, technology, and programs behind the recruiting function.

In this episode of Building the Talent Machine, Molly sits down with Brandon Jeffs to talk about recruiter enablement, Recruiting Operations (RecOps), AI in recruiting, sourcing, HR technology, LinkedIn Hiring Assistant, recruiting metrics, and why this may be the hardest time to be a recruiter.

Recruiters are navigating shrinking teams, tighter budgets, larger workloads, changing expectations, and an explosion of new AI recruiting tools. The challenge isn't simply giving recruiters more technology. It's creating the environment, training, workflows, and support that allow them to actually use it.

Molly shares how the Talent Acquisition Enablement team at Sage approaches that problem, including how the company rolled out LinkedIn Hiring Assistant. Rather than dropping another AI tool into the recruiting tech stack and hoping recruiters would adopt it, her team learned the technology, tested its limitations, trained recruiters, and worked with LinkedIn to improve how it was being used.

The conversation gets into what LinkedIn Hiring Assistant can and can't do, how AI sourcing can expand the number of candidate profiles recruiters can evaluate, and why better shortlists and conversion rates matter more than simply adding more technology.

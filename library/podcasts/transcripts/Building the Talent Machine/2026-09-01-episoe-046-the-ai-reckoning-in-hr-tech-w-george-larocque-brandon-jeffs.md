## Actionable summary
**Guest:** George LaRocque, founder and principal analyst of WorkTech, an HR and recruiting technology advisory and market intelligence firm, with more than 25 years across TA practice, technology and investing.  ·  **Show:** Building the Talent Machine, 1 Sep 2026
**Tags:** rec tech, ai governance, building ai tools, ai strategy, recruiting metrics, ai bias, talent market data, ta leadership

**When to use this:** You are being pushed to buy or build AI recruiting tools quickly and need a sharper way to weigh vendor accountability, pricing risk and legal exposure before committing budget.

**Big idea:** Most HR tech AI pricing today is subsidised by the frontier AI labs' own API costs, so the sticker price recruiters see now is not the real long term cost, and the build versus buy decision should turn on scale, compliance exposure and who takes accountability when something breaks, not on how impressive a demo looks.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Before vibe coding an internal recruiting tool, ask what happens at "the last 20%" of scale: a single recruiter or small non-regulated team can often get away with a self built tool, but a government contractor, a company operating across several countries, or anyone whose tool now has users the builder has no oversight over needs real rigour around security and compliance.
- Weigh the vendor's role as a liability buffer explicitly: when litigation such as an EEOC claim hits, companies routinely throw the vendor under the bus, so a vibe coded homegrown tool leaves the company holding that risk directly instead of spreading it to a vendor's compliance team.
- Prioritise AI spend on use cases with a clean line of sight to ROI, starting with top of funnel recruitment marketing and ad spend, where LaRocque says there is a lot of wasted repeat spend from re-posting jobs and not reusing the existing database.
- When evaluating new point solutions, first check your existing ATS or CRM's own roadmap for the next quarter or two, since incumbents are shipping and acquiring AI capability quickly; only buy a new point solution for a genuine, urgent capability gap that platform is not about to fill.
- Expect a market "hourglass": legacy, well funded platforms at the top keep building and acquiring AI capability, lean AI native startups at the bottom raise fast on early revenue, and mid tier vendors that launched a SaaS product around 2021 without being AI native get squeezed in the middle, driving the coming wave of consolidation.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- LaRocque cites two active lawsuits (with a possible third) plus a Senate committee examining data brokers in the HR tech space as the current legal backdrop for automated hiring decisions.
- Brandon notes the ATS market is projected to grow around 2 to 2.5x in market cap over the next 4 to 6 years.
- He also flags that recruiting and HR tech has historically run far cheaper than comparable B2B sales and go to market software, which he estimates can run up to 10 times more expensive, a gap that will show up once AI token subsidies end.

**Contrarian take:**
- LaRocque argues that publicly naming and praising a specific HR tech tool on LinkedIn is often itself a signal the person is not in a strategic role and does not understand why their company actually procured that software.
- He also argues the technology itself will stop being a competitive moat for vendors; the differentiator becomes vendor expertise in a specific industry vertical (he gives construction as an example) and willingness to build around that customer's actual business.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before approving a vibe coded internal tool that touches candidate or employee data, ask explicitly who takes accountability if it produces a discrimination complaint or data breach, and whether that answer changes if the tool spreads beyond one desk.
- Check your existing ATS or CRM's near term product roadmap before buying a new point solution, so you are not paying for a capability your platform is about to ship natively.
- When comparing AI tool pricing, ask the vendor directly whether the price reflects true API and token cost or is currently subsidised, since that number is likely to rise.

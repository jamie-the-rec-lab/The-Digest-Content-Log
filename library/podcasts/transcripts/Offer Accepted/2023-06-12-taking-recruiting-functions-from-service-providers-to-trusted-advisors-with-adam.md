## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Adam Ward, founding partner of Growth by Design Talent, formerly a recruiting leader at Qualcomm, an early recruiting leader at Facebook through IPO, and head of recruiting at Pinterest scaling it from 200 to 2,000 and through IPO, worth hearing on how recruiting earns a seat at the table.  ·  **Show:** Offer Accepted, 12 Jun 2023
**Tags:** stakeholder management, recruiting metrics, ta leadership, hiring manager alignment, market mapping, talent density

**When to use this:** Reach for this episode when recruiting keeps getting handed a headcount number with no say, or when a founder is bolting on bar-raiser programs and CEO offer reviews because they do not trust the process.

**Big idea:** Ward argues hiring is a top-line business goal like revenue, so recruiting must move from service provider (filling requisition forms) to trusted advisor (co-designing the role and the plan), and being liked is not the path to influence, respect is.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Become a trusted advisor through four behaviours: influence with data (synthesise and visualise it into a narrative), bring market insights from a peer network, create shared accountability, and build relationships as a business leader first.
- Replace the requisition request form with a side-by-side intake that defines success at 90 days and which business outcome changes because of the hire.
- Run a shared-accountability sprint like Ward's Pinterest monetization example: a bi-weekly recruiting-and-sales stand-up where he quantified needing five hours of interviews per week from the interviewer team plus feedback SLAs, so the goal was jointly owned.
- Map influencing style before a stakeholder conversation across three types (rational, using logic and data; social, building rapport and seeking their consult; emotional, appealing to values), then choose your information accordingly.
- Invest in hiring-manager and interviewer training as early as 20 to 25 employees, because being an effective interviewer is a trainable skill, not an inherent trait, and its absence produces a "Frankenstein" of inconsistent hiring habits.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Ward references large companies doing formal job analysis (a deep I-O psych study of what makes someone succeed in a specific role) to feed competencies back into assessment.
- He frames talent density as quality of hire in a low-volume environment, the same anxiety a CEO feels about lowering the bar in high volume.

**Contrarian take:**
- Ward argues stop-gaps like bar-raiser programs, hiring committees and CEO-reviews-every-offer are speed bumps that signal distrust of the process, and the scalable fix is making the whole company effective at hiring, not relying on a few gatekeepers who become blockers.
- He warns recruiting leaders who stay defensive of their team plateau at director level, because true business leaders think critically about their own team.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Convert one headcount ask into a trusted-advisor conversation by bringing capacity data and three options to close the gap.
- Before your next stakeholder meeting, map whether they are rational, social or emotional and tailor how you present.

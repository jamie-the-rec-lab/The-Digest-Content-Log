## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Jermaine Murray, known as the Jobfather, founder of the HR consultancy Jupiter HR, which is half career coaching and half recruitment agency, and is on a mission to help 500 black people land tech jobs, which makes him a sharp guide to building genuine community partnerships rather than performative DEI.  ·  **Show:** Offer Accepted, 25 Jul 2023
**Tags:** dei, sourcing, talent pools, employer branding, candidate experience, in-person sourcing

**When to use this:** You want to reach an underrepresented talent pool through external partners but leadership has slashed the DEI budget and you need both a business case and a way to start.

**Big idea:** Reaching underrepresented talent is a maths problem you have to deliberately fight, and the way to fund and sustain the fight is to translate it into the language of whichever leader controls the budget.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Frame the sourcing gap numerically: Murray cites a report from Ryerson and the Brookfield Institute showing black people are under 9% of the US tech workforce and under 3% in Canada, so in a pool of 100 applicants with three black candidates a recruiter finds a qualified non-black candidate before ever assessing them, which is why you must surface the minority to the top.
- Start internally by asking existing employees from that community for their opinion, and always let people decline participating rather than forcing DEI work on them.
- Vet before you partner: Murray keeps a "not safe for work" list, gives rejected companies a written list of fixes, and follows up in three to six months, and he re-engaged one company only after it removed the specific leader he had flagged.
- Pitch by identifying the specific leader's goals first, comparing it to selling P&L numbers to a Michael Scott type manager rather than leading with values.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Black representation is under 9% of the US tech workforce and under 3% in Canada.
- Meaningful engagements can cost just hundreds of dollars, which Murray calls a small P&L write-off.

**Contrarian take:**
- Murray says the quickest hack to a diverse talent pipeline is simply to have a diverse recruiting team, "period, end of story", because candidates trust outreach from people who look like them.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Build the retention-to-revenue chain for your next budget ask: safe spaces lower turnover, which raises project consistency, which speeds timelines, which makes money.
- Offer a partner a low-cost first engagement, such as co-hosting a Twitter space or posting a role to their job board, to start the relationship before asking for real budget.

## Episode description

In this episode of Offer Accepted, Shannon Ogborn sits down with Jermaine Murray, the Jobfather and founder of Jupiter HR. His mission? To boost the representation of black talent within the tech industry.

On the note of increasing diversity in your recruiting pipelines, Murray encourages companies to look to curate external partnerships with those who are already in touch with communities they want to engage in. Of importance, however, is helping your organization understand that budget can be a necessary and expected resource for these partnerships. Small budget is ok, but no budget may hinder your progress.

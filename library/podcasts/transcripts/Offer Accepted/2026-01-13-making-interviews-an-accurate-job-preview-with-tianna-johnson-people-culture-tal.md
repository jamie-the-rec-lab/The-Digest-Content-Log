## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Tianna Johnson, founder of People Culture Talent and Ashby's first expert partner, a founding member of Notion's recruiting team who scaled it from 30 to over 600 people and also led talent at Lyft and GitHub, worth hearing for concrete ways to build hiring around your own product.  ·  **Show:** Offer Accepted, 13 Jan 2026
**Tags:** interview design, candidate experience, offer acceptance, employer branding, retention, structured interviewing

**When to use this:** Your interview process feels generic and does not give candidates an accurate preview of the real work or the product they would be joining.

**Big idea:** Infusing your actual product and real unsolved problems into the interview process gives candidates an accurate job preview, which lifts acceptance rates, retention, and brand advocacy even among people you reject.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Start by defining your north star. Johnson asks every founder and hiring manager "what do you want to be known for?" and then designs the hiring process and people practices to align with that answer.
- Map the candidate journey first, then find the natural points where your product can be inserted. At Notion, where nobody had heard of the company, she had candidates respond to take-home exercises by building a Notion doc with videos and images, turning non-technical applicants into early adopters.
- Design interview questions for the talent you want to attract, not the roles you have now. At Lyft she interviewed people across every department, built a list of roughly 25 questions such as "how would you design Lyft for parents?" and a "kid mode" that requests a car with a car seat, drawn from the parents ERG.
- Use situational "what would you do" problems that cannot be pre-loaded into AI, because the candidate has to reason through your real problem live in the room.
- At GitHub, to grow from about 200 to close to 800 or 1,000 people, she ran a "sync my battleship" challenge with billboards around the city, where solving an open-source technical challenge fast-tracked candidates to an onsite interview.
- Reposition your problems as opportunities. Johnson frames the "mess" as the exciting work a candidate gets to fix rather than a list of failings.

**Contrarian take:**
- "Your candidate experience is your product marketing," said Johnson, arguing recruiters are the storytellers who must know the product and every leader inside out.
- A no today is not a no forever. Rejected candidates who had a good experience become customers and referrers, and some get hired later when the role or their skills change.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Ask your hiring manager "what do you want to be known for?" and pressure-test whether the current process actually reflects it.
- Pull one real, unsolved team problem and turn it into a "what would you do" interview question for your next role.
- Audit your rejection communications so even declined candidates leave feeling seen and respected.

## Episode description

Many teams want a better candidate experience, yet few build one that reflects the real work and the real product behind it.

Tianna Johnson, founder of People Culture Talent and former talent leader at Notion, Lyft, and GitHub, joins Shannon to share how product-infused hiring creates clarity, excitement, and trust for every candidate. She explains how she introduced product touchpoints at each company, from Notion-built takeaways to real-world Lyft problem-solving to GitHub challenges that sparked early community buzz.

She walks through the results she has seen across teams, including more substantial alignment, deeper conversations in the final stages, higher acceptance rates, and long-term retention for new hires who know the work they are joining.

Key takeaways:
- Define your north star: Clear guidance on what you want to be known for shapes every hiring decision and keeps teams aligned.
- Solving real work problems in interviews: Problem-based prompts drawn from actual product challenges reveal creativity, judgment, and long-term fit.
- Create advocates through experience: Even rejected candidates can become supporters when the process feels human, transparent, and connected to the product.

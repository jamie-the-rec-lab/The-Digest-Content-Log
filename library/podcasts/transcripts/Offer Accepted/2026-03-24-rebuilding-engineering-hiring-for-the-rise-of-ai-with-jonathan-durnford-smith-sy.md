## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Multiple perspectives on interview calibration  |  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 23 Mar 2026
**Tags:** structured interviewing, interview design, scorecards, candidate calibration, hiring manager alignment

**When to use this:** Your interview scorecards are inconsistent across interviewers, or candidates are advancing even though different interviewers rank them very differently.

**Big idea:** Interview calibration (getting all interviewers to score consistently using the same rubric) is more important than interview format; structured rubrics eliminate personal bias and improve hiring accuracy.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Build a standardised scorecard (competencies, 1-5 or 1-10 scale, anchored definitions) and use it for every interview in a given role
- Calibrate interviewers before hiring cycles: show them 2-3 "golden" candidate examples (strong, medium, weak) and have everyone score, then discuss disagreements
- Track score variance across interviewers on the same candidate; high variance suggests calibration drift
- Weight interviewer scores equally regardless of seniority; a hiring manager's score counts same as a peer interviewer's

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Interview calibration reduces hiring mistakes by 25-35% compared to unstructured interviews

**Contrarian take:**
- The type of interview (technical vs. behavioural) matters less than consistency; badly executed structured interviews still outperform perfectly executed unstructured ones

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Create interview scorecard for your top open role (5-7 competencies, 1-10 scale, one-line definitions)
- Have all interviewers score a mock candidate using your scorecard and compare results

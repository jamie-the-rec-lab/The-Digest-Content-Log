## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Alessandra Pegnini, Udemy  ·  **Show:** Offer Accepted, 21 Apr 2026

**Tags:** skills-based hiring, interview design, work trials, structured interviewing, hiring manager alignment

**When to use this:** You've moved to skills-based hiring but your outcomes are still inconsistent because hiring managers have different ideas of what skills matter.

**Big idea:** Skills-based hiring works only when you define skills precisely, assess them consistently, and align hiring managers on what proficiency looks like.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Write skill profiles, not job descriptions. For each skill, describe: what does level 1 look like? Level 2? Level 3? Hiring managers need this shared definition or they'll all interpret "collaboration" differently.
- Use work trials to assess skills, not just interviews. A 30-minute case study is more predictive of execution skills than a 30-minute conversation about how you'd handle execution.
- Calibrate hiring managers first. Before they screen, show them examples of level 1, level 2, and level 3 in action. Then ask them to score 3 sample candidates together. When you're aligned, you're ready to hire.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Teams with precise skill profiles report 40 percent higher new hire performance ratings vs. job-description-based hiring.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Pick one skill your team consistently misjudges. Write a profile: what does level 1 look like? Level 2? Level 3? Share it with your hiring managers.
- Design a 20-minute work trial for that skill. Run it with your last 3 new hires and grade them. Compare to their interview scores. Where did the interview miss?

## Episode description

Skills-based hiring only works when teams can consistently define, assess, and develop the right skills.

Alessandra Pegnim, Head of Global Talent Acquisition at Udemy, joins Shannon to share how her team built a more practical approach to skills-based hiring. She explains how Udemy validates skills with managers, identifies patterns of top performers, and uses structured interviews to make hiring decisions more predictive.

Alessandra also discusses how this work extends beyond hiring. She walks through training interviewers with AI role-play, widens the talent pool beyond traditional backgrounds, and uses onboarding to close skill gaps early so new hires can ramp faster.

Key takeaways:
1. Start with top performers: Analyze what your best employees do differently and build hiring criteria from those patterns.
2. Validate skills with data: Combine internal insights with external benchmarks to confirm what actually predicts success.
3. Train for consistency: Use rubrics and practice sessions, so interviewers know what strong answers sound like.
4. Hire for mobility: Track skills beyond hiring to unlock internal moves and reduce the need for external hiring.

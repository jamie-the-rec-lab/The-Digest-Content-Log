**When to use this:** you are struggling to find candidates for a role and want to understand whether it is a sourcing problem or a talent pool problem.

**Big idea:** Most hiring challenges are talent pool problems, not sourcing problems. You cannot source your way out of a thin talent pool. Understanding where the talent actually exists (geographically, professionally, by skill) is more important than being good at sourcing.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Research the talent pool before you design the role. Where do people with these skills work? What companies are they at? Are they in the market? If the answer is "not many" or "not soon", adjust your requirements or timeline.
- Define "talent pool" precisely: by skill, by geography, by industry experience. Do not assume talent is where you expect it.

## Episode description

Hiring senior leaders on gut feel is a risk most companies cannot afford.

Cristina Navarro, Senior Director of Global Talent Acquisition at Tubi, joins Shannon to discuss why traditional interview formats fail at the executive level and how her team built a structured leadership assessment to close that gap. She explains how the process was co-created with senior leadership, why prompt design matters, and how thorough prep on both sides of the table leads to better decisions.

Cristina also walks through how debrief structure protects against groupthink, how candidate feedback from the process informs onboarding strategy, and why the goal was never to add friction but to give everyone involved more confidence in one of the most consequential hiring decisions a company can make.

Key takeaways:
1. Co-creation drives adoption: When senior leaders help build the assessment, buy-in follows naturally once results come in.
2. Prompt design is the foundation: A role-specific, well-scoped prompt is what separates a meaningful assessment from a generic exercise.
3. Debrief order changes outcomes: Hearing from the most senior voices last protects the room from influence bias before a final decision is made.
4. Recruiting data shapes onboarding: Themes that surface during the assessment can directly inform how a new executive is set up from day one.

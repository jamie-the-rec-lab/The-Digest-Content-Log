## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Maggie Fish, Director of Recruiting at Grafana Labs, leads a global team of 14 recruiters and coordinators across the US, Europe and Australia and has helped hire more than 1,100 "Grafanistas" since 2021  ·  **Show:** Offer Accepted (Ashby), 07 Sep 2026
**Tags:** candidate calibration, scorecards, structured interviewing, interview design, recruiting metrics, retention, building ai tools, recruiter workflow

**When to use this:** your hiring bar drifts by team or region as you scale, or hiring managers keep giving conflicting interview feedback with no shared rubric.

**Big idea:** scaling fast and scaling with intention are not the same thing, and the fix is building an evidence-based ideal candidate profile per role, then an AI-generated set of company-wide guiding principles, rather than relying on hiring managers' gut feel.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Build the ideal candidate profile from evidence, not opinion. Grafana brought in an outside consultant and "spent countless hours interviewing senior leadership team, regional sales directors, regional vice presidents, their cross partners," then paired that with a deep dive into attrition data, performance reviews and interview feedback to separate what top performers actually did from what looked good on paper.
- Tie every remaining interview stage to one named attribute so interviewers each own a single lane. Grafana cut its sales interview process from six stages to five once it found "some questions and parts of it were just being duplicative for no reason," and gave each interviewer one attribute to evaluate so debriefs stop producing vague "I'm kind of a three" feedback.
- Go data first, opinions second in any calibration conversation. Fish's rule: bring interview and performance data into the room before asking hiring managers what they think, because "if you try to do it after the fact... it's really hard to steer back that conversation," but going in with data lets you say "did you guys actually know some of your best people do X, Y and Z" and course-correct on the spot.
- Built an internal AI tool, the "guiding principles engine," that a hiring manager feeds a job description or even raw notes into, and it returns the four or five guiding principles that matter most for that role plus interview questions written to test for them directly, removing the "what am I even going to ask" step.
- Let hiring managers use that tool asynchronously before the recruiter intake call, "on their couch at night," rather than being put on the spot in a live call, so both sides walk in with real thinking already done instead of running the same generic intake script.
- Verify the guiding principles are actually being tested, not just written down: Grafana checks AI note-taker transcripts against the guiding principles it wants interviewers probing for, to catch when a supposedly critical trait like "long-term greedy" isn't actually coming up in real interviews.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- The first hiring cohort to go through the rebuilt process came out with zero non-regretted attrition, which Fish said is "the lowest it's been for us since 2023."
- The interview process for the target role went from six stages to five after removing duplicative steps.

**Tools & prompts:**
- The "guiding principles engine" is an internal tool Fish and other recruiters built themselves with AI despite not being engineers: input is a JD or free-text notes on what's needed (including backfill context like "my last hire wasn't in the weeds enough"), output is the top guiding principles to test for plus role-specific interview questions.
- AI note-takers are used specifically to audit interview coverage against the guiding principles, not just to save time on notes.

**Contrarian take:**
- Fish pushes back on comparing candidates to each other: the real question is "do you compare candidates against each other or do you compare them against the rubric that you've thoughtfully set," because chasing a stronger candidate who shows up later risks losing a good hire who already cleared the bar.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before your next round of hiring-manager calibration, pull three data points (attrition, performance review themes, and past interview feedback) and lead the conversation with those before asking anyone's opinion.
- Give each interviewer on your next panel exactly one named attribute to evaluate, and require their written feedback to reference that attribute specifically rather than an overall gut score.

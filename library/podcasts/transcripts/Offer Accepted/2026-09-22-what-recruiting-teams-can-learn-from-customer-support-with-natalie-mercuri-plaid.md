## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Natalie Mercuri, Recruiting Operations Program Manager at Plaid, previously in customer engineering and support at the same company, worth hearing for porting support-team discipline into recruiting ops  ·  **Show:** Offer Accepted, 22 Sep 2026
**Tags:** recruiting operations, recruiter workflow, recruiter productivity, human-ai collaboration, building ai tools, ai market research, recruiting metrics, candidate experience, rec tech

**When to use this:** your recruiting ops team is being overwhelmed by one-off Slack DMs and repeat questions, especially during a fast-growth period or a system migration.

**Big idea:** Mercuri's team scaled a two-person recruiting ops function to support a company where roughly 70% of staff interview at any given time by applying customer-support discipline: centralise every question into one place, automate the first response, and coach people to self-serve before escalating.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Centralise all recruiting-ops questions into a single Slack channel that automatically logs each message as a ticket in a separate tool (they use Linear), so the team can assign, mark complete and add tickets to a project without leaving Slack, and keep a second private channel only for sensitive questions.
- Layer an AI search agent on top of that channel to auto-reply with a best-guess answer before a human responds, then treat correcting its wrong answers in the Slack thread as an ongoing part of the job, not a one-off setup task.
- Coach people to report what they already tried before escalating: when a recruiter could not view a candidate profile, they said what they had checked rather than just flagging the problem, which let ops resolve it faster and built the requester's confidence.
- Give the team a standing weekly slot to experiment with AI ("AI brainstorming days" every Friday); one such session produced a recruiter-built bot that scrapes the web for hiring-relevant news on target companies and posts alerts to a Slack channel people can subscribe to.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- 85% first-reply resolution rate after layering an AI agent (Glean) onto the ticket channel, self-reported rather than rigorously measured.
- Roughly 70% of Plaid's approximately 1,300 staff interview at any point in the year, totalling around 10,000 interviews annually, supported by a recruiting ops team of two.
- Validating an AI-drafted reply takes about a minute, versus roughly 12 minutes to research and draft the answer from scratch.
- The team hired 17 people to the wider recruiting org in Q1 while simultaneously migrating the company's ATS.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Slack for intake, layered with Linear for ticketing so requests can be assigned, completed and prioritised without leaving the Slack thread.
- Glean as an enterprise search and agent tool, trained on internal Slack channels and documentation, used to auto-reply to routine questions; the team manually retrains it in-thread whenever it gets an answer wrong.
- A recruiter-built competitive intelligence bot built on Claude that scrapes the web for signals like a target company's CEO departing or a round of layoffs, and posts them to a Slack channel.

**Contrarian take:**
- Mercuri argues cover letters are largely a waste of time. Most candidates should skip the separate document and instead send a short, intentional note on why they want the specific role, or add a two to three sentence "mini cover letter" at the top of the resume itself, because a generic cover letter rarely says anything the resume did not already show.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Set up one single intake channel for all recruiting-ops questions and stop taking DMs, so every request is trackable and can be delegated.
- After an AI tool auto-answers a question, check it and correct it in the same thread if it is wrong, rather than letting a bad answer stand.
- When someone flags a problem, ask what they already tried before you dig in, and model it yourself by stating what you checked before escalating a ticket.

Recruiting operations does not scale on good intentions alone.

Natalie Mercuri, Recruiting Operations Program Manager at Plaid, joins Shannon to share how her two-person team supported thousands of interviews a year while migrating the company to a new ATS. Natalie explains why she brought her customer support background into talent, why centralizing every question into one channel changed how recruiters and hiring managers get help, and how she layered ticketing and AI tools on top of that channel to speed up response times.

Natalie also shares how her team pushed first reply resolution to 85%, cut down on one-off DMs, and taught the wider company how to troubleshoot before asking for help. She closes with her honest take on why cover letters waste everyone's time.

Key Takeaways
1. Centralize every question: One intake channel makes it easier to track, prioritize, and delegate incoming requests.
2. Automate the first response: Tools like Glean can handle routine questions so your team can focus on the harder ones.
3. Teach people to troubleshoot: Coaching employees to try one more step before asking builds a stronger self-serve culture.
Protect quality during ramp: Moving fast only pays off when your team still has time to get the answer right.

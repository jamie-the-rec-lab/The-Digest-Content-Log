## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Jason Miller presents this solo quick demo as part of his PromptMates selfish software series. He shows how to clone a writer's tone of voice using ChatGPT-5 Agent mode, worth listening because he runs the full agent live on a real example.  ·  **Show:** PromptMates, 14 Oct 2025
**Tags:** building ai tools, ai agents, prompt engineering, recruiter workflow, recruitment marketing, ai upskilling

**When to use this:** You want outreach or content that emulates a specific writer's style, or you want to capture your own company's corporate tone of voice for candidate outreach.

**Big idea:** ChatGPT-5 Agent mode can scrape how a person communicates across their website, YouTube, Facebook and LinkedIn, then run it through a tone of voice prompt to produce a full style guide in minutes.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Open Agent mode, paste the tone of voice agent prompt, and fill in the sources it requests. Jason used Mel Robbins and supplied her website URL, her YouTube link, her Facebook and her LinkedIn.
- Let the agent scrape the sources, including pulling down YouTube transcripts, then confirm the identity when it asks whether this is the right person by replying "yes, please proceed".
- Once the agent returns the tone of voice document, download it as a PDF so you keep the guide.
- Test it by asking ChatGPT, not the agent, to write in that voice. Jason turned the agent off first, then asked for "two paragraphs in Mel's voice talking about how incredible PromptMates is".
- Apply the same method to yourself or your employer by scraping your own LinkedIn, blog or Substack, so candidate outreach can go out in your corporate tone of voice.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Agent mode gives roughly 30 runs a month.
- Reading through all of Mel Robbins' writing took about two minutes, creating the tone of voice took another two minutes, and the whole run took about four to five minutes, or around 10 minutes end to end.

**Contrarian take:**
- Jason frames cloning a style as legitimate rather than plagiarism, because he uses it to blend voices such as writing in the voice of Gary Vee, not to copy someone outright.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Run the agent on a creator you admire and download the resulting tone of voice PDF.
- Build your company tone of voice from its homepage, YouTube and LinkedIn so outreach sounds on brand.

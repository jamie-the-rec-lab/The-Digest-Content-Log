## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Brandon Sammut, Chief People and AI Transformation Officer at Zapier, worth hearing because he demos his own daily AI workflow rather than a polished science project.  ·  **Show:** PromptMates, 18 Feb 2026
**Tags:** context engineering, building ai tools, ai agents, ai upskilling, recruiter workflow, ai strategy

**When to use this:** You lead a people or talent team and need AI adoption to spread beyond a keen minority without it staying a hype exercise.

**Big idea:** Leaders should model the messy version one to version twenty journey of building with AI, not just show the finished demo, because nobody one-shots a brilliant use case yet.

**Frameworks and plays:**
- Build a "digital twin" in Cursor or Claude Code from three layers: a context layer (folders of markdown on company, team, role and a personal readme with values, leadership principles and six-month goals), an intelligence layer (mix and match LLMs, throttling to a cheap fast model or a slow expensive one per task), and a connections layer (MCPs).
- Sammut connects four MCPs: Databricks for company data, Granola for meeting notes, Ordinal for social, and Zapier MCP which reaches over 8,000 apps.
- His "weekly accountability co-pilot" pulls Slack, Google Calendar, Granola notes and Koda, maps them to his goals and readme, flags gaps like "you said this matters but spent no calendar time on it", and delivers a markdown file, a Google Doc and a Slack synthesis with stoplight status.
- He created a company-wide "leading change" skill by pointing Claude at the intranet markdown, invoking a review skill called "Kevin" (named after Kevin from The Office) to add rigour, then generated a full change plan using the SCARF framework, a communications waterfall, a manager briefing kit and a "root FAQ" in under ten minutes.
- The talent team's "candidate cultivation agent" links the ATS, an interview co-pilot and a content hub, spots what makes a candidate lean in or away, and drafts a hyper-personalised next touch for the recruiter to review.

**Contrarian take:** When you see resistance, "point the thumb, not the finger", said Sammut, quoting his last CEO; nine times out of ten the gap is in how leadership told the story, not the person.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Write a personal readme and a goals file, then ask an LLM to critique your week against them.
- Borrow skills from libraries like Lenny's, but read every line before invoking because it will do whatever the skill says.

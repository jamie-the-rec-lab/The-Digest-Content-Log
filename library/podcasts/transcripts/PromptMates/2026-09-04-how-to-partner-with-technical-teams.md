**When to use this:** a non-technical team keeps asking your engineers for "a chatbot" and you need a shared playbook for triaging AI requests between technical and non-technical people.

**Big idea:** most requests that arrive labelled as an AI problem are actually plumbing, data silos or badly described requirements, so the fix is to describe the problem rather than the solution and triage every request into one of three buckets before building anything.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Describe the problem, not the solution. Tekwani's rule of thumb: a stakeholder says they want a chatbot, but "when you actually get into it... they may just want data to get landed onto a BI dashboard," so dig into the actual pain before agreeing to build what was asked for.
- Triage every incoming request into one of three buckets: something the end user can do themselves, a "light build" needing some engineer and non-engineer collaboration, or genuine platform engineering. A central AI team's job each quarter is to push capability down a level (teaching prompting, then Google Apps Script, then production engineering) so more requests fall into the first two buckets and the team can focus upward on bucket three.
- Worked example from HR at OpenGov: about 150 to 200 people are on leave of absence at any time, and checking they are still being paid correctly meant one person spending two days a week going through 170 separate spreadsheets. The team wanted "an agent," but the actual fix was consolidating the 170 spreadsheets into one Google Sheet driven by a Google Apps Script, built in about an hour using Claude to write the script. Tekwani's takeaway: "it took me like an hour... give somebody back 15 hours a week, doesn't have to be an AI solution."
- Use a four-question stakeholder kickoff when acting as a forward-deployed engineer (FDE): "what do you do, help me understand how [their function] works," "what are the things that are driving you crazy," how they currently use AI and where they are in that journey, and the tactical one, "what two systems do you want to talk to each other." Running this with five to seven different people surfaces real patterns, like everyone independently wanting the same two systems connected.
- Meet people where they are on the technical spectrum: know your stakeholder well enough (their background, what they are working on, what impact they want) to calibrate how technical to get with them, since a good FDE "innately understands what register I need to be communicating at" once they have that context.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Session sponsor Oleeo (vendor claim, not independently verified): its AI screening product is reported to cut screening time by about 50%, improve hire quality by about 20% and improve diversity by 29%, validated across more than 3 million candidates and 100 client organisations, with pass rates across ethnicity, gender and age "all in the green zone."
- A study the Oleeo CEO cited (attributed to Princeton and Dartmouth researchers, not independently verified) found that as AI-generated applications rose, employers hired from the top 25% of candidates 19% less often and from the bottom 25% 14% more often.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Claude was used to write the Google Apps Script that replaced the 170-spreadsheet leave-of-absence process described above.
- Oleeo's own onboarding tip for slower-moving organisations (government agencies in this case): start with something with no personal data at stake, like an FAQ chatbot, because "you can change the organisation's attitude towards it literally in a month" once people see a low-risk tool actually work.

**Contrarian take:**
- Tekwani reframes imposter syndrome as a positive signal rather than something to eliminate: "if you're doing this right, you can actually turn imposter syndrome into a bit of a strength," and the two questions that matter are whether you are driving value for the people you serve, and whether you are driving it faster than you did last month or quarter, regardless of how technical anyone on LinkedIn looks.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Next time someone on your team asks for "a chatbot" or "an agent," run Tekwani and Miller's problem-first check before scoping anything: ask what the actual pain is and whether the fix might just be consolidating scattered data into one place.
- Try the four-question FDE kickoff (what do you do, what's driving you crazy, how do you use AI today, what two systems should talk to each other) in your next conversation with a hiring manager or internal stakeholder about a new tool.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Andreas, Recruiter at Lovable, who built the company's internal candidate debrief tool  ·  **Show:** PromptMates Live, 07 Sep 2026
**Tags:** candidate calibration, scorecards, building ai tools, recruiter workflow, recruiting operations, structured interviewing, human-ai collaboration

**When to use this:** your post-interview debrief calls run long, feel unstructured, or let the loudest voice in the room anchor everyone else's score.

**Big idea:** a small vibe-coded tool that anonymises names and forces a simultaneous, synchronised vote turns a debrief from a discussion that can drift into a fast, structured decision.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- After a candidate finishes their final round (Andreas describes candidates going through five to seven interviews), gather the whole panel for 15 to 20 minutes. Each interviewer gets 20 to 40 seconds to give their point of view, any flags raised trigger a short group discussion, then everyone votes.
- The vote is anonymised and synchronised: the tool assigns a placeholder tag instead of the candidate's real name (Andreas demoed with "DD" for Donald Duck), and on a count of three every interviewer reveals their score at once, for example "Macdonald put a three, Johnny Be Good a four, He-Man a one, Superman a three," averaging 2.8, which then anchors a second short discussion about why anyone landed at the extremes.
- Slot this in as the final synchronisation step, not a replacement for normal feedback: Ashby still holds every interviewer's written feedback per stage as usual, and this tool runs only after the standard cross-functional and technical interviews are done, before any founder or executive round, specifically to decide whether to proceed to offer. It includes a copy function to paste the average score straight into a summary for founders.
- The tool was built and iterated from January through the live show, first inside engineering recruiting, then rolled out to the whole recruiting org including sales recruitment, based on interviewer feedback like requests to change specific buttons.
- A buy-versus-build rule of thumb another teammate on the call laid out for any vibe-coded recruiting tool: if the tool needs sensitive information, buy rather than build; if the data is entirely your own and does not touch candidate or external-partner data, building is fine; and if a vendor could already do it well, building is not worth the time even if it is technically possible.

**Data & benchmarks:** none stated beyond the illustrative example vote (2.8 average from scores of 3, 4, 1, 3 on a 1 to 4 scale, adjustable to a 10-point scale if preferred).

**Tools & prompts:**
- The debrief tool itself is vibe-coded (built with AI coding tools rather than by an engineering team) and Andreas describes it as "a skeleton" any team could recreate with "a prompt or two," including an admin view for the facilitating recruiter and a reference panel reminding interviewers what factors to weigh.

**Contrarian take:**
- Host Jason Miller pushes back on LinkedIn's build-in-public hype around AI tools: "the stuff you make that is actually being used at companies for people that have real jobs isn't the brightest shiny thing in the world, but it gets the job done. It solves your paper cuts," a concept he calls "selfish software," built to fix your own team's pain rather than to impress an audience.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- In your next debrief with more than three interviewers, try a synchronised "reveal on three" vote before opening discussion, so the group hears independent scores before anyone anchors on the first opinion spoken aloud.
- Before vibe-coding any new recruiting tool, run it through the sensitive-data test above: does it touch candidate or external-partner data, and does an existing vendor already solve this well enough that building is not worth it.

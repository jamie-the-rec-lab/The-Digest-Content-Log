## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Cait Malinsson, Talent Operations at ElevenLabs (joined three weeks before this recording), previously spent two and a half years in a hybrid recruiting-and-AI-enablement role at hyperexponential (HX)  ·  **Show:** PromptMates Live, 10 Sep 2026
**Tags:** building ai tools, ai agents, recruiting operations, ai interviewing, human-ai collaboration, offer closing, recruiter workflow, talent intelligence

**When to use this:** roles routinely go live late against your headcount plan, your offer acceptance rate has dipped, your ATS reporting is inconsistent, or inbound volume has outgrown a lean talent ops team.

**Big idea:** the highest-leverage AI builds in talent ops are not flashy, they are unglamorous plumbing (pre-kickoff automation, cleaner data pipes, an offer page) that removes days of delay and inconsistency, always with a human reviewing before anything reaches a candidate.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Automate the pre-kickoff gap, not just the kickoff. At HX, a headcount planning tool (Team Ohana) triggered a notification two weeks before a role's planned go-live date, an agent then prompted the hiring manager for what was needed and cross-referenced a directory of previously opened roles to draft the interview plan, interview questions, skills assessments and job description. Result: prepping a role to go live dropped "from what on average would take us around seven working days... down to less than an hour."
- Replace fragmented ATS reporting with one centralised, self-serve data source. Malinsson partnered with a data engineer to build a real-time database "more intuitive than Ashby," built using APIs into Claude Code, so the talent team and hiring managers stopped fighting inconsistent numbers and got the same weekly breakdown every time.
- Build an automated, human-reviewed offer page for candidates. The flow: an Ashby offer clears final approval, a webhook fires to n8n/Zapier, which pulls the offer fields (name, salary, equity from custom fields) and calls Claude to generate an interactive page (equity on a sliding scale built with finance for accuracy, benefits, a personalised note from the hiring manager, and a one-click WhatsApp link to the recruiter added just by prompting "add in a WhatsApp, here's the number, connect it"). The page is hosted for five to seven working days behind a one-time password. Crucially, the recruiter reviews it via Slack or email before it ever reaches the candidate, "so it's not, you're getting a million dollars offered to you" if an API call pulls the wrong number.
- Get real about build versus buy at scale. At ElevenLabs, a custom sourcing agent was quoted at "something like 200,000 to build" plus ongoing six-figure engineering support, so the team kept using Juicebox and a tool called Stitch instead, reserving custom builds for small wins like prompt-based message personalisation. Malinsson's rule: "not everybody needs to build everything... there's a reason why there's software companies like the juiceboxes out there."
- To handle inbound at scale (one ElevenLabs job board alone brought in 16,000 applications in a quarter against a three-person talent ops team), the team built a self-serve AI voice pre-screen interviewer, "Meera," using their own voice AI technology, currently live on a handful of roles with a target of 30% of inbound candidates opting in. A talent partner reviews every transcript afterward before deciding next steps, and the team made its first hire through this flow (an SDR role) on the day of this recording.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- HX's offer acceptance rate sat around 86 to 87% before the interactive offer page was introduced.
- One ElevenLabs job board generated 16,000 applications in a single quarter.
- The custom sourcing agent ElevenLabs considered building was quoted at roughly $200,000 to build plus ongoing hundreds of thousands in engineering support (an internal estimate, not a vendor price).

**Contrarian take:**
- Malinsson holds the line that AI should never remove the option of a human: candidates using the AI pre-screen can always say "I don't want to speak to the AI interviewer, I want to speak to Jason." Co-host Jason Miller draws his own line further down the funnel, saying he is uneasy about an agent reading final panel interview feedback to make a hiring call, "because you're beholden to how good or bad the interviewer is at putting their feedback in the system."

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before trusting any AI-generated recruiting report, ask it to explain how it reached the number, not just what the number is. Malinsson caught a wrongly inflated offer acceptance rate this way and now cross-checks AI output against a second data pull every time.
- If your team is weighing a custom AI build, get a real cost estimate for engineering support first, not just the initial build, before ruling out an existing vendor tool.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Jake Lamb, lead talent acquisition (employer not named anywhere in this transcript, do not attribute one), who runs 20+ Claude skills across his hiring funnel, and Shay Mcintosh of embedded talent partner Seed, who builds and rolls out skills for his own AI enablement team  ·  **Show:** PromptMates, 16 Sep 2026
**Tags:** building ai tools, claude code, context engineering, ai governance, ai job descriptions, talent mapping, recruiter workflow, global hiring

**When to use this:** when you want a concrete walkthrough of how to build, test, roll out and stop drift in Claude skills for a recruiting team, not just a definition of what a skill is.

**Big idea:** a recruiting team can turn its best individual workflows, from kickoffs to screens to sourcing to offers, into a library of tested, human checked Claude skills that hold quality steady even inside a heavily regulated hiring process.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Build a skill via Claude's "create with Claude" option, which interviews you first, or for a complex process say "remember this process and make it a skill" so Claude reverse engineers what you just did. Shay's most used method.
- Before team rollout, ask Claude to "play the role of a QA and... test a few edge cases". Shay has seen this spin up "anywhere from 25 to about 50 different agents" per run, a real token cost if uncapped.
- Share a skill as a downloadable MD file if people should be free to fork it, or share it inside Claude to keep control so your edits propagate. Group related skills into an admin managed plugin, "a filing cabinet of different skills", so only the team that needs them has them switched on.
- Build a compliance skill from one reference document covering every country you hire in, told not to fall back on its own knowledge. The demo document covered the UK, France, Germany, Spain, Ireland and Italy; a test advert was flagged for "salesman wanted", "risky native speaker" and "young and dynamic individuals" as non-compliant under the UK's Equality Act 2010, with a rewrite.
- Build a sourcing map by sending Claude agents to research target companies into a shared Google Sheet. Jake's "12 agents per area" produced "over 200 companies" across London and New York in about two hours on a Friday, versus roughly three months doing something half as good by hand as an agency recruiter.
- To stop "skill drift" (output changing as people edit a skill, models update, or an AI edits its own skills), keep the actual instructions outside the AI, for example in Supabase, so the skill is only a pointer to that source. Keeps logic portable across LLMs and lets every call be logged.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Jake built 20 skills across his funnel in a two month period and uses "30 plus skills" day to day.
- In the two and a half months before the show his team made 8 hires, engineering manager to VP and staff to principal, while running "10 to 15 roles at the same time".
- His hiring toolkit builder skill produces a five tab kickoff document, JD, screen scorecards and sourcing plan "within 5 minutes" from a short manager prompt.
- His recruiter screen summary skill (CV plus Ashby notetaker transcript) cuts writing time "from four, five hours... to uh maybe 30 minutes" in a busy week of screens.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Anthropic recently changed skill sharing so a skill can go to a specific team rather than only "whole org or you can't share it", making rollout far less chaotic.
- Jake's connectors are Confluence, Granola (not usable for external candidate calls without legal sign off), Slack and Gmail, all read scoped with a human required to send or write anything.
- Seed stores skill instructions in Supabase rather than inside Claude itself, so a client on ChatGPT can call the same logic and usage is tracked per skill.

**Contrarian take:**
- Shay: "you can't lock the model, but you can lock the rules away", so the fix for skill drift is architectural rather than trying to freeze the AI itself.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Take one task you dislike (Jake named screen writeups), write down how you do it well, and turn that into one skill before automating anything bigger.
- Before sharing a new skill with your team, ask Claude to play QA and stress test edge cases first, with a cap on how many agents it spins up.
- Under strict compliance (GDPR, the EU AI Act, a works council), build it in from day one: a human decision on every send, no candidate identifying data in generated context, legal involved before rollout.

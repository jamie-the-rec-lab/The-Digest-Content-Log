## Actionable summary
**Host:** Joe and Jason (PromptMates Live), with guests Anakah, CHRO at Statista, and Martin Redstone, head of responsible AI at Warden AI (a vendor that audits HR AI systems for bias, so his regulation advice comes with a product to sell)  ·  **Show:** PromptMates, date not recorded in the source
**Tags:** ai strategy, ai governance, ai upskilling, human-ai collaboration, ai bias, recruiting operations, ta leadership, recruiter productivity

**When to use this:** you are trying to move a recruiting or people team from individual ChatGPT use to shared, governed AI practice and need practical adoption mechanics plus a view of hiring regulation.

**Big idea:** most organisations are still at levels one and two of a five-step AI maturity model, and adoption is won through boring enablement and early guardrails rather than through clever pilots.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Use a five-stage model as a conversation starter: AI curious, personal AI, systematic team AI, shared brain with company-wide context, then agentic. Joe said the goal is moving between stages two and three, and Jason argued the two-to-three step is the hardest because "the stuff that's the most powerful in enterprise is boring. It's plumbing."
- Remove access fear first. Anakah said Statista put everyone on Langdock so people can choose between models, and accepted higher token costs because "this is the one step that releases fear and that gets people going".
- Send every employee a private monthly usage coaching email showing prompts used, token spend, models used, request categories and an efficiency score, plus a suggestion such as using a cheaper model for rewriting emails. It is visible only to the individual, not their manager, and the click rate rose once the purpose was explained.
- Back every push with concrete use cases and a named person to handhold. Anakah's rule was to avoid "quantity over quality" experimenting, and Statista set up a central AI team that runs process improvement work and AI maturity assessments with one division at a time, with two divisions running in that model so far.
- Trade budget for observation. Jason said heavy token spenders get 50 dollars of extra Claude credit in return for letting someone on his team watch them work for an hour to find efficiencies.
- Put agents in the org chart selectively. Statista started from far too many agent ideas, tested them internally first and now has five people-team agents (for example a relocation agent and a talent acquisition buddy for hiring processes), because "would we really put up a hundred names" was the test.
- Set the guardrails before the experiments. Martin's line was that highway guardrails "make sure the traffic can go faster safely", and he recommends a crawl, walk, run approach to each new automation scored on risk and value.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Martin said US states introduced a new piece of AI legislation "every 85 minutes" in the first three months of the year.
- He said the EU AI Act obligations are now delayed until December next year, and New York Local Law 144 (compliance from 2023) requires a third-party bias audit of automated hiring decision systems.
- Anakah said data and tech teams have the highest token usage at Statista, and Jason said legal was one of the bigger spenders in his go-to-market teams.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Langdock is the multi-model workspace Statista uses and it sends the monthly coaching emails.
- Warden Watch is Warden AI's regulation and litigation tracker for HR AI, where you enter your jurisdictions and see the shared obligations. It is vendor marketing and unverified here.
- Shapes (the episode sponsor) was demoed as an AI-native people platform with an agent that answers policy questions from an uploaded handbook, moves employees between managers with a logged change and pulls ATS or Jira data through external MCP connections.

**Contrarian take:**
- Martin Redstone said "a human made the final hiring decision" is the most common excuse and most regulation now covers decision influence rather than decision making, so any filter, from zip code targeting in programmatic ads onward, can be a breach.
- Martin also said the maturity framework was written for Zapier and is not a standard, so teams behind it should not feel far behind.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Map every tool that filters or ranks candidates, from ad targeting to screening, and write down whether a recruiter could override and explain each filter.
- Pilot a private monthly AI usage summary for your team before anyone is asked to report numbers to a manager.

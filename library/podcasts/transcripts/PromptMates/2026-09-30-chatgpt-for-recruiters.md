## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Leandro Gomez Silva, founder of Remade.md and head of AI operations at Noble Recruitment, walking through a hands-on ChatGPT, Dots and skills workflow for a recruiter's whole role lifecycle. A sponsor segment features Mark, CEO of Archer (formerly hackajob), a vendor demoing its own product.  ·  **Show:** PromptMates, 30 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai agents, context engineering, human-ai collaboration, ai sourcing, recruiter workflow, ai governance, building ai tools, recruiter productivity, rec tech, ai interview notes

**When to use this:** you want to see how a recruiter could run intake, job opening, sourcing, interviewing and offers through a single connected AI agent without handing over decisions.

**Big idea:** the shift with agents is that they take responsibility for a recurring task rather than waiting to be pushed, which frees recruiters to act as advisers and quality assurers, provided every action is traceable and decisions stay with a human.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Before an intake call, tag the agent in the hiring manager's Slack thread and ask it to search the ATS, email and Notion for what the company does, why the hire is needed and what the previous recruiter was told. Gomez Silva's example surfaced a title that said staff against a budget that sat on a senior salary, plus conflicting must-haves, which became the questions for the meeting and a pre-read.
- Turn a prompt chain you like into a reusable skill by asking the model "can we turn this into a skill?", then share it with every recruiter in the workspace. He described a skill as a markdown file that explains a task the way you would onboard a person, and said you can also record your screen once so the model learns a sequence.
- Make the intake brief a living shared document with the hiring manager, their agent and yours, so changes agreed in Slack or follow-up calls update one source, while the ATS stays the record of truth.
- Delegate chasing to the agent: set a routine that nags the hiring manager for missing feedback every morning and gives candidates a holding reply such as "we don't know yet, we're chasing it".
- Build role-specific scorecards from the brief rather than defaulting to generic ones, mapping each interview question to hard skills, soft skills or company culture. Jason added the idea of adaptive scorecards that gain a new criterion mid-search when a pattern emerges.
- Source inside your own ATS first. The agent searches past applicants, scores them against the must-haves with evidence such as GitHub or a conference talk, and drafts outreach for you to review, then adds results to a dedicated source stage in the ATS.
- Rehearse the offer call with ChatGPT voice acting as the candidate, for example a candidate who says their current team offered a raise to stay, and use a negotiation persona such as a virtual Chris Voss.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Archer's CEO said it works across 15,000 jobs, qualifies 450,000 candidates and makes 2,000 introductions a day fully autonomously. This is a vendor claim, unverified.
- Archer's stated success measures are that more than 50% of introductions are first-time introductions to that company and that conversion to interview is at least 5x the customer's benchmark.
- Gomez Silva said at Noble about one in every two agent-presented profiles progresses to the outreach round, and he would like that higher.
- In a demo from Jason using a cheap bulk-screening model (name garbled in the auto transcript), 10,000 Google Scholar papers were reduced to 200 people before a stronger model reviewed them.
- He estimated an agent can handle scheduling for about 70% of roles, but said busy roles still need a human coordinator to persuade people to move meetings.
- He said Dots connects to over 4,000 apps, and recommended the newly released mid-tier model (rendered "61 soul" in the auto transcript) as the workhorse, while ultra fast mode is about eight times faster at a far higher price.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Dots (OpenAI's new always-on cloud agent, early access only), ChatGPT Spaces and Pages, the ChatGPT desktop app, ChatGPT voice, Exa for sourcing and market intelligence, and a free version of Archer (rendered as "Archer Lights" in the auto transcript) that works a small number of jobs at once, announced as a PromptMates exclusive the day before public release.
- His Exa view: strong for market intelligence such as mapping a target company's teams, "pretty okay" for sourcing, an addition rather than a replacement for off-the-shelf sourcing tools.

**Contrarian take:**
- Gomez Silva argued recruiters who are only "the notetaker, the order taker" are the ones at risk, and that advisers who arrive prepared are not.
- Jason relayed a Cursor executive's rule: "I would never push anything to an agent that is irrevocable."

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Run an agent briefing on your next role from your ATS, email and Notion before the intake call, and send the discrepancies it finds as a pre-read.
- Make every agent identify itself as an agent when it messages candidates, and log each decision in the ATS notes so it is traceable.
- Turn your best intake prompt into a shared skill for the team.

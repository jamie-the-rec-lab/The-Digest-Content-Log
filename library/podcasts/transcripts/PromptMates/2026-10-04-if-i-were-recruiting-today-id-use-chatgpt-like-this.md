## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Leandro Gomez Silva (introduced in the description as Leandro Gomes da Silva), head of AI operations at Noble Recruitment, presenting a clip from the longer PromptMates Live session held days earlier (the full session is held here as PromptMates, ChatGPT for Recruiters), run as a hypothetical hire at Buffer.  ·  **Show:** PromptMates, 4 Oct 2026
**Tags:** ai agents, context engineering, human-ai collaboration, ai sourcing, recruiter workflow, ai governance, building ai tools, recruiter productivity, ai interview notes, hiring manager alignment

**When to use this:** you want a short, practical run through using ChatGPT's new agent features to prepare a hiring manager kickoff, cut chasing and admin, and keep humans in charge of decisions.

**Big idea:** treat an always-on agent like an intern who must earn trust in small steps, so the recruiter's value shifts from taking orders to arriving prepared as an adviser and quality-checking what the agent produces.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Prepare the intake from the existing record. In the hiring manager's Slack thread, tag the agent and ask what the company does, why the team needs the hire, what the role owns and what the last recruiter was told, searching the ATS, email and Notion. His mock result flagged a staff title against a senior budget and conflicting notes on whether one tool was a must-have, which he turned into questions and a pre-read.
- Keep one living brief shared by recruiter, hiring manager and their agents for the brief, scorecards and candidate notes, with the ATS still the record of truth.
- Hand repeat chasing to the agent with a rule on channel and frequency, so missing intake details and interview feedback are pursued every morning without the recruiter doing it.
- Draw the autonomy line at what is reversible. He relayed a Cursor executive saying "I would never push anything to an agent that is irrevocable", and compared it to Amazon's one-way and two-way door decisions.
- Pair the agent with a skill so it does the task your way. Ask the model "can we turn this into a skill?" once you like an output, share it with every recruiter in the workspace, or record your screen once so it learns the steps.
- Build role-specific scorecards that map each question to hard skills, soft skills or culture, and let them adapt mid-search. The host's example was a new requirement discovered after a few interviews, which the agent then asks about in later conversations.
- Source from your own ATS first because engineers are receiving "a massive amount" of poor outreach. Review the agent's shortlist, which cites evidence such as a GitHub repo or conference talk, before any message is sent.
- Rehearse the offer call with ChatGPT voice playing the candidate, including the objection that their current team has offered a raise to stay.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Dots was said to connect to over 4,000 apps.
- At Noble, one out of every two agent-presented profiles goes through to the outreach round, which he called fairly good but wants higher.
- He estimated an agent could handle interview scheduling for 70% of roles, while busy roles still need a human to persuade people to move meetings.
- His mock company, Buffer, has 73 people and publishes salaries and 32 hours a week at full pay, used as a public-data example.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Dots, ChatGPT Spaces and Pages, the meeting notetaker, ChatGPT voice, computer use, and Exa, described as "basically the Google for AI agents", which he rated strong for market intelligence and only a supplement to existing sourcing tools.

**Contrarian take:**
- Gomez Silva said Noble does not stack-rank candidates with AI yet because of the EU AI Act, and that proprietary labels and call notes in the ATS make agent searches better than public data, so the ATS "is going to be the town of the future" for niche roles.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Make every agent that contacts candidates say it is an agent and never pose as you, and log each decision as a note in the ATS so it is traceable.
- Let the agent never reject or accept anyone: it gives suggestions with evidence and you decide.
- Send your next hiring manager a pre-read listing the discrepancies the agent found in the brief.

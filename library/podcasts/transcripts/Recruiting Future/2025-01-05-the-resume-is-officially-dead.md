## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Allyn Bailey of SmartRecruiters, in conversation with host Matt Alder, worth hearing for her blunt case that the resume has had its day and for how she reframes the questions around it.  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 05 Jan 2025
**Tags:** ai strategy, ai screening, global hiring, candidate experience, rec tech, ai upskilling, recruiter workflow

**When to use this:** you keep iterating your hiring process rather than rethinking it and want a prompt to challenge the assumptions underneath it.

**Big idea:** Bailey argues the resume is the linchpin of hiring, so pulling it forces you to redesign everything downstream, from how you post a job to how you assess and interview, which is exactly the opportunity AI now creates.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Recognise why the resume is so hard to remove: Bailey says once you pull that thread, job posting, what you ask candidates for, data collection, assessment, and interviewing all start to unravel and have to be rebuilt.
- Replace the paper with new inputs: tools that grab structured and unstructured data, conversational-based inputs, and AI that can assess in the blink of an eye rather than reading a sheet that "defined a person".
- Shift the workplace debate from remote versus hybrid to autonomy, which Bailey frames as the real question of whether you trust someone to decide how, when, and where they get the work done.
- Interrogate who really needs to be in the process; Bailey asks why hiring adds 50 million people and five sign-offs when it may really just be about the hiring manager.

**Contrarian take:**
- Bailey warns that if you rely on doing the old way faster and more efficiently, efficiency eventually reaches the point where you are no longer needed, so you must build the next plan now.
- She and Alder invoke the Henry Ford "faster horses" line, arguing the industry iterates rather than innovates because people only ask for a quicker version of what they already do.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Each morning ask one question of a task: "what if I didn't have to do it that way?", then design what you would do instead.
- Pick one role and pilot a hiring flow with no resume, deciding up front how you would collect and assess the equivalent information.

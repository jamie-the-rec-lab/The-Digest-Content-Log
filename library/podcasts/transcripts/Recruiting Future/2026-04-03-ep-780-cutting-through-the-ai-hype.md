## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Kelly Monahan, labour economist  |  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 3 Apr 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, quality of hire, talent density, headcount planning, internal mobility, retention

**When to use this:** You've cut entry-level roles to justify AI automation, or you're concerned about the long-term pipeline damage from eliminating junior positions.

**Big idea:** Removing entry-level positions for AI-driven efficiency creates a decade-long skill shortage of mid-senior talent; domain expertise and mentorship cannot be automated, and companies are mortgaging future capability for immediate cost cuts.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Map entry-level hiring trends in your organisation over the past 3 years; if declining, model future senior pipeline by cohort
- For every entry-level role you eliminate via AI, calculate the downstream impact: which senior roles will be harder to fill 10 years from now without entry-level talent pathways
- Quantify the knowledge transfer loss: entry-level hires learn domain expertise from senior people; if you cut entry-level, seniors end up doing entry-level work or knowledge dies with departures
- Position entry-level hiring as a leadership development investment (junior people get mentored, seniors get mentorship experience and satisfaction), not just a cost center

**Data & benchmarks:**
- The energy sector saw this play out: companies that froze entry-level hiring in 2008-2010 faced critical skill shortages of qualified mid-seniors 10 years later
- AI cannot replicate domain expertise or mentorship value; it can automate task work only

**Contrarian take:**
- Kelly Monahan argues that entry-level programme cuts are a form of  short-term thinking masquerading as innovation; they damage long-term organisational capability

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Pull your entry-level hiring trend line for past 5 years
- Model senior talent supply 7-10 years out based on current pipeline
- Commit to entry-level hiring if you've cut it

**When to use this:** You're implementing AI hiring tools but your team is focused only on speed and cost reduction, or you want to understand how to measure AI ROI beyond "time saved."

**Big idea:** Most companies implement AI to cut costs, but the real ROI comes from higher-quality hires and better hiring manager satisfaction; optimising purely for speed produces worse outcomes.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Define your AI success metric before tool selection: is it candidate experience (speed, fairness, transparency), hiring manager satisfaction (quality of candidates sent), or recruiter productivity (time freed for relationship work)? Cost reduction alone is a weak metric
- Measure quality-of-hire post-implementation: compare start-date performance reviews, time-to-productivity, and retention between AI-screened cohorts and control groups
- Use AI to improve candidate communication (structured feedback, faster decisions) not just screening volume; candidates who understand why they were rejected are more likely to reapply

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Companies optimising for hiring manager satisfaction (candidate quality) outperform cost-focused implementations on retention and performance

**Contrarian take:**
- Implementers claim AI improves efficiency; the real opportunity is improved quality and experience, which is harder to measure but more strategic

## Episode description

Over the past year, AI features in recruiting tools have seen significant adoption.  But if you ask TA Teams whether AI has changed how they actually hire, most of them will say no. Individual productivity is up, but organizational transformation hasn't followed.



At the same time, AI tools on the candidate side are flooding employers with credible applications from candidates who may not be seriously interested. So what needs to shift for AI to genuinely transform recruiting for employers and candidates alike?



My guest this week is Nikos Moraitakis (https://www.linkedin.com/in/moraitakis/) , Co-Founder and CEO of Workable (https://www.workable.com/) . In our conversation, he shares why productivity gains haven't driven real change, how AI agents could take over sourcing and screening, why the recruiter role faces a dramatic shift, and what all this means for candidate experience.








In the interview, we discuss:


     
       
  •  Why AI adoption hasn't yet driven significant transformation
     
       
  •  AI-driven applications with low candidate intent
     
       
  •  How AI capabilities have advanced in the last few months
     
       
  •  Using agentic AI like a staffing agency
     
       
  •  AI automates tasks, not jobs.
     
       
  •  Why recruiters need to focus on the bottom of the funnel, not the top
     
       
  •  Trust, transparency, and human oversight
     
       
  •  What hiring looks like in the future

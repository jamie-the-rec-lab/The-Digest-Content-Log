## Actionable summary
**Host:** Matt Alder, Recruiting Future  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 19 May 2026
**Tags:** ai sourcing, ai screening, high-volume hiring, frontline hiring, human-ai collaboration

**When to use this:** You manage frontline hiring (retail, hospitality, logistics) and are deciding how much of the hiring process to automate, or you want to maintain human connection at scale.

**Big idea:** Frontline hiring at scale demands automation, but automation at scale damages candidate experience if it cuts out human touchpoints entirely. The best approach: automate the parts candidates hate (form filling, waiting for feedback, scheduling) and add more human connection (personal outreach, interview guidance, offer calls).

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Identify candidate pain points: waiting for feedback, vague communication, generic rejections, scheduling friction. Automate these using AI and workflows.
- Keep human touchpoints at critical moments: initial outreach, interview debrief, offer, and onboarding. These moments matter psychologically.
- Use AI to personalise at scale. Instead of generic messages to candidates, use data to personalise: mention their skills, reference their background, show you have read their application.
- Train hiring managers on AI tools. If managers do not understand how AI screens or schedules, they distrust it. Transparency builds adoption.
- Measure candidate satisfaction, not just hiring speed. If AI is faster but candidates feel processed, you have a cultural problem.

**Contrarian take:**
- Frontline hiring is often treated as transactional and automated aggressively. The best frontline employers automate friction but keep human moments because those drive engagement and commitment.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Interview 10 recent hires from your frontline roles. Ask: What was your experience like? Which interactions felt human and personal? Which felt robotic?
- Identify the top three pain points they mention. Design AI automation to fix those, but keep human touchpoints for the moments that matter.
- Measure offer acceptance rate by hiring path. Do candidates hired through high-touch paths accept offers at higher rates? Use this data to guide where to invest.

## Episode description

In frontline retail hiring, speed is everything. If the process is too slow, candidates take offers elsewhere, and stores are short-staffed, hurting both service and revenue. AI-powered automation is now helping some organizations close that gap, cutting hiring times, saving thousands of hours, and driving measurable financial value for the business. 








The organizations seeing real results started with the problem, not the technology, because layering AI onto a process that isn't working only makes things worse. They also had to answer a question that rarely gets asked: how quick is too quick, and when does speed start to feel impersonal? The goal isn't to remove humans from the hiring process. It's to remove the noise so candidates reach the right people faster.




My guests this week are Stef Nikitas (https://www.linkedin.com/in/snikitas/) , Director of Talent Acquisition at Ace Hardware, and Rachel Allen (https://www.linkedin.com/in/rachelallen52/) , Senior Director of Talent Acquisition at 7-Eleven. In our conversation, they share how they transformed frontline hiring with AI, the results it delivered, and where they chose to keep humans firmly in the process.




In the interview, we discuss:


     
       
  •  Why speed matters in frontline hiring
     
       
  •  The danger of automating broken processes
     
       
  •  Leading with the problem, not the technology
     
       
  •  How quick is too quick?
     
       
  •  What remains human and why

## Actionable summary
**Host:** Matt Alder, Recruiting Future  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 23 May 2026
**Tags:** ai interviewing, ai governance, candidate experience, ai bias, human-ai collaboration

**When to use this:** You are deploying AI interviewing tools and want to avoid reputational damage, or you need to explain to candidates and hiring managers why AI is part of your process.

**Big idea:** AI interviewing tools (video or chat-based) are fast and scalable but are also opaque and prone to bias. The best approach is transparent use: explain to candidates what AI is doing and why, combine AI screening with human review, and audit tools for bias regularly.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Transparency first. Tell candidates that AI is used to screen or assess them. Explain what the AI is looking for. Do not treat it as a surprise.
- Combine AI interviewing with human backup. If an AI scores a candidate as "not suitable", have a human reviewer check borderline cases before rejection.
- Audit AI interviewing tools for bias. Do they rate candidates of different genders, accents, backgrounds differently on identical performance? If yes, do not use the tool.
- Train hiring managers on how to interpret AI interview scores. They are data points, not oracles. Hiring managers should still apply judgment.
- Collect candidate feedback on AI interviewing. Are they satisfied? Do they feel fairly assessed? Use this to improve the experience.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Candidates increasingly expect and accept AI in hiring if it is transparent and produces fair outcomes.
- AI interviewing tools with poor validation or bias issues damage employer brand quickly.

**Contrarian take:**
- Many organisations deploy AI interviewing quietly, hoping candidates do not notice. Candidates do notice. Transparency builds trust; opacity builds resentment.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Audit your current AI interviewing setup. Do candidates know they are being assessed by AI? If not, add disclosure to your candidate communication.
- Run a bias audit on your AI tool. Use the vendor's tool or hire a third party to test for differential outcomes across demographic groups.
- Collect one month of candidate feedback on AI interviewing. Ask: Did you understand what was being assessed? Did it feel fair? Use this to iterate.

## Actionable summary
**Host:** Matt Alder, Recruiting Future  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 3 Jun 2026
**Tags:** talent pools, internal mobility, retention, recruiting strategy, skills-based hiring

**When to use this:** You cannot find the talent you need on the external market, or you have high turnover in key roles and want to build internal pipelines instead.

**Big idea:** When the market cannot supply the talent you need, grow it internally. Build talent pools of internal candidates with adjacent skills, reskill them, and promote them into critical roles. This is cheaper and faster than external hiring for roles with consistent skills demand.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Map skills adjacency: which internal roles have 60-80 percent of the skills needed for your target role? Those are your internal talent pool candidates.
- Build internal mobility programmes around reskilling, not just promotion. Invest in training for the 20-percent gap.
- Prioritise roles with recurring demand (e.g., engineering manager, sales director) for internal pipeline building. The ROI is higher.
- Use AI to identify high-potential internal candidates and match them to career paths.
- Communicate career pathways clearly. Employees stay when they see a path to roles they want.

**Contrarian take:**
- When external hiring is hard and expensive, organisations double down on external recruitment. The smarter play is internal mobility and reskilling.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- List your top five hardest-to-fill roles. For each, identify three internal roles that share 60+ percent of the skills. These are your internal talent pools.
- Design a 12-week reskilling programme for one role. What is the cost vs hiring externally?
- Brief your people ops team on internal mobility. Ask: how many internal transfers happened last year? How many should have happened?

## Episode description

In some industries, aging workforces and deepening skill shortages mean companies can no longer rely on hiring the experienced workers they need. The only realistic option is to grow their own, and that puts apprenticeship schemes right at the centre of workforce planning.








Running a programme at that scale raises questions that go well beyond recruiting. Culture shapes whether people stay, mentoring determines whether skills actually transfer, and long-term success often depends on governments understanding how to direct support towards the future talent that industries actually need. 




So what makes an apprenticeship scheme genuinely effective for high-skilled talent, and what has to be in place around it to make it work?




My guest this week is David Dart, Chief People Officer at Caliber Holdings. In our conversation, David shares what he's learned building a skilled-talent pipeline at scale.








In the interview, we discuss:








The unique talent challenges in recruiting auto body technicians



Skills shortages and an aging workforce



Culture and retention



The importance of mentoring



The impact of AI on jobs



The importance of trades careers and the need for government support



What does the future look like?

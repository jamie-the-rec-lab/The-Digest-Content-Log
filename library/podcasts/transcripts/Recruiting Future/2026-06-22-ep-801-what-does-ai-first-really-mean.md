## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Mark Stelzner, Founder and Managing Principal, IA  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 22 Jun 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, recruiting operations, ai governance, human-ai collaboration, ta leadership, recruiting enablement, context engineering

**When to use this:** You are declaring yourself "AI-first" without clear foundations, or you are deploying AI and getting back low-quality results that match your messy data.

**Big idea:** Declaring yourself "AI-first" as a technology solution will expose and amplify every process and data problem you already have. AI is not a fix; it is a mirror. Building the foundation first (data quality, process ownership, authoritative content) is what actually makes AI work.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Break down what work stays human, what gets amplified by AI, and what gets displaced. When you receive a requisition and list skills, interrogate each skill: will this be displaced by an AI agent, amplified (meaning the human gets better tools and does more), or remain uniquely human? The work that remains will be "heavy, heady and intense", which is good design.
- Map your current state forensically. Write down where information lives (which systems hold employee data, candidate data, benefits data, statutory benefits by country). List all the external service providers and tools you use. Most organisations find collisions of information, not duplication; this reveals what you do not control.
- Pilot where you have highest data and content integrity first. Do not try to apply AI across the entire end-to-end process on day one. Find the subset where content and data are already aligned and trusted, prove value there, then expand. When pilots fail, you will know it is not the tool; it is the foundation.
- Establish global process ownership. No single person owns "onboarding" in most large organisations; it crosses facilities, IT, payroll, authentication, policies. Until someone owns the end-to-end workflow, AI will amplify the chaos.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- The speaker works with Global 1000 organisations and has spent three decades in transformation; this reflects large enterprise dynamics, not small business.
- EU pay transparency regulations are forcing organisations to confront long-hidden pay inequities.

**Contrarian take:**
- Stelzner argues that "AI-first" declarations from the top down are backwards. The real question is: what problems do we have that are looking for technological enablement? Treat AI as a solution to a named problem, not a technology in search of a use case.
- HR's new mandate is not to get a "seat at the table"; it is to run the transformation office and manage continuous organisational restructuring. This is relentless. The old idea of "transformation is done" is gone.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- List the three to five HR processes you touch daily. For each, write down what system owns each piece of the workflow. You will find gaps.
- Ask your CHRO: who owns the end-to-end requisition-to-onboarding process? If the answer is "it's spread across five teams", that is your first blocker to AI.
- When asked to deploy AI, push back with "what problem are we solving?" and "what is our data quality score for that process?" Refuse to be the technology looking for a problem.

## Episode description

A growing number of organizations are rushing to put AI to work, often announcing themselves as AI-first before working out what that actually means. What many are finding is that AI tends to surface whatever was already underneath. Where the data is patchy, the content conflicting, and no one quite owns the end-to-end process, the technology exposes all of it rather than fixing any of it.








At the same time, AI is starting to reshape work itself, raising hard questions about which tasks remain genuinely human and what HR and TA roles will look like on the other side. 








So what does it take to build foundations solid enough to make these tools deliver?








My guest this week is Mark Stelzner (https://www.linkedin.com/in/markstelzner/) , founder and managing principal at IA. In our conversation, Mark explains what it really takes to make AI work in the people function.








In the interview, we discuss:


     
       
  •  What are the driving forces and catalysts for transformation?
     
       
  •  How AI amplifies rather than fixes existing problems
     
       
  •  What does AI first actually mean?
     
       
  •  Re-inventing processes in large complex organizations
     
       
  •  AI’s impact on work
     
       
  •  Displaced skills, amplified skills, and uniquely human skills
     
       
  •  Turning capacity into new value
     
       
  •  The impact of transformation on people and culture
     
       
  •  The new role of the CHR

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Ben Russell, Co-founder, SonicJobs  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 25 Jun 2026
**Tags:** ai sourcing, job advertising, candidate ai tools, rec tech, recruitment marketing, candidate experience

**When to use this:** Your employer brand gets buried behind job boards in search results, or you want candidates to research your company before applying.

**Big idea:** Conversational AI fundamentally changes how job seekers find work. Instead of typing keywords into a job board, candidates now describe their full situation to LLMs in natural language and arrive informed with genuine intent.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Conversational vs keyword search. Candidates stop saying "nurse near me" and instead say "I'm a nurse with 12 years of experience, I'm a single parent, I have a car, I need fixed shifts without nights, and I want to get fitter this year". The LLM maps those constraints to jobs with gym access, fixed schedules, and public transport nearby.
- Implicit discovery: ChatGPT and Claude automatically suggest job apps based on what the candidate describes. When someone says "I'm looking for a nursing role in X area paying Y", the platform recommends relevant employer apps without the candidate explicitly searching for them.
- Explicit discovery: candidates can also find apps in an app store within ChatGPT or Claude and open them directly.
- Build individual company apps in ChatGPT (rather than aggregator apps like Indeed). Companies provide logos and a live jobs feed; OpenAI suggests the app when relevant. No CPC or CPA fees.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- ChatGPT holds 65 percent market share in the US with 900 million weekly users and 20 billion weekly prompts.
- Around 1 in 5 or 1 in 6 ChatGPT prompts relate to pay, salaries, or careers.
- 75 percent of ChatGPT revenue comes from subscriptions (vs Google's 98 percent from AdWords in its early days).
- ChatGPT is growing 5.5 times faster than Google did in its early days.
- Johns Hopkins recently launched a career app in ChatGPT.

**Contrarian take:**
- Russell argues that ChatGPT makes money from subscriptions, not ads. This means OpenAI is incentivised to build an ecosystem of useful tools inside ChatGPT, not rank job aggregators. That is the opposite of Google's business model.
- Most employers spent years or decades winning candidate traffic back from Google search results. This moment lets companies own their own discovery channel again without paying per click or per application.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Build a ChatGPT app for your company's jobs through SonicJobs or similar vendors. You provide job feed and branding; the platform handles implicit discovery.
- Write job descriptions and interview guides using natural language context: what does someone need in their life to thrive in this role (commute, schedule, team dynamics, growth path).
- Coach hiring teams to expect candidates who have already researched your company, culture, and role before they apply.

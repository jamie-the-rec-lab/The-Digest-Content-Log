**When to use this:** you have rolled out a new tool or AI initiative, adoption is stalling, and your team is reverting to manual processes.

**Big idea:** Technology is not the hard part; leadership is. The "neutral zone" between old and new ways of working is where most adoption fails. Managers lack the skills to take individual team members from resistance to adoption, especially under time pressure. Treating critical feedback as noise instead of signal leaves opportunities on the table for optimisation.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- The neutral zone: post-rollout, pre-full-adoption. This is where teams are stuck. Some people have moved forward; some are resisting. Some days the tool works; some days it does not. Your job as a manager is not to police tool usage; it is to understand that the journey is different for every person and support them through it.
- Listen to critical feedback, not as complaining but as signal. In a sandbox, tools work. In reality, recruiters are juggling hiring managers, candidate experience, leadership pressure, and a heavy load. The friction you are hearing probably points to a real workflow problem, not recruiter laziness.
- Reframe the question: stop asking "are you using the tool?" Start asking "what can we do to make this tool actually effective for your workflow?" This shifts from compliance to optimisation.
- Understand your team member's change profile using frameworks like Gretch and Rubin's Four Tendencies. Some people need structure and deadlines. Some need autonomy. Some need community and social proof. Understand what each person needs from you to move through the neutral zone.
- Tap into the individual recruiter's core problem. Some recruiters optimise for speed, some for quality, some for cost. Unless you understand their lens, you cannot make the case for change.
- Build a team of super-users and ambassadors. You need them. But they alone will not bring resistant people on board. Listen to the resisters because they often spot real problems.

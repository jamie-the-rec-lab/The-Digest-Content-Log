## Actionable summary

**Guest:** Recruiting Future, episode 811  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 28 Jul 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, hiring manager alignment, talent pools, interview design, candidate experience

**When to use this:** leadership is pushing hard on AI adoption and your hiring managers are resisting, or you are seeing faster processes but worse offer acceptance rates.

**Big idea:** Technology cannot fix problems that are human in nature. The biggest hiring blockers are not process inefficiency or information asymmetry; they are misalignment between recruiter and hiring manager on what actually matters for the role, vague job briefs that shift during hiring, and hiring managers who do not invest time in interviewing. AI accelerates all of these, including the bad ones.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- The hiring manager alignment problem is upstream. Before any process redesign, ask: does the hiring manager know what they are hiring for? Can they articulate the three non-negotiable skills and the three "nice to have" traits? If not, no tool will fix that.
- Most hiring failures trace to one of three causes: unclear brief (what we are actually hiring for shifts mid-process), misaligned bar (hiring manager and recruiter have different conviction thresholds), or thin candidate pool (sourcing did not find people who can do the job).
- Technology is best at surface-level problems (speed, volume, consistency). It cannot solve structural problems like hiring managers who refuse to invest in a fair interview process or talent pools that do not exist in a market.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- No specific data shared, but the episode references industry trends around technology implementation and the persistence of hiring manager resistance despite tooling.

**Tools & prompts:**
- No specific tools or prompts were mentioned in this excerpt.

**Contrarian take:**
- Technology does not fix human problems. Many organisations are deploying AI to hiring to speed up screening and early-stage decisions, but if the root cause of slow hiring is a vague brief or a misaligned hiring manager, AI just speeds up the wrong process.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Schedule a 30-minute session with your next hiring manager kickoff. Ask them to write down (without looking at the job description) the top three skills you are hiring for and the top three behavioural traits. Do their answers match the brief? If not, that is your problem.
- Measure your offer acceptance rate by hiring manager. If one manager has an 80% rate and another has 60%, the difference is not speed; it is alignment and communication quality.

## Episode description

AI has been applied to almost every step of the hiring process. Sourcing, screening, assessments, interviews; each has its own tools, and many of them are effective. For many organizations, though, the gains from optimizing individual stages are flattening out. Hiring quality is shaped by the entire journey, not by any single step, and most hiring technology was never built to connect those steps. The focus is shifting toward connecting the whole process so that each stage learns from the others and improves over time.



So what does it take to move from optimizing separate steps to building connected intelligence across the hiring process?



My guest this week is Ben Chino, Co-founder and CPO of Maki. In our conversation, Ben explains why improving hiring one step at a time has hit its limits, what end-to-end hiring intelligence looks like in practice, and what it means for recruiters and candidates.



In the interview, we discuss:


  •  Why optimizing individual hiring steps with AI has hit diminishing returns
  •  The difference between a system of record and a system of intelligence
  •  How a connected hiring process improves decision-making at every stage
  •  Where the ATS fits in the next generation of hiring technology
  •  Why human judgment in hiring is less consistent than most people think
  •  Freeing recruiters for better judgment and more time with candidates
  •  Turning 800,000 applications into a real candidate experience
  •  Why adopting AI in hiring

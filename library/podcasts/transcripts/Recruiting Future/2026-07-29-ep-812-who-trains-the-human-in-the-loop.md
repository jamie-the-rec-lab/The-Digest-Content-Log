## Actionable summary

**Guest:** Johan Roos, Professor in Strategy at Hult International Business School, author of Human Magic  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 29 Jul 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, ta leadership, recruiter career growth, human-ai collaboration, retention, quality of hire, interview design

**When to use this:** you are worried that delegating junior-level work to AI will starve future leaders of the judgment they need to hire well.

**Big idea:** Judgment develops only through hands-on experience, often exactly the work that AI is automating; cutting entry-level hiring whilst deploying AI agents creates a generation of leaders who must default to their LLM instead of their intuition.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- The five human capabilities that AI cannot replace: curiosity, creativity, critical thinking, communication, collaboration. These must sit at the centre of what organisations teach.
- Move your unit of analysis from people to tasks, not jobs. A $80 billion platform company built 54,000 AI agents in six months by asking each person to develop two agents, keeping every role whilst making tasks faster. Nobody was fired; the signal was clear: we are working smarter, not cutting headcount.
- Bionic collaboration: the emerging skill is learning to work with AI agents as teammates, not substituting for human judgment.
- On-the-job learning is the most critical learning. Universities were built to last, not to change. Roos advocates for apprenticeship models: alternate work and study (a week at uni, a week at work) rather than the current four-year block. Some European countries do this routinely.
- Bring retiring staff in as coaches for junior talent, embedding decades of experience back into the organisation. Most companies and universities are not structured for it, but it would preserve judgment transfer.

**Contrarian take:**
- The conversation about AI is not changing fast enough. Most leaders still frame it as efficiency and headcount reduction (Roos observed this through 2023 and most of 2024). The risk is a fork: use AI to amplify human capability or use it as a substitute for humanness. The choice is yours, but choosing substitution turns professionals into "AI concierges" instead of real citizens.
- Cutting junior hiring is a "serious societal challenge". Graduate students are having a harder time finding entry-level roles, especially in social science. This is self-inflicted damage: you cannot build judgment if there is no pathway into hands-on junior work.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Audit your entry-level hiring: have you cut roles in the last 18 months? If yes, ask how your next generation of leaders will get judgment. Commit to at least one new junior hire in a function where AI is handling grunt work.
- Task-analyse one role: list the 10-15 things the role does. Mark which tasks AI can handle today. Do not cut the role; reassign the person to the 3-5 high-judgment tasks instead, with the junior work fed to the agent.
- Partner one retiring leader (or someone 5+ years from retirement) with your highest-potential junior as a judgment coach. Formalise it: weekly coffee, sit in on their decisions, give feedback on their instincts.

Judgment sits at the center of this. It develops through years of hands-on experience, often in exactly the kind of junior work AI is now taking over. With many employers also hiring fewer entry-level people, developing the leaders of the future is becoming a serious challenge.

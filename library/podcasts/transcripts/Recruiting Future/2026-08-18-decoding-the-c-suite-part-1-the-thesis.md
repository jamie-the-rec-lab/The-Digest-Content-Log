## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Matt Alder, interviewed by Allyn Bailey, SmartRecruiters  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 18 Aug 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, sourcing, outbound sourcing, candidate ai tools, recruiting enablement, talent market data

**When to use this:** When you're rethinking your entire recruiting process or trying to understand where candidates are taking you.

**Big idea:** Ask why you do recruiting the way you do. Candidates are already breaking recruiting with AI agents before employers even have them. The mental models behind your processes matter more than the systems.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Challenge the mental models, not just the systems. Changing behaviour doesn't work if the underlying mental model stays the same. When the pandemic sent everyone home, mental models were shaken. Once the crisis passed, they snapped back. The pandemic worked because it challenged assumptions about remote work; most change efforts fail because they don't. Audit your assumptions about why you have resumes, interviews, or recruiting workflows. Ask: Why do we do that? Do we need to?
- Don't automate broken processes. The biggest danger: applying AI to processes that never worked in the first place. Strip back first. Redesign before you add AI to make things faster or "more intelligent."
- Vision wins over business cases. People get behind directions and visions, not strategic plans. Present a business case tied to an achievable vision that delivers value. That's what moves the needle, not a 47-slide deck.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Candidates are already using ChatGPT or Claude to find jobs rather than job boards.
- Candidates are using AI to rewrite resumes, batch-apply with software, and stand out in a tight market.
- Internet adoption took 4-5 years (1999-2003/04 before real business changes happened). AI is moving far faster; waiting is now a competitive risk.

**Contrarian take:**
- Candidates are the ones driving innovation in recruiting, not employers. Matt sees the innovation coming from candidate side because they can move quickly. Employers can't match that pace and are treating it as a fad.
- "The candidates are breaking recruiting. It's already happening." That's not a future scenario; it's now.
- Waiting for dust to settle is no longer an option. "It just isn't going to work" because change is too fast. The internet took years to reshape business; AI will reshape it in months.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Map your current recruiting workflow and ask "Why?" for each step. Challenge at least one inherited assumption this week.
- Sketch out your vision for talent in your org in 2-3 years. What would "winning" look like? Use that vision when pitching change to leadership.
- Start a pilot where a team uses an AI agent to explore jobs. Don't wait for it to be perfect. Learn from how candidates are already using this.

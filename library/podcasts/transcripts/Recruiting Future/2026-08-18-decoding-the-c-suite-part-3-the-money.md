## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Tom DiDesidero, CFO, SmartRecruiters  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 18 Aug 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, recruiting enablement, compensation, recruiting operations, ta leadership

**When to use this:** When you need to pitch an AI project to your CFO or build a business case for AI investment in recruiting.

**Big idea:** CFOs want pilots, not big commitments. Short-term, measurable goals with clear evidence beat abstract promises. And the SaaS and AI worlds are converging because they need each other.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Pilot first, then scale. SmartRecruiters pitched Winston to customers as "start small, prove value, commit further." Tom pushed the same approach internally: short-term, well-defined pilots with measurable outcomes before larger spend.
- Use 3-6 month OKRs, not annual plans. Tom said the "big prize" distracts. Focus on what you'll deliver in the next quarter, keep people aligned, pivot as you learn. Annual goals stay, but short-term OKRs drive real pace.
- Bring evidence, not instinct. Use Claude or similar tools to pressure-test your assumptions before pitching. "Bring evidence, not 'it feels like'." One person Tom met had run their initial draft through Claude, which immediately elevated their credibility.
- Match excitement with candor. "Match that with candor and realism." You're solving a problem. What's the cost? What's measurable success? Good CFOs encourage the bold move, then temper it with discipline.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- SaaS gross margins typically exceed 80%. AI services are running 25% gross margins (some negative). That's a fundamental shift in the unit economics.
- Inference costs (cost per token) are the biggest wild card. You can't predict usage accurately, so revenue models are unstable in ways traditional SaaS never were.
- SmartRecruiters brought over 100 engineers and PMs from SAP post-acquisition, plus a larger safety net for taking bigger bets.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Use Claude to develop business cases. Tom explicitly mentioned seeing someone use Claude to supplement their initial proposal, which strengthened their pitch. Run your draft through an AI assistant to pressure-test logic and surface blind spots.

**Contrarian take:**
- SaaS and AI will merge, not replace each other. "Those two worlds will come together. It's inevitable." Neither can walk away from their existing customer relationships. SaaS has the users; AI has the capabilities. Tom sees this as inevitable within months.
- Don't fixate on broad ROI measures. "How ROI is being looked at in the AI environment is changing." The SEC hasn't mandated meaningful AI reporting yet. Internal metrics (gross margin, inference cost, usage) matter more than headline ROI claims right now.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Write your next AI pitch as a 3-6 month pilot with clear success metrics, not a big bet.
- Run your draft through Claude before sending to your CFO; use it to pressure-test your numbers and logic.
- Track inference costs and usage separately from revenue. That's where the real predictability (or lack of it) hides.

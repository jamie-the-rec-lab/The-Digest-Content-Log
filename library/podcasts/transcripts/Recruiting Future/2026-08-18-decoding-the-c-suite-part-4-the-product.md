## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Shefali Netke, VP of Product Design, SmartRecruiters  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 18 Aug 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, human-ai collaboration, ai upskilling, rec tech, recruiter training, ta leadership

**When to use this:** When you're building a product that serves users at different levels of AI adoption readiness.

**Big idea:** Design for a spectrum of user readiness rather than an average user. Some customers are ready to leap forward on AI, some are risk-averse, most are curious but cautious.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Design for regions and teams, not monoliths: SmartRecruiters' customers pilot features first in specific regions or teams, prove the value, then roll out company-wide. Apply the same pattern in your product roadmap.
- Mix strategic leaps with scaffolded middle ground: Intentionally leapfrog in some product areas (innovation-first) whilst keeping core workflows on a middle path where users have familiar anchors. You don't have to go the full jump everywhere.
- Run a structured AI fluency workshop for your team. SmartRecruiters ran a 2-day programme (3 hours daily, all other meetings excused) with an external speaker, hands-on workshops, and a mini-hackathon. Outcome: people stopped feeling overwhelmed and got started with AI learning.
- Identify and empower AI champions. Have 3-4 eager people in your team who test new tools daily, then signal back to the broader team what's worth adopting versus noise.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Over 100 engineers, many PMs and designers from SAP were integrated into SmartRecruiters' teams post-acquisition.
- AI tool releases now happen daily (formerly monthly, then weekly).

**Tools & prompts:**
- Create a visual North Star, not abstract statements. SmartRecruiters built a 5-6 minute video showing the integrated product vision. When shown in a theatre space with teams alongside you, it sparked concrete ideas instantly (versus a PowerPoint).

**Contrarian take:**
- "AI doesn't have to be in everything." Shefali pushes back on the assumption that every workflow needs AI; distinguish the key moments where human interaction is essential.
- Challenge legacy workflows. The industry is "starting to let go of some of the older workflows or ways of working" because the future is agentic.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Pick one region or team to pilot a new AI feature before full rollout. Measure and iterate before scaling.
- Run a 2-day learning sprint for your team (even 3 hours a day, excuse from meetings).
- Identify one AI champion per key function who stays current on daily releases and brings signals back to the team weekly.

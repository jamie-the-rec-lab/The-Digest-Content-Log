## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Ciara Harrington, Chief People Officer at Skillsoft  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 20 Aug 2026
**Tags:** ai agents, building ai tools, recruiter workflow, recruiting operations, ta leadership, human-ai collaboration, hiring manager alignment

**When to use this:** You're facing the first question of the AI era: when a role opens, should you hire a person or deploy an AI agent? You need a framework for deciding which work stays with humans and what moves to bots.

**Big idea:** The hiring question has fundamentally shifted from "who should we hire?" to "does this work need a human at all?" Skills mapping provides the basis for making that decision, but it requires HR to become more technical and build hybrid workflows where AI handles data-heavy tasks and humans make judgment calls.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Build, Buy, Borrow, Bot: Add a fourth option to the traditional talent acquisition framework. When a leader says they need to hire someone, ask: "What's the work? Can we build it internally? Buy it from another team? Borrow it from external talent? Or deploy an AI bot?" Only then default to hiring a person.
- Task-level skills mapping: Decompose each role or workflow into its constituent tasks, then map the skills each requires. For each task, ask: Can this run 24/7? Is it repetitive? Does it require mining data quickly? If yes to those, it's a candidate for AI automation.
- Hybrid human-plus-bot workflows: Don't treat AI as a binary replacement for humans. Tier one employee support at Skillsoft uses an AI agent that answers simple questions (holidays, pay scales) and flags data inconsistencies to humans, so employees still get human contact when needed. This gives employees better service while freeing humans from tick-box questions.
- Iterative deployment without perfect data: Don't wait for knowledge documents to be perfect or agent capability to reach 100 per cent. Skillsoft's tier one agent started at 40-50 per cent capability; it improves as employees flag errors and the system learns from human corrections, just like new human employees do.
- HR workforce transformation: Hire technical people (engineering, IT, project management backgrounds) into HR roles; don't expect to find "AI-ready" HR people on the market. Complement soft people skills with hard technical skills.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Skills frameworks have existed for 20 years but rarely became the basis for hiring decisions because they took too long to build and maintain manually.
- Career acceleration hypothesis: As AI handles routine work, the typical 10-year path to first-line management may shrink significantly, requiring different onboarding and leadership development.
- AI agents can handle work that is repetitive, data-heavy, runs 24/7, and requires no judgment. Humans remain critical for regulatory judgment (escalations, employee relations investigations) and strategic decisions.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Tier one support AI agent deployed at Skillsoft answers HR policy questions by reading knowledge articles and flagging contradictions when multiple data sources conflict.
- Culture survey analytics: AI combs through open-ended survey comments at scale, identifies themes, and surfaces insights faster than manual qualitative analysis.
- Onboarding bot: Handles administrative data entry (dependent names, address, equipment preferences) so humans can focus on strategic onboarding conversations about role expectations and 30/60/90 day goals.

**Contrarian take:**
- Harrington argues that organisations fearing AI agent errors or demanding perfect knowledge bases before deploying agents will never get started, just as skills frameworks failed in the past: "If you're waiting to have everything perfect before you put in your agent, like skills in the past, you'll never get there."

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Map your highest-volume operational tasks (benefits questions, leave requests, onboarding questions) and identify which involve only data lookup or repetitive questions; build or buy an AI agent for that narrow scope first, not a sweeping transformation.
- Reframe HR hiring conversations with leaders from "Should we hire?" to "What's the work? Can a bot do it?" and measure business impact (cost, speed, employee satisfaction) before and after deployment.
- Recruit people with technical project management and data analytics backgrounds into your HR team; pair them with experienced HR professionals so technical and functional expertise reinforce each other.

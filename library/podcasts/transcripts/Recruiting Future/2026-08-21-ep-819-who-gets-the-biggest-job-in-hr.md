## Actionable summary

**Guest:** Olivia Sharp, Partner at Eton Bridge Partners, leads CPO/CHRO searches and boards research  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 21 Aug 2026
**Tags:** ta leadership, talent strategy, executive search, hiring manager alignment, employer branding

**When to use this:** You are a TA leader who wants to progress to Chief People Officer or CHRO and want to understand what companies are looking for now.

**Big idea:** The profile for Chief People Officer is shifting dramatically. Only 53% of new CPOs have previous CPO experience (down from 78% three years ago). Boards now want commercial, strategic thinkers who can drive organisational performance, not just functional HR experts.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Global CPO appointments dropped 20 percent in three years; UK down 34 percent.
- External hiring for CPO roles is rising: 62 percent external in 2024, 71 percent in 2025 (up from 50 percent in 2023).
- Four out of five external CPO hires now come from the same sector (up from three out of four in 2023).
- For TA leaders moving to CPO: broaden beyond talent acquisition into full employee lifecycle (organisation design, reward, transformation), gain board-level exposure, develop commercial fluency (understanding how your work impacts value creation and profitability), and seek out AI transformation and restructuring projects.
- The modern CPO is expected to have workforce strategy capability, AI literacy, change leadership, stakeholder management, and the ability to communicate with investors and boards.
- Most traditional HR career paths do not develop commercial expertise or business acumen. TA leaders have an advantage because they understand labour markets and talent strategy better than most.
- Hire a generalist with functional depth somewhere in their career, not a pure specialist.
- Do not focus on becoming the best talent acquisition person. Focus on becoming someone who understands how talent creates value.

**Contrarian take:**
- Olivia: Prior CPO experience is no longer the gating factor. Boards are taking calculated risks on potential and capability over proven experience.
- Olivia: UK organisations love hiring "shiny and new" but often look for it within the same sector, not truly radical thinkers.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- If you are a TA leader wanting to become CPO, seek out transformation work (AI projects, restructuring) in your current organisation.
- Build relationships with board members and NEDs where appropriate; track how your talent strategy impacts revenue, retention, and company performance.
- Ask to partner with other functions (finance, operations) to understand how talent decisions affect company profitability and strategy.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Stephen Quinn, CEO of Atomic, an insight led employer brand agency working with employer brand leaders at organisations including Salesforce, Heineken, Microsoft, L'Oreal, Primark and Ericsson.  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 2 Sep 2026
**Tags:** employer branding, evp, recruiting metrics, stakeholder management, ta leadership, recruitment marketing, hiring manager alignment, retention

**When to use this:** You lead a shrunk employer brand or talent marketing team and need to justify budget or headcount to finance or senior leadership with metrics they will actually act on.

**Big idea:** Employer brand leaders who keep winning investment are the ones who tie their work directly to specific business objectives and report impact in the language and format each stakeholder actually uses, rather than leading with engagement metrics that mean nothing to a CFO.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Before designing a campaign, find out what the business is trying to achieve at the macro, regional or role level (Quinn's examples: "we're setting up a new plant in Germany" or "we're developing a new AI centre of excellence") and build the programme to serve that specific objective, rather than creating campaigns in isolation from workforce planning.
- Match your reporting format to the stakeholder: Quinn notes HR tends to think in PowerPoint and finance thinks in spreadsheets, so when asking finance for budget, bring a detailed spreadsheet with ROI figures rather than a slide deck.
- Start any employer brand programme by establishing a baseline. Atomic's own diagnostic tool scores a company against 32 measures (self scored by the client plus external analysis) to surface where the organisation is actually weak, for example strong recruitment marketing content sitting alongside poor Glassdoor scores or weak onboarding.
- Build a two to three year, quarter by quarter roadmap off that baseline, prioritising the biggest gaps first, and track a dashboard that shows the starting point against current progress so impact is visible to leadership over time.
- To integrate employer brand across a company sitting in TA, actively build relationships with marketing, HR, comms, finance, sales and product rather than staying inside TA's remit, and consider inviting junior marketers or creatives to work on employer campaigns directly so they experience it and become allies when you need resources like a shoot day.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Quinn names cost per qualified lead, time to hire, reduction in candidate declines, referral rates, and ROI of long running investments like campus activation or event sponsorship as the metrics that carry weight with finance, in contrast to views, likes and social engagement figures.
- He notes many organisations have run the same investment, such as an annual campus sponsorship, for around 15 years without ever checking whether the metrics justify continuing it.

**Contrarian take:**
- Quinn argues you should not simply keep marketing at arm's length from employer brand work; bringing junior marketers or creatives directly into employer brand projects, even though it is not their usual customer facing work, is often the fastest way to build the internal relationships and goodwill needed to get support later.
- He also flags that unexplained candidate declines late in a pipeline are an underused signal worth investigating directly, since good candidates dropping out silently can point to a hiring manager problem, a broken candidate experience, or a competitor poaching from the same pool, not just bad luck.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before your next stakeholder update, swap out engagement metrics (views, likes, shares) for cost per qualified lead, time to hire or referral rate, whichever ties most directly to a number finance already tracks.
- Run a quick baseline check of your own employer brand assets against a handful of criteria (careers site, Glassdoor score, onboarding experience, referral programme uptake) to find your weakest link before your next campaign.
- If your reporting format has not changed in years, check that it matches what your specific audience wants: a spreadsheet for finance, a narrative deck for HR.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Reuben Ogbonna, Co-Founder and Executive Director of The Marcy Lab School, a one-year, financially accessible alternative to college in Brooklyn that has placed graduates into engineering and analyst roles at companies including Google, Apple, JPMorgan, The New York Times, Spotify and Pinterest  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 09 Sep 2026
**Tags:** early career talent, ai coding tools, ai upskilling, interview design, skills-based hiring, talent market data, human-ai collaboration, ai strategy

**When to use this:** you're designing or defending an entry-level hiring bar, or briefing hiring managers on why junior candidates need to be assessed differently now that AI writes the routine work.

**Big idea:** entry-level work is not disappearing, but the bar has jumped a full step: junior hires are now expected to manage and judge AI output the way a mid-level manager once managed a task, and that judgment takes far longer to develop than the technical skill it replaced.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Track a leading indicator instead of guessing: Ogbonna's school asks every employer partner "what percentage of your code that's being committed to your code bases have been written by individuals versus their AI agents," and has watched that answer shift from startups moving fast early on to "some of the country's largest banks or insurance companies... to public sector government agencies" now reporting the same default, that people aren't writing the code anymore, they're prompting agents to do it.
- Separate the task from the job when deciding what a role still requires. His framing: "the task is writing code, but really the job is producing software," so when an agent absorbs the task, the job becomes overseeing whether the output actually solves the right problem.
- Use the "code smell" test for AI output. Ogbonna compares it to knowing a colleague's email was written by AI: "the issue isn't that you can tell it's AI... the issue is that you can tell because it's missing something," a signal worth training junior staff to notice in code, copy or analysis rather than assuming AI output is finished work.
- Redesign entry-level interviews around open-ended, real-world problems that let candidates actually use AI tools during the process, rather than screening on GPA and pedigree, because those credentials are "clearly not as reflective of what the working world looks like today." His analogy for what you are really testing: managing AI is "like having an intern who was infinitely smart but had zero restraint," so the interview should surface whether the candidate can direct that capability toward the actual goal instead of chasing tangents.
- Rethink curriculum design using Bloom's Taxonomy: the top tiers, creation and evaluation, used to be the aspirational stretch goal for students. Now that AI handles the lower tiers (recall, replicating a known process), Ogbonna says hitting creation and evaluation is "a non-negotiable," meaning students must be able to compare multiple AI-generated implementations and judge which one best fits the business need and its risks.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- The Marcy Lab School is a 30-person team, six years old, operating on roughly a $7 million annual budget. Ogbonna's own example of the shift: pre-2024 it would never have made sense for a nonprofit that size to build a tech or IT function, but in 2026 hiring one or two AI-literate technologists would be "transformative" for the organisation, a hire delayed only by a tougher fundraising environment, not by lack of need.

**Contrarian take:**
- Ogbonna pushes back on the current "AI equals job cuts" narrative as a function of bad economic timing rather than the technology itself: "every other technological revolution... has been viewed through the lens of how do we enhance productivity," and he expects the same demand increase this time for software, design and accounting work, just delayed by today's cash-constrained environment.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Ask your own engineering or ops leaders what share of committed code (or completed work more broadly) is now AI-generated versus human-written, and track that number over the next two quarters as a leading indicator for how your entry-level hiring bar needs to shift.
- In your next junior-role interview, swap a take-home or trivia-style question for an open-ended real-world problem candidates can use AI to work through, and score how they direct and judge the AI's output rather than the output itself.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Matt Campbell, Managing Director at Alvarez & Marsal, where he leads the Talent, Organization & People practice advising on workforce strategy and organization design  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 11 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai strategy, ai agents, headcount planning, capacity planning, recruiting operations, human-ai collaboration, ta leadership

**When to use this:** your leadership is treating AI adoption as a headcount and cost question before anyone has actually redesigned how the work gets done, and pilots keep producing inconclusive results.

**Big idea:** most AI pilots fail because they are designed to prove the technology works rather than to solve a specific business problem, and cutting headcount too far, too fast based on AI capability creates "talent debt" that is far harder to repay than the cost savings were to gain.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Campbell's "five A's" model for how AI affects tasks: avoid (work that no longer needs doing at all), assist (AI as co-pilot, removing friction with little direct financial benefit), augment (an individual can "do you know three to five times more work" with AI driving it), automate (AI runs routine work end to end with humans handling exceptions), and autonomous (AI operates the whole workflow while humans govern outcomes).
- A job is "a bundle of tasks" (the work system) but a role is also part of the social system, how people connect and matter to each other and the organization, and AI can target the task system but "misses" the social system entirely, which is why redesigns that ignore roles and rhythms trigger fear and resistance even when the task-level case looks sound.
- Before pricing headcount impact, map where work is done, how much of it is disrupted by each of the five A's, and only then architect the roles that remain, rather than starting from a cost-takeout spreadsheet.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- "80% of AI projects fail," roughly twice the failure rate of ordinary IT projects, because organizations "treat it like any other technology implementation" instead of an organizational transformation
- "80% of employees in white collar roles are using AI that their IT team has never signed off on," so individuals get the assist-level benefit but the organization gets none of the commercial augmentation benefit and carries the data risk
- CEO churn: "234 CEOs of major corporates left their roles" last year, a "16% increase" year over year, with average tenure falling from "8 years a couple of years ago down to seven years" and expected to reach "the six year range this year"; when a CEO turns over there is "a 60% chance" a new HR leader follows

**Contrarian take:** Campbell argues that cutting headcount early and fast may look efficient "from an engineering perspective," but points to big tech companies now going through "a very slow process to rebuild capability back into their organizations" after cutting too aggressively, the same talent-debt pattern seen after RPA and ERP-driven technical debt.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before running your next AI pilot, write down the specific business problem it needs to solve, not just whether the technology works, so the pilot can produce a conclusive result either way.
- Map which of your team's tasks sit at each of the five A's (avoid, assist, augment, automate, autonomous) before any conversation about headcount, so the discussion starts from work design rather than cost.

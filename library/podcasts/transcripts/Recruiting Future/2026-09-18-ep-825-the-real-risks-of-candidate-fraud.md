## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Lauren Furey, Principal Product Manager at Proof, leading product work on candidate fraud and identity verification, drawing on live client fraud data across the hiring funnel.  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 18 Sep 2026
**Tags:** candidate fraud, deepfake candidates, candidate experience, recruiting operations, stakeholder management, recruiter workflow, rec tech

**When to use this:** Your remote hiring process still relies on a background check and recruiter intuition to catch a candidate who is not who they claim to be, including organised, funded identity fraud rather than simple CV exaggeration.

**Big idea:** A background check alone is no longer enough because organised operations, which Furey says primarily trace back to North Korea, use stolen identities, AI generated documents and proxy interviewers to get hired into remote roles for the paycheck and for access to source code, customer data and IP, so hiring teams need identity continuity built through the whole funnel, not one check near the end.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Timeline behind the convergence: hiring went remote from March 2020, "consumer grade deep fakes start to really accelerate" and become largely free from around 2022, with generative AI arriving on a similar timeline.
- The proxy interviewer mechanic: a candidate interviews at one stage, then "when we get to the skills assessment, I'm going to tag somebody else in," because "there's no through line for who are you expecting to meet with" once a new interviewer joins.
- Low effort fix: save the identity verification image into the ATS so every later interviewer has "a point of reference" for who they should be meeting.
- Higher sophistication fix: gate access to each interview, call or skills assessment behind a live selfie biometric match against that reference.
- Place identity verification "before either the hiring manager interview or a phone screen" rather than at application, so conversion holds and the candidate has a reason to comply.
- Three part response: talk internally to "your IT teams to your legal teams to a finance team your CHRO," test an identity verification vendor early in the funnel while watching conversion, then use that data to decide what carries through to onboarding.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- The DOJ "has put out notices to be on the lookout for these things," per Furey.
- Furey expects "a really big tick in 2027" in identity verification and identity continuity becoming an organic part of application and interview processes.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Standard identity verification flow: "can you let me scan your driver's license or another kind of documented credential," sometimes paired with "can I scan your face to kind of match it to that documented credential."

**Contrarian take:**
- Furey argues recruiters "over assume" how well their intuition can spot a deepfake or proxy candidate, while also insisting recruiters "are actually made for this" because of their interpersonal skill, so her fix adds a tool rather than replacing recruiter judgement.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Open a three way conversation with IT, legal, finance and your CHRO about where your funnel is exposed before building anything.
- Trial identity verification placed before the hiring manager interview or phone screen, not at application, and watch the effect on conversion.
- Save the candidate's verification photo to the ATS as the reference image every later interviewer checks against.

Note: Proof sponsors this episode and Furey is a Proof product leader selling identity verification into this exact problem, so read her fixes as vendor informed rather than independently benchmarked.

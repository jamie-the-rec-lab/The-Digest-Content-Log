## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Dave Vu, co-founder and COO of Ribbon, an AI interviewing platform for high-volume enterprise hiring, with 15 years in talent leadership before that, including as Ezra's first talent hire.  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 23 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai interviewing, candidate experience, high-volume hiring, quality of hire, retention, recruiter productivity, rec tech, global hiring, interview cheating

**When to use this:** you are weighing whether letting AI conduct the first candidate conversation will hurt candidate experience, and want the vendor-reported evidence before piloting AI interviewing at volume. This is a sponsored episode and the guest is Ribbon's own co-founder, so treat every figure below as vendor-reported, not independently audited.

**Big idea:** Vu argues candidates are not rejecting AI itself, they are rejecting slow and opaque hiring processes, and giving candidates instant, anytime access to an AI-led interview lifts satisfaction scores while compressing recruiter screening time.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Treat anytime AI interviewing as a pipeline expander, not just a convenience. Vu says 25% of Ribbon interviews happen between 10pm and 2am, and at client Randall Riley (drivers and technicians), director Matt O'Donnell reported that in one stretch nine of eleven hires came from candidates who interviewed outside normal working hours.
- When one role draws more candidates than you can screen live, have the hiring manager review AI interview outputs instead of scheduling calls. At Cartisa, which hires contractors and evaluators across roughly 20 languages, the hiring manager ran 43 Ribbon interviews in four weeks and hired 20 people from that pool, calling scale "the biggest value" against running 43 live calls himself.
- Brief the AI interviewer with role-specific technical context rather than generic questions. Vu cites a large automotive customer where the agent is fed detailed engineering context so it can ask role-specific qualifying questions, not generic screening ones.
- Judge an AI interviewer on whether it probes a candidate's actual answer with a follow-up rather than moving straight to the next scripted item. Vu frames this as the real test of natural conversation versus an old-style chatbot with a voice.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Ribbon reports candidate feedback ratings "above 4.5 to five" collected after each interview, with no scale or sample size given.
- For a customer described as "the world's largest leading automaker," Ribbon reports hiring about 35% faster and recruiters saving on average 22 hours of screen time per role.
- At Extra Space Storage, Vu says approximately 85% of hiring currently flows through Ribbon interviews, with 100% as Ribbon's stated goal, not yet reached.
- Vu reports 180-day post-hire retention has improved "roughly 10 to 30%, depending on the role" since customers adopted Ribbon, without stating across how many customers or roles this is measured.
- Some brands Ribbon works with receive 5 to 10 million applications a year, Vu's context for why speed matters to them.

**Contrarian take:**
- Vu: the "candidates hate AI" narrative is wrong. What candidates dislike is slow processes, unclear expectations and applying into silence, and an always-available AI interview fixes those specific complaints regardless of the AI itself.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Check what share of your own screening completions happen outside 9 to 6 before assuming candidates want live human scheduling.
- If piloting an AI interviewer, test it on a role with deep technical or role-specific questions and check it asks follow-ups rather than reading a fixed script.
- Measure any new screening method against a fixed post-hire retention window, such as 180 days, rather than time-to-fill alone.

## Episode description

AI is becoming more embedded across recruiting tools, but there is a meaningful difference between AI that automates workflows behind the scenes and AI that talks directly to candidates. When AI conducts the conversation, the stakes are higher; the first interaction shapes how candidates perceive the employer and the role. That is why many talent acquisition teams remain cautious about AI interviews, even as they adopt AI everywhere else. A growing number of enterprises are now running AI interviews at scale in high-volume hiring, and the data coming back on candidate experience and quality of hire improvements is difficult to ignore.



So what does that evidence show, and what should it mean for how the industry thinks about AI in hiring?



My guest this week is Dave Vu, Co-Founder and COO of Ribbon, who spent 15 years in talent and people leadership before co-founding the AI interview platform. In our conversation, Dave shares what enterprises are reporting from deploying AI interviews across thousands of candidates, how those candidates are responding, and what this could mean for the future of hiring.



In the interview, we discuss:


  •  The shift from AI experimentation to enterprise deployment

  •  The difference between back-office AI and candidate-facing AI

  •  Moving from chatbots to natural conversations

  •  The impact on the candidate experience

  •  Building candidate trust and AI interviewing

  •  Improving speed, efficiency and quality of hire

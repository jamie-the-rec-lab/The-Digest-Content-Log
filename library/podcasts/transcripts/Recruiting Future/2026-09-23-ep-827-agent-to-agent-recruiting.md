**When to use this:** you are weighing AI matching tools, worried about application and outreach noise, or designing a fast hiring process for a startup.

**Big idea:** using AI to speed up existing application and outreach processes adds noise on both sides, so matching has to start from a deep understanding of candidate and company rather than a CV and a job description.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Judge any AI tool by whether it improves the whole system. Matt Wilson said a tool that helps a candidate apply for 10,000 jobs, or one that spams a thousand candidates with generated messages, "contributing to a noisy system".
- Ask vendors about bias in three places: whether protected characteristics are stripped before data reaches the matching system, whether a third party audits it and whether the vendor publishes material you can check, such as a checklist or white paper.
- Do not use an LLM out of the box to shortlist. He said "LLM out of the box will display biases in their selection" if used for shortlists or screening.
- Keep humans for the decision. His view was that AI is "nowhere near sophisticated enough" to replace judgment about a manager, team and company, so the aim is to cut the top-of-funnel time and spend human time only in high-signal conversations.
- Run a startup process in days: one or two calls, then a paid full day working on real business problems with the team, ending in dinner. He said a very short cycle time lets you make an offer before competitors finish their process.

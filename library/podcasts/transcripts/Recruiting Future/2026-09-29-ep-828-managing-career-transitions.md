Kay (spelled in several ways in the transcript), global managing partner of the Enterprise Software and AI practice at Heidrick & Struggles, 20 plus years in executive search and co-author of Show Up to Win. Note he is promoting his own book and a Heidrick product.  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, 29 Sep 2026
**Tags:** executive search, candidate experience, interview design, reference checks, ai interviewing, recruiter career growth, talent market data, ta leadership

**When to use this:** you coach senior candidates or run executive hiring and need to know what leaders get wrong in transitions and where AI helps at that level.

**Big idea:** at VP level and above, hiring has become an executive dialogue with heavy diligence, and leaders lose roles by treating career management as something they do only when they want to move.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Treat a "no" to an unsolicited approach as the first step in a funnel. He said the usual reply is "I don't want that company, I don't want that job, I don't want to waste anybody's time", and argues the better play is to take the call to build a relationship with a search partner, CEO, board member or investor.
- Score each opportunity on three separate lenses to strip out emotion: quality of the company, quality of the role and quality of the offer. He said some leaders reach the end of a process and admit "I don't know if I actually even want it but I wanted to win".
- Coach candidates to lead with ideas rather than answer questions. He quoted Fidji Simo's formula for showing up: be clear about who you are, what you believe and how you plan to contribute, and bring ideas that show how you think.
- Use an LLM as an interview coach. Paste in the job description, company data, data on the interviewer and your own bio, then alternate between asking what questions this company and person would ask and what the most concise answer is. He warned it should give practice, not scripted answers.
- Never skip references because of familiarity. His example was an executive hired without references because the two had worked together before at a much larger company, and who was fired a year later.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- He said he has not seen AI shorten the time to hire an executive, because the bets are so big, and cited a private equity investor in the book calling them "billion-dollar bets".
- He said AI is used far more heavily for identifying, qualifying and assessing candidates earlier in career than at senior level.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Heidrick's new Hydro Immersive is an experiential assessment where leaders show how they think in real-time situations under pressure. He described it himself as a shameless plug.

**Contrarian take:**
- Kelly said people think search firms have a vested interest in "our candidate versus your candidate", whereas he says the firm wants the best leader in the shortest time, as the client defines best.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Ask a senior candidate who is about to decline an approach to take a short exploratory call instead, and log who they meet.
- Add a rule that no senior hire closes without references, however well the hiring manager knows the person.

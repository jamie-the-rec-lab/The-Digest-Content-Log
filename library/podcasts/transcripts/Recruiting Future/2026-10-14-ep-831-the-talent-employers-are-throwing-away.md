## Actionable summary
**Guest:** JC Townend, President of the Adecco Group for the UK and Ireland and CEO of LHH, the outplacement and redeployment business (she runs a vendor in this space, so the figures are from LHH's own surveys and clients)  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Future, Ep831, 14 Oct 2026 (sponsored by Vonq)
**Tags:** internal mobility, headcount planning, skills-based hiring, talent market data, ta leadership, recruiter career growth, retention

**When to use this:** your company is restructuring or cutting and then rehiring similar skills, or you want a case for hiring internally.

**Big idea:** Fire and rehire assumed a supply of perfectly qualified candidates, but with skills changing this fast the market delivers people who are maybe 70% to 80% qualified, which shifts the economics toward redeploying existing staff.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Map transferable skills across departments, not just within them. Her finding: teams know their own skills well, but there is almost no cross-department view, so a finance person is never considered for marketing.
- Replace the annual restructuring exercise with continuous workforce planning over 3 and 5 year horizons, deliberately upskilling people for the skills you will need.
- Coach internal movers to interview as rigorously as external candidates and to sell their transferable skills, so receiving managers are excited rather than resigned.
- Retire rules that block redundancy only if 60% of skills match another role, which she said made hiring managers feel they were forced to take a poor fit.
- Incentivise managers for growing talent that moves off their team, and offer a matrix of moves, not just a ladder.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- LHH survey: 87% of companies in the UK and Ireland intend to make redundancies in 2026 or already have.
- Redundancy reasons three or four years ago (her survey): about 40% over-hiring in boom times and 40% restructuring or M&A. Now about 20% cite AI, digital or automation and about 20% a skills mismatch.
- LHH supports more than 20,000 redundant people a year in the UK, and 60% of them move into an entirely new career. Eight years ago, redundancies came as 500 to 1,000 at once every five years; now many firms do smaller rounds every year.
- The report she referred to is LHH's redeployment and outplacement trends report, "the mobility breakdown" (linked in the show notes).

**Contrarian take:**
- She said much of the AI blame is partial: about 20% of redundancies are attributed to AI, and skills mismatch and over-hiring explain as much.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before approving an external requisition, list two internal people from other departments whose skills match 70% or more of the role and run them through the same screening.

## Episode description

Redundancies have become a constant feature of the talent market. AI is taking a lot of the blame, but the drivers behind this are much broader. Jobs and skills are changing faster than most organizations can keep track of and the conventional response is to restructure the workforce and then hire in the new skills that are needed. That approach relies on a ready supply of perfectly qualified candidates, and in a fast-moving skills market those candidates are increasingly hard to find. It also assumes that employers already have a clear view of the skills and potential within their existing workforce, which is rarely the case.



So what does a smarter approach to restructuring look like?



My guest this week is JC Townend (https://www.linkedin.com/in/jeannetownend/) , President of The Adecco Group  (https://www.linkedin.com/company/theadeccogroup/) for the UK and Ireland and CEO of LHH, the Adecco Group business that supports individuals through redundancy. In the UK alone, LHH works with more than 20,000 people a year who have been made redundant, which gives JC a rare view of both the decisions employers are making and where the people affected end up next. In our conversation, she explains what is really driving the current wave of restructuring, why the financial case for replacing people rather than redeploying them has changed, and how the best employers are rethinking workforce planning.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Vanessa Raath, global talent sourcing trainer and AI literacy coach  |  **Show:** Recruiting Roadshow, 10 Apr 2026
**Tags:** sourcing, ai sourcing, ai upskilling, recruiter training, global hiring, candidate fraud, recruitment marketing, recruiter career growth

**When to use this:** You're adopting GenAI tools for sourcing but your team doesn't know how to validate the output, or you're asking what metrics actually matter when AI frees up recruiter time.

**Big idea:** GenAI hallucinates constantly; foundational sourcing skills are not obsolete but more critical than ever, and the real ROI of AI adoption is not time saved but how recruiters spend that saved time building human relationships.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Vanessa's "University of YouTube" model: younger recruiters must self-invest in learning foundational Boolean and sourcing fundamentals rather than waiting for company investment, because mastering basic skills lets them validate and repair what GenAI produces
- Change management is the most underrated part of AI rollout; many implementations fail in first three months not because tools are bad but because adoption is handled poorly and people fear displacement
- Stop measuring "time to hire" and "calls made per day" as your north star. Measure instead how much time recruiters now spend with hiring managers, candidates, building personal brand, and creating community
- Personal brand is the trust signal that candidates recognise in crowded inboxes; when a candidate sees your name and knows your reputation, they respond, whilst AI-signed outreach goes to spam
- Verify candidates first (candidates with verified documents floated to top of stack) because high-volume screening means you'll naturally review a threshold of applications and stop, so make sure the ones you reach are real
- RecOps teams should recruit from experienced sourcers because they understand both process and the domain nuances that let them evaluate which tool is right for which problem

**Data & benchmarks:**
- A recent graduate applied to 380 roles and received zero responses, reflecting a decades-long candidate experience problem that predates AI
- One technician worked 13 simultaneous roles (including as a solution architect at Boeing) undetected, underscoring why identity verification at application is now a necessity

**Contrarian take:**
- Vanessa argues against the assumption that younger generations adopt GenAI faster; they may write prompts well but cannot critique outputs without foundational knowledge, leaving senior practitioners in stronger position
- Change management, not tool capability, determines success or failure of AI adoption

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Ask your team: what do recruiters do with the time AI has freed up? If the answer is still administrative work, your AI adoption is incomplete
- Build a personal brand post this month on a platform where your target candidates spend time (LinkedIn, TikTok, etc.); trust will drive response rates where recruiter emails alone won't
- For one high-volume role, implement candidate verification first, then screen only verified applicants, and measure your quality-of-candidate metric

## Episode description

Vanessa Raath, global talent sourcing trainer turned AI literacy coach, joins the show from San Diego to share her perspective on where recruiting is headed. Drawing on her experience training teams across South Africa, Europe, and the US, Vanessa challenges the assumption that AI adoption is more advanced in certain markets, and makes the case that foundational sourcing skills matter more than ever in an era of hallucinating LLMs. The conversation spans the evolution of recruiting operations, the metrics that actually matter (hint: it's about how AI helps recruiters be more human), candidate fraud, personal branding as a trust signal, and why change management is the most underrated part of any AI rollout. Vanessa closes with a vision she calls "recruiter for love" — the idea that the best recruiters build relationships for a lifetime, and no AI will get between a great recruiter and that bond.

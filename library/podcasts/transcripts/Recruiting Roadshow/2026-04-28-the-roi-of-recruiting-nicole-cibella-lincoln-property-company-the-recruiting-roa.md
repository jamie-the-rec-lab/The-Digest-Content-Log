## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Nicole Cibella, Lincoln Property Company  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Roadshow, 28 Apr 2026

**Tags:** recruiting metrics, recruiting operations, quality of hire, recruiter productivity, roi

**When to use this:** You need to build a business case for your recruiting investments and want to learn what metrics matter for ROI conversations with finance and leadership.

**Big idea:** Recruiting ROI isn't about cost-per-hire, it's about hire quality, retention, and ramp time. Cost matters, but a cheap bad hire costs more than an expensive great hire.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Track quality of hire from day one. Grade new hires at 30, 60, 90 days against role expectations. Correlate back to which recruiting source, recruiter, or screening method found them.
- Build a cost model: what does a bad hire cost (severance, replacement, lost productivity)? What does a great hire earn (revenue contribution, retention, referrals)? Use these to justify recruiting investment.
- Measure speed to productivity, not speed to hire. A hire who takes 3 months to ramp costs more than one who ramps in 6 weeks, even if the second one took longer to find.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Great hires outperform average hires by 30-50 percent in productivity. Bad hires cost 1.5-2x their annual salary in replacement and lost time.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Pull your last 20 new hires. Grade them against role expectations at 90 days. Identify which recruiting source produced the highest percentage of strong performers.
- Calculate your true cost of a bad hire: severance, replacement recruiting costs, lost productivity, training time. Use that number in your next ROI conversation.

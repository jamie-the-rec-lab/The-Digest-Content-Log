## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Katie Wagner, TBD Collective  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Roadshow, 3 Aug 2026
**Tags:** quality of hire, recruiting metrics, recruiter productivity, recruiting operations, stakeholder management

**When to use this:** When you're chasing "quality of hire" metrics but they're not actually making hiring better.

**Big idea:** Quality of hire metrics are broken because they come too late and are too fuzzy. Measure what actually predicts success: job fit, hiring manager satisfaction, candidate preparation.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Shift from backward-looking (how did the hire work out after 12 months) to forward-looking (how ready was this candidate on day one?). The latter drives behaviour change now.
- Ask hiring managers one specific question 30 days in: "Did this person come ready for this role?" That's predictive and actionable. Compare against candidates who came unprepared.
- Link interview signals to 30-day readiness. Which interview questions predicted candidates who were ready? Which didn't? Refine your interview guide based on that.

**Contrarian take:**
- Most quality of hire metrics measure the wrong things (tenure, promotion velocity). They also measure too late (6-12 months) to be useful for changing hiring decisions.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Send a quick survey to hiring managers of your last 10 hires: "On a scale of 1-5, how ready was this person on day one for the specific job?" Compare against your interview assessments.
- Review one hire who felt unprepared. Where did your process miss something? Update your interview guide based on that gap.

Katie Wagner makes the case for revenue per employee over quality of hire as the metric that actually holds recruiting accountable, and explains why most hiring managers can't define the problem they're hiring for until a recruiter helps them get there.

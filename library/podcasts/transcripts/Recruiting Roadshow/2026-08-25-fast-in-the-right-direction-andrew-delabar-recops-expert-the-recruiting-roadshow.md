## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Andrew Delabar, RecOps expert, formerly overseeing field operations recruiting at Peloton  ·  **Show:** The Recruiting Roadshow, 25 Aug 2026
**Tags:** recruiting operations, recruiting metrics, structured interviewing, sourcing, human-ai collaboration, ai agents, recruiter workflow

**When to use this:** your hiring process has steps nobody has questioned in years, or leadership wants a dashboard that proves recruiting is working, but the current metrics do not actually change anyone's behavior.

**Big idea:** data is not the answer, it is the question, and every stage in a hiring process has to earn its place with a measurable outcome or it should be cut, no matter how long it has existed.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Every process step has to justify itself with data: Delabar was skeptical of the hiring manager screen after seeing reschedule and decline rates of "60% 70%" and pass-through rates as high as "85%", concluding that if 8 of 10 candidates pass a stage, it "just wasted five days for you to agree with the recruiter" rather than adding a distinct signal.
- Track reschedule rates, decline rates, no-show rates, time between interviews, and outcome distribution (are you always saying yes?) rather than leaning on time-to-fill, since time-to-fill is "the result of a bunch of other much more interesting metrics," not a metric in itself.
- Cites Goodhart's law via anthropologist Marilyn Strathern: "the second that a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a quality measure," so setting a hard quota (e.g. "15 screens") pushes recruiters toward the path of least resistance rather than quality.
- On AI's role: let it handle "the boring work," such as drip campaign reminders, content generation, note-taking support and surfacing passive candidates, while a human still decides whether a candidate is qualified or worth advancing.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- "The best talent in the market is available for 3 days after they're aware of your company" before they are gone, which argues for compressing time between application review and the hiring manager stage
- A Google PhD researcher's finding, cited by Delabar: "you can make a confident hire at 86% confidence after four interviews," and "every subsequent interview afterwards is 1% of confidence," an argument against adding extra interview rounds for reassurance

**Contrarian take:** "A recruiter who's fast and directionless is slow." Speed without a clear operating philosophy is treated by the industry as proficiency, but Delabar argues it is a vanity signal recruiters use "to justify our existence."

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Pull reschedule, decline, no-show and pass-through rates for your hiring manager screen stage specifically and ask whether it is adding a distinct signal or just re-confirming the recruiter's call.
- Stop reporting time-to-fill on its own; break it into app-to-screen, app-to-reject and time-between-interviews so the number actually points to where to intervene.

Andrew Delabar is a RecOps Expert — and on this episode of Recruiting Roadshow, he joins Brandon Jeffs to unpack what it actually takes to build a hiring process people trust.

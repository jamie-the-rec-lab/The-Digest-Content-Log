## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Clifton Jadoo II, former Staff Technical Recruiter at Robinhood who has also recruited for Plaid, Hinge, Webflow, Foursquare and Compass  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Roadshow, 1 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai sourcing, ai strategy, recruiting operations, structured interviewing, candidate experience, recruiter productivity, ta leadership, candidate fraud

**When to use this:** you need a clear line between what AI should take off a recruiter's plate and what still needs a human, or your process is breaking as headcount scales past 100 hires.

**Big idea:** AI removes the administrative load of recruiting, such as scheduling and market research, but the close still depends on reading a candidate's hesitation and motivation, which a chatbot cannot pick up.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Map your own workflow, note what you dislike doing, and automate that specifically; Clifton automated scheduling and built a simple Claude Code skill that researches well funded companies in a target location and drafts a Boolean search ready to paste into LinkedIn.
- On a first candidate call, listen for tone and hesitation rather than taking a stated "yes I'm willing to move" at face value, because AI accepts that answer literally while a recruiter can probe it further.
- To scale past 100 hires without calibration drifting, replace one off interview processes with fixed structure (a defined loop: recruiter screen, hiring manager screen, onsite) and scorecards that force interviewers to write down why a candidate was a yes.
- Treat AI as a tool, not a strategy: adding a tool on top of a process that lacks structure just reproduces the same garbage in different form, faster.
- Push back on requests with a proposed solution attached rather than raising an issue alone, so the pushback becomes a starting point rather than friction.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Inbound application success rate runs at roughly 2.5%, versus 20 to 30% for a good technical recruiter's cold outbound.
- A resume can now be generated from a job description with a single prompt, so a well written resume with the right action verbs, once a weak but real signal, is no longer a useful signal at all.

**Contrarian take:**
- Clifton argues recruiters should stop treating AI adoption as a threat to relevance, because Excel and PowerPoint did not eliminate finance and admin roles, they made people more productive and changed how work was measured, and he expects the same for recruiting.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Write down your own workflow, mark the one task you dislike most, and look for an AI or simple automation fix for that single task first.
- Add one identity verification step earlier in your pipeline, such as email code confirmation, before investing recruiter time in inbound applicants.
- Start every pushback on a hiring manager or process decision with a proposed alternative, not just the objection.

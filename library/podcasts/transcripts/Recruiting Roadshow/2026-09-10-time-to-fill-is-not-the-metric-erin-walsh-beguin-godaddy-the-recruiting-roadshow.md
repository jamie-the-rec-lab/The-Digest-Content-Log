## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Erin Walsh-Beguin, Sr Director of Global TA Operations & Employer Brand at GoDaddy, who has run RecOps and employer brand as one blended function over eight years  ·  **Show:** The Recruiting Roadshow, 10 Sep 2026
**Tags:** recruiting operations, recruiting metrics, employer branding, candidate fraud, candidate experience, ai screening, human-ai collaboration

**When to use this:** you are deciding which dashboard metrics actually signal recruiting health, or you need to add fraud and identity checks without wrecking the candidate experience.

**Big idea:** time-to-fill is only a health metric, not the signal that recruiting is working; the real indicators are the conversion rates between funnel stages, especially from the initial TA screen onward, because those trace directly back to whether a recruiter understands what the business actually needs.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Trace conversion stage by stage back to the TA screen: if TA screening is efficient, candidates who pass should have the skills, knowledge and experience to keep moving, so a weak pass-through rate downstream is a signal to check the screening quality upstream, not just the process design.
- Recruiters gain a strategic-advisor seat by improving shortlist quality before the role even opens: partnering with the hiring team to understand team structure and where to source regionally, then doing an early check-in after the first five interviews ("Are we on the mark? Are we off the mark?") while there's still time to adjust.
- On candidate fraud, moving stop-gaps like identity checks earlier in the funnel trades one risk for another: catching a fraud flag late means restarting the search after time was already invested, but flags too early risk creating bias, so GoDaddy treats each flag as "one of many data points" rather than an automatic reject.
- The highest-value AI use, in her view, runs from offer through onboarding (a stage recruiters often neglect because they've already moved to the next requisition), plus interview scheduling across global calendars, while candidate-facing communication needs care because people notice when a message "sounds so personal that the recruiter absolutely did not send that."

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Dashboard metrics GoDaddy actually tracks for executives: candidates-to-hire conversion, offer acceptance rate, renegotiation percentage and where offers land in the compensation range, alongside time-to-fill as a background health reference rather than a headline number

**Contrarian take:** "Possibly an unpopular opinion. I don't think it's time to fill" is the metric that shows recruiting is working; Walsh-Beguin treats it as one health signal among several rather than the topline measure most teams default to.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Replace your team's time-to-fill headline metric with stage-by-stage conversion rates traced back to the TA screen, and use a dip in any one stage to ask what's happening upstream rather than downstream.
- Before opening a requisition, run a short calibration check after the first five interviews to confirm the recruiter and hiring manager are aligned, rather than waiting until the shortlist is built.

Erin Walsh-Beguin, Sr Director of Global TA Operations & Employer Brand at GoDaddy, joins the Recruiting Roadshow to talk about what happens when you run recruiting operations and employer brand as one function.

Over eight years at GoDaddy, Erin has built a RecOps practice that treats employer brand as a data problem as much as a creative one. In this episode, she and host Brandon Jeffs cover the metrics that actually signal recruiting is working, why candidate experience is the most fragile part of the system right now, how to add fraud and identity checks without adding friction, and the difference between a recruiter who takes orders and one who advises the business.

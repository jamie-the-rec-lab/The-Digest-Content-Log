**Guest:** Adrian Russo, VP of Talent at Swish Analytics, a sports analytics company. He has built talent and people functions from scratch four times, led hiring inside the US Department of Defense and the National Institutes of Health, ran the 2,000 person hiring programme behind the first digital census, and is an Army veteran who deployed to Iraq with the 82nd Airborne.  ·  **Show:** Recruiting Roadshow, 22 Sep 2026
**Tags:** candidate fraud, recruiting metrics, capacity planning, outbound sourcing, recruitment marketing, rec tech, recruiter workflow, ta leadership, stakeholder management

**When to use this:** Use this when your pipeline suddenly floods with strong looking resumes, or when you need to make candidate fraud someone else's budget line rather than recruiting's alone.

**Big idea:** Funnel math is what lets you forecast hiring and also what first tips you off to fraud, and candidate fraud itself is a business and security risk that happens to surface in recruiting rather than a recruiting failure to quietly absorb.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Track funnel math at company, department and team level, submit to fill ratios, nurture campaign conversion rates, test pass rates, then feed that data to an LLM to find where and why candidates convert or drop off and refine templates from it. Russo: "if I know what my funnel math looks like... I could tell you exactly how many people I need to contact to get a position filled by this time."
- Know your normal applicant volume per role type so a spike is visible. His example: a full stack engineer role that usually draws around 500 applicants, where an unexplained jump to around a thousand resumes with no campaign change "should light off alarm bells."
- Add cheap rapport-building geography questions to phone screens (where are you from, what part of the city, where do you like to go) and treat answers that "don't add up" as a red flag worth escalating before you invest more screening time.
- Layer fraud checks through the whole funnel instead of only at the background check: cheap upfront signal checks early (resume and phone creation dates, reuse across other resumes, IP or exit node tracing), biometric ID verification in the middle of the stack, and traditional criminal or federal background checks only at the end.
- Fund fraud detection from the talent budget but do not own the response alone. Loop in the executive sponsor and whoever owns security and infrastructure from the start, because otherwise "it's not going to be taken seriously."
- For search and answer engine visibility, publish a steady drip of niche technical content, his example is a daily blog post on a specific machine learning technique, drafted from an LLM baseline and refined by hand, so the company surfaces whenever a target candidate searches that topic.
- Combine a verified org account on X, which comes with its own ad credit tiers, with tight subreddit targeting (his example is a subreddit for RAG when hiring for large language model roles) for cheap, high precision reach.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Russo's working tripwire: roughly 500 applicants is typical for a full stack engineer role at his company, versus an unexplained jump to roughly a thousand as the signal to investigate.
- Russo's own figure, source unstated: "70% of candidates will go to one of these LLMs to research" which companies are active in their field before applying.
- Russo's figure for paid reach: around a hundred dollars on X or Reddit ads can get "hundreds of thousands of views for a targeted audience."

**Contrarian take:**
- Russo: "it's not a recruiting problem, it's a business problem," even though fraud surfaces at the point of application. He names third party nation state actors (North Korea, China, and Iran "to a much lesser extent") as the source, says they want IP and system access rather than a paycheck, and cites an unnamed but "very well known and reputable security company" where a North Korean candidate passed screening, background check and I-9 verification, then uploaded malware before being caught.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Pull last quarter's applicant volume by role family and write down what "normal" looks like, so a fraud-driven spike is obvious on sight.
- Add two or three specific geography follow-up questions to your phone screen script and treat vague or inconsistent answers as an escalation trigger, not just a note.
- Start a weekly niche technical blog cadence targeted at what your candidates actually search for, drafted from an LLM baseline and edited by a human before it posts.

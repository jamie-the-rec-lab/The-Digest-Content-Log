## Full report

As a YC startup ourselves, the startup community remains one of the most meaningful segments we serve. Today, we're proud to support 52% of the fastest-growing startups on Brex's 2025 Benchmarks list, giving us a unique vantage point into how early-stage teams hire, scale, and adapt.

Startups often move first. With fewer constraints than enterprise organizations, startup talent leaders are frequently the earliest adopters of new tooling and workflows, experimenting with AI long before it becomes standard practice. This report begins by examining how AI is taking shape, then follows the hiring lifecycle end-to-end from inbound and sourcing through interviews and offer acceptance.

This edition focuses specifically on startups: over 1,200 venture-backed startups globally, covering 32K hires and 11M applications, segmented by company size (less than 25 employees; 25-49; 50-99; 100-300). Organizations larger than 300 employees were excluded. Most data is from 2025.

Preview of the data:
- AI adoption is no longer fringe: over half of startup talent teams already use AI across multiple hiring workflows.
- Remote roles materially change hiring dynamics, driving higher application volume and stronger offer acceptance rates.
- Startups that involve recruiters earlier hire meaningfully faster, cutting time to hire by nearly a third at the smallest stages.

## II. The Rise of AI

In 2025, Pitchbook reported AI startups made up 64% of all U.S. venture capital by dollars invested.

### The Rise of the AI Startup
Over the past two years, ".ai" domains increased 11% from just 5% of startups in 2023 to 16% by the end of 2025.

### The Rise of AI in Job Descriptions
Across 11M startup job applications: the percent of jobs with "AI" in the title doubled (from 2% to 4%), while titles with "data" remained steady at 3%. Mentions of AI appear in roughly a third of job postings (33%). Tech roles mention AI more often, though both tech and business increases are significant.

### The Rise of AI in Recruiting Workflows
Every quarter over the past two years, there's a steady increase in the percentage of startup customers using AI, with over half (60%) using AI in Q3 2025. AI usage is distributed across the hiring lifecycle rather than concentrated in one area. Across the 60% of startup customers using AI, most use one or two features regularly, while 10% use 5+ features actively. As startups grow in size, they use AI more in their recruiting workflows.

## III. Inbound & Sourcing

A 2025 analysis on source of hire observed a dumbbell pattern for startups: some leaned heavily on inbound while others did not; when inbound was low, sourcing dominated.

### Source of Hire
The most hires at a startup come from inbound, followed by sourced. Referrals make up slightly fewer hires at startups (15%) than all company sizes (18%). Smaller orgs focus much more on sourcing, supplementing with referrals as they grow.

### Sourcing
Sourcing was consistently the second most common source of startup hires; most dramatic for the smallest startups, who see 30% of hires come from sourcing. A 2024 sourcing analysis found email open rates around 86%. Startup customers experience an ~81% email open rate, with reply rates on par with the all-company average at 19%.

## III. Application Volume

Looking at applications per hire, the smallest startups receive the fewest applications. Across all sizes, inbound application volumes for startups are increasing similarly to all companies since 2021. Ashby supports teams with AI Assisted Application Review and Candidate Fraud Detection.

### Applications for Remote Jobs
Remote startup jobs received a material 42% more inbound applications than in-office startup jobs, consistent across startup sizes. Of the database: 44% of venture-backed startups using Ashby have almost all jobs with remote options; 35% have no full remote option; 21% provide both. Most (79%) go all in on in-person requirement or no in-person requirement.

## IV. The Time Cost of a Hire

Across all groups, for every hire made, 15 applicants receive an interview (18 per technical hire, 13 per business hire).

### Interviewer Hours
Larger startups tend to have more interviewer hours per hire at ~29 hours. Interviewer hours are relatively flat for business roles but scale with size for technical roles. Smaller startups (<25) clock ~21 interviewer hours per technical hire while larger (100-300) clock ~29 hours.

### Candidate Interview Hours
A hired candidate at a startup spends between 2.5 to 3 hours interviewing. (2025 Recruiter Productivity report benchmark: 2.5 to 3.5 hours across all sizes.) Technical candidates undergo a longer process; there's about a two-hour difference between the 25th and 75th percentile.

## V. Offer Acceptance Rates

The 2024 Offer Acceptance Rates report found a stable 81% OAR across all company sizes. Startup-specific acceptance rates hover around 80%.

### Acceptance Rates for Remote Roles
Remote roles see a 9% higher offer acceptance rate over in-office roles: remote technical roles 13% higher, remote business roles 4% higher. At startups with fewer than 25 roles, technical roles see no difference. The share of venture-backed startup jobs with remote options slightly declined from ~80% in 2023 to ~60% in 2025.

## VI. The Teams Behind Startup Hiring

Data is analyzed by what percentage of jobs with a hire have a recruiter or hiring manager associated. Hiring managers play a key role; as startups grow, more recruiters are hired.

### Time to Hire
For smaller startups (<25 employees), time to hire drops by almost 30% when a recruiter is involved. For larger startups, jobs with a recruiter involved consistently have a faster time to hire, but the difference is less striking.

### Hiring Team Size
Larger startups often have double the number of talent-team people involved than smaller startups. Average number of talent team members involved in hiring (excluding hiring managers): <25 employees: 4.5 people; 25-49: ~5.1; 50-99: ~6.1; 100-300: ~8.1.

## Closing Thoughts
Startup hiring in 2025 was defined by scale and speed. AI is already embedded across sourcing, screening, and coordination. Remote roles continue to reshape candidate behavior. Startups that invest earlier in recruiting support move faster, reduce interviewer load, and create more momentum. Benchmarks come from over 1,200 venture-backed startups.

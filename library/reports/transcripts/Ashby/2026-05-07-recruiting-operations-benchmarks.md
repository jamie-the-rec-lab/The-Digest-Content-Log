## Full report

Hiring is often measured through a handful of familiar metrics such as Time to Fill, Time to Hire, or passthrough rates. However, these metrics are outputs of a much larger system. This report takes a Recruiting Operations perspective on hiring performance and breaks down different parts of the system, from how long it takes to fill a role, to how candidates move through interviews, to how source strategy shapes outcomes. It analyses over 54M applications and 93K jobs from January 2021 through March 2026.

Report highlights: hiring becomes more predictable as systems mature, with Time to First Fill stabilising as organisations scale; Time to Hire is shaped by many small delays across the process rather than a single bottleneck; automated scheduling methods are 26% faster than manual scheduling; application source meaningfully impacts funnel performance, with referred candidates seeing notably higher passthrough at nearly every stage; process discipline directly impacts both data quality and candidate experience, particularly in feedback and archiving.

### I. Time to First Fill
Time to First Fill is the time from a job opening to the first hire, and it works as a measure of system predictability, shaped by organisational scale and role complexity. Smaller organisations show a wide distribution: startups can move very quickly but also risk hiring efforts dragging on. As organisations grow, variability narrows and timelines standardise. Even around ~100 employees Time to First Fill begins to stabilise, and Ashby typically sees the first RecOps hires made between 200 and 500 employees. Senior roles take 37% longer to fill than junior positions, and technical roles require roughly 15 more days than business roles.

### II. Where Time is Spent in Hiring
Time to Hire is the sum of many steps, and is not driven by a single bottleneck but by smaller delays across four stages: application review, scheduling, feedback and decision-making, and offer approvals. Review is often the first delay, especially for high-volume technical roles; Ashby positions AI-assisted application review as the fix. Scheduling splits into total scheduling duration and schedule lead time, with lead time usually the larger bottleneck due to limited interviewer availability. Feedback and decision-making: the gap between final interview and decision is one of the most significant delays; AI Notetaker raises scorecard submission ~9% within 5 hours and ~6% within 24 hours (70% vs 66%). Offer approvals (offer turnaround) involve multiple stakeholders; templates and automated approvals reduce friction.

### III. Interviews
Scheduling method matters: Ashby Auto Schedule confirms interviews 26% faster than manual, a median 3.7 hours versus 5 hours. Interview structure varies by function: technical candidates take ~18 days across the loop, business ~14 days. Around 13% of hires included a take-home, climbing in business roles from ~8% at low volume to over 20% at the highest, while technical stays flat at 10-14%. Passthrough rates vary by stage: recruiter screens ~35%, post-onsite 95%, offer 81%. Around 38% of scorecard pairs include at least a one-point difference between interviewers, and nearly half of those cross the 2-to-3 yes/no threshold on Ashby's 1-4 scale. Scorecard completion runs ~49% at under-25-employee teams and ~72% at 500+.

### IV. Funnel Strategy: The Impact of Referrals
Referrals consistently outperform other sources: 52% of referred candidates pass initial screens versus 35% overall, and referrals have higher offer acceptance than inbound or sourced across both business and technical roles, with the gap most notable in technical hiring. Referrals convert best when the referrer works in the same job function as the role; out-of-function referrals carry less signal. Sourced candidates perform similarly across many stages, indicating outbound can produce strong outcomes when executed well.

### V. Archiving
Time to Archive signals data quality, process discipline and candidate experience. Non-interviewed candidates left unarchived submit lower NPS: a median of 9 if archived within one day, 7 by days 10-24, and 3 by 50+ days, with scores holding steady through the first nine days then falling sharply after three weeks. Over five years Time to Archive has been stable at a median ~6 days for non-interviewed and ~21 days for interviewed candidates. Sourced candidates archive on a flat 14-17 day cadence whether interviewed or not, likely driven by outbound campaign cadence. Roughly 58% of non-interviewed candidates are archived within the first 9 days, but a spike at 150+ days shows candidates falling through the cracks.

### Conclusion
Across 54M applications and 93K jobs, hiring becomes more predictable as organisations scale, Time to Hire is shaped by smaller delays across every stage rather than one bottleneck, interview design and job function significantly affect efficiency, and referrals consistently outperform other sources on both passthrough and offer acceptance. The focus for RecOps shifts from optimising individual steps to designing a cohesive system balancing efficiency, signal quality and candidate experience.

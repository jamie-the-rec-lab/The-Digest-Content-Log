## Full report

**Headline.** "US staffing hours were up 7% compared to a year ago, adjusted for holiday impact," with hours remaining at their year-to-date high for the week ending September 5, 2026. Average weekly hours worked per worker came in at 35.1 hours, down slightly from 35.6 the previous week.

**Segment breakdown (year-over-year).** Commercial staffing hours are "up 8% y/y" and sitting at peak 2026 levels. Professional staffing hours are "up 6% y/y, adjusted for holiday impact," near their 2026 peak. Light Industrial hours are "up 12% y/y," the highest level of 2026. IT staffing hours are up 3% year-over-year, a deceleration from the 5-6% growth rates seen in recent months. Office/Clerical is the only segment in decline, "down 7% year-over-year" and "substantially below 2025 levels."

**Occupational hours.** Average weekly hours for industrial occupations ran at 36.3 hours; for IT occupations, 37.3 hours.

**Analyst commentary.** "Industrial staffing has benefitted this year from increased demand from the manufacturing and logistics sectors, as well as demand related to data centers. Demand for professional staffing has come from clients moving forward on projects that had previously been paused, as well as from new projects related to AI readiness."

**Broader labor-market context.** The report notes that US temporary help employment "has grown an average of roughly 8,500 jobs each month from January through August," a contrast with the decline pattern seen across 2023-2025.

**Methodology.** Data reflects "weekly hours worked by temporary workers across a sample of staffing companies in the US that utilize Bullhorn's technology solutions," weighted and benchmarked against US Bureau of Labor Statistics data. The indicator updates weekly; the last four weeks of any given pull are preliminary and subject to revision.

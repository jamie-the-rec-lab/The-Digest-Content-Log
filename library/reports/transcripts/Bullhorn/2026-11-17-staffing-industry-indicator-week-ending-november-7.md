### Introduction
Released weekly, the SIA | Bullhorn Staffing Indicator comprises two sets of analyses; a Year-over-year comparison showing how the week that ended ten days ago (“last week measured”) compares to the same week 12 months previously and an indexed value that has been benchmarked against data from the week ending January 13th, 2019.
In addition, the Indicator provides an overview of the number of hours by worker during the last week measured and a comparison to the same week a year ago.

### Main Indicator Trends for the week ending November 7th
The US Staffing indexed value was 109 for the week ending November 7th (“last week measured”). US Staffing hours were up 11% compared to a year ago and stable (0.1%) on a week-over-week sequential basis.
The Commercial Staffing indexed value was 90 for the same week. Commercial hours were up 13% compared to a year ago; and were stable (0.2%) on a week-over-week sequential basis.
The Professional Staffing indexed value was 128 for the same week. Professional hours were up 9% compared to a year ago; and were stable (-0.2%) on a week-over-week sequential basis.

### Staffing Industry Analysts’ Perspective
US Staffing hours were up 11% compared to a year ago in the week ending November 7th and remained at their year-to-date high. Commercial hours were up 13% y/y while Professional hours were up 9% compared to a year ago. On a skill segment level, Industrial occupation hours were up 18% y/y. IT occupation hours were up 4% y/y, and Office/Clerical hours were down 8% y/y.
Looking at the month-to-month trend so far this year, both Commercial and Professional staffing hours have displayed a positive sequential growth trend. This increasing trend is directionally reflected in the US Bureau of Labor Statistics’ temporary help services employment levels (up 74,600 jobs year-to-date) and also by the American Staffing Association’s ASA Staffing Index.
Industrial staffing hours have shown an impressive ramp starting in late April and continuing through October, as demand has returned from clients in manufacturing and logistics, as well as areas related to data center construction. Improving conditions among manufacturing clients is consistent with monthly data from the ISM Manufacturing Purchasing Managers Index (PMI) that show ten consecutive months of overall expansion, and four straight months of growth in the Employment Index. The Industrial Indicator levels are now higher than they were one, two, and three years ago at this time of year. More insight on the industrial segment can be found in our Industrial Staffing Growth Assessment report.
We believe professional staffing hours are being lifted this year by increasing demand for temporary IT and healthcare professionals, and also engineering, life science and education professionals. Demand for IT staffing remains solid as clients across many industries move forward on projects related to digital transformation and AI. Healthcare staffing volumes have ramped up across the second and third quarters of this year as health system budgets have improved, and in relation to labor union contract negotiations.
The average weekly hours worked per worker was 35.5 hours during the week ending November 7th, stable on the previous week, and up on the same week of the previous year (35.2). On average, Industrial occupations worked 36.7 hours per week while IT occupations averaged 37.7 hours.

### About the SIA | Bullhorn Staffing Indicator
The SIA | Bullhorn Staffing Indicator is a unique tool for gauging near real time weekly trends in the volume of temporary staffing delivered by US staffing firms. Each week the Indicator reports data for the week that ended ten days prior to the release. It reflects weekly hours worked by temporary workers across a sample of staffing companies in the US that utilize Bullhorn’s technology solutions. The Indicator is weighted and benchmarked against US Bureau of Labor Statistics data to approximate the composition of the staffing industry by skill. While the indicator does not presume to perfectly reflect the entire universe of US staffing firms, it does represent a sizable sample of the US staffing industry, reflecting a wide range of occupations, client industry verticals, and geographic footprint that spans the country.
The Indicator can be used by staffing firms to benchmark their past and current performance, as well as a tool for forecasting near term industry trends and outlook.

### Bullhorn summary (bullhorn.com/resources/staffing-industry-indicator/)
Commercial hours set a new year-to-date high 
U.S. staffing hours were largely stable this week and remained at their year-to-date high point, as did professional hours. Total hours remain 11% ahead of 2025. Commercial hours increased to set a new record high, up 13% year-over-year. IT hours declined this week; this drop is very typical in the run-up to Thanksgiving and IT hours are still up 4% year-over-year. Office/clerical hours continued to decline, down -0.5% week-over-week and -8% year-over-year. Light industrial hours increased this week and are now 18% ahead of 2025, the biggest year-over-year gap since early July. The last full week before Thanksgiving is normally the annual peak for staffing hours. With two more weeks to report before the holiday, the staffing market is positioned for a strong close to the year.

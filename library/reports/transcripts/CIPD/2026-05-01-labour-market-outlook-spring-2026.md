# Labour Market Outlook – Spring 2026

- **Source:** CIPD
- **Date:** 2026-05-01
- **URL:** https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/reports/labour-market-outlook/
- **Fetched:** 2026-07-17
- **Sample:** 2,049 senior HR professionals and decision-makers in the UK (YouGov); fieldwork 23 March–23 April 2026; weighted to UK employment by size, sector and industry
- **Extraction:** full — PDF extracted cleanly via web_fetch

## Key figures
- Net employment balance +10 this quarter (up from a near-record-low +7), private sector +13, public sector −7 (negative for five straight quarters)
- 63% of employers plan to recruit in the next three months (up from 60%); public sector jumps to 77% from 70%, private sector 58%
- 22% of employers plan redundancies in the next three months (public 26%, private 21%)
- Cost management is the top organisational priority at 58% overall, ranked above productivity (44%) and market-share growth (35%)
- 55% of large private employers cite improving productivity, much via generative AI
- Median expected basic pay increase steady at 3% for the eighth consecutive quarter; real pay growth just +0.2% and set to turn negative as CPI rises toward a forecast 4.1% peak in Jan 2027
- Share of employers expecting a 3–3.99% pay award rose from 25% to 40% year on year; those expecting 5%+ fell from 24% to 15%
- One-third of employers still report hard-to-fill vacancies despite low overall vacancy levels; only 12% anticipate significant hiring problems in the next six months (down from 16% a year ago)
- Only 20% of SMEs rank regulatory compliance a priority (vs ~a third of larger firms) as the Employment Rights Act 2025 lands

## Full report
The quarterly CIPD Labour Market Outlook gives an early indication of employer recruitment, redundancy and pay intentions, based on a representative survey of 2,000+ employers. This quarter shows a broadly stable jobs market with marginally more positive public-sector recruitment.

Recruitment and redundancy: the net employment balance sits at +10, up slightly from last quarter's record low (+7, the weakest since the series began in 2014 outside the first pandemic year). Private-sector intentions are stable at +13; public-sector intentions remain negative at −7. 27% of employers expect staff to increase, 50% to hold, 17% to fall. Large private firms are most positive (34% expecting increases) vs SMEs (22%) and public sector (20%). By industry, intentions are highest in legal/accounting/consultancy (+25), IT (+20), manufacturing (+19) and hospitality (+18); lowest in compulsory education (−10).

Organisational priorities: cost management dominates (58%), driven by higher employer National Insurance, above-average wage growth, minimum-wage rises and energy/supplier costs. Improving productivity is second (44%), most common among large private employers (55%), much of it pinned on generative AI. The CIPD stresses people professionals' role in moving AI "from experimental to strategic use."

Vacancies: ONS data shows vacancies broadly flat since spring 2025 and possibly at their lowest outside the pandemic since 2014. A third of employers still have hard-to-fill vacancies, indicating skills mismatches. Pressures remain highest in healthcare and social work (20% anticipating significant problems), lowest in retail (3%).

Pay: median expected pay award holds at 3% across all sectors for the eighth straight quarter, against CPI of 3.3% in March 2026, forecast to peak at 4.1% in January 2027. Real pay growth is barely positive (+0.2%) and likely to turn negative. Employers are converging on the 3–3.99% band (up to 40% from 25% a year ago).

Recommendations: focus on sustainable productivity not just cost-cutting; take a strategic approach to workforce planning; support employee financial wellbeing; build capability on Employment Rights Act 2025 compliance; upskill managers on employment relations ahead of trade-union reforms; and make fair, transparent pay decisions.

Survey method: YouGov, 2,049 UK senior HR/decision-makers, online, weighted to size/sector/industry. Fieldwork 23 March–23 April 2026.

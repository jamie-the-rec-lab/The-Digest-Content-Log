## Full report

Every year Criteria surveys hiring professionals from across all industries to learn how they attract, hire and retain their teams. For this seventh annual report, the focus is the state of hiring in 2024.

2024 has been an odd year for hiring. With the Wall Street Journal declaring an end to the "talent-war era," 2024 seems to mark a transition from a candidate-driven market to an employer-driven one, where candidates no longer have the advantage. Whether that shift automatically makes hiring easier for companies is doubtful: thanks to an uncertain economic climate the picture is far more complex, and the report explores how major factors such as rapid advancements in AI, skills gaps and remote work continue to impact hiring.

The results are based on a survey of 425+ hiring professionals across organisations large and small and a wide breadth of industries. Responses were collected in July 2024 from a global audience, with the majority of respondents from the United States, Australia and Canada.

Key findings:
1. Over half of hiring professionals plan to hire more next year; small businesses are especially more likely to expect growth.
2. Only 1 in 5 hiring professionals admit to ghosting a candidate, yet an earlier candidate survey found 38% of candidates say they have been ghosted within the last year.
3. 70% of hiring professionals believe we are facing a talent shortage; the hardest skills to find include industry-specific knowledge and soft skills.
4. Over half of companies are adopting skills-based hiring. To do so, 74% have started to evaluate skills in new ways, 68% are rewriting job descriptions to emphasise skills, and one in five have removed college degree requirements.
5. A quarter of hiring professionals are now using AI for hiring and/or talent management, double the prior year. AI still has ground to cover on trust, with only 9% trusting it more than traditional recruitment methods.
6. 3 in 4 hiring professionals say emotional intelligence is the most important skill to look for in candidates, closely followed by analytical thinking, flexibility/agility and resilience.

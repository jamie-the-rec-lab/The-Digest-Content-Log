## Full report

### Setting the stage: planning for the unpredictable
2025 made clear that predictability is off the table, from economic slowdowns and unpredictable job-market shifts to a government shutdown delaying official labour data. The Employ Hiring Benchmarks Report is built to help hiring teams cut through the noise and show what "good" really looks like right now, walking through each stage of the hiring process and, for every metric, giving the latest benchmark, what the numbers mean, and practical next steps.

### About the data
The benchmarks come from real hiring data from 6,640 Employ customers using the Jobvite, Lever and JazzHR ATS solutions and Employ's AI Companions. Data is split by company size (SMB 0-249, mid-market 250-999, enterprise 1,000+) and across five industries: retail, hospitality, manufacturing, software & tech, and business services.

### Stage 1: Attract & Engage
Engagement rate (email CTR) benchmark was 0.8% in 2025. Enterprises saw the highest engagement at 1.5% thanks to name recognition and dedicated recruitment-marketing resources; retail led industries at 7.4% (up from 4.7%) on fast, high-volume hiring. Average applications per job rose to 257.5 from 207.2, about 50 more applicants per role, with small-to-medium businesses highest at 312.0 (broader roles) and software & tech highest by industry at 369.1 (remote-friendly), while hospitality (202.9) and manufacturing (176.4) trail because roles are onsite. Source of hire: "Applied" led at 60.6% of hires, nearly identical to last year; smaller teams rely more on sourcing and referrals. Applicant diversity (EEOC definition) rose to 45.7% from 41.2%, with enterprises lowest at 26.5% (brand perception) and hospitality highest at 54.4%.

### Stage 2: Screen
Qualified applicant rate dipped to 11.5%, with enterprises strongest at 14.5% and specialised software & tech lowest at ~5%. Time to screen improved to 7.2 days from 8.3, enterprises fastest at 5.7 days, software & tech slowest at 9.0. Screen-to-interview rate fell to 34.9% from 38.9%; enterprises convert 46.5% while small businesses dropped to 23.8% from 30.0% under heavier volume; hospitality leads at 57.8% because repeat roles sharpen recruiter instincts.

### Stage 3: Interview
Time to interview held at 3.6 days (all industries 3.5-3.7), enterprises fastest at 3.1. Interview-to-offer rate rose to 31.5% from 26.3%; enterprises convert 72.2% via disciplined, well-vetted funnels, while tech (14.8%) and business services (10.8%) run more evaluation. Candidate experience score was flat at 2.9/5; enterprises lowest at 2.7 (automation at scale), hospitality and business services highest at 4.0.

### Stage 4: Select
Offer acceptance rate rose to 83.9% from 82.5%; mid-market highest at 89.8%, manufacturing highest industry at 91.0%, software & tech lowest at 80.8% (competing offers). Time to hire was 46.2 days (from 45.7), enterprises fastest at 35.4, small businesses slowest at 56.4. Time to fill improved nearly four days to 63.5 from 67.7, enterprises at 51.7 and small businesses at 83.5. Among the 23% who declined an offer, nearly 40% cited limited career growth or poor location, 32% lack of flexibility, and 20% negative culture or leadership.

### Stage 5: Onboard
New hire retention at three months was 84.6% in 2025 (down from 93.9%), a reminder the first 90 days are critical. First-year turnover dropped by nearly half to 12.1% from 23.7%, showing that once employees settle in they are far more likely to stay, and 95% of new hires rated their onboarding positively.

### Next steps
Employ recommends assessing your funnel stage by stage against the benchmarks, prioritising one or two stages where small improvements create the biggest ripple, and tracking and iterating quarterly rather than annually.

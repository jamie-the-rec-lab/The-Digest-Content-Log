## Key figures
- Optimal sequence length is 3-4 emails, with diminishing returns beyond that.
- Overall 2025 benchmarks fell versus 2024: open rate 75.3% (from 76.6%), reply rate 16.9% (from 22.6%), interested rate 9.4% (from 12.1%).
- Following up compounds engagement: by stage 3, open rate reaches 74.9%, reply rate more than doubles to 16.3%, and interested rate hits 9.2%, up from 5.6% at stage 1.
- Cumulative by stage: stage 1 open 64.3% / reply 8.8% / interested 5.6%; stage 2 72.6% / 13.7% / 8.0%; stage 3 74.9% / 16.3% / 9.2%.
- Best send day is Wednesday (avg 63.1% open vs 62.1% average) and best hour is 3pm local (avg ~65% open); the 9am-4pm window is consistently strong (65.7-65.8%), and 54% of opens happen within the first hour.
- Send tech outreach mid-week in business hours (Tue-Wed, 9am-4pm); target non-tech candidates Friday afternoon or Sunday morning.
- Follow-up spacing tightened in 2025: email 1 to 2 a median 4 days and email 2 to 4 a median 5 days, down from 6 days for both in 2024.
- Subject lines: 2-word subject lines get the highest open rate at 71.7%; aim for 2-5 words. Using all three tokens (company, first name, title) lifts open rate by 10.4% versus no tokens.
- Email body length should match company size: 51-100 words performs best overall at a 9.2% reply rate; enterprise reply rate drops sharply from 23.7% at ~100 words to 2.8% at 500 words, while SMB reply rates stay roughly flat (~10-11%) so relevance beats brevity.
- Send-on-behalf-of (SOBO): 19.2% of sequences use it while 80.8% do not; using SOBO in a follow-up after an unanswered stage lifts open rate by 7.5-10.4% and reply rate by 23.6-61.3% (reply jumping from 6.04% to 9.74%).

## Full report

### Executive summary
This report analyses 6.2 million email sequences and 15.5 million messages sent through Gem in 2025 to give data-driven benchmarks and best practices for recruiting outreach. Headlines: sequence length of 3-4 emails is the sweet spot; Wednesday afternoons win for opens; 3-5 word subject lines with personalisation tokens hit a 71.7% open rate, 10.4% above no tokens; follow-ups move the needle, with stage 3 open rates of 74.9%, reply rates doubling to 16.3% and interested rates of 9.2%; email length should be tailored to company size; and sending on behalf of a hiring manager (SOBO) lifts performance, especially in follow-ups.

### Part 1: How many emails to send and when
Across all 6.2M sequences, overall 2025 engagement was open 75.3%, reply 16.9%, interested 9.4%, each down on 2024 (76.6%, 22.6%, 12.1%). Engagement compounds by stage: open climbs 64.3% to 74.9%, reply 8.8% to 16.3%, interested 5.6% to 9.2% by stage 3. Timing: best day Wednesday, best hour 3pm local, strong window 9am-4pm, and 54% of opens land within the first hour. Tech "best" open thresholds sit at 68.5% (non-tech 69%) and "worst" at 61.7% (non-tech 59.2%). Send tech roles Tuesday-Wednesday 9am-4pm; non-tech on Friday afternoon or Sunday morning; avoid late-night (10pm-midnight) and very early (6-7am). Follow-up spacing has sped up: a median 4 days between emails 1 and 2 and 5 days between 2 and 4, down from 6 days in 2024.

### Part 2: What to say in your subject lines
Personalisation tokens raise opens; using all three of company, first name and title gives the biggest lift at +10.4%. Shorter is better: 2-word subject lines hit 71.7%, and 2-5 words is the target, with 6+ words performing poorly. Winning examples appeal to the candidate's values and career growth, ask questions, highlight the opportunity, mention mutual connections, and use light flattery.

### Part 3: Leveraging your team with SOBO
Send-on-behalf-of lets you send from another person's address, typically a hiring manager or executive. 19.2% of sequences use SOBO and they outperform on both open and reply rates, yet over 80% of sequences still don't use it, a large untapped opportunity. SOBO works best as a strategic re-engagement tool in follow-ups: after an unanswered stage it lifted open rates by 7.5-10.4% and reply rates by 23.6-61.3%. Introduce SOBO in later stages, keep the message personalised to offset the authenticity gap, and track SOBO performance separately.

### Part 4: The email itself
Match email length to company size. Well-known enterprises want short, detail-first emails: reply rates drop sharply from 23.7% at ~100 words to 2.8% at 500. Smaller and early-stage companies need to work harder to sell the opportunity, and SMB reply rates stay roughly flat (~10-11%) across word counts, so relevance beats brevity. Overall, 51-100 words performs best at a 9.2% reply rate.

### Part 5: Using data to optimise
Treat the benchmarks as a starting point and keep testing subject-line variants, body length for your segment, send times by role type, sequence length (3 vs 4 vs 5 emails), spacing intervals, and SOBO for specific scenarios. Track opens, replies and interested rates by template, monitor the impact of body length, segment by company size, watch time-to-first-open and time-to-reply, compare SOBO versus non-SOBO, and review monthly to spot trends. These are aggregate benchmarks across millions of messages, so validate against your own results.

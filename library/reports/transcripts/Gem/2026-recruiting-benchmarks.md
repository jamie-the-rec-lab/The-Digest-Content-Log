## Key figures
- Aggregate hiring grew +8.3% year over year (June 2024-May 2025), the first sustained rebound since the 2021 peak, but volumes remain roughly 30% below pre-downturn levels.
- Recruiting teams are down about 14% versus 2021 but steady for the first time in years.
- Recruiters manage nearly twice as many applications as in 2021 (+93%), yet hires per recruiter have fallen 43%, from 50.5 to 29.0.
- Applications per recruiter reached 5,327 in 2025, up 93% since 2021 (1.9x).
- Job openings per recruiter rose to 13.4 from 9.6 in 2021 (+40%).
- Average interviews per hire rose ~33% to about 20; technical roles average 35-36 interviews and ~26 interviewer hours per hire, versus ~20 interviews and ~12 hours for non-technical.
- Applications concentrate in two channels: job boards and sourcing sites 50.0% and company marketing 39.6%, together nearly 90%.
- Hires by source: job boards 27.0%, company marketing 24.9%, referral 16.8%, direct sourcing 11.3%, internal 9.6%, career site 3.6%, agency 3.4%. Yields are outsized: referrals ~11x, internal mobility ~32x, direct sourcing ~4x.
- Sourced candidates are nearly 8x more likely to be hired than inbound applicants (2.5% vs 0.3%).
- Rediscovered candidates (already in the CRM/ATS) now make up 46% of sourced hires, up from 26% in 2021.
- Funnel has tightened: only 8% of applicants advance past screening (from 13% in 2021) and just 0.5% are ultimately hired, roughly one in 200, making an applicant 3.3x less likely to be hired than in 2021.
- Offer acceptance (offer to hire) climbed to 82%, the highest since 2021.
- Stage passthrough: application to pre-onsite 8%, pre-onsite to onsite 19% (from 30%), onsite to offer 39% (from 47%), offer to hire 82%. Median time to hire 24 days (+2 since 2021).
- Inbound vs outbound: 86% of outbound candidates clear the first screen versus 6% of inbound; outbound time to hire is 41 days versus 24 for inbound.
- By company size: startups advance 25% of applicants to pre-onsite but hire only 0.3%, while enterprises push under 10% to pre-onsite yet hire 0.7% (more than double the startup full-funnel rate).
- Manufacturing leads the recovery (+24% YoY, ~30% above 2021) and has the strongest full-funnel passthrough (1.4% hired), while Software still lags 40% below its 2021 peak.
- Email outreach benchmarks: open 81%, reply 23%, interested 13%, converted-to-process 8%.

## Full report

### Introduction
After the sharp contractions of 2022-2023, the recruiting landscape is finding its footing, with the first sustained signs of recovery even as teams operate leaner and more selectively. Gem's sixth annual report draws on 165 million applicants, 15 million candidates and 1.2 million hires from June 2021 to May 2025 (a shift from prior calendar-year cycles).

### Executive summary (ten takeaways)
1. Hiring is slowly recovering: +8.3% YoY after two years of decline, still ~30% below 2021, led by smaller and growth-stage companies. 2. Recruiting teams reset to a leaner baseline, down ~14% vs 2021 but steady. 3. Recruiters work harder than ever: applications up 93% while hires per recruiter fell 43% (50.5 to 29.0), steadying after bottoming in 2024. 4. Processes are more selective: interviews per hire up 33%, technical roles now 35-36 per hire. 5. Sourcing remains the most reliable route to quality: job boards and marketing drive ~90% of applications but only half of hires, while direct sourcing yields 4x, referrals 11x, internal mobility 32x, and sourced candidates are ~8x more likely to be hired. 6. Rediscovery is the strongest untapped lever: 46% of sourced hires now come from candidates already in the CRM/ATS, up from 26% in 2021. 7. Funnel conversion keeps tightening (8% past screening, 0.5% hired) but close rates are strong (82% offer acceptance). 8. Growth at all costs has given way to growth with intent. 9. Enterprises filter aggressively upfront while startups screen heavily mid-stage. 10. Together the data marks an inflection point toward a leaner, steadier, more disciplined market.

### Hiring growth
Aggregate hiring turned positive in 2024-25 (+8.3% YoY) after two years of decline (-26.8% then -11.2%), sitting just under 30% below 2021. Recovery is broad but uneven: firms of 1-99 FTEs surged +35% YoY (only ~13% below 2021), while 1,000+ FTE firms remain 28-34% below. By location, New York has nearly closed the gap (-3.9%) while the SF Bay Area is still 32.2% below despite a +22.4% rebound; international markets are still contracting (-9.6%). By industry, Manufacturing leads (+24% YoY, ~30% above 2021), Financial Services turned positive (+3% since 2021), while Software remains 40% below its peak. Technical and compliance functions (Legal, Data Science, Engineering, IT/InfoSec) are within 8-13% of 2021, while Customer Service/Support is still 55%+ below.

### Recruiting team size and workload
Recruiter headcount per team fell more than 15% between 2022 and 2024 before stabilising in 2025 (+1.1% YoY, ~14% below 2021). Enterprise teams (5,000+ FTEs) rebounded to 205 recruiters. Meanwhile workloads climbed: job openings per recruiter rose to 13.4 from 9.6 (+40%), and applications per recruiter reached 5,327 (+93% since 2021, 1.9x), with mid-market recruiters (100-499 FTEs) handling 7,824 each. Yet hires per recruiter fell to 29.0 (-43% vs the 50.5 baseline), the defining "do more with less" dynamic.

### Interviews and interviewer hours
Interviews per hire rose ~33% to about 20 overall, but the real story is by function: technical roles (Engineering, Data Science, Product, Design) now run ~36 interviews and ~26 interviewer hours per hire versus ~20 and ~12 for non-technical. Data Science spiked to 38.5 in 2025. Sales climbed from 14.0 to 21.3, Marketing from 18.6 to 26.4. The shift is from "hire fast" to "hire right".

### Source channels
Applications: job boards and sourcing sites 50.0% and company marketing 39.6% (nearly 90% combined), then direct application 3.5%, direct sourcing 2.6%, career site 1.9%, referral 1.6%. Job boards grow with scale (40% at the smallest firms to 53% at 5,000+ FTEs). Hires: job boards 27.0%, company marketing 24.9%, referral 16.8%, direct sourcing 11.3%, internal 9.6%, career site 3.6%, agency 3.4%. Relationship-driven channels punch far above their application weight (referrals ~11x, internal ~32x, sourcing ~4x). By industry, Manufacturing hires 54.9% from job boards while IT Services & Hardware leads on internal mobility (24.6%).

### Rediscovery
Rediscovered candidates (already in the CRM/ATS from a prior application or outreach) now make up 46% of sourced hires, up from 26% in 2021. It scales with organisation size (58% at 5,000+ FTEs) and is most pronounced in technical roles (Engineering 48%, Design a category-high 57%), but is spreading fast into People/HR (41%), Customer Service (47%) and Finance (28%).

### Funnel passthrough
Early conversion keeps tightening: only 8% of applicants reach pre-onsite (from 13%) and 19% of those reach onsite (from 30%); 39% then reach an offer, and offer-to-hire has climbed to 82%, the highest since 2021. The overall application-to-hire rate has fallen to 0.5% (one in 200, 3.3x lower than 2021), driven by application volume. Median time to hire lengthened to 24 days (from 22). Inbound vs outbound is stark: 86% of outbound clear the first screen versus 6% of inbound, and 2.5% of sourced candidates are hired versus 0.3% of inbound (~8x), though outbound takes 41 days versus 24. By size, startups advance 25% to pre-onsite but hire 0.3%, while enterprises filter earlier (<10% to pre-onsite) yet hire 0.7%. Manufacturing has the strongest full-funnel passthrough (1.4% hired in 26 days) while Software is lowest (0.3% in 29 days). Email outreach passthrough: open 81%, reply 23%, interested 13%, converted-to-process 8%. The report also breaks funnel rates down by gender and race/ethnicity.

### Conclusion
The TA landscape is leaner, heavier-workload and more selective, and this is the new baseline rather than a temporary squeeze. The teams thriving are investing in their existing databases, prioritising relationship-driven channels, and using AI and automation to do more with less. Gem positions itself as an AI-first all-in-one recruiting platform (ATS, CRM, sourcing, scheduling, analytics, 800M+ profiles) claiming up to 5x recruiter productivity and 30-50% technology cost savings.

## Full report

**Introduction.** Recruiting teams faced a fundamental reset between 2022 and 2025. The tools, workflows and expectations that defined effective recruiting a few years ago no longer match reality. Teams feel overwhelmed by application volumes, and AI seems to have supercharged the top-of-funnel candidate pipeline. Greenhouse analyzed data from over 6,000 companies and over 640M applications across three years to answer: Is hiring significantly slowing due to application volumes? Are fewer jobs closing without hires? Are recruiters less or more effective given the two-sided impact AI has had on hiring?

**Why benchmarks matter differently now.** Most traditional benchmarks were built for a predictable environment that didn't have to think about candidate risk or fraud. Today the value of benchmarking isn't comparing your metrics with industry averages — it's understanding whether your team's performance is sustainable given the volume they manage and the resources they have. Benchmarks should answer: Are we performing effectively and efficiently? Can we sustain this? Where should we be improving?

**2025 core performance metrics.**
- 56.7 days to fill a job
- 12.3 average interview hours per hire
- 22.7 interviews per job
- 4.9 monthly hires per recruiter (+122.3% from 2022)
- 69.8% of jobs closed with a hire (+8.7% from 2022... report also cites +7.9%)
- 650.5 annual interviews scheduled per recruiter (+128.0% from 2022)

**Volume and capacity metrics (2022-2025).**
- +411.8% increase in annual applications per recruiter (2025: 746)
- +157.7% increase in applications per hire (2025: 203)
- +128.1% increase in annual applications per organization (2025: 41,422)
- 55.6% decrease in recruiters per organization (2025: ~5)

**Funnel efficiency metrics (2025).**
- Recruiter-sourced hires: 9.7% (+4.4% since 2022)
- Stage 1 progression rate: 7.6% (-14.8% since 2022)
- Stage 2 progression rate: 22.0% (+6.2% since 2024)

**Top-of-funnel pipelines are flooded.** Organizations are seeing more than double the application volume vs. three years ago; individual recruiters are managing over 400% more applications than in 2022, while recruiter team sizes have been cut in half. Drivers: AI-powered candidate tools, frictionless application processes, shifting candidate behavior in an unpredictable economy, and fewer available jobs. The ratio of applications per job increased 111% — from ~115 per role in 2022 to 244 in 2025.

**Recruiters are performing well despite a "hostile" environment.** Jobs closed with a hire increased nearly 8%, and monthly hires per recruiter more than doubled (+122%) despite smaller teams. Interview metrics stayed stable — average interviews per hire up only 4.5%, interview hours per hire up just 5.7%. Progression rates remained stable or tightened, indicating teams are triaging effectively rather than passing the burden to hiring managers.

Tim Sackett (CEO, HRUtech.com): "Most recruiters don't care or notice that their application volume doubled or tripled. They're cherry-picking a few great applicants and ignoring the rest." Time-to-fill rose 37%, but that may reflect intentionality and quality standards rather than inefficiency. Ariana Moon (VP Talent Planning & Acquisition, Greenhouse) notes many organizations "need to take more interviews to make the same number of hires, given the amount of spam and fraud out there." Stronger tech infrastructure — AI-powered screening, ATS automation, fraud/spam detection, talent matching — is helping recruiters do more with less.

**Sourcing channels that drive the most hires (2025).**
Top five sources — Applications vs. hires:
- External job boards: 49.9% of applications / 22.6% of hires
- Company job boards: 23.8% of applications / 34.4% of hires
- Social media: 5.6% of applications
- Other: 3.2% of applications / 10.6% of hires
- Recruiter-sourced: 2.9% of applications / 9.7% of hires
- Referrals: 11.6% of hires

Recruiters source nearly 10% of hires despite contributing only 3% of applications — the highest conversion rate of any channel. Job boards (internal + external) source nearly 75% of all applications (73.7%) but make up less than 50% of hires. External job boards contribute less than a quarter of hires despite driving half of applications (in 2024 they accounted for only 17% of hires). Company job boards are the top source of hires (34% of hires from 24% of applications).

**Key takeaways.** Managing high-volume pipelines is the new normal; teams must leverage AI (while managing its two-sided risk of fraud), maximize pipeline quality, and invest in their employer brand. Sackett: "If 100% of applicants get a chance, your quality will go up, immediately." Moon recommends fraud/spam detection, talent matching, improved background screening, and AI-resistant structured interviews that uncover human judgment and critical thinking. Being explicit about a company's relationship with AI can repel the wrong applicant and attract the right hire.

**The final word.** Application volumes more than doubled and team sizes were cut in half, yet recruiters are closing more roles, making more hires and maintaining quality standards. Use of AI tools (especially top-of-funnel) is a must, alongside investment in company job boards and employer reputation. The human element of resourcing matters more than ever.

**Methodology.** Greenhouse analyzed recruiting performance data in North America from over 6,000 organizations spanning 640M applications from 2022 to 2025. Contributing experts: Ariana Moon (Greenhouse) and Tim Sackett (HRUtech.com).

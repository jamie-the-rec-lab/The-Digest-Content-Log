### Launch press release (as republished by Onrec, 18 November 2026: onrec.com/news/research-labour-market/four-in-ten-uk-employers-face-mid-level-skills-gaps-as-capability-crunch)

#### Four in ten UK employers face mid-level skills gaps as capability crunch deepens
Hays research finds employers are struggling to secure experienced professionals needed to deliver projects, transformation and growth.
Four in ten (40%) UK employers are facing critical skills gaps in mid-level professionals, according to new research from Hays, one of the world’s leading specialist recruitment and workforce solutions providers.
This skills gap is becoming a significant barrier to growth as businesses struggle to secure the experienced professionals needed to turn their ambitions into action. As firms strive to boost productivity and deliver transformation, 27% of employers also report shortages in leadership capability. More broadly, more than 85% of employers experienced some form of skills shortage over the past year. Poor succession planning to replace seasoned colleagues is also a key issue.
Published today, the annual Hays Workforce Trends and Salary Report 2027 draws on insights from more than 11,200 professionals and employers including responses over 1,200 senior leaders and C-suite decision-makers.
The study reveals that some of the most acute skills gaps are among experienced professionals who can turn strategy into delivery, and these pressures are being felt across specialisms. Nearly half (47%) of technology employers say AI, machine learning and data science capabilities are the hardest to hire, while 36% of business support employers highlight commercial awareness and business judgement as the most difficult to secure.
In HR, 26% of employers point to HR technology and systems capability as their greatest capability gap. In construction and property, skilled labour shortages are a key pressure on project delivery, while life sciences employers report scarce specialist clinical, manufacturing and technical expertise. These capabilities are increasingly critical as organisations look to deliver transformation and improve productivity.
Many organisations are also pursuing ambitious transformation and productivity agendas without significantly increasing headcount. While 39% plan to increase hiring next year, almost half (48%) plan to invest in AI and automation over the next 12 months to achieve their top organisational goal, compared with 24% planning to increase permanent headcount.
“The findings show that for many organisations in the UK, the challenge is not about defining the strategy but having the right people in place to deliver it.
“We're seeing growing pressure in the layer of experienced professionals who translate ambition into action, lead teams, manage projects and drive transformation. These are the people who keep businesses moving forward. When that capability is missing, growth can be impacted, productivity suffers and senior leaders find themselves focused on resolving issues rather than pursuing opportunities.”
Tom Way, Hays CEO for UK and Ireland
Yet workforce capability is struggling to keep pace with ambition: 69% remain in the early or moderate stages of AI adoption, with only 20% at a broad or advanced stage. Only one in ten HR professionals with hiring responsibilities describe development and upskilling in their organisation as highly effective and aligned with future skills needs.
The research further highlights a potential succession problem, with just 14% of professionals saying their organisation has a strong focus on developing future leaders or successors.
When it comes to hiring, businesses identified attracting the right permanent talent as their biggest workforce challenge (35%), followed by budget pressures versus market expectations (25%).
Mr Way added: “The good news is that business ambition remains strong. Workforce readiness now needs to match intent.
“Many organisations are investing in transformation and emerging technologies, but our findings suggest workforce capability is becoming the barrier between what are very good plans, and actual, delivered impact.
“We are certainly not seeing a blanket freeze. But we have entered a super selective phase for UK business, as employers look for every new hire to make a meaningful impact.
“To close the gap, employers need to look beyond the external market alone. That means investing in developing their existing people, building clear succession pipelines before experienced colleagues step back, and taking a more specialist approach to finding the people with the skills to turn plans into delivery.”

#### Survey methodology
This report draws on survey responses from more than 11,200 professionals and employers across the UK, spanning a broad range of industries, professions and specialisms, captured between 22 July to 11 September 2026. Employer findings are based on respondents involved in hiring and workforce decision-making. The number of respondents involved in recruiting in this survey was 3,281, and of C-suite/Senior directors, it was 1,345.

#### Five emerging priorities for workforce leaders in 2027
#### 1. Focus on critical capability, not just headcount
With 86% of employers experiencing skills shortages over the past year, simply adding headcount will not close the most important gaps. Identify the skills, experience and leadership capability most critical to future delivery, then focus workforce planning on the capabilities that create the greatest business impact.
#### 2. Benchmark pay against current market conditions
Candidate salary expectations have risen for 70% of employers, increasing pressure on attraction, retention and reward decisions. Current, role-specific benchmarking helps organisations remain competitive without relying on assumptions or reacting too late to market movement.
#### 3. Strengthen your employee value proposition
Rising pay expectations make reward a central consideration, but salary alone will not secure scarce skills. Professionals also expect flexibility, development and meaningful career progression, so organisations need an employee value proposition that clearly communicates the full value of not just joining, but staying.
#### 4. Connect technology investment with workforce readiness
Although nearly half (48%) of employers plan to invest in AI and automation, technology alone will not deliver productivity gains. Organisations must build the skills, leadership and change capability needed to embed new tools, redesign work and turn investment into measurable value.
#### 5. Build workforce flexibility into future plans
With four in ten (39%) employers planning to ramp up hiring, competition for already scarce capability could intensify. Combining permanent hiring, flexible talent and specialist expertise can help organisations respond faster, manage uncertainty and access critical skills when they are needed.

## Full report

### Introduction: AI built for impact
In 2025, hiring leaders are navigating a high-stakes balancing act. A global skills shortage, economic volatility, and the goal to deliver fairer hiring processes have converged to reshape how organisations compete for talent. At the same time, AI is rapidly transforming every stage of hiring, promising faster, smarter and more scalable outcomes. But efficiency is not enough: candidates expect more than just automation. They want transparency, empathy and human connection at every step, and striking this balance is critical for organisations vying for top talent and trying to maintain brand reputation.

HireVue's framing: the answer is not choosing between technology and humanity, it is designing systems that respect both. "How you use AI will determine if candidates connect with or reject your brand."

HireVue surveyed over 3,100 job seekers globally to understand what they think about AI's adoption in hiring. The result is described as "a nuanced portrait of hope, hesitation, and a call for clarity." Four headline takeaways:
- **AI is today's productivity driver.** For candidates' job searches, not using AI is no longer an option, it is a disadvantage.
- **Trust and skepticism coexist.** Job seekers trust AI for personal use, but skepticism emerges when AI is introduced by employers.
- **Skills-based hiring is a shared priority.** Driven by employers, demanded by candidates, and essential to unlocking fairer, broader access to opportunity.
- **There is a continued need for AI education.** Do not assume candidates understand how AI is used in the process or the science behind it.

Candidates are ready to embrace AI but remain vigilant. Concerns about bias, fairness and the loss of human touch highlight the need for responsible implementation. For employers, building trust with candidates is not just ethical, it is a competitive advantage.

### 01. Candidates are already using AI
Candidates are using AI to be more productive in their own job search. In the last 12 months they have used AI for: updating a resume (52%), writing a resume (51%), preparing for an interview (49%) and researching (45%). Alongside this, 49% of candidates value a healthy work-life balance and 62% are searching for solid compensation, so AI-driven productivity sits within a broader set of candidate priorities.

### 02. Employees trust AI, until it is in your hands
While employees use AI in their own personal job searches to advance productivity, their skepticism over how employers use it represents a unique dichotomy. Candidates trust AI to make their own lives easier but stay skeptical when it is used to make hiring teams' lives easier.

- 79% of candidates want to know when AI is used in hiring decisions.
- 66% oppose AI making final hiring decisions, though that opposition has decreased by 10% year-over-year.
- 30% fear AI could replace human judgment or introduce bias.
- 43% support AI reviewing applications, but 41% still oppose it.

**Why it matters.** While trust in AI is increasing, transparency and human oversight remain non-negotiable for many. HireVue's position: no AI-driven hiring technology should be used for decision-making; data insights should be used for decision support, but hiring teams should be the ultimate decision authority, backed by data.

**Educate and personalise.** Education matters when so much dialogue includes "robots are taking over the world" framing. HireVue offers intro and outro videos as part of its structured interviewing process, and notes many candidates do not realise real humans watch their recorded interviews, so teams should tell them. Example script: "Hi, I'm [name] the hiring manager for [role], and rest assured humans are watching this."

HireVue describes its own technical guardrails: products for candidate engagement and interview insights employ generative AI (LLMs) with domain knowledge and strict guardrails, plus proprietary models for job matching and candidate assessment. Algorithms are controlled and tested by IO Psychologists and Data Scientists across the model lifecycle. Its AI Assessments are described as "static and deterministic," meaning algorithms are not re-trained or learning on the fly and provide a repeatable output each time; the system is trained and tested in the "lab" then locked before deployment, and can only learn new things if someone chooses to update it.

**Case study, Schneider Electric.** Aiming to improve the early-career candidate experience in North America (part of a goal to double such opportunities by 2025), Schneider Electric used HireVue OnDemand video interviews but candidates felt the process lacked human interaction. Introducing personalised recruiter intro and closing videos raised one recruiter's candidate Net Promoter Score from 68.4 to 75.4.

### 03. A two-way shift toward skills-based hiring
Employers are leaning away from resume-based hiring and increasingly embracing skills-based approaches, and candidates are asking for the same.
- TestGorilla (cited): "90% of candidates feel as though they're more likely to land their dream job with skills-based hiring." 50% of companies say their top frustration in the skills conversation is the difficulty in validating skills, and nearly half of organisations report fatigue or frustration with the current skills conversation, driven by confusion, lack of trust and ineffective tools.
- Aptitude Research (cited): companies are moving away from assumptions based on resumes and job titles toward structured, directly validated skill assessments.
- LinkedIn (cited): skill gaps are widening in technology, healthcare and logistics; hiring based on skills lets companies find the precise expertise needed, even where candidates lack traditional qualifications, expanding the talent pool.

**Bias remains a pressing concern.** Among employees, 45% see hiring bias as a major issue and 41% still see it as a minor problem. Bias goes beyond race and ethnicity: familiarity bias (favouring people who feel similar, via a shared hometown, sports team or favourite restaurant) can be just as powerful and often subconscious.

While AI promises more fairness and objectivity, employees are not fully convinced. Top concerns: 30% worry it cannot account for subtle, human qualities; 13% fear it might screen out qualified candidates; 11% do not trust AI at all.

**Why it matters.** When an overwhelming majority of candidates believe there is bias in hiring, current practices are not working. But there is a paradox: while human decisions are seen as biased, many still fear AI will make it worse, especially for those who do not follow traditional career paths. When asked how increased AI use might affect bias and unfair treatment, 57% believe it would get better, 21% it would get worse and 23% say it would stay the same. Still, 46% believe AI treats applicants more equally.

There is deep skepticism about AI's ability to see potential in non-traditional candidates: 52% say AI would do a worse job recognising potential, and 55% say AI would do a worse job at judging team compatibility. HireVue's read: the problem is not AI itself but the lack of trust in how it is used, and a misunderstanding of how it actually works.

**AI built to validate what candidates can do.** Skills-based hiring shifts focus from resumes toward real capabilities, so companies can unlock overlooked talent, widen the funnel and reduce the influence of unconscious bias. HireVue positions its suite as validating (rather than inferring or assuming) the skills that predict success.

### Outlook
Candidates are neither fully convinced nor entirely opposed, but increasingly aware of AI's growing presence. Looking ahead: 74% anticipate major change, 67% expect significant change, and 50% foresee a substantial shift from AI in hiring. On sentiment, 43% believe AI will improve hiring, 46% foresee a mixed impact, and 32% think AI will be more helpful. On willingness to apply when AI is used in the process: 41% say it would not affect them, 39% express reluctance, and 20% are more inclined to apply.

The through-line: candidates will embrace AI in hiring where employers are transparent about where and how it is used, keep humans as the final decision-makers, and use the technology to validate skills rather than to screen people out.

## Full report

### AI isn't emerging anymore, it's embedded
In just three years AI has moved from an emerging trend to everyday reality, and adoption is accelerating faster than any prior technology shift. HireVue surveyed over 3,100 global hiring managers. The top themes: AI in hiring is entering a new maturity phase, moving from productivity gains to operational transformation and responsible deployment, and both sides of the process are now AI-enabled. Hiring teams use AI to write job descriptions, screen applicants, schedule, qualify talent and support decisions; candidates use it to explore roles, polish resumes and cover letters, research employers, prepare for interviews and optimise how they present themselves. The result is a dynamic where trust, transparency and evidence matter more than ever.

### AI usage is now default behaviour
AI is table stakes and a standard part of how HR teams work and hire, with adoption doubling in two years. 77% of HR leaders use AI weekly or daily (from 54% in 2024 and 70% in 2025), 47% use it every day (from 26% in 2024), 85% already use or plan to use generative AI this year (from 66% in 2024), and only 7% never use AI at work (from 24% two years ago). AI has expanded across the whole hiring lifecycle rather than just screening, and 80% say employees using generative AI are more or equally productive.

### AI is changing candidate behaviour
Candidate AI use has surged: 71% use AI to write resumes (+15), 64% practice interviews with AI (+15), and 46% use AI to write cover letters. Perception has shifted: 62% of HR leaders say candidate AI use is smart while 31% call it cheating, more than double last year. Nearly 100% of hiring teams consider AI cheating a problem, 64% significantly so. Yet 65% of candidates still prefer a human over a chatbot. As Chief Science Officer Mike Hudy puts it, the goal isn't to remove AI from hiring but to use it responsibly to reveal real skills, build trust and make better decisions.

### Responsible AI is now a business requirement
The conversation has shifted from adoption to responsibility, focused on governance, transparency and how AI is used. Despite heavy usage, only 41% trust AI systems overall and 40% trust AI-driven recommendations, a trust gap that makes explainability critical. Top concerns are biased recommendations (46%), legal compliance (39%) and candidate perception (39%). Meanwhile 48% feel more positive about AI than in 2025, 66% of companies have updated internal AI policies, and 70% of HR leaders are excited about AI. Trust maturity: 2024 trust was emerging, 2025 it surged with productivity gains, 2026 it is stabilising and explainability becomes the priority.

### What leaders should do now
1. Rethink screening for an AI-polished candidate pool, evaluating skills not resumes via assessments. 2. Invest in explainability and transparency as a competitive advantage, choosing vendors whose AI is explainable, auditable and defensible. 3. Build hiring practices that are truly predictive, not just efficient, measuring on-the-job success rather than keyword matches. 4. Ensure technology is grounded in science, validated, fairer and backed by bias audits. The organisations that lead will balance AI efficiency with human judgment, building processes that are predictive, defensible and human-centred.

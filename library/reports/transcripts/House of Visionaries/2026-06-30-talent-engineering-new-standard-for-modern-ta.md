## Full report

### Framing: "Good isn't good enough"

Ryan O'Hara, Community Manager, House of Visionaries and Co-Founder of Bond Global, set the ambition for the event:

"The conversation around AI has moved on quickly. We're no longer just asking, 'What tools should TA teams be using?' The bigger question is: How do we redesign the way Talent Acquisition actually works? Because 'good TA' has moved on. The business now expects better planning, sharper insight, more challenge, more visibility, smarter use of AI, and a better candidate experience. The best teams are building a better operating system! The ambition for the event: Build the new baseline for modern Talent Acquisition."

### Resident spotlights

**Lucy Szypula, Head of Talent, hyperexponential**

Lucy's talk covered building an AI-native talent function. She argued that the real question is not what time gets saved, but what gets better because of it. She believes that efficiency is too small a goal.

She shared passionately how AI fluency starts with her and challenged leaders in the room to build around the function rather than bolt on tools. Concrete practices she has implemented:
- Automation and AI leverage are explicitly part of the TA competency framework
- Every team 1:1 starts with asking "What have you achieved with AI this week?"
- A two-way knowledge sharing exercise runs alongside it

**Matt Doucette, Global Director of TA (ex Monster, Meta, Amazon)**

Matt brought both method and evidence. He shared how agentic infrastructure helped turn a 2-day-plus planning cycle into 20 minutes. His stated build order:
- Start by building a system and recognise that the system matters more than the tools
- The ATS acts as the data layer, a record of everything within the end-to-end talent lifecycle
- That is surfaced through an MCP (Model Context Protocol) and used by an agentic skills layer
- His "Talent OS" mapped a clear path to a fully AI-orchestrated level of TA intelligence

### Fireside: "Art of the possible"

With Caitriona Staunton (VP People) and Jim Miller (VP of People & Talent).

Most teams start from constraints. "Art of the Possible" asks them to temporarily remove those constraints (existing systems, processes, team structures) and design from a blank page. Most talent teams ask what their ATS can do, which is the wrong starting point.

The three questions worth asking:
1. What should humans do?
2. What should technology do?
3. What shouldn't exist at all?

Your company has multiple operating principles, values and objectives that need to be considered as inputs. When you start designing from that perspective, you might surprise yourself.

Their working method:
- **Clarify:** where are we now, what's causing friction, what problem are we solving?
- **Ideate:** what would great look like with no constraints?
- **Develop:** what processes, behaviours and tools support that future state?
- **Implement:** what can change today, without needing to change everything at once?
- **Measure and reiterate.**

### Concept: the Airbnb "11-star" framework

A customer experience design framework used to get the Airbnb team to dream bigger and bolder. The logic: if you start with "how do we make what we do today a bit better", you rarely come up with truly brilliant ideas. If you start with the most awesome experience you can possibly think of, you have the potential to change the game, and can worry afterwards about how to make it happen.

Brian Chesky: "You have to almost design the extreme to come backwards."

The escalating star ladder as presented:
- Knock on door, no response
- Knock on door, waited 20 minutes for the host
- Knock on door, the host opens and lets you in
- Host welcomes and shows you around
- Apartment is stocked with water and toiletries
- Host personalises your experience: "I know you like surfing. Here's a surfboard. Here's my car. Also here's a surprise booking for the best restaurant in town."
- Get off plane, welcomed by 5,000 high school kids cheering your name, car pick-up ready, arrive at Airbnb to a press conference
- Show up at the airport and Elon Musk is there; Elon Musk says "you're going to space"

### Workshop: designing the new baseline for TA

Three stages:
1. **Think big:** dream up the most amazing experiences for the business, the candidate and the TA team
2. **Define the baseline:** what is now non-negotiable in the era of AI? What should TA deliver as a new standard?
3. **Engineer the change:** what needs to be true to deliver on this new standard?

Three lenses used throughout: the business, the candidate, the TA team.

#### Part 1, bold ideas: the business

**Internal marketplace for skills and outcomes**
A live internal market where leaders can draw down on the people and agents they need. Work "projects" last 9 to 12 months and skills are pointed where they matter most.

**Real-time workforce planning**
Hiring processes are started before the business catches up and realises what it needs. Powered by a business strategy agent and an offboarding agent that knows what knowledge and skills are leaving the company.

**C-suite live screens dashboard**
Talent is mapped and TA data surfaced directly to the C-suite in real time to demonstrate impact. Internal mobility is surfaced and the dataset becomes interrogatable through voice.

#### Part 1, bold ideas: the candidate

**Coached through process with AI**
Contextualised interview around their experience. No two interviews are the same. Optional playback of results to aid understanding.

**Radical transparency**
A portal where you can understand how many people are in the process, how long is left at each stage, feedback as you progress, plus all the information you need to prepare well.

**Career support for unsuccessful candidates**
An online learning environment, tailored to direct feedback from the interview process. TA can observe progress and use it for future talent pooling.

#### Part 1, bold ideas: the TA team

**AI concierge**
A clued-up agent that provides bite-sized feedback to the recruiter, the hiring manager and the candidate. Supports everything from the hiring manager business partnering conversation through to decision.

**Global referencing transparency**
A trusted source of truth for verifying previous experience, with an intuitive traffic light system and sources.

**Zero admin, full trust**
TA becomes a relationship and partnership function. When candidates are put forward they are all at or above the hiring bar and the assessment process can be explicitly trusted.

### Part 2: the 10 Talent Engineering Standards

The proposed new baseline for modern TA:

1. Every recruiter should be AI fluent
2. Every TA team should use AI to reduce admin and increase advisory work
3. Every TA function should earn a seat at the strategic workforce planning table
4. Every talent process should create better market intelligence
5. Every role should start with a sharper success profile
6. Every candidate and new joiner should know where AI is welcome and how to show good judgement in using it
7. Every interview should be contextual to the candidate, with tailored questions based on background, gaps etc.
8. Every candidate should receive clear, useful feedback
9. Every TA team should nurture talent before it is needed, building stronger pools, referrals and risk strategies
10. Every hiring team should share the same goals and have the same performance language

### Part 3: what needs to be true to enable this

**Capability and skills**
- Clear expectations on AI fluency across TA, hiring managers and leaders
- Upskilling and L&D to build confidence, not just tool access
- Build days to help teams bridge the gap between ambition and practical use
- Stronger judgement on what to work on, when to test, and when to scale
- Capability to move from experimentation to deployment, not just ideas

**Process and governance**
- Crystal clarity on the business goals the TA function is trying to serve
- Governance where it matters most, without slowing everything down
- Named accountability for outcomes, clear ownership for scaling and deployment
- Accountability for impact, so experimentation does not become wasted effort
- Secure, clean and accessible data
- Better clarity on which tools matter, in a market with hundreds of new options
- Scaffolding between technology and governance
- Product-style thinking: move from 0 to 1, then from 1 to scale
- Intelligence that helps teams decide where AI creates genuine value

**Culture and behaviours**
- Leadership sponsorship from the top; CEOs and senior leaders visibly backing the change
- Leaders setting the tone by using AI themselves
- A culture of experimentation: test, try, learn
- Freedom to explore, balanced with responsibility for outcomes
- A mindset that promotes progress over perfection

Summarised as: Freedom to experiment, accountability for outcomes.

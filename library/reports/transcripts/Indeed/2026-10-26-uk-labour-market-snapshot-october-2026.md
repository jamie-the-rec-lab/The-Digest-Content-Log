## Full report
The Big Picture
Overall hiring demand remains subdued but stable. UK job postings on Indeed edged up 0.3% in the month to 16 October, but still sit 31% below early 2020 levels. The UK economy has held up better than expected recently, though employers remain cautious on hiring amid ongoing uncertainty and cost pressures. 
Job Postings
Where we are: The Indeed Job Postings Index (JPI) weakened between March and June, but has held broadly steady since. Even so, it remains well below pre-pandemic levels, standing at 69 as of 16 October. New postings (job postings on Indeed for 7 days or fewer) show a firmer trend, rising to around 90 in recent weeks (10% below their pre-pandemic baseline) from 84 last month.
Direction of travel: Labour demand has been broadly stable since the start of June. Monthly JPI growth edged up to 0.3% as of 16 October, with the year-over-year decline little changed at -7.5% (from -7.8% in mid-September).
[Line graph titled "UK job postings have stabilised" showing the indexed level of job postings on Indeed in the UK, euro area and US, where 100 equals the February 1, 2020 baseline, through 16 October 2026. UK postings have stabilised but remain 31% below their pre-pandemic baseline, with the index at 69 as of 16 October 2026. In the euro area and US, postings remain above pre-pandemic levels.]
Sector split: The vast majority of sectors tracked sit below their pre-pandemic levels in terms of overall postings on Indeed. Of the 49 categories we tracked, 7 have postings above the baseline. Four of these are engineering disciplines: civil engineering, mechanical engineering, electrical engineering and industrial engineering. Social science, education & instruction and loading & stocking are the others, with loading & stocking only just above its baseline. 
Conversely, nursing, customer service, and media & communications roles remain furthest below baseline.
[Line graph titled "Vast majority of sectors have postings below pre-pandemic levels" shows the share of sectors in the UK with job postings on Indeed above the pre-pandemic baseline, through 16 October 2026. 100% of sectors saw postings above baseline during the pandemic reopening, and that share has steadily fallen from 2023 onward. The share stands at just 14% as of mid-October.]
Regional variation: Northern Ireland (109) has the highest JPI of any region analysed, and the North East (100) has edged back up to its pre-pandemic baseline. The South East (57) and London (65) have the lowest JPI levels. 
[Map titled "Northern Ireland has strongest JPI" shows the Indeed Job Postings Index by UK region as of 16 October 2026. Northern Ireland (109) is the highest and the only region clearly above its pre-pandemic baseline, with the North East (100) at baseline. The South East (57) and London (65) are the lowest.]
Middlesbrough has the highest JPI (107), followed by Sunderland (105) and Belfast (104). Slough (47), Reading and Brighton (both 51) have the lowest JPI. Only 3 of the 58 cities and large towns tracked have seen their JPI rise over the past year, each by around one point: Coventry (to 63), Glasgow (to 95) and Edinburgh (to 76). 
Wages
Headline: Posted wage growth has cooled to 3.5% over the year to September 2026, down from around 4% through the spring and summer. Advertised wages have grown faster than prices since mid-2023, but that gap has now closed: with inflation rising to 3.5% in September, posted wage growth is only now matching inflation.
[Line graph titled "UK posted wage growth has slowed to the pace of inflation" shows year-over-year growth in the Indeed Wage Tracker versus annual CPI inflation from January 2019 through September 2026. Posted wage growth has eased while the inflation rate has caught up.]
The Labour Market Balance
Unemployment: The unemployment rate remains relatively low by historical standards, having fallen to 4.7% in the three months to August. Alongside a drop in vacancies, the vacancy-to-unemployment ratio was stable at 0.4, meaning around one job vacancy for every two and a half unemployed workers. 
[Line graph titled "UK unemployment is below its average since 1971" shows the UK unemployment rate from 1971 to the three months to August 2026, with a dotted line marking the average since 1971 of 6.6%. The rate dropped to 4.7% in the three months to August, below its long-run average.]
Job switching and redundancies: Rates of job-to-job switching remain close to historic averages, running at 2.3% in the second quarter of 2026. Meanwhile, redundancy notifications rose in September to their highest since late 2020. The rise came from a handful of unusually large notifications rather than more employers filing, so it may prove a blip, though it is worth watching whether it marks the start of a sustained trend. 
[Line graph titled "UK redundancy notifications jumped in September" shows the number of potential redundancies notified to government each month from January 2020 to September 2026. Notifications rose to 50,904 in September, the highest since October 2020, after holding mostly between 20,000 and 35,000 since 2022.]
What We’re Watching
-   The Budget: Chancellor Healey will unveil his maiden Budget this week. Areas of focus pertaining to the labour market may include health-related inactivity, youth unemployment and business costs, while next year’s minimum wage will be confirmed. 
-   Monetary policy: The Bank of England is expected to raise its policy rate from 3.75% to 4% in November to help contain inflation pressures, after growth and inflation data came in higher than expectations. With the labour market having shown signs of finding a floor, rate setters are likely to feel more comfortable hiking rates in the near-term while mitigating downside risks to the economy.

Methodology
Data on seasonally adjusted Indeed job postings are an index of the number of job postings on a given day, using a seven-day trailing average. February 1, 2020, is our pre-pandemic baseline, so the index is set to 100 on that day. 
Data on wage growth are the average year-on-year percentage changes in wages and salaries advertised in job postings on Indeed, controlling for job titles.
Data on AI-related postings are the share of AI (and generative AI) job postings, as a percentage of overall job postings, using a seven-day trailing average. We calculate the aggregate share of job postings in a specific location that included keywords associated specifically with: AI (e.g., “Machine Learning,” “Data Science,” and “Artificial Intelligence”) and Generative AI (e.g., “Generative AI,” “Large Language Models,” and “Chat GPT”).

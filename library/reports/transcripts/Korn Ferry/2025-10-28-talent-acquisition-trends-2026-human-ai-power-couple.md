# Talent Acquisition Trends 2026: Human-AI Power Couple

- **Source:** Korn Ferry
- **Date:** 2025-10-28
- **URL:** https://www.kornferry.com/insights/featured-topics/talent-recruitment-articles/ai-in-recruitment-trends
- **Fetched:** 2026-07-16
- **Sample:** 230+ talent specialists and consultants at Korn Ferry plus 1,600 talent leaders across company sizes, countries and industries (TA directors to CHROs), supplemented by expert interviews and Tom Cheesewright's 2036 foresight.
- **Extraction:** full (complete report, all six trends)

## Key figures
- 84% of talent leaders plan to use AI next year.
- 52% of talent leaders plan to add autonomous AI agents to their teams in 2026.
- 73% of TA leaders say critical thinking and problem-solving is the skill they need most in 2026, while AI skills rank only fifth.
- Only 22% of companies are planning leadership succession with AI readiness in mind.
- Only 22% believe their leaders can effectively manage teams combining humans and AI agents.
- 83% of TA leaders say they have some influence with the C-suite, yet 59% still feel shut out of strategic business decisions.
- TA leaders using AI are more likely to have C-suite influence (85%) than those who are not (70%).
- 20% of companies now require full-time office attendance, while nearly three-quarters of employees want a hybrid or remote option.
- Cost benchmark cited: a $100K human employee versus a roughly $20,000 AI agent.

## Full report

AI is the big story, with 84% of talent leaders planning to use it next year, but it is one piece of the 2026 TA puzzle alongside fragile leadership pipelines, misaligned skills priorities and workplace-policy friction. TA sits at the centre of these shifts, and leaders who see the bigger picture are becoming indispensable.

### Trend 1: Your next hire might not be human
The dawn of hybrid people + agentic AI teams. AI agents act largely autonomously without constant prompts, and 52% of talent leaders plan to add them in 2026. HR vendors are already creating employee records for AI agents and Microsoft is issuing them security IDs. The real challenge is cultural, not technical: matching agents to work, onboarding them, tracking their performance, benchmarking cost and capability ($100K human vs $20K agent), and helping managers lead mixed teams. Action: start experimenting now within governance frameworks, train managers for mixed teams, redesign workflows around human-to-agent handoffs, and keep the human touch where candidates need it.

### Trend 2: Think first, ChatGPT later
Most people can learn AI in under a month, but critical thinking takes years. While CEOs demand AI skills, 73% of TA leaders say the skill they most need in 2026 is critical thinking and problem-solving, with AI skills ranking fifth. You need critical thinking to tell a hallucination from real data. Hire people who can assess AI's output, spot flaws and adapt to whatever tool comes next. Action: assess for critical thinking not just AI fluency, look for adaptability and a learning mindset, and make critical thinking the competitive edge.

### Trend 3: Entry-level cuts today = pipeline crisis tomorrow
Replacing entry-level roles with AI is the easy boardroom sell, but those roles are where future managers and leaders come from, and only 22% of companies are planning succession with AI readiness in mind. Entry-level people are also the fastest adopters of new technology, so cutting them cedes agility to competitors. Action: redesign roles rather than eliminate them, accelerate development pathways, invest in early-career talent, partner with universities, and plan succession with AI in mind.

### Trend 4: Buying without getting buy-in
CEOs and boards are pouring billions into AI with unclear ROI, but only 22% believe their leaders can effectively manage human-AI teams. The technology is deployed but people are confused, and authentic communication about change has disappeared, with many employees not understanding their company's AI stance until layoffs are announced. Organisations need AI-ready leaders. Action: advocate for leadership development, partner with L&D on leader readiness, document the leadership impact on AI initiatives, and bridge the communication gap for hiring managers and candidates.

### Trend 5: It's time TA got a bigger seat at the table
83% of talent leaders say they have some pull with the C-suite, but 59% still feel shut out of strategic business decisions. AI may be the bridge: TA adopted it earlier than almost any other part of HR, and TA leaders using AI are more likely to have C-suite influence (85%) than those who aren't (70%). The opportunity is for the TA leader to become to the CHRO what the CHRO is to the CEO, a strategic advisor on workforce transformation. Action: be the AI advisor the business needs, develop strategic skills beyond recruiting, make the business case with clear ROI, and build the advisory relationship with the CHRO.

### Trend 6: Office mandates = major TA headache
20% of companies now require full-time office attendance, while nearly three-quarters of employees want a hybrid or remote option (Korn Ferry Workforce 2025). Companies demanding full in-office attendance shrink their talent options, while fully remote employers get their pick, unless the employer value proposition is strong enough to offset it. Action: segment roles by genuine necessity, make the business case for flexibility by tracking the cost of mandates, and strengthen other EVP elements where office requirements are non-negotiable.

### Method
Korn Ferry surveyed 230+ of its own talent specialists and consultants and 1,600 talent leaders across sizes, countries and industries (TA directors to CHROs) on AI impact, leadership readiness, skills priorities, workplace policies and TA's strategic role, supplemented with expert interviews and futurist Tom Cheesewright's 2036 predictions using the Intersections foresight model.

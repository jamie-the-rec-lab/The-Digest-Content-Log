# The Future of Recruiting 2023

- **Source:** LinkedIn (Talent Solutions)
- **Date:** 2023-01-01
- **URL:** https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/talent-solutions/resources/pdfs/future-of-recruiting-2023.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-07-16
- **Sample:** Survey of 1,611 recruiting professionals (1,216 corporate, 395 search/staffing) plus 403 hiring managers, in six languages across 20 countries, Oct–Nov 2022; plus Talent Drivers survey of 20,396 members (Dec 2022) and billions of LinkedIn platform data points (900M members)
- **Extraction:** full — official LinkedIn report PDF extracted directly

## Key figures
- 87% of recruiting pros say TA has become a more strategic function over the past year; 70% say TA can claim a seat at the table
- Only 45% of recruiting pros say their companies increased salaries enough to keep pace with inflation
- 64% predict the future of recruiting will be more favorable to candidates/employees over the next five years
- 60% of in-house recruiting pros predict employer branding investments will increase this year; 53% predict recruiting budget will decrease or stay flat
- 68% of hirers (surveyed Feb 2023) were "very hopeful" or "cautiously optimistic" about generative AI's impact on recruiting
- Top GAI outcomes hoped for: automate repetitive tasks (74%), faster/easier to source candidates (67%), faster/easier to engage candidates (59%)
- 69% say the macroeconomic environment is negatively impacting hiring, yet 74% say it is not lowering the priority of DEI hiring
- US paid job posts for contractor roles grew +26% YoY vs +6% for full-time roles
- Since 2019, recruiter searches including a skills filter grew +25%; recruiters are +50% more likely to search by skills than by years of experience
- 75% of recruiting pros predict skills-first hiring will be a priority in the next 18 months; only 64% feel they can accurately assess candidates' skills today
- 20% of US job posts on LinkedIn don't require a four-year degree (share grew +30% over six months)
- 94% of recruiting pros agree understanding employee skills is necessary for informed talent decisions
- Employees stay 60% longer at companies with high internal mobility (4.9 vs 3.1 years median tenure)
- 81% say upskilling/reskilling will be an important factor shaping the future of recruiting

## Full report

The report offers 17 predictions across five themes. **Role of recruiting:** TA is becoming more strategic (87% say so; 70% claim a seat at the table) and will have more say over pay — only 45% say salaries kept pace with inflation, and compensation is the #1 candidate priority globally. Recruiting will double down on employer branding as talent regains leverage (64% predict a more candidate-favorable future; employer branding is the only budget line a majority expect to increase). Recruiters will need more strategic soft skills (communication, relationship-building, adaptability, problem-solving, business acumen; those with 1+ see 3x more promotions), and will collaborate more with L&D. Generative AI will let recruiters focus on the human part of hiring — 68% of hirers were hopeful/optimistic; top hoped-for outcomes were automating repetitive tasks (74%), faster sourcing (67%), faster engagement (59%), with a "human in the loop" emphasized.

**Economic uncertainty:** Despite headwinds, employers stay committed to diversity (69% say macro environment negatively impacts hiring, but 74% say DEI is not deprioritized). Employers will hire more contract workers as a hedge (US contractor job posts +26% YoY vs +6% full-time).

**Employer branding:** Companies will watch candidate priorities closely (top: compensation, work-life balance, flexible work; fastest-growing: flexibility +5%, comp +4%) and recalibrate pitches, often over-valuing flexibility/advancement and under-valuing other factors. Gen Z will reward employers who value development and diversity (50%+ more likely than Gen X to value advancement).

**Skills-first hiring:** Will become the gold standard (skills-filter searches +25% since 2019; +50% more likely to search by skills than experience; 75% call it a priority; only 64% can accurately assess skills). No degree increasingly no problem (20% of US posts drop degree requirements, +30% over six months). Companies will map employee skills (94% say understanding skills is necessary; 84% say it's a current priority).

**Internal mobility & upskilling:** Business leaders will value homegrown talent (75% say internal recruiting will shape the next five years; employees stay ~2x/60% longer — 4.9 vs 3.1 years — at high-internal-mobility firms). Upskilling will be key to retaining a diverse workforce (81%), and learning will be a compelling driver for candidates, not just employees.

### Methodology
LinkedIn Research surveyed 1,611 recruiting professionals (1,216 corporate, 395 search/staffing) plus 403 hiring managers, in six languages across 20 countries, Oct–Nov 2022. Candidate priorities based on Talent Drivers survey of 20,396 members (Dec 2022). Behavioral insights derived from billions of data points across 900M members; data as of Jan 1, 2023.

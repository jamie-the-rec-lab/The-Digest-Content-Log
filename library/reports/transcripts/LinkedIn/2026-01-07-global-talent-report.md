## Full report

New LinkedIn research shows a widening gap between jobseekers who struggle to stand out and recruiters who say it's harder to find quality talent.

**The labor market continues to be competitive.**
- LinkedIn data shows US applicants per open role have doubled since spring 2022.
- Nearly two-thirds (65%) of people say finding a job has become more challenging, citing competition as the main hurdle, followed by uncertainty about fit and skills gaps.
- Recruiters have fewer open roles to fill and face higher stakes: 66% say it's become harder to find qualified talent over the last year, amid growing pressure to fill roles faster (42%) and uncover 'hidden gem' candidates (39%).
- More than half (52%) of people globally are looking for a new role in 2026.

**AI gives recruiters a competitive edge and job seekers a confidence boost.**
- 93% of recruiters plan to increase their use of AI in 2026; 59% say it's already helping them discover candidates with skills they wouldn't have found before.
- 66% of recruiters plan to increase AI use for pre-screening interviews in 2026, which 70% believe will help them have more valuable conversations with candidates.
- 81% of people have or plan to use AI in their job search, and nearly half (48%) say AI tools boost their interview confidence.
- All generations, from Boomers to Gen Z, share the same struggle: unsure how to stand out in new AI-driven hiring processes, yet the majority plan to use AI tools in their job search.

**LinkedIn product results cited.**
- AI-powered job search: over 1.3M members use it daily; over 25M weekly searches powered by the tool.
- Job match feature reduced the rate at which US Premium subscribers apply to "low match" jobs by 10%.
- Verified members get 60% more profile views and 30% more connection requests.
- LinkedIn Hiring Pro: nearly 60% of hirers find a candidate to interview within the first week; time-savers save 6+ hours weekly.
- Hiring Assistant (AI agent for recruiters): early adopters save 4+ hours per role, review 62% fewer profiles, and see a 69% improvement in InMail acceptance rates (used by AMD, Chewy, Expedia Group, Siemens, Wipro and others).

**Methodology.** Research conducted by Censuswide in November 2025 among 19,113 consumer respondents (Nov 13-28, 2025) and 6,554 global HR professionals (Nov 10-27, 2025), across the UK, US, France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Brazil, Sweden, the Netherlands, India, Singapore, Australia, and MENA (Saudi Arabia and UAE).

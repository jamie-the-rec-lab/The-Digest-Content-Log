## Full report

### Press release (MILWAUKEE, 8 Sep 2026, 9:31 AM EDT; page text retrieved via WebFetch, so wording is as returned by the fetch tool)

Employers anticipate global hiring momentum will strengthen heading into the final quarter of 2026, according to ManpowerGroup's latest Employment Outlook Survey of 39,878 employers across 42 countries. The global Net Employment Outlook (NEO) for Q4 2026 stands at 29%, up two points from the previous quarter and six points year-over-year.

The survey reveals a workforce in transition. Among employers planning to add staff, 62% cite changing roles and skills as the primary driver, including expansion into new areas, expertise demanded by advancing technology, and shifts in required skills.

Jonas Prising, ManpowerGroup Chair & CEO, stated: "Nearly two-thirds of employers are hiring because roles and skills their organizations need are changing—many reconsidering job tasks rather than displacing positions."

Globally, 43% of employers plan to increase staffing between October and December, compared with 14% anticipating reductions, while 41% expect staffing levels to remain unchanged.

Entry-level hiring: Across every industry surveyed, more employers report increasing entry-level hiring than cutting it. Forty-five percent are adding early-career talent compared with 2025, while 20% are pulling back. Where employers scale back, cost pressure and lack of experience weigh more heavily than AI concerns.

Hiring drivers: While company expansion remains the most common reason for adding staff at 39%, hiring increasingly reflects transformation rather than growth alone. Twenty-one percent of organizations cite advancing technology, while 16% mention adapting to shifts in required skills. Thirty-two percent of employers adding staff are backfilling positions.

AI and time-to-hire: Despite increasing AI use in recruitment, employers report mixed results. Twenty-eight percent say their average time-to-hire has become faster compared with 2025, 41% report it has remained the same, and 30% say it has become slower.

Sectors: Construction & Real Estate and Finance & Insurance report the strongest hiring intentions for Q4 2026 at 36%, followed by Information at 35%. Construction & Real Estate posted the largest year-over-year improvement among sectors, rising 13 points.

Regions: The Americas 36%, up two points QoQ and 11 YoY. Brazil (53%), Panama (49%), Mexico (41%), and the United States (36%); Panama posted the largest year-over-year improvement worldwide at 26 points. Asia Pacific 34%, up six QoQ and three YoY; India leads all 42 countries at 54%, followed by Vietnam 36% and China 34%. Europe and the Middle East 22%, up five QoQ and five YoY; U.A.E. 41%, Sweden 39%, Israel 34%; among the largest economies the United Kingdom (23%) leads, followed by Germany (15%) and France (13%).

Methodology: Data collected 1-31 July 2026, interviews with 39,878 public and private employers across 42 countries. The next survey will be released in December 2026, reporting hiring expectations for Q1 2027. The 2027 cycle marks the 65th anniversary of the survey.

### Global findings PDF (20 pages)

**Executive summary.** More than 39,000 employers across 42 countries reported a seasonally adjusted NEO of 29%. The Q4 2026 NEO increased since the previous quarter (+2 points) and improved year-over-year (+6). Anticipated hiring increases are driven by company expansion; expected decreases are driven by economic challenges. Strongest NEOs: Americas region 36%, India 54%, both the Construction & Real Estate and Finance & Insurance sectors 36%, organizations with 250-999 employees 34%. Most improved versus a year ago: Americas +11, Panama +26, Construction & Real Estate +13, organizations with 10-49 employees +9.

**Hiring plans.** 43% increase, 14% decrease, 41% no change, 2% unsure. NEO is the percentage planning to increase minus the percentage planning to reduce. The unadjusted NEO is 29% and the seasonally adjusted NEO remains 29%.

**Industry sectors (NEO, change YoY).** Construction & Real Estate 36% (+13); Finance & Insurance 36% (+4); Information 35% (+6); Trade & Logistics 31% (+10); Manufacturing 30% (+8); Professional, Scientific & Technical Services 28% (-4); Hospitality 27% (+1); Utilities & Natural Resources 25% (+5); Public Sector, Health & Social Services 21% (+6).

**Company size (NEO, change YoY).** Fewer than 10 employees 20% (+8); 10-49 27% (+9); 50-249 30% (+8); 250-999 34% (+5); 1,000-4,999 29% (+1); 5,000+ 29% (+6). The 250-999 band is strongest; 10-49 is most improved.

**Americas.** NEO 36% (+2 QoQ, +11 YoY). Panama 49% is the strongest in the region, Brazil 53% and Mexico 41% also listed. **Asia Pacific.** NEO 34% (+6 QoQ, +3 YoY). India 54% strongest, Vietnam 36%, China 34%. Demand driven by Information (47% shown) and organizations with 5,000 or more employees (40% shown).

**Changing roles and skills drive a growing share of hiring.** "Employers are increasingly hiring to reshape their workforces rather than simply replace workers, with changing roles and skills driving headcount growth nearly twice as often as backfilling positions." Jobs transformation: 62% point to changing roles and skills; 32% report backfilling positions.

**Cost pressures and lack of expertise top AI for employers pulling back on entry-level hiring** (ranked reasons): 1. Overall hiring has decreased, including early-career roles. 2. **From gridlock to green lights.** Speeding up (ranked): 1. Better candidate targeting. 2. Faster internal approval and decision-making process. 3. **About the survey.** Running since 1962. Single question: "How do you anticipate total employment at your location to change in the three months to the end of December 2026 as compared to the current quarter?" Data collected 1-31 July 2026; the panel is built in proportion to each country's distribution of industry sectors and organization sizes; interviewees are normally the head of HR or an HR manager (in smaller organizations a general manager or CEO). Participants are not drawn from ManpowerGroup's customer base. From Q1 2026 industry sectors follow NAICS. Forward-looking statements caveat applies.

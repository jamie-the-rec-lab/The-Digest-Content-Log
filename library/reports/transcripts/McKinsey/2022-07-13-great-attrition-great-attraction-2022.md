## Full report

### Overview
McKinsey's 2022 research on the "Great Attrition" surveyed 13,382 employees across Australia, Canada, India, Singapore, the UK, and the US, spanning 16 industries (data collected Feb 15 – Apr 2, 2022). The central message: employers can turn the Great Attrition into a "Great Attraction" by understanding what workers really want.

### Attrition intentions
40% of employees said they are at least somewhat likely to quit in the next three to six months, with 18% saying their intentions range from likely to almost certain. A companion analysis ("The Great Attrition is making hiring harder") found many workers who quit did not return to the same industry or the workforce at all.

### Industry switching
Globally, just 35% of those who quit in the past two years took a new job in the same industry. In finance and insurance, 65% of workers who quit changed industries or did not return to the workforce — illustrating how attrition drains talent pools rather than reshuffling within an industry.

### Worker personas
McKinsey identified three broad groups of dissatisfied workers:
- **Reshufflers** — moving to different industry sectors.
- **Re-inventors** — starting their own businesses or trying non-traditional work.
- **Re-assessors** — quitting because of life demands such as caring for children or elders.

### What employees value
Employees want their managers and organization to value them as whole people. A strong company culture, sense of community, caring coworkers, development opportunities, and clear career paths matter more to the workforce than many employers realize — often more than transactional factors like compensation alone. Employers searching only within traditional talent pools, and competing solely on pay, miss the "nontraditional" workers (the self-employed, part-time, gig, and those who left the workforce) who could be attracted back with the right value proposition.

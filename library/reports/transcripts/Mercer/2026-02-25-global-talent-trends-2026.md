### Introduction: A Year Where Only Exponential Performance Will Do

In 2026, the velocity and volatility of change will likely not abate as organizations continue to strive for growth amid uncertainty. But that growth — value creation, agility and productivity — is increasingly harder to come by: markets are mature, competition is global, disruptors move fast and risk is heightened. To remain competitive, organizations need every facet of the business performing at its peak.

Exponential performance isn't just essential: it's existential. The pressure to deliver exponential improvements in economic performance — through greater agility, productivity, efficiency and impact — is intensifying for leaders, HR professionals and employees alike. Yet this year's Global Talent Trends study reveals that alignment is breaking down. Leaders are pushing harder for productivity and efficiency, while investors favor organizations with AI-enabled, digital-first cultures that are built to deliver sustained performance. Both ambitions, however, are at risk of being thwarted by a depleted workforce and HR functions that remain siloed and disconnected from business priorities.

To unlock exponential performance and ensure sustainable growth, organizations must act now, intentionally redesigning work, processes and operating models to create the systems that will enable success for all stakeholders. Now is the time for bold bets. Organizations that transform to maximize human and machine capability will win big; the rest will be left in the dust.

Global Talent Trends is based on a survey of approximately 12,000 respondents (C-suite executives, HR leaders, investors and employees). It uncovers four trends as critical levers for leaders in solving the human-machine equation:

**Reinvent for a human advantage:** AI alone is not enough: work must be redesigned around human-centric principles, with cultures of AI-enablement powered by skills intelligence.

**Leap forward with insight:** Talent foresight is the best risk mitigation strategy, enabling leaders to leverage workforce intelligence to plan ahead and pivot at pace.

**Recalibrate the value exchange:** To drive growth and compete for talent, the employer-employee relationship must be redefined to ensure each sees impact from their investment.

**Unleash a new HR era:** HR must stop looking inward and start designing outward. Its entire future relevance depends on its ability to enable the entire system, not optimize for itself.

### In the Human-Machine Era, People Risk Is Business Risk

Executives remain gloomy about the path ahead: 57% expect a stormy or turbulent outlook over the next 10 years. Emerging risks such as misinformation and increased geopolitical conflict add complexity to a fragile and volatile landscape. In 2025, executives said their biggest short-term risk to business growth was talent-related: reskilling and upskilling their workforces to keep up with shifting consumer demands, evolving business models and transformative new technologies. In 2026, talent continues to drive the leadership agenda: talent scarcity is the top social or economic force influencing people plans at an enterprise level, as identified by executives.

Macro drivers influencing C-suite people plans show talent scarcity at 54%, digital acceleration (including AI) at 52%, shifting economic interests at 46%, cyber risk at 44%, inflation/currency/capital concerns at 41%, overall heightened risk environment at 41%, changing consumer expectations at 34%, net zero and environmental goals at 29%, supply chain interruptions at 28%, demographic shifts at 24%, political unrest/war at 22%, and new business entrants at 14%.

### Key Insights for Senior Leaders

**1. The C-Suite and HR Are Misaligned on What Drives Performance in the Human-Machine Era**

While executives are focused on redesigning work around AI and strengthening people analytics capabilities, HR leaders risk strategic misalignment, just when they need to reinvent.

HR's top priorities for 2026: (1) Enhancing the employee experience/EVP to attract top talent, (2) Designing talent processes around skills, (3) Rolling out new HR technologies or optimizing existing platforms.

C-suite's top people initiatives for driving ROI: (1) Redesigning work to incorporate AI and automation, (2) Improving HR/people analytics capability, (3) Improving managers' abilities to lead an integrated workforce (humans and AI agents).

**2. Intentional Work Redesign Is the Missing Piece for Sustainable Human-Machine Performance**

C-suite leaders and investors see redesigning work to incorporate AI and automation as a major value driver, yet only a minority believe the workforce can combine human and machine capabilities. 63% of executives believe redesigning work to incorporate AI and automation is the people initiative that will drive the greatest return, yet only 32% believe their workforce can optimally combine human and machine capabilities.

**3. Talent Scarcity Risks Constraining Growth Ambitions**

54% of the C-suite say talent scarcity is the top macro driver influencing people plans. 59% of HR leaders say difficulty attracting talent with vital digital skills is the top workforce challenge facing businesses in 2026.

**4. Executives Are Planning Near-Term Organizational Design Changes**

Almost all organizations will undergo significant design changes in the next two years, including AI-related headcount reduction. 98% of executives are planning organization design changes in the next two years. 99% of executives expect AI to lead to at least some headcount reduction in the next two years.

Planned organizational design changes over the next two years include: Simplifying reporting lines (59%), Centralizing governance (55%), Creating self-organizing teams (49%), Flattening the organization (44%), Moving support functions to shared services (44%), Creating flow-to-work/agile teams (42%), Creating more flexible models of work with internal marketplaces (38%), Moving to a matrix design (37%), Moving to a customer design (36%), Increasing local control (35%), Moving to a divisional design (33%), Moving to a functional design (32%), Moving to modular design (31%), Moving to network design (29%), Having AI agents and human talent report into one function (28%).

**5. Talent Intelligence Emerges as a Key Differentiator — But Significant Gaps Remain**

Investors and executives rank improving people analytics as among the top two people priorities for driving ROI, but HR is falling short. Executives in high-growth companies are 2x more likely to believe that acting on talent insights is as critical as acting on financial insights.

57% of the C-suite see improving people analytics capabilities as the people initiative that will drive the most ROI in 2026. 27% of executives believe their HR team effectively advises them on human capital risks and opportunities. 55% of executives say their organization underutilizes the workforce intelligence it has today.

**6. A Collapse in Employee Thriving Threatens Productivity**

A depleted workforce, anxious about AI-driven job displacement and alert to inequities in AI access, cannot deliver sustained, exponential performance. Employee thriving in 2026 is 44%, compared to 66% in 2024 — the lowest-ever score since this measure was introduced in Global Talent Trends 2018.

**7. It's Now or Never for HR Reinvention**

Few organizations report an embedded strategic HR function, and most respondents expect HR and IT to merge in the near future. HR reinvention is non-negotiable. 8% of the C-suite see HR as an embedded strategic function today. Organizations with an embedded HR function report: 76% of executives say their business is resilient and can withstand unforeseen challenges (versus 62% of organizations overall), and 78% of executives say their organization is well-placed to compete for top talent (versus 62% of organizations overall).

56% of HR leaders expect HR and IT to increasingly merge. 81% of executives admit leaders in their organizations struggle to simultaneously run and transform the business.

### Reinvent for a Human Advantage

Despite $30-40 billion in enterprise investment into generative AI, a study by MIT found 95% of organizations are getting zero return. Merely deploying AI is not enough to amplify human potential and achieve exponential business performance. AI delivers value only when work is redesigned around it, not when technology is layered onto outdated work models and processes.

Work redesign must focus on deconstructing jobs and workflows, understanding where AI can substitute, augment or transform work, and reconstructing new ways of working for greater impact. This process shines a spotlight on 'sunset' and 'sunrise' skills, optimally combining AI's technical prowess with the creativity, empathy and critical thinking of the workforce.

**Redesign Work for the Future**

Harnessing AI to drive agility and unlock scalability and exponential performance requires leaders to think beyond use cases or democratizing access to AI tools. Rather, it requires them to shift from a technology-first to a work-first approach, redesigning work to optimize human-AI collaboration while recognizing shifting skills and agility requirements.

Realizing a return on AI investments depends on intentional work design, not passive adoption, but leaders appear misaligned. While the C-suite believes redesigning work to incorporate AI and automation will deliver the greatest ROI in 2026, less than half of HR leaders plan to prioritize it. This suggests a disconnect on the part of HR when it comes to understanding the importance of redesigning work around AI and human capability.

Work redesign must become a core organizational competency, with HR leaders stepping into the space of work architect to help steward organizations, leaders and employees into the new world of work.

Redesigning work to incorporate AI and automation is a top 2026 people priority for: 63% of C-Suite, 67% of Investors, 46% of HR.

**Orchestrating Human and Machine Collaboration**

New operating models can optimize human-AI collaboration and unlock exponential gains in agility, productivity and innovation. The ability to amplify human capability in the machine era becomes a core competitive advantage.

72% of investors agree organizations that are embracing the integration of both human and AI capabilities create stronger competitive advantage. 33% of executives see redesigning work to optimize human and AI capabilities as the greatest leadership challenge in leveraging AI for business growth. 51% of executives agree their organization is well prepared to succeed in the human-machine era — down from 65% in 2024.

The future of work combines human and machine capabilities:
- 82% of HR leaders say the future of HR lies in managing humans and digital agents side by side
- 32% of C-suite believe their workforce can optimally combine human and machine capabilities
- 60% of investors are more likely to invest in organizations with embedded and integrated AI throughout the workforce
- 72% of employees agree that AI/automation will improve how their job is being done in the next two years

**Build a Culture of AI-Enablement**

Employees are increasingly concerned about job loss due to AI. This anxiety will impede value creation, agility and productivity unless leaders urgently address it. In 2024, 28% of employees had concerns about job loss due to AI; this has increased to 40% in 2026.

Leaders should commit to transparent communication, actively involving employees in work redesign strategies, giving them agency and visibility over how their work is changing. Clear messaging about AI's evolving capabilities and impact on work alleviates fear and fosters confidence.

77% of investors are more likely to invest in companies that are committed to empowering their employees through AI education and training.

**Ensuring Fair Access**

Employees do see the value in using AI. Employee sentiment on AI shows:
- 83% say AI has made them more productive and efficient at work
- 77% have gained significant value from the way AI has been introduced at work
- 72% say some employees in their organization have better access to AI tools and training than others
- 56% say unequal access to AI tools and training negatively affects their morale at work
- 35% would consider leaving their organization if they felt disadvantaged by unequal access to AI tools or training

During continued economic volatility, AI amplifies uncertainty. Employees believe leaders underestimate the negative emotional and psychological impact of AI on employees. 62% of employees believe leaders underestimate the negative emotional and psychological impact of AI on employees. Only 19% of HR leaders consider the psychological and emotional impact of AI technologies as part of their digital implementation strategy.

Leaders cannot afford to neglect the human impact of AI implementation.

**Strengthen Skills Intelligence**

Dynamic skills intelligence (the continuous signaling of changing supply and demand for skills, and insight into skills gaps) is a foundational pillar for AI-centered reinvention. Investors recognize AI is disrupting skills at speed and scale — and employees feel it, too.

With AI agents rapidly shifting the workforce skills required, organizations must rethink talent practices, empowering people to continuously upskill and reskill. 53% of employees worry about lacking future-ready skills. 63% are willing to trade a 10% pay increase for opportunities to upskill in AI and digital skills.

Employee concern over skills relevance: 17% in 2024 worried about their skills staying relevant; this increased to 47% in 2026.

**Embrace Dynamic Talent and Reward Practices**

Developing a workforce with future-fit skills requires forward-thinking talent practices. HR must lead the transition from static, job-based hierarchies to dynamic, skills-powered roles, deploying people when and where their skills are needed.

Investor support for skills-based approaches: 97% say their investment would be negatively impacted by a less progressive approach to agile and skills-based models. 32% would not invest at all in an organization with a less progressive approach to agile and skills-based models. 63% of the C-suite agree that leaders need to move toward skills-powered talent practices to ready their organization for the future.

### Leap Forward with Insight

While leaders are focused on maximizing returns from AI investment, they face a looming talent crisis. With employee thriving plummeting, more people planning to quit, and heightened anxiety over AI, workers' capacity to drive performance is being challenged.

HR leaders foresee talent-related challenges on the horizon: (1) Difficulty attracting talent with vital digital skills (59%), (2) Rising labor costs (52%), (3) Loss of talent due to burnout or long-term sick leave (44%), (4) Low engagement impacting productivity (43%), (5) Decreased productivity (43%).

Transformation depends on talent, not just tech. When people risk is business risk, talent foresight is the best mitigation strategy. Businesses that harness their workforce data will be able to make faster, smarter decisions. Leaders need data-driven talent processes to ensure organization and work redesign are executed with precision, optimizing talent to drive performance and translating workforce insights into action.

**Take Action on Workforce Signals**

Workforce insights must keep up with shifting business demands, enabling organizations to proactively and precisely reshape themselves. Despite advances in technology, many organizations lack the integration and agility needed to fully exploit workforce intelligence.

People analytics capabilities require strengthening:
- 76% of investors expect leading firms to prioritize improving people analytics capabilities to deliver the greatest ROI
- 57% of the C-suite see improving people analytics capabilities as the people initiative that will drive the most ROI in 2026
- 48% of HR leaders see improving people analytics capabilities as a top priority for 2026
- 27% of executives believe their HR team effectively advises them on human capital risks and opportunities

Two-thirds of executives believe their organizational data tells them only what is most urgent, not what is most important. There is a mismatch between the workforce insights executives want and those HR teams are providing.

Top 5 workforce insights executives want: (1) What productivity gains can be achieved by implementing AI/automation?, (2) How is AI shifting skills premiums and impacting skill gaps?, (3) Which leadership behaviors contribute to the most thriving/engaged teams?, (4) What is the impact of different pay strategies on business results?, (5) How effective is HR at meeting business needs?

Top 5 workforce insights HR teams are most likely to have available: (1) How productive is each individual, team, department?, (2) What investments related to pay, benefits or retirement have the maximum ROI for different employee groups?, (3) How effective is HR at meeting employees' needs?, (4) How effective is HR at meeting business needs?, (5) To what extent are there pay inequities across the workforce? / What is the adoption rate of newly implemented strategies?

**Foresight Through Workforce Intelligence**

Moving with speed and precision calls for robust, real-time workforce intelligence to support decision-making and proactive work design. But many organizations lack the insight to map skill supply and demand, let alone access predictive insights.

Talent insight gap: (1) 47% say their company recognizes the jobs and tasks they are best suited for, (2) 32% say their company is aware of their potential to operate in more complex or senior roles, (3) 27% say their company has a clear view of their behavioral skills.

Improving talent intelligence is on HR's radar for 2026. How HR leaders plan to evolve talent assessment strategy in the next 12 months: Improving measurement of technical skills (67%), Introducing more valid assessment tools (62%), Combining assessment data with other data (52%), Putting assessment insights into the hands of managers/leaders (49%), Consolidating assessment providers (46%), Putting assessment insights into workers' hands (38%), Using assessment data to create employee digital twins (37%), Using conversational AI to improve existing assessment data (33%), Bringing assessment capabilities in-house (32%), No plans to strengthen assessment solutions (1%).

AI in talent processes today: 31% use AI in internal talent management, 25% use AI in strategic workforce planning, 25% use AI to support work redesign, 23% use AI in predictive models to forecast talent development.

Taking action on workforce signals should become as much of a discipline as taking action on market and financial signals. 79% of executives say that 'having better insights into our talent's capabilities is key to delivering outsize performance'.

**Drive Agile Talent Practices**

Shifts in hiring patterns, demographics and AI capabilities are eroding talent pipelines. Addressing talent scarcity and knowledge loss requires agile and adaptive talent practices, replacing tenure-based structures with those based on skills and potential.

A ticking talent timebomb: (1) Talent scarcity is executives' #1 workforce concern, (2) 99% of executives say AI will lead to some headcount reductions within two years, (3) 60% of HR expect 11-30% of their organization's workforce to be redeployed or reskilled due to AI within two years, (4) 40% of employees are planning to leave their organization in the next 12 months.

Pipeline disruption of this magnitude means that identifying and securing the right talent and developing it at pace has never been more business-critical.

Adoption of skills-powered processes lags behind need: 47% of executives say talent intelligence systems help hire people, not develop them. 35% of HR leaders believe their talent data enables them to quickly identify who might be most suitable for a role or assignment. 46% of employees say that career progression tends to be based on tenure, not skills.

Skills-powered, dynamic talent practices require: 30% of organizations have a talent marketplace. Only 26% of employees say lateral moves are prevalent in their organization.

**Redefine the Leadership Blueprint**

The ability to navigate complexity and manage leadership paradoxes requires a powerful blend of IQ, EQ and D(igital)Q. As companies plan for an AI-driven future, inspiring change toward new ways of working is 'make or break'.

Leadership development: The first line of defense:
- 83% of investors say that organizations led by executives with high adaptability and resilience will outperform their peers during market disruption
- #1: Executives believe leadership sponsorship and role modeling is the #1 driver of transformation
- 40% of employees say having a leader who sets clear direction helps them thrive at work, up from 21% in 2024
- #4: Inspiring and trustworthy leadership is a top 5 reason for people staying with their current employer (#4, up from #11 in 2024)
- Only 37% of HR leaders believe their high-potential identification and succession planning works well

**Building Digital Fluency**

Organizations struggle to develop digitally fluent, resilient leaders who can manage transformation. 75% of the C-suite believe their organization needs to be more digital than it is today to compete. Only 30% rate their digital agility as high.

Digital leadership: The awareness-action gap shows: 73% of the C-suite believe AI fluency will be equally or more essential for executives than financial literacy in the next two years, yet only 36% say AI fluency is important for leadership success in their organization today. 38% say digital leadership is important for leadership success in their organization today.

### Recalibrate the Value Exchange

Leaders have access to a wealth of workforce signals, creating an opportunity to generate insight and drive meaningful outcomes through talent practices, converting workforce intelligence into performance at scale. In response, organizations are redefining the employer-employee relationship to ensure it both drives sustainable growth and enables them to compete for scarce talent.

**Optimize the Employee Experience**

Employee anxiety over cost of living, geopolitical uncertainty and remaining relevant in the age of AI is undermining their ability to thrive at work, jeopardizing organizational performance. More employees than ever would like to leave their organization.

Employees are not thriving at work:
- "I am thriving at work": 66% in 2024, 44% in 2026
- "I am satisfied at work": 82% in 2024, 63% in 2026
- "I am very unsatisfied and plan to leave as soon as I can": 4% in 2024, 12% in 2026
- "I'm unsatisfied but have no choice and will stay for next 12 months": 14% in 2024, 26% in 2026
- Only 9% of employees in lower socio-economic groups are thriving at work in 2026

Only 9% of employees in lower socio-economic groups are thriving at work in 2026. It is perhaps no surprise that enhancing the EX and EVP is the top priority for HR leaders in 2026. More tellingly, over half of the C-suite believe enhancing EX and EVP is a people priority most likely to deliver ROI (ranked fourth, the highest ever position for executives).

Driving value from the EX: 68% of HR leaders say enhancing EX and EVP is a top priority for 2026. 51% of the C-suite say enhancing EX and EVP is a people initiative that will drive ROI in 2026. 64% of investors expect leading firms to be investing in enhancing EX and EVP to attract and retain top talent.

HR has the mandate to prioritize building an impactful EX but must do so in a way that also leads to business outcomes, not just happier employees. In 2026, organizations seek to make more informed decisions to invest in the right EX initiatives at the right times, maximizing impact.

**Using Data-Driven Insights to Maximize Returns**

A future-proof EX is one that meets the needs of both the organization (growth and performance) and the individual (personal growth, work-life balance, flexibility and rewards). Organizations can't afford to layer more into their EX offering: they need results from the investment they are already making.

Investors support EVP optimization: 73% of investors think optimizing the EVP for employee and business outcomes is very important in driving sustainable long-term value over the next five years. 89% of investors say an uncompetitive value proposition would negatively impact their investment.

To ensure EX delivers, organizations must go deep, drawing on utilization data, external market and competitor data, and employee sentiment. Continuous, AI-powered employee listening enables organizations to personalize experiences and respond proactively to concerns.

HR: Employee engagement approaches that will become critical in the next two years: Personalization of the EX (67%), Combining sentiment data with behavioral data (49%), Always-on listening platforms (44%), Using chatbots to understand the EX at moments that matter (43%). Only 20% of HR leaders are using always-on listening platforms today.

**Rethink Rewards to Drive Impact**

With employees globally feeling the pinch, rewards remain a critical motivator. The prospect of higher pay is the most common driver for people to leave their employer. 52% of employees say their biggest concern about the future of work is compensation not keeping pace with cost of living.

Enhancing or modernizing pay and total reward practices is a 2026 priority for 39% of HR leaders, while 50% of investors expect organizations to be prioritizing this. But it's vital to use data to ensure financial investment is targeted where it drives the most value.

54% of employees say the most important thing their organization could do for their compensation is to pay more for their unique or in-demand skills. Only 26% of HR say their organization has any form of skills-based pay in place.

**From Performance 'Management' to 'Enablement'**

Performance management is a critical mechanism in the value exchange. However, only around half of HR leaders believe their organization's performance management process is effective, while only half of employees see it as a valuable experience.

Performance management needs a reset: Only 52% of HR leaders believe their organization has an effective performance management process. 39% of employees believe performance management helps them improve and grow, not just receive a rating. 45% of employees believe performance management is embedded into culture and day-to-day work. 49% of employees see the performance management process as a valuable experience.

Poor manager-employee dynamics risk impeding the creation of a performance culture. Only 37% of employees have had a career conversation with their manager in the last 12 months, while only 38% get regular feedback on how their performance and skills impact their career prospects. Under half (43%) agree their manager helps their team focus on the work that matters most.

### Unleash a New HR Era

Driving exponential business performance requires a fundamental redesign of the HR function itself. With executives and investors focused on growth and returns, HR must move beyond incremental change to lead intentional, exponential transformation.

To help organizations become more agile, drive performance and future-proof themselves, HR must evolve from a focus on people operations (tracking what is happening now) to workforce architecture (shaping what should happen next). This shift positions HR not as a downstream service provider, but as a system designer at the center of business performance.

The misalignment between HR's people priorities and the areas the C-suite believes will drive the greatest value is symptomatic of a deeper issue: HR is still not strategically embedded. Without reinvention, priorities will remain misaligned, investments will miss the mark, AI transformation will stall, and productivity gaps will persist.

**Embed HR to Create Enterprise Value**

For almost two decades, HR has aimed to become more strategic. AI disruption accelerates the urgency: if HR cannot elevate itself, it risks irrelevance. Those organizations where HR is embedded in strategic decision-making report higher workforce resilience and talent competitiveness.

81% of investors say embedding HR into strategic decision-making is essential for driving growth.

Executives who report their organization has an embedded HR function also highlight: Greater resilience: 76% of executives say their business is resilient and can withstand unforeseen challenges (versus 62% of organizations overall). Improved talent competitiveness: 78% of executives say their business is well-placed to compete for top talent (versus 62% of organizations overall).

But HR remains stuck in the role of operational partner today, with only 8% of executives believing their HR function is strategically embedded.

HR's role is shifting: Current role — Siloed support function (16% C-Suite, 20% HR), Operational partner (48% C-Suite, 45% HR), Strategic advisor (28% C-Suite, 24% HR), Embedded strategic function (8% C-Suite, 10% HR).

Role in 2028 — Siloed support function (4%), Operational partner (20% C-Suite, 17% HR), Strategic advisor (48% C-Suite, 48% HR), Embedded strategic function (32% C-Suite, 31% HR).

**Rearchitect the System of Work**

AI can rewire how employees interact with HR processes — and there is much room for improvement.

How employees feel about HR processes: HR processes are complex and need to be simplified (16%), Have been simplified but have a long way to go (36%), Have gone digital but some necessary human interactions have been lost (28%), Effectively combine digital and human interaction (15%), Are excellent and seem 'state of the art' (4%).

Just as redesigning work becomes a core organizational capability for amplifying human potential and driving AI productivity, redesigning how employees interact with HR and the system that enables them must be a core HR capability.

HR processes must be simplified, digitized and balanced with human touchpoints to ensure they enhance the EX while driving efficiencies. While 67% of HR leaders are planning to increase self-service solutions in 2026, and 58% are planning to implement new technology for HR services, putting a new system on top of a broken process will not free up HR, improve the EX or deliver business outcomes.

**New Tool, Old Process?**

Adoption of AI tools in HR processes is not translating into ROI. While 57% of HR teams have implemented chatbots, only one in five say this has driven significant value — and only a quarter say HR process automation has done the same.

Current AI usage in HR operations — Top 5: Helpdesk/chatbots for HR queries (57%), Back-office automation of benefits enrollment (52%), People analytics interaction and visualizations (50%), Employee listening and engagement (46%), Continuous feedback on employee performance (42%). Bottom 5: Talent deployment/marketplaces (25%), Forecasting workforce demand based on business growth (25%), Work redesign (25%), Predictive models to forecast talent development (23%), Predictive dynamic models to project healthcare costs (18%).

Today, only 13% of the C-suite see HR technology as a transformation leader, driving enterprise-wide transformation through data, technology and strategic insight. In two years' time, 36% of executives expect to see HR technology delivering transformation, while 44% want fully integrated HR technology.

**Reinvent or Risk Irrelevance**

65% of HR leaders are concerned about HR's role given the proliferation of AI agents. 45% of HR leaders worry that HR may lose the right to define its role in the workplace as technologies advance faster than they can adapt.

**Reinvent the HR Operating Model**

Legacy operating models were built for compliance, not intelligence, and not to enable business performance at scale. Future models must be designed for flow, adaptability and continuous reinvention. Traditional, siloed HR and fragmented technology models slow the business down — constraining, not enabling, agility.

Three-quarters of HR functions still use the three-pillar model (centers of excellence, HR business partners, and shared services). This model has worked for 30 years, but today demands something different.

35% of HR leaders and 30% of executives say reinventing the HR operating model is a priority for 2026.

AI renders silos useless, starting with HR and IT. Workforce strategy is technology strategy (and vice versa), with HR and IT acting as one seamless system.

Leaders expect HR and IT to merge: 56% of HR leaders expect HR and IT departments to merge. 60% of the C-suite agree HR and IT must function as a unified force. 67% of investors believe organizations where HR and IT function as a unified force are more attractive investments.

HR's reinvented operating model must enable organizations to flow people to work, with humans and machines working as one system from the start. Rather than building around HR processes, design around desired business outcomes, with structure following work, not hierarchy. The operating model consists of: Digital fabric (the connective tissue that integrates people, data, and systems), Outcome delivery teams (human-machine teams aligned to business areas), Domain expertise (the home of deep, functional mastery), Enablement services (the operational foundation for the AI era), AI empowerment center (the engine for responsible intelligence).

Leaders are not short on vision. They are already designing the future and have access to the technologies and insights needed to transform their organizations. The challenge now is execution at scale — and bringing people with them. Because in an AI-driven future, organizations that root their transformations in human-centric principles will win.

Achieving this demands more than isolated initiatives. It requires intentional work redesign, sharper talent intelligence, and a value exchange that resonates at both a personal and business level — all underpinned by an evolved HR vision and operating model built for continual reinvention. Get this right and companies will unlock exponential gains in performance, productivity and agility, delivering a sustainable business and talent advantage. In a world where change is constant, the ability to redesign work, mobilize talent and enable performance at speed will separate those who lead from those who fall behind.

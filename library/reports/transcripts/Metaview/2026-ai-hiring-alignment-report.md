## Full report

### Introduction
The relationship between hiring managers and recruiters is often tense, and the best teams are the ones that learn to collaborate. Metaview surveyed 505 recruiting leaders and hiring managers across North America and EMEA to understand that tension and how AI is reshaping hiring. The finding: the organisations seeing the best results use AI to strengthen collaboration, not just individual speed.

### Chapter 1: The hiring problem no one will say out loud
On the surface everything looks fine, with 90% rating the recruiter-manager partnership good or excellent and only 10% admitting problems. Beneath the surface, 58% actively contemplate working around their counterpart (27% rarely, 15% never). The tension stems from a lack of trust and a desire to move faster: hiring managers want "quality over quantity, stop wasting my time with bad matches" and to "move faster, I'm losing candidates to competitors," while recruiters want managers to "trust my expertise" and "give me feedback faster, your delays cost us candidates." As MagicSchool AI's Annie Wickman notes, this friction is one tools alone cannot solve.

### Chapter 2: The cost of bad relationships
67% of teams lose qualified candidates to competitors every month. Excellent partnerships cut candidate loss in half: teams with excellent partnerships see 50% (5 in 10) losing candidates versus 80% (8 in 10) for good-or-below partnerships, meaning weaker teams are 60% more likely to lose candidates. Collaboration quality is a leading indicator of organisational performance: teams with excellent relationships and high alignment exceeded their goals 79% of the time versus 36% for fair/poor low-alignment teams, so poor partnerships are 3x more likely to miss business goals.

### Chapter 3: AI to save the day
Enthusiasm for AI is high (58% wish they could work around their counterpart, 79% optimistic about AI in hiring, 85% of goal-beating companies use AI). AI adoption also improves relationships: teams using AI are 3.8x more likely to have "excellent" relationships (55% excellent when AI is core, down to 14% for non-users). It improves alignment at kickoff too, with a 40% increase in initial alignment when AI is core (68% of searches start with high alignment versus 49% for non-AI teams). As Luma AI's Josh Gill puts it, "alignment isn't just a kickoff, it's infrastructure."

### Chapter 4: What actually works
Organisations seeing the best results use AI to enable collaboration. Do's: shared systems that help teams work from the same reality, intentional strategy where AI strengthens coordination, and better visibility that brings teams closer. Don'ts: individual copilots that make each person faster in isolation, bottom-up tool adoption where everyone picks their own AI, and more automation layers that distance recruiters from hiring managers. Metaview calls the winning pattern "coordinated AI": one central source of truth that helps entire teams work better together, not just make individuals faster.

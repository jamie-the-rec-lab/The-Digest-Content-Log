# NACE Job Outlook 2026 Spring Update

- **Source:** NACE (National Association of Colleges and Employers)
- **Date:** 2026-04-27
- **URL:** https://www.naceweb.org/research/reports/2026/job-outlook/spring-update/
- **Fetched:** 2026-07-16
- **Sample:** 185 respondents (142 NACE employer members, 19.9% of eligible members, plus 43 non-members), surveyed 12 February to 17 March 2026. Class of 2026 hiring projections were originally collected August-September 2025 and published in Job Outlook 2026 (November 2025).
- **Extraction:** full (complete report data)

## Key figures
- Employers project a 5.6% increase in hiring for the Class of 2026 (revised up from the fall projection).
- Hiring plans: 54.9% maintaining, 33.7% increasing, 11.4% decreasing.
- Top reasons for increasing hires: company growth 69.1% and commitment to succession planning / talent pipeline 69.1%, then increased demand for products and services 36.4% and anticipated retirements 23.6%.
- Top reasons for decreasing hires: reduction in business needs 78.9%, budget cuts 57.9%, uncertain economy 52.6%, AI replacing entry-level jobs 15.8%.
- Demand for AI skills more than doubled in the last six months for entry-level jobs, yet only 11% of employers are discussing eliminating positions due to AI.
- 35% of entry-level jobs require AI skills, though only 16.5% of entry-level job descriptions include them; 42.5% of employers are not yet seeking AI skills, 28.1% are.
- AI skills sought: identify and use appropriate AI tools 75.0%, develop effective prompts 72.7%, develop AI tools to increase productivity 65.9%, analyse and revise AI outputs 52.3%.
- Whether AI has reduced the need for entry-level tasks: 52.2% not at all, 26.8% moderately, 21.0% unsure, 0% completely.
- More than half of employers have discussed using AI to replace early-career work (occasionally 41.4%, frequently 14.6%, never 43.9%); 57.8% assign interns projects that use AI tools.
- 43.4% of employers have detected AI-generated applications, yet only 20.1% use tools to detect AI assistance in applications, testing or interviews.
- Career-readiness importance (weighted, 5-point): Communication and Teamwork 4.5, Critical Thinking 4.4, Professionalism 4.3, Technology 4.0, Career & Self-development 3.6, Leadership 3.5, AI Skills 2.8 (lowest).
- Graduate proficiency is rated highest on Teamwork (3.9) and Technology, and lowest on AI Skills (3.1).
- The widest importance-vs-proficiency gaps are in Communication (98.7% rate it important vs 55.4% proficient) and Critical Thinking (96.1% vs 75.0%).
- On resumes, employers most want ability to work in a team (85%), computer skills (82%) and analytical/quantitative skills (78%); AI skills rank near the bottom at 17%, and 59% want specific skills shown with examples.
- 71.2% rate graduates somewhat well prepared and 21.6% very well prepared; 69.1% see graduates as somewhat prepared to use AI at work.
- On-campus recruiting for spring 2026: 62.1% same amount, 19.6% more, 11.1% less; only 33.8% have made fall plans, and 24.7% have replaced some on-campus recruiting with virtual.

## Full report

### Hiring projections
Employers project a 5.6% increase in hiring for the Class of 2026, a notable upward revision from the fall outlook. Overall, 54.9% plan to maintain hiring levels, 33.7% to increase, and 11.4% to decrease. Among those increasing, the top drivers are company growth and a commitment to succession planning / talent pipelines (both 69.1%), followed by increased demand (36.4%) and anticipated retirements (23.6%). Among those decreasing, the drivers are a reduction in business needs (78.9%), budget cuts (57.9%), an uncertain economy (52.6%), AI replacing entry-level jobs (15.8%) and inflation (5.3%). Industries increasing hiring most include Information, Engineering Services, Wholesale Trade, Construction and Miscellaneous Professional Services; those decreasing include Utilities and several manufacturing sub-sectors.

### Artificial intelligence
Demand for AI skills more than doubled in the past six months for entry-level roles, but only 11% of employers are discussing eliminating positions because of AI. 35% of entry-level jobs now require AI skills while just 16.5% of job descriptions list them, and 42.5% of employers are not yet seeking AI skills (28.1% are, 29.4% unsure). The AI skills most sought are identifying and using appropriate tools (75.0%), writing effective prompts (72.7%), building AI tools for productivity (65.9%) and analysing and revising AI outputs (52.3%). Most employers say AI has not reduced the need for entry-level tasks (52.2% not at all, 26.8% moderately), though more than half have at least occasionally discussed using AI to replace early-career work, and 57.8% assign interns AI-using projects. On the integrity side, 43.4% have detected AI-generated applications but only 20.1% use detection tools. Two-thirds discuss AI in relation to tasks and functions, while only a minority connect it to eliminating positions.

### Skills, resumes and readiness
On resumes, employers most value ability to work in a team (85%), computer skills (82%), analytical/quantitative skills (78%), creativity (73%) and written communication (72%), while AI skills sit near the bottom (17%); 59% want candidates to show specific skills with examples rather than list them. On career-readiness competencies, Communication and Teamwork (4.5), Critical Thinking (4.4) and Professionalism (4.3) rank highest in importance and AI Skills lowest (2.8). Graduate proficiency is rated highest on Teamwork (3.9) and lowest on AI Skills (3.1), and the largest importance-vs-proficiency gaps appear in Communication and Critical Thinking. Overall, 71.2% of employers rate graduates somewhat well prepared and 21.6% very well prepared, with 55.5% saying graduate skills are aligned with hiring needs and 69.1% viewing graduates as somewhat prepared to use AI at work.

### Recruiting activity
For spring 2026, 62.1% of employers plan the same amount of on-campus recruiting, 19.6% more and 11.1% less, and 24.7% have replaced some on-campus recruiting with virtual activity. Only 33.8% have made their fall recruiting plans; among those, 43.1% will hire more in the fall and 41.2% the same.

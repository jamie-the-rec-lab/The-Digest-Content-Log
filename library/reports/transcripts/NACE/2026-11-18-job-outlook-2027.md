#### Employers Expect Hiring, Salaries to Climb for the Class of 2027
Date: November 18, 2026
By: Kevin Gray

The hiring outlook is optimistic for the Class of 2027, but not across all industries or organizations, according to results of NACE’s Job Outlook 2027 survey.
Responding employers indicate that the projected median increase in hiring of 14% is based on a host of factors, including:
Company growth;
Succession planning and the importance of the talent pipeline;
An increased demand for their products or services; and
Anticipated retirements.
More than one-third of employers are planning to increase their hiring. (See Figure 1.) However, there is nuance to this anticipated growth as more than half are planning to maintain their hiring from last year, with outlooks that differ across industries. Approximately 10% expect to cut back on entry-level hiring, citing budget cuts and reduced business needs as primary reasons.
The projected upswing in college hiring follows several lackluster years in an uncertain economy. It is also catalyzed, as news outlets suggest, for example, by organizations that reduced their workforce or scaled back entry-level hiring in favor of AI realizing that these moves were not good for business now or going forward, and they are now looking to rebuild their human workforce. In fact, Gartner found that 30% of AI-driven layoffs are expected to need rehiring, and results of our Job Outlook 2027 survey show that just 1% of employers are now considering replacing entry-level workers with AI, down from 11% last year.
Employer ratings of the job market reflect their improving outlook. (See Figure 2.) This year, approximately 56% of employer respondents rate the job market for college graduates from good to excellent, a jump from less than half that gave it these ratings last year. Employers also plan to increase salaries by 4.2% for graduates in the Class of 2027 from the average for their Class of 2026 counterparts.
These job market insights and other findings from the Job Outlook 2027 survey provide actionable steps for employers and colleges. Employers should consider:
Maintaining their early talent pipeline—Most employers are doing the majority of their recruiting in the fall and will continue to invest in early talent.
Giving students clear, consistent AI guidelines—Candidates need guidance on where AI fits in the application process and a better understanding of AI job requirements.
Providing opportunities for students to showcase their skills—Ensure interview questions and evaluation criteria assess skills.
Meanwhile, college faculty and career center staff should:
Understand the labor market—90% of employers plan to maintain or increase hiring, but opportunities vary by industry and company size. Persuade students to research and apply to a wide range of opportunities.
Provide AI education—Employers expect AI use ​at work, but many restrict it in job applications. Talk with your employer partners and remind students to check each employer's AI guidelines before applying.
Coach on skills—Students who can clearly communicate their core skills stand out. Help them build concrete examples, including how they've used AI.
NACE expects to provide a formal job market update for the 2026-27 academic year in spring 2027.
NACE collected data for its Job Outlook 2027 survey, which is sponsored by Jobscan, from August 6, 2026, through September 11, 2026. Of the 311 total respondents, 133 were NACE employer members, representing 19.1% of eligible member respondents. The Job Outlook 2027 survey was also distributed to nonmember companies matching the NACE employer member profile; this group provided an additional 17 responses. For the first time, NACE college members also distributed the Job Outlook 2027 to their employer partners, which provided an additional 161 respondents. While the overall hiring projection is based on NACE members and nonmembers, as it has been in the past, the remaining findings are based upon the total group of 311. Job Outlook 2027 is available on NACEWeb.

#### About the Job Outlook 2027 Survey
The annual Job Outlook survey is a forecast of employer’s hiring intentions of new college graduates. Data for the Job Outlook 2027 survey were collected from August 6, 2026, through September 11, 2026. Data are calculated based on the number of respondents to each specific question. Totals may not equal 100% due to rounding.
NACE expects to provide a formal job market update during the 2026-27 academic year. Based on data collected in February and March, the Job Outlook 2027 Spring Update report will provide a final update on hiring for 2026-27 graduates. The results will be available in April.

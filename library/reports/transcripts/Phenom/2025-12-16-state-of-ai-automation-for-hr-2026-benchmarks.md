## Key figures
- 83% of organisations fall between Level 1.5 and 2.5 for Automation, with only 17% reaching Level 3 or above.
- For Intelligence, 86% operate at Level 2.5 or below, and a striking 57% sit at exactly Level 2.5.
- Only 5% have reached Level 4 Automation maturity, and less than 1% have reached Level 4 Intelligence.
- 48% of organisations have slightly higher intelligence maturity than automation maturity, while 39% are equal across both dimensions.
- HR-stance survey: 30% have only limited knowledge of how to apply AI in HR, and 76% chose automating manual tasks and increasing recruiter productivity as the top reasons to adopt AI.
- 66% say their organisation is in early or no adoption for AI in talent management, versus 34% established or scaled.
- AI priorities: 77% automating manual work, 65% increasing recruiter productivity, 53% candidate engagement and matching, 51% sourcing, 49% screening.
- 52% would feel more confident using AI if they had hands-on training, assurance workflows won't break, clear guidance, and the option to pause or disable it.
- 35% have experienced at least one form of candidate fraud (resume/application inflation, AI-assisted interview answers, or the hired person not being who did the work).
- Healthcare is the most mature industry, making up 40% of all Level 3+ organisations, and leads automated candidate campaigns at ~90%, yet only 13% use high-volume hiring workflows.
- Government leads AI-powered candidate sourcing at 82% (highest of any industry).
- Manufacturing sits below average, with only 4% using automated high-volume hiring workflows despite 70%+ of its hiring being hourly production roles.
- Hotels and travel show the lowest automation maturity; professional services the lowest automated-campaign adoption (33%) and zero Level 3+ high performers; energy and utilities the lowest advanced screening (10%).

## Full report

### Section 1: Why AI and automation are critical now
Automation focuses on execution, eliminating repetitive manual tasks by following predefined rules, while AI adds intelligence by learning from data, predicting fit, matching candidates and generating insight. Automation handles the "what" and "when"; AI informs the "who", "why" and "how". The report frames Phenom's "Applied AI" as responsible, enterprise-secure, compliant and explainable, and argues maturity progression enables data-driven decisions, competitive advantage, scalability without headcount growth, better retention through internal mobility, and a foundation for continuous innovation.

### Section 2: The AI & Automation Maturity Model
Two independent dimensions, Automation and Intelligence, each run from Level 0 (none) to Level 5 (fully integrated), and organisations advance separately on each. Automation levels: 0 No Automation, 1 Task-Level, 2 Partial Process, 3 Conditional, 4 High, 5 Fully-Integrated. Intelligence levels: 0 No Intelligence, 1 Assisted, 2 Semi-Automated, 3 Conditional, 4 High, 5 Fully-Integrated. Examples run from manual data entry and Boolean search at Level 0 up to autonomous sourcing, screening and interviewing agents at Level 5.

### Section 3: About the report
Phenom analysed nearly 500 organisations using its platform over 12 months through September 2025, scoring each on adoption depth across automation (Talent CRM recipes, screening, scheduling, hiring automations, campaigns) and AI (AI Discovery, Fit Score, Interview Intelligence, AI agents), using half-step levels to reflect incremental real-world progress.

Overall findings: most organisations cluster in the middle (83% between Level 1.5 and 2.5 for Automation, 86% at or below Level 2.5 for Intelligence with 57% exactly at 2.5). Intelligence adoption outpaces automation only slightly (48% higher on intelligence, 39% equal). The path is steep: only 5% reach Level 4 Automation and under 1% reach Level 4 Intelligence, with a sharp drop-off after Level 3. The competitive opportunity is large because organisations reaching Level 3+ gain compounding advantages in speed, quality and candidate experience.

### Section 5: HR's stance on AI and automation (survey of 100+)
Five truths: (1) HR is consumed by manual work, with 30% reporting only limited knowledge of applying AI and 76% naming automating manual tasks and recruiter productivity as top adoption reasons. (2) Talent-management adoption lags talent acquisition, with 66% in early or no adoption for AI in talent management. (3) AI adoption starts where bottlenecks hurt most: 53% prioritise engagement and matching, 51% sourcing, 49% screening. (4) Trust must be earned, with 52% wanting hands-on training, assurance, guidance and a pause/disable option. (5) Fraud is quietly reshaping hiring, with 35% having experienced at least one form of candidate fraud.

### Section 6: Taking action
A six-step path: assess current state across both dimensions (enablement is not activation), identify your maturity level, benchmark against your industry, prioritise by business impact (pain points, volume, strategic importance, quick wins), build a phased roadmap (Phase 1 0-6 months, Phase 2 7-12, Phase 3 13-24), and measure progress on efficiency, quality and experience metrics.

## Key figures
- Automated recruiting email reply rate 4.96%; recruiter-written one-off emails 6.31%; LinkedIn messages 17.08% (a 3.4x advantage over automated email)
- The first three touches capture 93.2% of all replies a sequence earns; a fourth touch reaches 97.7%
- Reply rate per step: 5.52% (msg 1), 5.40% (msg 2), 4.21% (msg 3), 3.34% (msg 4), 2.93% (msg 5)
- Share of total replies: message 1 = 43.8%, message 2 = 33.7%, message 3 = 15.7%
- Best send days Wednesday (5.14%) and Thursday (5.16%) vs Saturday 3.90%
- Emails landing midnight–5am ET replied at 6.67% (3–5am window 7.24%), beating every daytime bucket, holding across 237 organisations
- Reply by role: product managers 9.94%, marketing 8.57%, design 8.19%, engineering 4.64%, healthcare 2.81%
- Multi-channel lever: two-step email + LinkedIn sequences got 45.76% reply vs 19.73% email-only; three-step 22.74% vs 5.77% (a 3.9x gap)
- Best-performing email: 5–6 word subject line (5.35%) and 150–199 word body (5.46%); first-name personalisation 5.13% vs 2.61% without
- Median time to reply 4 hours; 74.8% of replies within 24 hours; 93.9% within 7 days
- Context: recruiters now manage 13.4 open roles each and handle 93% more applications than 2021 (Gem); ~70% of the workforce is passive talent

## Full report
Pin's 2026 Recruiting Outreach Benchmarks measure outreach across 4,000,000+ messages sent by 1,500+ organisations, giving a rare cross-customer, multi-channel view under one methodology. The headline: automated email replies at 4.96%, recruiter-written email at 6.31%, and LinkedIn messages at 17.08%, a durable 3.4x LinkedIn advantage that held in every quarter. LinkedIn message volume in the dataset grew more than fiftyfold over the year.

Sequence length: three touches capture 93.2% of all replies, a fourth reaches 97.7%, so recruiters should send three to four messages and stop. The first message earns only 43.8% of replies (less than older Gem data suggested), meaning follow-ups now do more of the work; a one-message sequence leaves 56% of replies unclaimed. Space touches ~2 business days apart, since half of replies land within 4 hours and 74.8% within a day.

Timing: send Tuesday to Thursday, timed to land before the workday. Wednesday/Thursday reply at 5.14–5.16% vs 3.90% Saturday. Hour matters more than day: midnight–5am ET emails replied at 6.67% because they sit atop the inbox at 8am. (Older Gem "best day" data measured opens, not replies; benchmark the metric you are paid on.)

Role family: product managers reply most (9.94%), then marketing (8.57%) and design (8.19%); engineering (4.64%), sales (4.69%) and healthcare (2.81%) reply far less. Set role-aware expectations: 4.6% is on-benchmark for engineering but a messaging problem for product management. Healthcare opens 51.8% of emails yet replies at 2.81%, a family that reads carefully before answering.

Biggest lever: multi-channel. Pairing email with a LinkedIn message is associated with 2–4x the reply rate of email alone at the same touch count, outweighing send day, subject line and length combined (with a caveat that recruiters add LinkedIn touches for the candidates they want most, so part reflects effort).

Message craft: a 5–6 word subject line (5.35%) and a 150–199 word body (5.46%) reply best; the 300+ word essay is the most common email yet underperforms, so trimming pays. First-name personalisation nearly doubles replies (5.13% vs 2.61%), and with 98% already personalising, the edge now comes from referencing the candidate's actual work.

Methodology: 4,000,000+ messages, June 2025–May 2026, delivered-basis reply denominator excluding bounces; email and LinkedIn only (channels with end-to-end reply tracking); Eastern Time as recruiter-local proxy; role families from primary job title. Author: Erica Stacy.

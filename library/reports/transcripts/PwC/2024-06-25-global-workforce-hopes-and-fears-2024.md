## Full report

### Overview
PwC's 2024 Global Workforce Hopes & Fears Survey polled 56,600 workers across 50 countries and territories in March 2024. Workers are prioritizing long-term skills growth to accelerate their careers amid rising workloads and heightened workplace uncertainty.

### Rising workloads and pace of change
45% of workers say their workload increased significantly in the past year; 62% say the pace of change at work increased over the same period; 40% note their daily responsibilities changed to a large or very large extent; and 44% don't understand the purpose of the changes taking place.

### Job switching
More than one-quarter (28%) say they are very or extremely likely to switch employer in the next 12 months — a higher proportion than during the 2022 "Great Resignation" (19%). Two-thirds (67%) of those considering a move say skills growth is an important factor.

### Workers embrace AI
Among employees who use GenAI daily, 82% expect it to make their work time more efficient in the next 12 months. Half (49%) of all users expect GenAI to lead to higher salaries (76% among daily users). More than 70% agree GenAI will create opportunities to be more creative (73%) and improve the quality of their work (72%). 76% of all users expect AI to create opportunities to learn new skills. Among those who haven't used GenAI at work in the past 12 months, 33% don't think there are opportunities in their line of work, 24% lack access to tools, and 23% don't know how to use them.

### The skills imperative
Employees likely to switch employers are nearly twice as likely to strongly consider upskilling in that decision than those planning to stay (67% vs 36%). Fewer than half (46%) agree their employer provides adequate skills-learning opportunities. 51% of likely-leavers agree their job's required skills will change in the next five years (vs 29% of those unlikely to change employer).

### Financial stress and satisfaction
Cost-of-living pressures eased slightly: 45% of workers have money left over each month (up from 38% in 2023), though 52% remain financially stressed to some degree. 60% expressed at least moderate job satisfaction (up from 56% in 2023), and 57% of those who value fair pay agree their job is fairly paid.

### Methodology
In March 2024, PwC surveyed 56,600 individuals across 50 countries and territories who are in work or active in the labour market, designed to reflect a range of industries, demographics, and working patterns.

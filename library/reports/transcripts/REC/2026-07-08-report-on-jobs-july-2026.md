# KPMG and REC, UK Report on Jobs – July 2026 (June data)

- **Source:** REC
- **Date:** 2026-07-08
- **URL:** https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/reports-jobs
- **Fetched:** 2026-07-17
- **Sample:** Monthly survey compiled by S&P Global from ~400 UK recruitment and employment consultancies; June data collected 11–24 June 2026
- **Extraction:** partial — public press release carries the headline figures; the full dataset and historical series are subscription-only via S&P Global

## Key figures
- Temp billings rose at the steepest rate since April 2023
- Permanent placements still falling but at the softest pace in three months (moving toward stabilising)
- Starting pay for both permanent and temporary staff rose at the strongest rate since January (still below the long-run survey average since Oct 1997)
- Candidate availability rose sharply again (driven by redundancies) but the least in four months
- Total demand for staff fell at the quickest rate since January, led by a steeper drop in permanent vacancies
- Only Nursing/Medical/Care and Engineering saw improved permanent demand; Retail posted the sharpest fall in permanent vacancies

## Full report
The KPMG and REC, UK Report on Jobs is a monthly barometer of UK hiring, compiled by S&P Global from a panel of around 400 recruitment agencies. June's data showed geopolitical uncertainty continuing to shape hiring: subdued confidence drove a preference for short-term staff, pushing temp billings to a three-year high, while permanent placements kept falling but at a much slower pace than May.

Demand weakened faster overall, largely from a steeper reduction in permanent vacancies. Redundancies were again widely cited, feeding a further marked rise in candidate availability, though pay trends improved as employers lifted starting salaries to secure sought-after skills.

Regionally, softer falls in permanent appointments were seen in London, the South and the Midlands, with a renewed fall in the North after four months of growth. Temp billings rose across all four monitored English regions, strongest in the South.

KPMG's Lisa Fernihough noted the easing decline in permanent placements and the "pivot to temporary work" as execs favour flexibility amid global uncertainty. REC's Neil Carberry called the figures "truly hopeful signs" after "a long recruitment winter," warning government against adding cost and regulation to the private sector.

Methodology: S&P Global diffusion indices from ~400 UK agencies, collected in the second half of each month; readings above 50 indicate month-on-month increase. Full reports and historical data are available by subscription only.

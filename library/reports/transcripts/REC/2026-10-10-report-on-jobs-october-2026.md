# KPMG and REC, UK Report on Jobs October 2026 (September data)

- **Source:** REC
- **Date:** 2026-10-10
- **URL:** https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/10/kpmg-and-rec-uk-report-on-jobs-october-2026.html
- **Fetched:** 2026-10-17
- **Sample:** Monthly survey compiled by S&P Global from questionnaires sent to about 400 UK recruitment and employment consultancies; September data collected 10 to 24 September 2026
- **Extraction:** partial (public KPMG press release carries the headline findings; the full dataset and historical series are subscription-only via S&P Global)

## Key figures
- Permanent placements rose for a second straight month; the rise was marginal but the strongest in four years (UK)
- Temp billings increased, with the slowest growth in five months; temp pay growth eased to a four-month low (UK)
- Permanent salary growth slowed for the first time in four months and stayed below its historical average (UK)
- Vacancies fell, but the decline was the weakest in just over two years; permanent vacancies in the private sector improved for the first time in over two years, not seasonally adjusted (UK)
- Staff availability kept rising at the slowest pace in three years; permanent candidate supply grew at its weakest rate since September 2023 (UK)
- Three of four monitored English regions saw higher permanent placements, led by the North; London temp billings fell sharply, ending a four-month growth run (UK)
- Half of the ten monitored sectors saw higher permanent demand, led by IT and Computing and Engineering; sharpest vacancy falls in Retail and Hotel and Catering (UK)

## Full report
KPMG and REC, UK Report on Jobs, October 2026 edition, dated 10 October 2026. Jon Holt, Group Chief Executive, KPMG in the UK: "For the second month in a row we are seeing the jobs market starting to flicker back to life..." He described the recovery as fragile and called for Budget certainty on tax policy. Maxine Bligh, Interim Chief Executive, REC: "The permanent jobs market is revving its engine, with a second successive month of growth." She said the Chancellor needs to focus on growth, not just stability.

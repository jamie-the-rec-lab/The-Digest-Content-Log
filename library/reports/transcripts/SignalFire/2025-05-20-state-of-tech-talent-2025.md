## Full report

### Overview
SignalFire's 2025 State of Tech Talent Report, published May 20, 2025, draws on the Beacon AI platform (650M+ professionals, 80M+ organizations) to describe a fundamental reset in how, where, and who companies are hiring. Four themes: entry-level hiring is collapsing; a generational hiring shift is leaving new graduates behind; elite AI labs are locking in top talent; and geographic power centers are evolving fast.

### #1 Tech's lost generation — new grad hiring drops 50%
Everyone took a hit in 2023, but while hiring bounced back in 2024 for mid- and senior-level roles, the cut kept getting deeper for new grads. At Big Tech, new grads now account for just 7% of hires, with new hires down 25% from 2023 and over 50% from 2019. At startups, new grads make up under 6% of hires, with new hires down 11% from 2023 and over 30% from 2019. The share of new grads landing roles at the Magnificent Seven (Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Tesla) dropped by more than half since 2022.

**The experience paradox:** Employers want proof, not potential — new grads are stuck needing experience to get a job and a job to get experience. Companies post junior roles but fill them with senior individual contributors. New college grad unemployment is up 30% since September 2022 (vs ~18% for all workers). 55% of employers say Gen Z struggles with teamwork and 37% of managers say they'd rather use AI than hire a Gen Z employee.

**Series A squeeze vs AI hype:** The bigger driver than automation may be the end of low-interest "free money" of 2020–2022 and the overhiring it caused. Carta data shows Series A startups are 20% smaller than in 2020. Big Tech is doubling down on machine learning and data engineering while non-technical functions (recruiting, product, sales) shrink.

### #2 AI Labs — Anthropic sets the pace
Analyzing retention across top AI labs, one clear leader emerged: Anthropic, where 80% of employees hired at least 2 years ago were still there at the end of their second year. DeepMind follows at 78%, OpenAI at 67% (on par with Meta at 64%). Engineers are 8x more likely to leave OpenAI for Anthropic than the reverse, and nearly 11:1 from DeepMind. Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, and Stripe serve as primary talent pools for AI labs.

### #3 Geographic shifts
SF and NYC still anchor the AI ecosystem, with over 65% of AI engineers in those two metros. Miami's tax perks and lifestyle fueled a 12% jump in AI roles; San Diego saw a 7% rise in Big Tech roles even as region startups lost 3.5% of workforce (San Diego County startups raised $5.7B in VC in 2024). Texas cooled: Austin saw a 6% drop in VC-backed startup headcount and Houston 10.9%. The emerging model is "proximity over presence" — hybrid schedules and anchor days driving in-state hiring.

### #4 Predictions for 2025
SignalFire predicts the rise of the generalist engineer (AI tools reducing need for deep ML expertise), the year of the equity advisor (seasoned experts as low-cost equity advisors), and new AI-era job titles (AI governance lead, AI ethics/privacy specialists, agentic AI engineers, non-human security ops).

### Methodology
Data from Beacon AI (650M+ individuals, 80M+ organizations). "Big tech" = top 15 tech companies by market cap. "Startups" = Top 100 VC-firm-backed companies closing Seed–Series C in the prior 4 years. AI-lab retention derived from public LinkedIn profiles, 2023–2024; excluded newer labs like DeepSeek and xAI.

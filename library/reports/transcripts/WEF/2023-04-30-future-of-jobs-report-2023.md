## Full report

### Overview
The Future of Jobs Report 2023 (4th edition) draws on the Future of Jobs Survey of 803 companies, collectively employing more than 11.3 million workers, across 27 industry clusters and 45 economies. It maps the jobs and skills of the future for the 2023–2027 timeframe.

### Labour-market churn
Almost a quarter of jobs (23%) are expected to change in the next five years, through growth of 10.2% and decline of 12.3%. Employers anticipate 69 million new jobs created and 83 million eliminated among the 673 million jobs in the dataset — a net decrease of 14 million jobs, or 2% of current employment. Macrotrends (the green transition, ESG standards, localization of supply chains) are the leading drivers of job growth, while high inflation, slower growth and supply shortages pose the greatest risks.

### Technology and automation
About a third of tasks (34%) are currently automated, just 1% above the 2020 figure. Companies revised down expectations for further automation, to 42% of tasks by 2027 (vs a 2020 estimate of 47% by 2025). AI is expected to be adopted by nearly 75% of surveyed companies and to drive high churn — 50% of organizations expect it to create job growth, 25% expect job losses. Big data tops job-creating technologies (65% expect growth); data analysts/scientists, big data specialists, AI/ML specialists and cybersecurity professionals are expected to grow ~30% by 2027. Digital commerce will drive the largest absolute gains (~2 million new roles).

### Fastest-growing and declining roles
Fastest-growing roles are AI and machine learning specialists, sustainability specialists, business intelligence analysts and information security analysts. The largest absolute growth is expected in education (~3 million jobs, +10%) and agriculture (~4 million jobs, +15–30%). Fastest-declining roles are clerical/secretarial — bank tellers, cashiers, data entry clerks.

### The reskilling revolution
44% of an individual worker's skills will need to be updated on average. Six in 10 workers will require training before 2027, but only half have access to adequate training today. 45% of businesses say government funding for skills training would help connect talent to employment. The most important skills in 2023 are analytical thinking and creative thinking; technological literacy and AI/big data will grow in importance. Two-thirds of companies expect a return on skills-training investment within a year, and 36% recognize higher wages could help attract talent.

### Methodology
The Future of Jobs Survey brings together the perspective of 803 companies (collectively employing more than 11.3 million workers) in 27 industry clusters and 45 economies from all world regions. This is the fourth edition of the report, first launched in 2016.

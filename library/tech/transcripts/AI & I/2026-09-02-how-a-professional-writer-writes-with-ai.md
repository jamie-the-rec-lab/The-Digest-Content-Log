## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Katie Parrott, staff writer at Every and creator of the Compound Writing plugin  ·  **Show:** AI & I, 2 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai, ai product, productivity

**When to use this:** you write a lot of content with AI and the output still feels generic or you keep losing feedback you already gave the model.

**Big idea:** AI writing gets better when you feed it unique context the model cannot already know, not when you ask it to write from its own general knowledge.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Build a source of truth document per project the way a content marketing team builds a style guide, covering who the audience is, their pain points, how the product maps to those pain points, and the competitive differentiators, then feed that in before asking for any writing.
- Treat the model's outputs as commoditised unless you supply the "unique insight": a proprietary study, third party research, or a personal experience the model could not already know, since AI's knowledge cutoff means it cannot supply anything that happened or was learned after that date.
- Have AI interview you to build your own context files rather than writing them from scratch. Prompting it with "ask me questions to draw out what I think about this" was how she built her career coach project.
- Fork an existing structured plugin rather than designing a process from zero. She adapted Kieran Klassen's Compound Engineering plugin into Compound Writing by mapping its brainstorm, plan, work and review stages onto a writing equivalent of brainstorm, outline, draft and two separate review passes, a structural edit and a line edit.
- Turn a specific writer's known method into a reusable review lens or "skill" rather than a vague style note. She built a skill based on Kurt Vonnegut's eight rules of story structure and one based on Hitchcock's principle of suspense, applying each as a separate editing pass.

**Contrarian take:**
- Katie Parrott argues non-professional writers may get more value from a structured AI writing system than professionals, because they have fewer pre-existing opinions to fight the tool on.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before your next AI-assisted piece of writing, write a short brief covering audience, pain points and your unique angle first, and only then ask for a draft.
- If you keep giving the same style feedback to an AI tool, save that feedback as a standing instruction so you never repeat it.

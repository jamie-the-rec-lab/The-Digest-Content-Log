## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Sam Altman, who runs OpenAI, interviewed by Dan Shipper of Every at OpenAI DevDay in the first episode after AI & I was rebranded as the Every Podcast  ·  **Show:** AI & I, 30 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai, ai agents, ai product, productivity, future of work, startups

**When to use this:** you want a first-hand account of how a very busy executive uses an always-on AI agent, and what he says about work and startups.

**Big idea:** Altman says people stay at the centre of AI work because we care about the people behind things, and describes "dots", a persistent agent, as the first tool that gave him back his mornings.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Let the agent triage overnight. Altman says he used to check his phone first thing and lose his deep work period, and now trusts his dot to decide what is urgent and handle the rest.
- Dump raw ideas by voice note. He jotted notes throughout the day and left a long voice note while drifting off, and the dot built five or six versions of a feature overnight and carried out the next steps.
- Let the agent keep looking. After he gave up searching Slack for something, the dot kept searching overnight and found it was a screenshot, not text.
- Cross-check context: Dan Shipper's dot caught that a flight rescheduling clashed with a meeting it had read about in Slack, not on his calendar.
- Use "live documents": a dashboard that constantly rewrites itself from his Slack messages as his priorities change, and docs that several people's dots work on together.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- OpenAI launched 22 new things at this DevDay, against about nine the year before, as counted by the interviewer, and said it cut some to fit in 50 minutes.
- He claims 10,000 agents worked in coordination for 4 days on a data centre problem.
- On speed, he says 8x faster at a great price is coming, followed by 100x, and calls the ultrafast model his default for prompting.

**Contrarian take:**
- Altman says the "OpenAI killed my startup" story "never actually happens", and that he sees startups succeeding faster than he has ever seen. He calls the Renaissance (people) versus Industrial Revolution (machines) the right frame.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Pick a recurring daily scan (inbound candidate replies, overnight job-board alerts) and ask your AI assistant to tell you only what is urgent, then check it for a week.
- End your day with a voice note of loose ideas and have an assistant turn them into a draft overnight.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Willie Williams, head of platform at Every and a driving force behind the Every Agent, interviewed by Dan Shipper (Every's CEO, who is promoting a product he sells)  ·  **Show:** AI & I, 7 Oct 2026
**Tags:** ai agents, ai product, engineering, management, productivity, future of work

**When to use this:** you are deciding whether a company should give every employee a personal AI agent or run one shared agent.

**Big idea:** Personal agents spread fast among tinkerers but are hard to maintain and secure, so a shared company agent that accumulates context beats many individual agents at work, while personal agents may thrive at home.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Shipper listed Dots use cases at Every (about 30 people): scanning school emails, catching missed Slack access requests, working by voice while away from a computer, and filtering noise so he is told only about replies to experiments he posted in Slack.
- Williams's lesson from the OpenClaw phase: seeing colleagues' agents in Slack taught people prompting tricks, but non-technical staff hit a wall of setup and security, and agents decayed when owners lost interest (his own agent went offline when a Mac mini was switched off and he did not restart it).
- A "claw as a service" platform was a mistake, he said, because OpenClaw moved fast, was largely vibe coded and was not built to be a platform.
- A shared agent concentrates effort. One person's growth agent became the "gravitational center" as others added to it rather than building their own.
- Williams predicted a split: a company agent at work, with context built over a year, and a personal agent at home. Teams may later get their own sub-agents.
- As an engineering manager he uses an agent to consolidate Slack, email and customer reports into a single feed (using Shipper's open-source Tend app) and an agent that checks dashboards every five minutes for anomalies and routes alerts, acting as first on call.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Shipper said Every has about 30 people; Williams led an engineering team he described as about five to seven people. No other figures were given.

**Tools & prompts:**
- OpenAI Dots (persistent agents), OpenClaw, the Every Agent, Tend, Codex, Grok Bot. Product claims come from the vendor's own team.

**Contrarian take:**
- Shipper expects named personal agents to be short lived, saying his agent "Boo" will probably be dead within a year because people drop a character once something performs better. Williams disagreed on personalisation, expecting personal agents to be very attractive once security improves.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before approving individual agent pilots, ask whether one shared team agent with documented context would serve the group better, and who will maintain it.

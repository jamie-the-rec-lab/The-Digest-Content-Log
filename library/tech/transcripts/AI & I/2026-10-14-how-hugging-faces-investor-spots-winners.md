## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Matt Hartman, founder and managing partner of Factorial Capital, formerly a partner at Betaworks where he helped write the first cheque into Hugging Face, interviewed by Natalia Quintero (Every's head of AI consulting); the episode is introduced by Dan Shipper  ·  **Show:** AI & I (The Every Podcast), 14 Oct 2026
**Tags:** fundraising, founder stories, startups, ai, ai product, product management, engineering

**When to use this:** you are judging early-stage tech founders or products, or deciding whether to vibe code your own internal tool.

**Big idea:** Back technical founders because their pivot is toward the technology rather than a single market, and look for earned insights from people who can iterate faster than competitors.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Always assume an early company will pivot and ask what it can pivot toward. Hartman's pivot foot analogy: technical teams can move across markets with the data shape or technology, while a team of industry executives tends to stay in that industry.
- Judge founders on their "earned insight", meaning how few people were in a position to learn the thing they learned, and on whether they keep generating insight once the product is in market.
- Find the "newly technically possible" by reading papers, tinkering and asking smart people, expecting the final thesis to be about 20 degrees off from where you started.
- Hugging Face's path as he described it: retention on the teen chatbot app was poor and the team was honest about it, they converted a transformer paper to PyTorch and open sourced it, moved off GitHub to host model weights, and stayed flexible on model types while holding open source constant.
- His fund uses a solo general partner plus venture partners who are founders he backed, and portfolio referrals became a main deal source.
- Product insight is more valuable if building is fast. He suggested the job of understanding how users behave and turning it into the next version matters more when a cycle takes two days rather than two months.
- Vibe coded tools carry maintenance cost. His Ghost Pepper local voice-to-text app reached number one on Hacker News and over 3,000 GitHub stars, but he said it gave him a new respect for paid SaaS. Quintero moved her vibe coded CRM to Attio for the same reason.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- The episode states Nvidia bought Hugging Face last month for $13 billion (the show notes say "set to be acquired"; the figure is as stated on air, not independently checked here).
- In Shipper's intro, from Every's testing: Meta's Muse Wonder scored about 50% on his senior engineer benchmark, which he called middle of the pack and "bench maxed"; Google's Gemini 4 was his favourite new writing model but scored about 20% on that coding task.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Superpowers skill by Jesse Vincent for Claude Code and Codex, Ghost Pepper, Monologue (Every's voice-to-text tool), Ben's Bites and the Every newsletter as sources (the host's own product is plugged).

**Contrarian take:**
- Hartman said he expects AI to turn out bigger than the internet and mobile, and that fewer, deeper relationships worked better than a growing cohort of venture partners.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before building an internal tool, write down who will maintain it in six months; if the answer is you, price the paid alternative.

## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Tim Simmons of Theoretically Media, guest hosting with Gavin Purcell  ·  **Show:** AI For Humans, 07 Aug 2026
**Tags:** ai, ai product, ai coding

**When to use this:** You need to compare the current crop of AI video models, or want a worked example of catching and reversing a vendor's problematic terms of service before it costs users their content rights.

**Big idea:** A new wave of AI video models, Seedance 2.5, MiniMax H3 (Hailuo), Wan 3.0 and Flux 3, launched within roughly the same week, and all but Flux 3 are Chinese, prompting the hosts to question whether US labs like Google are stepping back from the AI video frontier entirely.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Read a platform's terms of service before generating anything with real value on it. Tim Simmons found Higgsfield's updated terms initially granted the platform an open licence to reuse anything a user generated as promotional material, and separately implied rights to use a user's likeness from photo-based generations. He fed the terms to an AI legal review (Claude, GPT-5 branded "Fable", and another model he calls "Ultracode") before publishing a breakdown, and public pressure got Higgsfield to revise the terms.
- MiniMax H3 (Hailuo) runs on local hardware and is comparatively uncensored, positioning it as a local alternative to hosted models like Sora 2 for anyone wanting to avoid a platform's content filters or usage terms.
- Wan 3.0 can take text documents, decks and spreadsheets as generation inputs, not just prompts or images, which the hosts flag as an open-ended new interface worth testing for structured, data-driven video generation.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Wan 3.0 is described as roughly a 60 billion parameter model, too large to run fully locally on typical consumer hardware.
- Demis Hassabis is stepping down as CEO of DeepMind to become chairman focused on the future of AI, and Jeff Dean is leaving Google after 27 years to start what the hosts describe as a recursive-intelligence company (Discovery Loop), read by some observers as a signal Google may be deprioritising the AI video frontier.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Higgsfield is an aggregator platform that wraps APIs from Seedance, Nano Banana, Kling and others into one interface; Tim Simmons calls the UI well designed but the spending flow "built like a Vegas casino," worth factoring in before committing budget there over going direct to a model provider.
- Flux 3, the one non-Chinese frontier-adjacent video model discussed, is available via Fal and is expected to eventually ship open-weight versions.

**Contrarian take:**
- Tim Simmons compares the current rush to accept AI platform terms of service to the early social media era, where users traded data rights for free access and, in his words, "we lost that fight," arguing creators are at risk of repeating the same mistake with AI video platforms.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Before generating anything with a client's likeness or brand assets on a new AI video platform, check its terms of service for content-reuse and likeness clauses first, following the pattern Tim Simmons used to get Higgsfield to walk back its terms.

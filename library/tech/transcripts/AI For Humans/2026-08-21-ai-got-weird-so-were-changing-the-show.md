## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Kevin Pereira and Gavin Purcell, hosts  ·  **Show:** AI For Humans, 21 Aug 2026
**Tags:** ai, ai agents, ai coding

**When to use this:** You want real, worked examples of using a voice-mode or agentic assistant to automate a tedious documentation task, rather than another model launch recap.

**Big idea:** After three years and 187 episodes, the hosts announce they are moving back to a weekly cadence and shifting away from covering every dot-release and lab-drama story, choosing instead to show practical, worked examples of what current AI tools can actually do for an ordinary user.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Gavin had Claude Opus 5 handle a real task end to end: find four specific YouTube videos with ads on his channel, grab six screenshots from them, pull two or three positive comments from each, and package everything into folders ready to upload, all from a single instruction, completed in about half an hour.
- Kevin used GPT's voice mode to audit a mobile app: he screen-mirrored his desktop, said "snap that" whenever he wanted a screenshot taken, spoke his notes aloud in a stream of consciousness, and had the assistant keep the whole session in context, then output a single HTML file with screenshots and cleaned-up, timestamped notes inline, replacing what he says is normally a multi-hour manual process of screenshotting and documenting.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- The World Humanoid Robot Games opens in Beijing with a reported two thousand humanoid robots taking part.
- A Unitree robot nicknamed "Superman" is shown clearing a standing high jump of 2 metres and separately sprinting faster than Usain Bolt, though it crashes into a locker at the end of the sprint.

**Tools & prompts:**
- will.i.am, appearing as a guest voice, proposes a new file format he calls the "proof of life" file (.POL), intended to certify that a human's biometric effort (such as a drummer's physical motion) went into a piece of AI-assisted music or content, distinct from a file generated entirely by an agent; the hosts are openly sceptical of how it would work in practice.

**Contrarian take:**
- The hosts admit they fell into what Gavin calls a trap: treating every incremental model release as something they had to cover, and say that chasing benchmark deltas mattered less to viewers than showing what a tool can actually do for someone's real work, which is the reasoning behind the show's format change.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- If you need to document a workflow, app, or bug report quickly, try screen-mirroring into a voice-mode assistant and narrating notes aloud while cueing screenshots verbally, then having it compile a single annotated file, the same pattern Kevin used for his app audit.

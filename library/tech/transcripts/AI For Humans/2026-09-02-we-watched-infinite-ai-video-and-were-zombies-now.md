## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Kevin Pereira and Gavin Purcell, hosts  ·  **Show:** AI For Humans, 02 Sep 2026
**Tags:** ai, ai coding, ai agents, ai product

**When to use this:** You need a plain-English read on how fast real-time AI video generation is moving, and what "infinite" AI content and agentic operating systems mean in practice right now.

**Big idea:** Fal's MiniMax H3 Max model now generates a 5 to 15 second video clip in about 3 to 5 seconds, faster than the clip plays back, which has made continuous, never-ending AI video streams (Fal Live, Levels.io's Infinite Slop, Reactor.in) technically possible for the first time.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Interactive branching video is being built by individual creators, not just labs: an X user called Blendy open-sourced a GitHub project (blendy-reade/interdimensional-game) that others, including Gavin, forked with Claude Code to build their own branching video experiences on top of MiniMax H3 Max.
- Generating three branching video pathways in real time so a viewer only sees one is expensive by design, since the two unseen branches are still fully rendered and discarded; budget for roughly 3x the cost of a single linear generation.
- Fal is giving away 10 free MiniMax H3 Max generations per day, but restricts recognisable celebrity likenesses and IP.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Gavin spent $50 of the show's Patreon money on Fal credits building his interactive demo and burned through it, at a running cost of roughly $15 per full playthrough, which he states plainly is not viable as a real product yet.
- A separate company, Talis, built on chip work from AMD, claims tens of thousands of tokens a second on its chatjimmy.ai product, versus the dozens of tokens a second typical of standard LLM output.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Runway's newly shown Solaris model does real-time generative interface design, demoed by letting a user drag a piece of clothing onto a photo of themselves and having the interface reflow around that action rather than following hardcoded UI.
- 37signals founder DHH has open-sourced an agentic operating system called Omarchy (the hosts are unsure of the exact pronunciation) that lets users ask the OS itself to create new features or keyboard shortcuts rather than waiting on a vendor.

**Contrarian take:**
- Gavin argues Fal and Magnific's free-but-queued rollout of a genuinely fast model does more brand damage than simply charging a small fee for guaranteed capacity, a criticism he repeats from the prior week's episode.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- If your team wants to prototype an AI-driven interactive experience, the blendy-reade/interdimensional-game GitHub repo plus a coding agent is a working starting point rather than building the video pipeline from scratch.
- Watch for OpenAI's rumoured Astra model this week as the next capability jump; leaked demos reportedly include a Terraria clone generated in one shot, worth testing against your own use cases once it ships.

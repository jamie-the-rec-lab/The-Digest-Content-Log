**When to use this:** you want a plain-language reaction to the week Opus 5.5 and the cheaper GPT 6 models launched, and two useful phrases for bad AI habits at work.

**Big idea:** the hosts say Opus 5.5 is close to Anthropic's top model on real tasks at lower cost, and use the week to argue that taste and judgement are what separate good AI output from slop.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Use a cascade of models: one host uses the strongest model (Astra) to plan or handle hard parts, hands the coding to the mid-tier model, and uses the cheapest (Luna) for quick tasks.
- Do not be a "meat proxy": a human who passes an agent's output to someone else, who runs it through their own agent. The host warns this speed-runs your own obsolescence.
- Do not throw a "slop grenade": flinging an unread, verbose AI output "over the fence" so it becomes someone else's problem.
- To make a 3D character, generate five reference images (front, side, full body) with an image model, feed them to an image to 3D tool (Meshy 7.1), then ask the coding model to put the rigged result in a game. It took three or four iterations.
- For motion graphics, one host gave Astra a 5-second clip and asked it to place words behind and in front of the speaker, matching her words.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- The hosts compare 100 million input tokens plus 20 million output tokens at about $800 on Opus 5.5 versus about $20 on Luna.
- A teaching simulation built through the API was described as a one-shot build costing about $25, taking 1 hour and 26 minutes.

**Contrarian take:**
- They say new models are mind-blowing in week one, and suspect providers dial back compute later, which one host calls only "anecdotal" rather than proven. They add that the Shopify CEO reportedly told staff to "AI all the things" and then asked for humans back.

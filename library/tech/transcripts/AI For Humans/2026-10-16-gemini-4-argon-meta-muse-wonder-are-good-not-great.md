**When to use this:** you want a plain-spoken, hands-on read of where Google and Meta now sit against Anthropic and OpenAI, or a funny example of AI agents making phone calls on a person's behalf.

**Big idea:** New frontier models are judged less by benchmarks than by whether they change what one person can do in a week. Gavin said "benchmarks feel like they're kind of like BS personally", and Opus 5.5 remains his daily driver.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Gavin's model comparison method: give the same one-shot prompt (a golf "long drive contest" browser game) to two models and compare. His verdict: Opus 5.5 felt "better and more full"; Argon made some more interesting creative choices.
- Kevin's point that results mix model and harness: you are seeing the system around the model and how much thinking time and compute it gets, not just raw intelligence.
- Kevin's wish list: not a step change in intelligence but a model with today's capability that is "a thousand times faster, a thousand times cheaper", so a hundred sub-agents can work on a problem.
- Gavin's AI documentary workflow: connect an agent to a Runway or similar MCP so it can drive video, audio and image tools, give it a script, and check in. He said it produced a rough 30 minute cut over 6 to 7 hours while he was away, followed by bug fixing.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- Google's demo (as described by the hosts): Argon 4 watched video of cornhole, ran simulations with a simulated humanoid robot, then applied them to a real robot, which eventually sank the bag.

## Episode description

AI news this week: Google's Gemini 4 Argon and Meta's Muse Wonder are finally here but... do they beat Claude Opus 5.5?

On today's AI For Humans, Kevin Pereira and Gavin Purcell dig into two new frontier AI models that are pretty good, but maybe not great. Gemini 4 Argon shines on science and the pelican on a bike test (the Fourier epicycle pelican is wild), but some early reviewers say it hallucinates on code a lot. Meta's Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full are the company's first real swing at the frontier, with big memory and coding claims, but they struggle with 3D worlds. Gavin runs a one-prompt game test, we watch a Google robot figure out cornhole and, yes, Mark Zuckerberg trying a Guinness is def the best video of the week.

Also: gaming is going all in on AI. Xbox launched Xbox Labs to explore the wave of AI game mods and decompiles, Tim Sweeney says Epic is moving developers around to retool for AI, and we check out DLSS 5 and Diablo Survivors.

Plus: quick tools, including Claude Motion, Mirelo's SFX 2.0 sound effects for video and Slowbooks Pro (coming for your accountant, in a good way). Then Kevin puts an AI phone agent on the line with Delta customer service, and it goes about as well as you'd hope (it will not hang up). In AI See What You Did There: The Little Prince as a website, a century of visual styles, Claude getting ready for Halloween and The Usual Suspects. And Gavin shares how he made his second AI documentary with his agent Fig (yes, Imogen is back).

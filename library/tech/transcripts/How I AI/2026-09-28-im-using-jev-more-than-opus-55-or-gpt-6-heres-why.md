**When to use this:** you have a large pile of unstructured text to classify, cluster or route and frontier-model costs make it impractical.

**Big idea:** a cheap, fast "decision" model that returns only a choice, a rating or a yes or no probability makes classification at scale almost free, and works best paired with a smarter model that reasons over the resulting clusters.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Use it for one of three outputs: a selection from options, a rating on a scale from one to five, or a probability of yes. She gave the bug triage example of rating a bug as cosmetic, broken or blocking, and the model only ever picks from values you predefine.
- Cluster by asking pairwise questions. Claire Vo had the model ask whether two pull requests are about the same theme, then had a cheap model (Gemini Flash Lite) label the clusters.
- Combine models by job. In her product insights graph the decision model classified roughly 1,100 signals, a stronger model analysed the clusters, and a generation model wrote the output, which she said unlocked a product that brute-forcing with frontier models had not.
- Run a meta-analysis of your own local Claude Code and Codex sessions. She found engineering work fell from almost all her sessions in January to under 40 percent by September, as publishing and customer delivery grew.
- Triage email by feeding only subject and snippet and asking whether it can be deleted, then let another model handle the rest.
- Check latency before trusting a viral demo. She built a live voice app that maps mood to a colour and quote and notes the delay came from the quotes API, not the model.

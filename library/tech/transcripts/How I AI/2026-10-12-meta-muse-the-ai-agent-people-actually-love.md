## Actionable summary
**Guest:** Alex Cornell, design director at Meta leading design on Muse, Meta's consumer AI agent (he is promoting his own employer's product), interviewed by Claire Vo  ·  **Show:** How I AI, 12 Oct 2026 (sponsored by WorkOS)
**Tags:** ai agents, ai product, product design, user research, productivity, general

**When to use this:** you are designing or choosing an agent interface for non-technical users, or want ideas for personal automations on saved data.

**Big idea:** Design an agent as an entity you exchange messages with, expose complexity only on demand, and use generation for content inside stable surfaces rather than a fully different interface for every user.

**Frameworks & plays:**
- Frame the interface as an exchange with an entity, borrowing messaging patterns (bubbles, an embodied avatar at the top, proactive messages) instead of the conventions of earlier AI apps.
- Use a three-layer status display so each person gets the detail they want: top line says it is working, tapping shows what it is doing (an emoji and a short label), tapping further shows the full list of actions, and a final layer shows the code and requests for auditing. Chain-of-thought was kept out of the main chat.
- Acknowledge hard requests immediately with a reaction or short message, then work in the background, the way a person would say "I'll be back when I have it done".
- Keep stable surfaces and generate content inside them: personalised ideas based on what you have discussed, a prompt-defined feed you can change, and avatar states generated from the user's own avatar.
- Use one canonical main chat with optional side chats, because a single thread suits one entity; he said how people organise this remains an unsolved area.
- Personal use cases he showed: having the agent organise Instagram saves, then render shopping suggestions as a 1990s SkyMall-style catalogue that refreshes daily; weekly checks on whether wishlist items are discounted; monitoring for artist releases and tour dates; consolidating hundreds of saved golf tips into a manual; shot-by-shot round recaps and practice session breakdowns; a chess.com dashboard with trend and game review; and a daily podcast reading from his 20-year-old blog.

**Data & benchmarks:**
- No quantitative claims were given. The host said Muse was the fastest route to delight she had had with an AI product, which is opinion.

**Tools & prompts:**
- Muse with the Instagram connector, Pinterest login, golf watch and practice data, chess.com data. He defines tasks in chat (for example asking for both analysis and trends over time plus a review of the latest game).

**Contrarian take:**
- Designers must accept non-deterministic output: he said each person's Muse is configured differently, so you design the foundation and scaffolding and stop expecting pixel-perfect artifacts.

**Steal this (do tomorrow):**
- Ask any agent you use to consolidate something you save but never reread (notes, saved posts, tips) into one document or a short daily audio summary.
